## How to use it

1. Go group by group. Tap **Yes** or **No** on each line. Tap the same button again to clear it. If a line does not apply to your outlet, leave it blank.
2. A **No** shows the MDG clause, the circular's point number where there is one, and the class of irregularity. The count at the bottom updates as you go.
3. Your No answers are listed under the checklist, the most serious class first. Start there.

Your answers are kept only in this browser on this device. Nothing is sent to us. If you clear your browser data or use a private window, they are gone. The **Clear all answers** button removes them at any time.

## Where the lists come from

- **The 20 minimum service obligations.** A circular dated 20.03.2025, issued under MDG clause 5.1.18, lists them. It is a joint format for the oil companies and sets no penalty of its own {{circular}} {{5.1.18}}. The lines above are our own words, grouped by area rather than in the circular's order. The circular's point number is shown on each NO. Point 7, the toilet protocol, is spread over nine lines, taken from the checks in clause 5.1.14(b) {{5.1.14(b)}}. See the [20 service obligations in detail](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations).
- **The records.** Six kinds of record are listed in clause 5.1.12 {{5.1.12}}. See [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep).
- **The daily checks.** They come from Chapter 1: the morning density {{1.4.1(c)}}, the density after a tanker {{1.2(xiv)}}, the water dip {{1.4.1(b)}}, the seals {{1.4.2(a)}}, the 5 litre measure and the nozzle test {{1.4.2(b)}} {{1.4.2(d)}}, the leak check {{1.4.2(e)}} and the testing equipment {{1.5(vi)}}.

This list does not cover every irregularity in Chapter 5. Stock variation, overcharging, unauthorised purchases or sales, tampering with a dispensing unit and automation, for example, have their own clauses {{5.1.11}} {{5.1.13}} {{5.1.6}} {{5.1.4}} {{5.1.16}}. The oil company may also send later circulars, and MDG counts them as part of the service standard {{1.6(xii)}} {{5.1.18}}.

## What the class beside a No means

MDG sorts every irregularity in Chapter 5 into critical, major or minor {{8.1}}. The minimum action for each:

| Class | Minimum action in MDG | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Termination of the dealership at the first instance | {{8.2}} |
| Major, most cases | Suspension of sales and supplies for 15 days the first time, 30 days the second time, termination the third time | {{8.3}} |
| Minor | A warning-cum-guidance letter the first time, Rs 10,000 per irregularity the second time, Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third time | {{8.4}} |

- The amounts and steps are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action {{8.5.1}}.
- An irregularity must be established before any action is taken {{8.5.2}}.
- The second and third instance are counted over the previous five years, and irregularities under the previous guideline count too {{8.5.5}}. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).
- Several of the minor irregularities listed in 8.4(iii)(a) to (h) found together count as one for the purpose of action {{8.4}}.
- A few major irregularities have different actions {{8.3}}. The [penalties page](@penalties) has them.

Where the note beside a NO says Chapter 8 names no class for that exact check, MDG gives none. Irregularities it does not name are dealt with by the oil company after an enquiry, in line with the principles of natural justice {{8.5.4}}.

A NO here is not the same as an irregularity. A missing item becomes an irregularity only when an inspector finds it and the oil company establishes it.

## What the count does not mean

The count is for your own use. The circular records each of the 20 points as YES or NO and sets no score or pass mark {{circular}}. This page adds up your own answers so you can see what is left to fix. It does not predict what an inspector will find.

> **Note:** a NO on a seal, on the morning density or on a record can matter far more than ten minor items. Read the class beside each NO, not only the count.
