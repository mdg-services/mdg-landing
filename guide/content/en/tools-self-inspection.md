## How to use it

1. Go group by group and tap **Yes** or **No**. Tap again to clear a line. Leave blank any line that does not apply.
2. A **No** shows the MDG clause, the circular's point number where it has one, and the class.
3. Your No answers are listed under the checklist, most serious first. Start there.

## Where the lists come from

- **Service obligations:** [the 20 in detail](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations). The circular sets no penalty of its own {{circular}}; a shortfall attracts the minor penalties {{5.1.18}}.
- **Records:** [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep) {{5.1.12}}.
- **Daily checks:** Chapter 1 of MDG, for example the morning density {{1.4.1(c)}} and the seals {{1.4.2(a)}}.

## What the class beside a No means

MDG places every Chapter 5 irregularity in one of three classes {{8.1}}:

| Class | Where the minimum action starts |
|---|---|
| Critical | Termination at the first instance {{8.2}} |
| Major | Mostly suspension of sales and supplies, 15 days at the first instance; two items have other actions {{8.3}} |
| Minor | A warning-cum-guidance letter, then money penalties {{8.4}} |

{{penalty-note}}

See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid). Where the note beside a NO says Chapter 8 names no class, the oil company decides the action after an enquiry {{8.5.4}}.

## What the count does not mean

The count only adds up your own answers. The circular sets no score or pass mark {{circular}}, and a NO is not an irregularity until the oil company establishes it {{8.5.2}}. The list also leaves out other Chapter 5 irregularities, such as stock variation and overcharging {{5.1.11}} {{5.1.13}}, so all YES does not mean a clean inspection.

> **Note:** a NO on a seal, on the morning density or on a record can matter far more than ten minor items. Read the class beside each NO, not only the count.
