## What the oil company supplies

All of it is free of cost {{2.6(B)}} {{2.7}} {{2.8}}.

| Item | Clause |
|---|---|
| One-litre aluminium containers with wooden boxes | {{2.6(A)}} {{2.6(B)}} |
| Random-numbered plastic seals | {{2.6(B)}} {{2.7}} |
| ISI-marked stainless steel sealing wire | {{2.6(B)}} {{2.7}} |
| Sealing pliers | {{2.6(B)}} {{2.7}} |
| Printed Annexure 3 labels, in sufficient numbers | {{2.8}} |

The company may also add seals or holograms {{2.7}}. It must position an adequate number of these at your outlet {{2.6(B)}}.

## What you do

- **Ask in time.** The dealer asks the company for the materials {{2.6(B)}}.
- **Count what a load needs.** Each tank lorry needs 2 x 1 litre of MS and/or 1 x 1 litre of HSD, drawn before unloading {{2.3}}. See [the tank lorry retention sample](@sampling-retention). For example, a lorry with both MS and HSD needs 2 + 1 = 3 empty containers.
- **Expect a replacement.** When the company's representative or a mobile lab takes containers from you, he replaces them with empty standard ones {{2.6(B)}}.

For company-owned tank lorries, the supply location keeps a stock of containers for when the lorry supplies your outlet {{2.6 Note}}. MDG gives no more detail on this.

## What MDG does not say

MDG sets no re-order quantity, no number of days for "in time", no way of asking, and no step for a lorry that arrives when the containers have run out. General practice, not an MDG rule: note how many empty containers, seals and labels are left at each receipt, ask in writing before the stock falls below what the next delivery needs, and speak to your sales officer before decanting if you have none.
