## The steps and the preferred times

MDG words each time below as "preferably", and also says the time frame is to be adhered to {{2.10}}.

| Step | Preferred time | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Samples are coded and reach the lab | Within 10 days of drawal | {{2.10}} {{2.5(D)}} |
| The lab tests them | Within the next 15 days | {{2.10}} |
| The oil company tells you the result, pass or fail | Within 5 days of getting the lab report | {{2.10}} |
| All of it | About 30 days from drawal | {{2.5(F)}} |
| Special drive: samples reach the lab | Within 15 days of drawal | {{2.10}} |
| Special drive: testing is finished | Within 30 days of the lab receiving them | {{2.10}} |
| Mobile lab, failed sample: sets handed to the oil company's representative | Within 5 days | {{2.4.4}} |

For example (made-up dates): on the preferred times, samples drawn on 3 March reach the lab by 13 March, are tested by 28 March, and you hear by 2 April {{2.10}}.

## If everything passes

The oil company tells you in writing, and the retained samples can be disposed of {{2.5(F)}}. After a mobile lab check that passes, no further action is taken and the balance samples go back to your outlet {{2.4.4}} {{7.6}}.

## If a sample fails or a notice follows

- To have your own retained sample re-tested, ask within 10 days of getting the lab report. See [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed) {{2.5(H)}}.
- Where samples were drawn at an inspection, the show cause notice runs from the test results {{8.5.6}}. See [the show cause notice](@inspection-show-cause-notice).

## What MDG does not say

MDG does not say what follows if a preferred time is missed, or how to chase a late result. General practice, not an MDG rule: write to your oil company with the date the samples were drawn and ask where they stand.

General information, not legal advice. For your own notice, speak to an advocate or your oil company.
