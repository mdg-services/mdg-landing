## The checklist

| Item | Why | What MDG says |
|---|---|---|
| Calibrated hydrometers | Read the density of each sample | Mandatory, calibrated, in good working condition, shown on demand {{1.5(vi)}} |
| Calibrated thermometer | Gives the temperature that goes with the reading | Same clause {{1.5(vi)}}; temperature is recorded with density {{1.2(viii)}} |
| ASTM density conversion table (Table 53B) | Brings the reading to 15°C | {{1.5(vi)}} {{1.2(iv)}}. See the [15°C calculator](@tools-density-at-15) |
| Clear glass container | Holds the sample taken from each tanker compartment after about 20 litres are drained | {{1.2(viii)}} |
| Density register | Where every result is written | Annexure 1 format {{1.2(xiv)}} {{annexure 1}}. See [the register](@density-register) |
| Whatman 2 filter paper | Petrol quality check, handed to a customer who asks | {{1.4.1(a)}}. See [the filter paper test](@density-filter-paper) |
| Water finding paste | Water in each tanker compartment; a suitable one for ethanol blends | {{1.2(vii)}} |

## Which hydrometers

MDG gives the fuel ranges at 15°C {{7.6}} but no hydrometer scale. The reading is taken at the sample's own temperature, which shifts it. Worked out with the 15°C calculator, a petrol of 720 to 775 at 15°C reads about 697 to 779 between 10°C and 40°C; a diesel of 810 to 845 reads about 792 to 848.

General practice, not an MDG rule: choose hydrometers whose scales cover those readings, and ask your oil company which set it accepts.

## What to check

General practice, not an MDG rule:

- **Hydrometer:** no crack or chip, scale readable, stem clean.
- **Thermometer:** the liquid column is unbroken and the marks are legible.
- **Table:** on hand and readable. MDG names no edition, so ask your oil company which one it uses.
- **Papers:** keep any calibration paper you are given. MDG sets no format.

## If something is missing or broken

- **No named penalty.** MDG's irregularity lists do not name missing density equipment. For unlisted matters the oil company acts after an enquiry {{8.5.4}}.
- **The records still fall due.** Without the instruments there is no morning or after-tanker density. A missing reference density at an inspection stops sales and supplies of every product and samples are drawn. It is a major irregularity {{5.1.9}} {{8.3}}, and a failed sample brings the action for adulteration {{5.1.9}}. See [the 10 AM rule](@density-reference-10am).
- **The circular's kit line.** The inspector marks "Q&Q kit" YES or NO {{circular}}. MDG does not say what it holds, so ask your oil company whether your density set counts.
