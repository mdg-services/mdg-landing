## What MDG says about the notice

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers, applied through the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. This page sets out what they say about the steps after an irregularity.

MDG says a show cause notice is issued in all cases of irregularities, and that it must indicate all of them {{8.5.6}}. The notice goes out together with the reports and other documents it rests on {{8.5.6}}. The dealer then has 10 days to reply {{8.5.7}}.

No action can be taken against a dealer until the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}. For an irregularity MDG does not list, the company acts after an enquiry, following the principles of natural justice {{8.5.4}}.

## The clock, step by step

| Step | Time allowed | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Show cause notice issued | Within 30 days of the inspection. If petrol or diesel samples were drawn, within 30 days of the test results. | {{8.5.6}} |
| What comes with it | All the reports and other documents the notice rests on | {{8.5.6}} |
| The dealer's reply | 10 days from the date the notice is issued | {{8.5.7}} |
| Speaking order | Preferably within 45 days of receiving the reply | {{8.5.8}} |
| Personal hearing | Before termination is recommended or approved, for critical irregularities | {{8.6}} |
| Fine, where the action is a fine only | Within 30 days of the date of the company's notice | {{8.7}} |

The 45 days for the speaking order is a target: the clause says "preferably" {{8.5.8}}. The 30 days for the notice and the 10 days for the reply are not worded as targets {{8.5.6}} {{8.5.7}}.

The oil company's authorised officer reviews the charges and the reply, then passes the speaking order. It is to set out the irregularities in full, the dealer's reply, and detailed reasons why the reply is or is not acceptable {{8.5.8}}.

## An illustration with made-up dates

For example, an inspection on 5 October 2026 finds a minor irregularity that is a second instance, so the listed action is a Rs 10,000 fine {{8.4}}. No samples are drawn. The amount is the minimum MDG lists, and the company may act more strongly {{8.5.1}}. The second-instance count looks back five years and includes the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}.

| Date | What happens | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| 5 Oct 2026 | Inspection | |
| 20 Oct 2026 | In this example, the notice is issued with its reports. The latest date allowed was 4 Nov 2026, which is 30 days after the inspection. | {{8.5.6}} |
| 28 Oct 2026 | In this example, the reply is sent and received. It was due by 30 Oct 2026, which is 10 days after the notice was issued. | {{8.5.7}} |
| 12 Dec 2026 | The speaking order is passed, preferably by this date: 45 days after the reply is received. | {{8.5.8}} |
| 15 Dec 2026 | In this example, the company's notice of the fine is dated this day. | {{8.7}} |
| 14 Jan 2027 | Last day to pay: 30 days after the date of that notice. | {{8.7}} |
| 15 Jan 2027 | If the fine is still unpaid, the 15-day suspension of sales and supplies starts, on the 31st day. | {{8.7}} |

If samples had been drawn on 5 October and the test results were dated 4 November, the notice would be due within 30 days of 4 November, which is 4 December 2026 {{8.5.6}}.

MDG does not say how the days are counted or whether the first day is the day of the notice.

## Some action comes before the notice

Some findings stop sales on the spot, and samples are drawn and tested. Action follows the test result:

- The right density figure is missing at inspection: sales and supplies of all products stop {{5.1.9}}.
- Positive stock variation beyond the limit: sales and supplies of all products stop {{5.1.11}}.
- A tampered W&M seal: sales through that dispensing unit stop, and the unit is sealed {{5.1.2}}.
- A density or filter paper failure: sales and supplies of all products stop {{2.5(K)}}.

For a toilet finding, clause 5.1.14(b) says a letter listing the penalty under MDG is issued instantly {{5.1.14(b)}}. MDG does not say whether that letter takes the place of a show cause notice.

## The hearing before termination

Where a critical irregularity may lead to termination, the head of the oil company's State office, State Office (Retail) or Zonal office, or a nominee, gives a personal hearing to the dealership's signatories or their nominees. This comes before termination is recommended or approved {{8.6}}.

If they stay away on the date fixed, they get one more chance. If that is missed too, the matter can go ahead without them (ex parte), on the facts available {{8.6}}. Clause 8.6 speaks of critical irregularities only. The appeal in clause 8.9 is open only against a termination {{8.9}}. See [what MDG says after a termination order](@inspection-appeal).

## Paying a fine

This applies where the action for a major or minor irregularity is only a fine, with no suspension, such as the Rs 10,000 and Rs 25,000 minor fines {{8.4}} {{8.7}}. As with other amounts in MDG, these are minimum actions, and the company may act more strongly {{8.5.1}}.

1. The fine is due within 30 days of the date of the company's notice.
2. If it is not paid, sales and supplies are suspended for 15 days from the 31st day.
3. The fine is still to be paid within that suspension. If it is not, the suspension is extended by another 15 days.
4. If the fine is still unpaid when the extended suspension ends, the dealership is terminated {{8.7}}.

See [repeat offences and unpaid fines](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

## Findings by government authorities

Central and State Government authorities can check a dealership under other laws. If such an authority establishes a malpractice or irregularity, the oil company treats it as one under MDG and takes the prescribed action once the authority advises it {{8.7}}.

## The same periods in MDG 2012

The 30-day notice, 10-day reply, 45-day speaking order and 30-day fine periods read the same in MDG 2012 as last amended on 30.11.2022 (Ver. 7), which MDG 2024 replaced {{2012:8.5.6}} {{2012:8.5.7}} {{2012:8.5.8}} {{2012:8.7}}. The hearing rule is the same too, except that the 2012 text names the Regional office where the 2024 text names the State Office (Retail) {{2012:8.6}}.
