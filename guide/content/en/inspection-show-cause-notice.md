## What a show cause notice is

In plain words, a show cause notice is the oil company's notice that lists the irregularities it says it found and gives you a set time to reply before it decides. MDG says one is issued in all cases of irregularities, and it must indicate all of them {{8.5.6}}.

Before any action is taken, the irregularity has to be established {{8.5.2}}. For an irregularity that MDG does not list, the company acts after an enquiry, following the principles of natural justice {{8.5.4}}.

## The clock, step by step

| Step | Time allowed | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Show cause notice issued | Within 30 days of the inspection. If MS or HSD samples were drawn, within 30 days of the test results. | {{8.5.6}} |
| What comes with it | All the reports and other documents the notice is based on | {{8.5.6}} |
| Your reply | 10 days from the date the notice is issued | {{8.5.7}} |
| Speaking order | Preferably within 45 days of receiving your reply | {{8.5.8}} |
| Personal hearing | Before termination is recommended or approved, for critical irregularities | {{8.6}} |
| Fine, where the action is only a fine | Within 30 days of the date of the company's notice | {{8.7}} |

The 45 days for the speaking order is a target: the clause says "preferably" {{8.5.8}}. The 30 days for the notice and the 10 days for the reply are not worded as targets {{8.5.6}} {{8.5.7}}.

The oil company's authorised officer reviews the charges and your reply, then passes the speaking order. It has to set out complete details of the irregularities, your reply, and detailed reasons why your reply is or is not acceptable {{8.5.8}}.

## A dated example

For example, an inspection on 5 October 2026 finds a minor irregularity that is a second instance, so the action is a Rs 10,000 fine {{8.4}}. No samples are drawn.

| Date | What happens | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| 5 Oct 2026 | Inspection | |
| 20 Oct 2026 | In this example, the notice is issued with its reports. The latest date allowed was 4 Nov 2026, which is 30 days after the inspection. | {{8.5.6}} |
| 28 Oct 2026 | In this example, your reply is sent and received. It was due by 30 Oct 2026, which is 10 days after the notice was issued. | {{8.5.7}} |
| 12 Dec 2026 | The speaking order is passed, preferably by this date: 45 days after the reply is received. | {{8.5.8}} |
| 15 Dec 2026 | In this example, the company's notice of the fine is dated this day. | {{8.7}} |
| 14 Jan 2027 | Last day to pay: 30 days after the date of that notice. | {{8.7}} |
| 15 Jan 2027 | If the fine is still unpaid, the 15-day suspension of sales and supplies starts, on the 31st day. | {{8.7}} |

If samples had been drawn on 5 October and the test results were dated 4 November, the notice would be due within 30 days of 4 November, which is 4 December 2026 {{8.5.6}}.

MDG does not say how to count the days or whether the first day is the day of the notice. If a date is close, ask your oil company to confirm in writing how it counted.

## Some action comes before the notice

Some findings stop sales on the spot. These are safeguards while samples are tested, and the penalty comes after the notice process:

- The right density figure is missing at inspection: sales and supplies of all products stop {{5.1.9}}.
- Positive stock variation beyond limits: sales and supplies of all products stop {{5.1.11}}.
- A tampered W&M seal: sales through that dispensing unit stop, and the unit is sealed {{5.1.2}}.
- A density or filter paper failure: sales and supplies of all products stop {{2.5(K)}}.

For a toilet finding, clause 5.1.14(b) says a letter listing the penalty under MDG is issued instantly {{5.1.14(b)}}. MDG does not say whether that letter takes the place of a show cause notice. If you receive one, ask your oil company's office.

## The hearing before termination

For a critical irregularity that may lead to termination, the head of the State office, State Office (Retail) or Zonal office of the oil company, or a nominee, gives a personal hearing to the signatories to the dealership or their nominees. This happens before the termination is recommended or approved {{8.6}}.

If they do not attend on the appointed date, one more chance is given. After that the case may be processed ex parte, based on the available facts {{8.6}}. Clause 8.6 speaks of critical irregularities only. See [what happens after a termination order](@inspection-appeal).

## Paying a fine

This applies where the action for a major or minor irregularity is only a fine, with no suspension, such as the Rs 10,000 or Rs 25,000 minor fines {{8.4}} {{8.7}}.

1. Pay within 30 days of the date of the company's notice.
2. If you do not, sales and supplies are suspended from the 31st day for 15 days.
3. If the fine is not paid within that suspension, it is extended for another 15 days.
4. If it is not paid within the extended suspension, the dealership is terminated {{8.7}}.

See [repeat offences and unpaid fines](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

## Findings by government authorities

Central and State Government authorities can check a dealership under other laws. If such an authority establishes a malpractice or irregularity, the oil company treats it as one under MDG and takes the prescribed action once the authority advises it {{8.7}}.

## The same periods in MDG 2012

The 30-day notice, 10-day reply, 45-day speaking order and 30-day fine rules read the same in MDG 2012 as last amended on 30.11.2022 (Ver. 7), which MDG 2024 replaced {{2012:8.5.6}} {{2012:8.5.7}} {{2012:8.5.8}} {{2012:8.7}}. The hearing rule is the same too, except that the 2012 text names the Regional office where the 2024 text names the State Office (Retail) {{2012:8.6}}.

> **Note:** this page is general information about what MDG 2024 says. It cannot tell you what to do about a notice you have received. For a specific notice, speak to a lawyer, and ask your oil company's office to confirm dates and papers.
