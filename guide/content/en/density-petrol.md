## Petrol density limits at a glance

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Minimum petrol density | 720 kg/m³ at 15°C {{7.6}} |
| Maximum petrol density | 775 kg/m³ at 15°C {{7.6}} |
| Grades covered | MG, E5, E10, E12, E15 and E20, all BS VI {{7.6}} |
| Tanker on arrival | Within ±3.0 kg/m³ of the invoice density {{1.2(iv)}} |
| Your morning reading | Recorded at 15°C before sales, by 10 AM {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}} |

The petrol table has one density row, and no separate figure for premium petrol {{7.6}}. See [E20 petrol](@density-e20) and [premium petrol](@density-premium-petrol). Diesel has other ranges: [diesel density](@density-diesel). Both fuels side by side: [density range](@density-range).

## A hot-day reading of 715 can still be inside the range

Warm petrol reads lower on the hydrometer and cold petrol reads higher, but the limits are at 15°C. Convert first, with the ASTM table or the [15°C calculator](@tools-density-at-15) {{1.2(iv)}} {{1.5(vi)}}. For example (made-up samples, worked with that calculator):

| Hydrometer reads | Sample temperature | Density at 15°C | Against 720–775 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 715.0 | 35°C | 733.0 | Inside |
| 777.0 | 10°C | 772.7 | Inside |
| 770.0 | 25°C | 778.1 | Outside |

So a petrol sitting exactly on the limits reads like this on the hydrometer:

| Sample temperature | Petrol at 720 (15°C) reads about | Petrol at 775 (15°C) reads about |
|---|---|---|
| 20°C | 715.5 | 770.8 |
| 30°C | 706.4 | 762.4 |
| 40°C | 697.2 | 754.0 |

The printed ASTM table decides if it differs in the last digit {{1.2(iv)}} {{1.5(vi)}}. For other temperatures use the [density temperature chart](@tools-density-chart), and see [why density changes with temperature](@density-temperature).

## If the reading is outside 720–775

1. Take the density and the temperature from the same sample {{1.2(viii)}}. If the figure surprises you, read both again (general practice, not an MDG rule).
2. Convert to 15°C. The [density checker](@tools-density-check) does it and says inside or outside.
3. For a tanker, compare with the invoice. More than 3.0 kg/m³ apart is outside the limit {{1.2(iv)}}.
4. If you doubt the quality, MDG says to contact the supply location or the area field officer {{1.2(ix)}}. That step is written for a tanker being decanted. For your own tank, ask your oil company.

At an inspection, samples go to lab tests that include density at 15°C. A sample that fails on density is acted on as established adulteration {{7.4}}, a critical irregularity with termination at the first instance {{8.2}}.

{{penalty-note}}

> **Note:** your hydrometer reading is not the lab result. A higher or lower figure inside the range is not better or worse: see [high or low density](@density-high-or-low).
