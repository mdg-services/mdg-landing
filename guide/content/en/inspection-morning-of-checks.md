## The morning routine

| When | What is done and written down | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Before the first sale | A nozzle sample; its density at 15°C goes in the density register. Latest time: 10 AM | {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}} |
| In the morning | Pump meter readings, and the product dip and water dip of each tank, taken separately. The water dip is part of the daily stock register | {{1.3}} {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| Daily | The Weights & Measures seal on each metering unit and totaliser, and that the unit's certificate is valid. A tampered seal stops sales on that unit | {{1.4.2(a)}} |
| Daily, before sales | Every nozzle tested for correct delivery; the quantity used for the test goes in the stock register. See [the 5-litre test](@quantity-5-litre-test) | {{1.4.2(d)}} |
| Daily | A look for leaks in the pump, hose and nozzle; a leak is reported to the oil company at once | {{1.4.2(e)}} |
| Daily | The toilet checks, with the maintenance sheet kept and displayed | {{5.1.14(b)}} |
| Before every delivery | The meter at zero, shown to the customer | {{1.4.2(f)}} |

MDG gives an hour only for the density. For the others it says "daily", "in the morning" or "before sales". General practice, not an MDG rule: follow one fixed order each morning, so no check is skipped.

## What the inspector asks for

Before 10 AM, the inspector asks for the previous morning's reading, or the after-tanker reading if a load came the previous day. After 10 AM, it is this morning's reading, or the one taken after today's unloading if a load has already arrived {{5.1.9}}. What a missing figure costs: [the 10 AM rule](@density-reference-10am).

For example (a made-up outlet): a tanker is unloaded at 6:30 PM on Monday. An inspector arrives at 8 AM on Tuesday. The figure that must exist is the one taken after Monday's unloading {{5.1.9}}.

Records and their periods: [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep). The evening before: [what to lay out](@inspection-night-before-checklist).
