## What MDG asks for

| Who | What | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Dealer, on arrival | Matches the seal or security lock number on the invoice with the lorry's | {{1.1(ii)}} |
| Dealer, before decanting | Checks the condition of the seal or lock on the delivery manifold and manhole covers, against the invoice | {{1.2(ii)}} |
| Supply location | Seals or locks the lorry as per industry practice and records the numbers on the invoice | {{3.2}} |

The other invoice checks are on [tanker and invoice do not match](@tanker-documents-do-not-match). The density, dip and sample checks that follow are on [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker) and [retention samples](@sampling-retention).

## What MDG does not say

MDG has no separate rule for a seal that is broken, missing or numbered differently. It does not say whether to refuse the load, hold it, or which officer to call for a seal problem, and it does not say what the dealer writes down.

The nearest instruction is about quality: in doubt, contact the supply location or the Area Field Officer for further instructions {{1.2(ix)}}. Whether a damaged seal counts as such a doubt, MDG does not say; ask your oil company how it wants these cases handled.

General practice, not an MDG rule: hold the unloading until the supply location answers, and write the seal numbers you actually found on your copy of the invoice.

## An example

For example (a made-up outlet): the invoice shows lock number 5107 for the manhole covers, but the lock on the lorry reads 5170. The dealer has found a mismatch at the seal check {{1.2(ii)}}. MDG gives no next step for the mismatch itself. In practice, the dealer holds the unloading, calls the supply location, and writes down both numbers and the time of the call.
