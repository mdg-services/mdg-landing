## What the rule says

MDG 2024 asks you to keep two kinds of density record, and together they form your **reference density**: the figure an inspector checks your fuel against.

- **Every morning:** draw a sample from the pump nozzle and record the density at 15°C before you start selling. The latest time allowed is 10 AM {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}}.
- **After every tanker:** once the load is in the tank, dispense at least 50 litres through the pump to push out the old product in the line. Then take a sample from the nozzle and record the density of the mixed product in the density register {{1.2(xiv)}}.

Until a tanker is decanted, the reference is the density recorded in the dispatch document, which the supply location writes on the invoice {{2.10}} {{3.1(h)}}. After decanting, the reference is the mixed-tank figure you recorded {{2.10}}. The invoice figure and the after-decant figure each have a column in the density register, in the format of [Annexure 1](@density-register).

## Which figure the inspector asks for

The answer depends on when the inspector arrives and whether a load came that day {{5.1.9}}.

| Inspector arrives | Density they ask for |
|---|---|
| Before 10 AM | Yesterday morning's figure, or yesterday's after-tanker figure if a load was received yesterday |
| After 10 AM, no load received today | This morning's figure |
| After 10 AM, a load received today | The after-tanker figure for today's load |

If the right figure is not in your register at that moment, it counts as "non-availability of reference density".

## What happens if it is missing

1. Sales and supplies of **all products** are suspended at once {{5.1.9}}.
2. Samples of all products are drawn and sent to the lab.
3. **If the sample passes**, you face the penalty for this irregularity. It is a major irregularity: 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies the first time, 30 days the second time, and termination of the dealership the third time {{8.3}}.
4. **If the sample fails**, the action is the one for adulteration, which is a critical irregularity with termination at the first instance {{8.2}}.

To decide whether this is a first, second or third instance, the oil company looks at the five years before the date the irregularity is established {{8.5.5}}. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

> **Note:** a missing morning figure takes minutes to prevent and can stop sales of every product at your outlet {{5.1.9}}. Treat the 10 AM entry as the first job of the day.

## A worked example

An outlet receives a petrol tanker at 2 PM on a Tuesday.

- **Tuesday 7:30 AM:** the manager records the morning density from the nozzle before sales start. This is Tuesday's reference until the load arrives.
- **Tuesday 2:45 PM:** after decanting, staff run 50 litres through the pump, take a nozzle sample and record the mixed-tank density.
- **Wednesday 8:15 AM:** an inspector arrives before 10 AM. The figure they ask for is Tuesday's after-tanker density, because a load came on Tuesday.
- **Wednesday 11:30 AM:** if instead the inspector came after 10 AM with no load that day, they would ask for Wednesday morning's figure.

If the Tuesday after-tanker entry had been skipped, the 8:15 AM inspection would find no reference density, and sales and supplies of all products would be suspended at once {{5.1.9}}.

## What to keep ready

- A calibrated hydrometer and thermometer, and the ASTM density conversion table, in working order {{1.5(vi)}}.
- The density register for the current and previous financial years {{5.1.12}}.
- Each day's morning entry made before the first sale {{1.4.1(c)}}, and each tanker's after-decant entry made once at least 50 litres have gone through the pump {{1.2(xiv)}}.
