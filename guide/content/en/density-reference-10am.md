## What the rule says

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They ask for two density records, and together these make your **reference density**: the figure an inspector measures your fuel against.

- **Every morning:** before the first sale, take a sample at the pump nozzle and write down its density at 15°C. The latest time is 10 AM {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}}.
- **After every tanker:** the line between tank and nozzle still holds the earlier product, so run at least 50 litres through the pump to clear it. Then take a nozzle sample and write the density of the mixed product in the density register {{1.2(xiv)}}.

Until a tanker is decanted, the reference is the density in the dispatch document, which the supply location writes on the invoice {{2.10}} {{3.1(h)}}. After decanting, the reference is the mixed-tank figure you wrote down {{2.10}}. The density register has room for both the invoice figure and the after-decanting figure: see [what the register should hold](@density-register).

## Which figure the inspector asks for

The inspector asks for the latest figure that should exist at the time of the visit. Two things decide which one that is: the clock (10 AM) and whether a tanker has been unloaded that day {{5.1.9}}.

- **A visit before 10 AM:** the previous morning's reading, or, if a tanker came the previous day, the reading taken after that tanker was decanted.
- **A visit after 10 AM, with no tanker unloaded yet today:** this morning's reading.
- **A visit after 10 AM, with a tanker already unloaded today:** the reading taken after that unloading.

If the figure that should exist is not in your register at that moment, MDG calls it "non-availability of reference density" {{5.1.9}}.

## What happens if it is missing

Sales and supplies of every product are stopped at once, and samples of every product are drawn and sent to a lab {{5.1.9}}. The lab result decides what comes next.

- **The sample passes.** The penalty for the missing record applies. It is a major irregularity. The minimum action is a 15-day suspension of sales and supplies the first time, a 30-day suspension the second time, and termination of the dealership the third time {{8.3}}.
- **The sample fails.** The action is the one for adulteration, a critical irregularity with termination at the first instance {{8.2}}.

Four qualifiers go with these amounts:

- They are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}.
- No action can follow until the irregularity has been established {{8.5.2}}.
- To decide whether this is a first, second or third instance, the oil company looks at the five years before the date the irregularity is established, and that count includes instances under the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}.
- If other irregularities are found at the same visit, 8.5.3 says each is counted as an instance, with a compounding effect {{8.5.3}}.

See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

> **Note:** the penalty for a missing record applies even when the fuel passes the lab {{5.1.9}}.

## An example

This is an illustration with made-up times. Take an outlet that receives a petrol tanker at 2 PM on a Tuesday.

- **Tuesday 7:30 AM:** the manager notes the morning density at the nozzle before sales start. This is Tuesday's reference until the load arrives.
- **Tuesday 2:45 PM:** after decanting, staff run 50 litres through the pump, take a nozzle sample and note the mixed-tank density.
- **Wednesday 8:15 AM:** an inspector arrives before 10 AM. The figure asked for is Tuesday's after-tanker density, because a tanker came on Tuesday.
- **Wednesday 11:30 AM:** had the inspector come after 10 AM with no tanker that day, the figure asked for would be Wednesday morning's.

Had the Tuesday after-tanker entry been skipped, the 8:15 AM visit would find no reference density, and sales and supplies of every product would stop at once {{5.1.9}}.

## What to keep ready

- Calibrated hydrometers and thermometers, and the ASTM density conversion table, in working order {{1.5(vi)}}.
- The density register for the present financial year and the one before it {{5.1.12}}.
- Each morning's entry made before the first sale {{1.4.1(c)}}, and each tanker's after-decanting entry made once at least 50 litres have gone through the pump {{1.2(xiv)}}.
