## Who fixes what

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. Chapter 4 divides the work between the company's maintenance representative and the dealer. The dealer's daily checks sit in Chapter 1.

| Situation | The oil company | The dealer | Clause |
|---|---|---|---|
| A pump or other equipment owned by the company breaks down | Arranges the repair | Handles the equipment properly, does the day-to-day upkeep the maintenance manual describes, and tells the company promptly | {{4.2(a)}} |
| The dealer's own equipment | | Keeps it in good working order at all times | {{4.2(b)}} |
| A pump delivers short or excess | The representative, or the dealer, arranges recalibration at once with the Weights & Measures Inspector | Tells Weights & Measures and the controlling office. Does not run the unit until it is re-stamped and set right | {{4.3(a)}} {{1.4.2(d)}} |
| A minor pipeline leak | The representative arranges the repair | | {{4.3(b)}} |
| A major pipeline leak | The representative asks headquarters for instructions, advises the dealer to suspend sales and notes the totaliser reading and tank dip on the job report | Suspends sales on that advice and signs the job report | {{4.3(b)}} |
| Water seeping into a tank | The representative checks the tank openings and arranges the fix: new packing and gaskets, and any missing caps put back | | {{4.3(c)}} |
| Product suspected lost through a tank leak | | Reports it to the controlling office | {{4.3(c)}} |
| Water found in a tank | Takes a water dip on every visit. Steps are taken to remove any water found | Takes a dip every day and reports any water at once to the controlling office | {{4.4}} {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| Leaks in the pump, hose or nozzle | | Looks for them every day and reports them at once | {{1.4.2(e)}} |

## When the pump breaks down

1. **Tell the company promptly** about any breakdown of its equipment {{4.2(a)}}.
2. **If the fault is short or excess delivery,** the representative or the dealer arranges recalibration at once with the Weights & Measures Inspector {{4.3(a)}}. Tell the Weights & Measures Department and the controlling office too. Weights & Measures does the re-stamping. Until that is done and the delivery is set right, the dealer does not operate the unit {{1.4.2(d)}}.
3. **If a Weights & Measures seal is broken,** the representative informs the concerned company official and enters it in the pump maintenance report. The dealer signs that report {{4.3(a)}}.
4. **If the dealer's own daily check finds a seal tampered with,** sales through that unit are suspended forthwith and the controlling office is told immediately. Sales stay suspended until the seals are replaced {{1.4.2(a)}}.

See [meter and totaliser seals](@quantity-seals) for the seal rules.

## Leaks

The dealer looks for leaks every day in the pump, the delivery hose and the nozzle, and reports any leak to the company at once {{1.4.2(e)}}.

MDG does not say what makes a pipeline leak minor or major, or who decides. The clause is written around the company's maintenance representative: he arranges the repair of a minor leak and asks his headquarters for instructions on a major one. For a major leak, three things happen meanwhile {{4.3(b)}}:

- the representative advises the dealer to suspend sales;
- the totaliser reading and the underground tank's dip are noted on the job report;
- the dealer signs the job report.

If the dealer suspects product is being lost through a leak in a tank, the dealer reports it to the company's controlling office {{4.3(c)}}.

### A worked example

For example, at a made-up outlet (an illustration, not a real case), the representative visiting at 11:00 AM finds a wet patch over the diesel pipeline. He thinks the leak is major and calls his headquarters. He asks the dealer to stop sales. He notes the totaliser reading and the dip of the diesel tank on the job report. The dealer reads the entries and signs. Sales stay stopped until headquarters gives its instructions.

## Water in the tank

- **Daily dip.** The dealer records the water dip every day, and it forms part of the daily stock register. If water is found, the dealer reports it at once to the controlling company office {{1.4.1(b)}}. A morning record of the pump meter reading and of each tank's product and water dips is kept as well {{1.3}}.
- **The representative's dip.** He takes a water dip on every visit to the outlet. If water is found, steps are taken to remove it {{4.4}}.
- **Seepage.** If water is getting in, he checks the tank openings and arranges the fix: new packing and gaskets, and any missing caps put back {{4.3(c)}}.
- **Ethanol-blended petrol.** After the water is removed, the ethanol content is checked before the product is released. If it is outside the desired range, sales stop and the controlling office is told {{4.3(c)}}. The test method follows the Indian Standards that Annexure 7 names: IS 2796 for E5 and E10 petrol, IS 17586 for E12 and E15, and IS 17021 for E20, each in its latest revision {{annexure 7}}. Standards are revised from time to time, so check the current version. The clause does not print the desired range. Ask your oil company.

Water in a tank lorry compartment at receipt is a different case. See [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water) and [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste).

## The Maintenance Job Report (Annexure 6)

The company's fitter or chargeman fills in a standard report after working on the pumps and other equipment the company has placed at the outlet. It is printed in Hindi and English {{4.1}}. In short, the form records {{annexure 6}}:

- why the fitter came (a routine visit, maintenance or a breakdown) and the date of the last visit;
- which equipment was looked at, by name, model, serial number and product;
- the result of a numbered checklist, each item with a yes or no answer and a remarks column, covering the pump's working parts, hoses and nozzles, the metering unit's Weights & Measures seal, tank openings, vents and dip points, water in the tank, and any unauthorised fittings;
- the repairs done, the parts replaced or needed, and the times of arrival and departure;
- the signatures of the dealer and the company's representative.

The report is made in three copies: one for the company's divisional engineer or PME, one for the outlet and one for the company's file. The form also carries a printed list of possible pump complaints.

Keep the outlet's copy. Pump maintenance records covering this financial year and the one before are on the list of records a dealer must keep {{5.1.12}}. MDG does not say that the outlet's copy is that record. Ask your oil company. Not keeping the specified records is a major irregularity when the records since the last inspection are not kept {{8.3(v)}}, and a minor one when those are kept but earlier ones are missing {{8.4(i)}}. See [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep).

## What MDG does not say

- **How fast the company must attend a breakdown.** Chapter 4 sets no time {{4.2(a)}}.
- **What happens if it does not come.** Chapter 4 gives no remedy. General practice, not an MDG rule: put the breakdown in writing to your controlling office, with the date and time you first reported it, and keep a copy.
- **Who pays for parts or repairs.** Chapter 4 does not say. Ask your oil company.
- **What counts as a minor or a major leak.** Not defined {{4.3(b)}}.
