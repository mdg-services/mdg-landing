## Who fixes what

Chapter 4 splits the work between the company's maintenance representative and the dealer. The dealer's daily checks sit in Chapter 1.

| Situation | The oil company | The dealer | Clause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company's own dispensing pumps and equipment | Arranges breakdown maintenance | Handles them properly, does the day-to-day upkeep per the maintenance manual, and informs the company promptly of any breakdown | {{4.2(a)}} |
| Dealer's own equipment | | Keeps it in good working condition at all times | {{4.2(b)}} |
| Pump delivering short or excess | Representative or dealer arranges recalibration at once with the Weights & Measures Inspector | Notifies Weights & Measures and the controlling office. Does not run the unit until it is re-stamped and set right | {{4.3(a)}} {{1.4.2(d)}} |
| Minor pipeline leak | Representative arranges the repair | | {{4.3(b)}} |

| Water seeping into a tank | Representative checks all tank openings and fixes packing, gaskets and missing caps | | {{4.3(c)}} |
| Suspected loss of product through a tank leak | | Reports it to the controlling office | {{4.3(c)}} |
| Water in the tank | Water dip on every visit. Steps to remove water found | Daily water dip. Reports any water immediately to the controlling office | {{4.4}} {{1.4.1(b)}} |

## When the pump breaks down

1. **Tell the company promptly** about any breakdown of its equipment {{4.2(a)}}.
2. **If the fault is short or excess delivery,** the representative or the dealer arranges recalibration at once with the Weights & Measures Inspector {{4.3(a)}}. Tell the Weights & Measures Department and the controlling office too. Weights & Measures does the re-stamping. Until that is done and the delivery is set right, the dealer does not operate the unit {{1.4.2(d)}}.
3. **If a Weights & Measures seal is broken,** the representative informs the concerned company official and puts it in the pump maintenance report. The dealer signs that report {{4.3(a)}}.
4. **If a seal is found tampered by the dealer's own daily check,** sales through that unit are suspended forthwith, the controlling office is told immediately, and sales stay suspended until the seals are replaced {{1.4.2(a)}}.

See [meter and totaliser seals](@quantity-seals) for the seal rules.

## Leaks

The dealer looks for leaks every day in the pump, the delivery hose and the nozzle, and reports any leak immediately to the company {{1.4.2(e)}}.

MDG does not say what makes a pipeline leak "minor" or "major", or who decides which it is. The clause is written around the company's maintenance representative: he arranges the repair of a minor leak and informs his headquarters about a major one. For a major leak, three things happen in the meantime {{4.3(b)}}:

- sales are suspended on the representative's advice;
- the totaliser reading and the underground tank's dip are noted on the job report;
- the dealer signs the job report.

If the dealer suspects product is being lost through a leak in a tank, he reports it to the controlling office of the company {{4.3(c)}}.

### A worked example

This is an illustration, not a real case.

At 11:00 AM the representative visiting an outlet finds a wet patch over the diesel pipeline. He thinks the leak is major and tells his headquarters. He asks the dealer to stop sales. He notes the totaliser reading and the dip of the diesel tank on the job report. The dealer reads the entries and signs. Sales stay stopped until headquarters gives its instructions.

## Water in the tank

- **Daily dip.** The dealer records the water dip every day. It forms part of the daily stock register. If water is detected, he reports it immediately to the controlling company office {{1.4.1(b)}}. A daily record of the pump meter reading and of the product and water dips of each tank, taken in the morning, is also kept {{1.3}}.
- **The representative's dip.** He takes a water dip on every visit to each outlet. If water is found, steps are taken to remove it {{4.4}}.
- **Seepage.** If water is seeping in, he checks all tank openings and fixes the packing, gaskets and missing caps {{4.3(c)}}.
- **Ethanol-blended petrol.** After the water is removed, the ethanol content is checked before the product is released. If it is not in the desired range, sales stop and the controlling office is told {{4.3(c)}}. The test method follows the BIS standards named in Annexure 7 {{annexure 7}}. The clause does not print the desired range. Ask your oil company.

Water in a tank lorry's compartment at receipt is a different case. See [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water) and [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste).

## The Maintenance Job Report (Annexure 6)

The standard report for the company's dispensing units and other equipment at the outlet is printed in Hindi and English {{4.1}}. The form, in short {{annexure 6}}:

- records the purpose of the visit: routine, maintenance or breakdown;
- lists the equipment, with name, model, serial number and product;
- has 17 numbered checks with a yes or no column and a remarks column, from the rate of delivery and filters to leaks in hoses and nozzles, the Weights & Measures seal calibration, tank manholes, vents and dip points, the water dip reading of the tank in cm, tank inter-connection, unauthorised fittings and the quality of the silica gel in the vent pipe of ethanol-blended petrol;
- lists repairs done, parts replaced and parts needed, with arrival and departure times;
- carries the signatures of the dealer and the company's representative;
- comes in three copies: white for the Divisional Engineer/PME, pink for the outlet, yellow as the file copy;
- ends with a list of 17 possible pump complaints, from a leaking nozzle or hose to a register that jumps, a meter that does not reset to zero and water in the underground tank.

Keep your copy. Pump maintenance records for the current and previous financial years are on the list of records a dealer must maintain {{5.1.12}}. MDG does not say that the pink copy is that record. Ask your oil company. Not keeping the specified records is a major irregularity when the records since the last inspection are not maintained {{8.3(v)}}, and a minor one if the records since the last inspection are kept but earlier ones are missing {{8.4(i)}}. See [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep).

## What MDG does not say

- **How fast the company must attend a breakdown.** Chapter 4 sets no time {{4.2(a)}}.
- **What happens if it does not come.** There is no remedy in Chapter 4. General practice, not an MDG rule: put the breakdown in writing to your controlling office, with the date and time you first reported it, and keep a copy.
- **Who pays for parts or repairs.** Chapter 4 does not say. Ask your oil company.
- **What counts as a minor or a major leak.** Not defined {{4.3(b)}}.
