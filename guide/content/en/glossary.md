Short forms and terms from MDG 2024, in A to Z order. Each entry says what it means in plain words and gives the clause where there is one. Where MDG uses a short form without spelling it out, we say so. A meaning that is common in the trade but not printed in MDG is marked as trade usage.

## Numbers and A to C

- **3-tier sampling.** The sampling system of Chapter 2. A lab compares three samples to confirm that the fuel the pump sells is the fuel the oil company supplied: the supply location's sample, the tank lorry retention sample the dealer keeps, and the sample drawn from the pump nozzle {{2.1}} {{2.5(G)}}. See [how sampling works](@sampling).
- **Adulteration.** A foreign substance put into MS or HSD, illegally or without authority, so that it fails the BIS specification. Results of the sample under test that fall outside the test method's permissible limits when compared with the reference sample also count, as does any further requirement for identifying adulteration that the competent authority issues {{5.1.1}}. A critical irregularity {{8.2}}.
- **Annexure.** A form, label or table at the end of MDG 2024. There are 13 numbered annexures, plus 12a and 13a. Annexure 1 is the density record {{annexure 1}}.
- **ASTM Table 53B.** The conversion table a dealer uses to check a tanker's density at 15°C against the invoice {{1.2(iv)}}. The dealer must have it, with calibrated hydrometers and thermometers, and keep them in working order {{1.5(vi)}}. Use the table itself; do not convert by rule of thumb.
- **ATG.** One of the automation parts of an automated outlet. An ATG switched off or not working without authorisation is an irregularity {{5.1.16}}. MDG does not spell it out; in the trade it is read as automatic tank gauge.
- **BIS.** Bureau of Indian Standards. MDG tests petrol against IS 2796, IS 17586 and IS 17021, and diesel against IS 1460 and IS 16531 {{5.1.1}}.
- **Branded fuel.** Fuel sold as a branded product, as opposed to normal MS or HSD. Intentionally selling normal MS or HSD as branded fuel is a major irregularity {{5.1.10}} {{8.3}}. See [branded fuel and overcharging](@penalties-branded-fuel-overcharging).
- **CA / CSA / DSM.** Staff titles in the 20.03.2025 circular. Every dispensing unit must be manned by one of them during working hours {{circular}}. The circular does not spell them out. In the trade DSM is usually read as driveway salesman, the term MDG uses in {{5.1.17}}.
- **Clinical tests.** The tests a mobile lab runs on nozzle samples at the outlet. For petrol: appearance, colour, density at 15°C and distillation. For diesel, the same plus kinematic viscosity at 40°C {{7.4}}. They run even when the density test passes {{7.4}}.
- **Composite density.** The density of the mixed product in the storage tank after a delivery, recorded once at least 50 litres have been dispensed through the pump {{1.2(xiv)}} {{2.10}}. See [the 10 AM rule](@density-reference-10am).
- **Controlling office.** The oil company office that controls your outlet; MDG does not define it further. The dealer reports water in a tank, tampered seals and suspected loss of product from a leaking tank to it {{1.4.1(b)}} {{1.4.2(a)}} {{4.3(c)}}.
- **Critical irregularity.** The most serious class: seven irregularities, such as adulteration, tampered seals and unauthorised purchases or sales. Termination at the first instance {{8.2}}.

## D to M

- **Darpan QR.** A QR code the dealer must display near the toilet so customers can give feedback {{5.1.14(b)}}. MDG names it "Darpan" and does not say where the code comes from.
- **Decanting.** Unloading a tank lorry into the outlet's underground tanks. MDG lists the dealer's steps in order {{1.2}}. See [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).
- **Density at 15°C.** The density of the fuel stated at 15°C. The invoice records it, and the dealer's own check must be within ±3.0 kg/m³ of it {{1.2(iv)}}. See [density](@density).
- **Dip rod.** The rod that measures the product level in each tank lorry compartment. It comes with the tank lorry, with a calibration certificate, and carries Weights & Measures certification {{1.1}} {{1.2(vii)}}.
- **DU (dispensing unit).** The fuel dispensing machine on the island. The dealer checks its Weights & Measures seal every day and every nozzle before sales {{1.4.2(a)}} {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- **FTL.** Named in {{2.5(H)}} as one place (FTL-Noida) where a dealer can ask for their own retained sample to be tested after a failed sample, besides a PSU laboratory of their choice in the same or a nearby state. The request is due within 10 days of receiving the lab report, and the dealer bears the cost {{2.5(H)}}. MDG does not spell out the short form. See [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed).
- **GPS.** The heading of 3.2 reads "Sealing / GPS", and the text there speaks of GPS and VTS together; the supply location is responsible for its smooth operation {{3.2}}. See VTS.
- **HSD.** High Speed Diesel, the diesel sold at the pump. MDG's test tables also call it automotive diesel fuel {{7.6}}.
- **Instance.** The count of an irregularity: first, second, third. The count runs over the five years before the date the new irregularity is established, and instances under the previous MDG in that period count too {{8.5.5}}. See [repeat offences](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).
- **IQCM.** Industry Quality Control Manual, the oil industry's manual for quality checks and sampling at supply locations {{2.2.1}} {{3.1}}.
- **LDO.** Appears in MDG 2024 only in "SKO-LDO dealerships" {{5}} {{8.10}} and is not spelled out. In the trade it is read as light diesel oil.
- **Major irregularity.** The second class: seven irregularities, such as refusing an inspection, missing reference density, missing records since the last inspection and overcharging. Usually 15 days' suspension, then 30 days, then termination {{8.3}}.
- **MDG.** Marketing Discipline Guidelines. First written in 1981-82; the edition in force is MDG 2024, effective 24.10.2024 {{introduction}} {{cover page}}. See the [MDG 2024 guide](@hub).
- **Minor irregularity.** The third class: missing air, telephone or first aid box, records from before the last inspection missing, display lapses, poor housekeeping, a dirty toilet, customer service gaps, short delivery with seals intact. A warning-cum-guidance letter, then Rs 10,000, then Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}}.
- **Mobile lab.** The oil industry's mobile laboratory. It makes surprise quality checks at outlets {{7.1}}, and tests nozzle samples of petrol and diesel there {{2.4.4}}. See [mobile lab](@sampling-mobile-lab).
- **MPD.** Used next to DU for the dispensing machine, as in "dual DU / MPD" {{5.1.2(a)}}. Each of its nozzles must show the product name {{5.1.17}}. MDG does not spell it out; in the trade it is read as multi-product dispenser.
- **MS.** Motor Spirit, the petrol sold at the pump. MDG notes that motor spirit and motor gasoline are one and the same product {{7.6}}.

## N to R

- **NANO.** "No Automation, No Operation". Automated outlets are designed on this principle. The dealer must keep automation working, report any fault to the company official at once and act as that official directs {{1.5(viii)}}. A generator or UPS must cover power failures {{1.5(ix)}}. See [automated outlets](@automation).
- **OMC.** Oil marketing company. In MDG 2024 it means the public-sector oil marketing companies {{introduction}}. The text writes their names as IOC, BPC and HPC {{8.8}}, and the handing-over form at its end names IndianOil, Bharat Petroleum and Hindustan Petroleum.
- **PDS.** Public Distribution System, under which kerosene (SKO) goes through wholesale dealers to ration shops {{6}}.
- **PESO.** The petroleum and explosives authority. MDG prints its full name as "Petroleum & Explosives Standards Organization" and tells the dealer to observe its rules {{1.5(ii)}}. Tank lorries need a valid Explosives License from it {{3.1(i)}}.
- **PMSBY and PMJJBY.** Short for the Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana and the Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana, two insurance (bima) schemes. MDG 2024 makes minimum wages and other benefits, including these two, a dealer duty {{1.5(x)}} {{5.1.18}}. The full names are printed in MDG 2012 {{2012:5.1.18}}. See [staff wages and uniform](@service-staff-wages-uniform).
- **Proof level.** A level marking on the tank lorry's calibration certificate and dip rod. The dealer checks the Weights & Measures markings on the rod at the bottom and at the proof level {{1.2(v)}} {{1.2(vi)}}. MDG does not define it further.
- **Q&Q kit.** Named in item 14 of the 20.03.2025 circular next to the first aid box {{circular}}. The circular does not spell it out or list the contents; in the trade it is read as the quality-and-quantity kit. Confirm with your oil company what yours should hold.
- **Reference density.** The density an inspector compares your fuel with. Until the tanker is decanted it is the density printed on the invoice. After that it is the composite density in your tank {{2.10}}. At an inspection, the figures asked for are your morning density (due by 10 AM) and, after a load, the composite density {{5.1.9}}. See [reference density at 10 AM](@density-reference-10am).
- **Reproducibility limit.** The difference between two test results that the test method itself allows. MDG writes "reproducibility/permissible limit". A sample is deemed to have failed if its results and the reference sample's fall outside it {{2.10}}. MDG does not define the term or print the numbers.
- **Retention sample (tank lorry).** A sample of each tanker load that the dealer keeps: 2 x 1 litre of MS and/or 1 x 1 litre of HSD, sealed, labelled and signed by dealer and driver. The dealer keeps the last two loads for each tank {{2.3}} {{2.9.2}}. See [retention samples](@sampling-retention).
- **RO.** Retail outlet, the petrol pump {{introduction}}.
- **RSP.** Retail selling price. The dealer must display the authorised retail selling prices of MS, HSD, CNG and Auto LPG {{5.1.15}}. The 20.03.2025 circular uses the short form {{circular}}.

## S to Z

- **Show cause notice.** The written notice that lists the irregularities. It is issued within 30 days of the inspection, or within 30 days of the test results where samples were drawn, together with the reports it relies on. The dealer has 10 days to reply {{8.5.6}} {{8.5.7}}. See [show cause notice](@inspection-show-cause-notice).
- **Shrinkage allowance.** A loss allowance that applies only at some locations and times. Annexure 8 shows how its price allowance (Rs per KL) is turned into a percentage of the net price, which is applied to the receipts of the period under inspection {{5.1.11}} {{annexure 8}}. See [shrinkage and TVA](@stock-shrinkage-tva).
- **SKO.** Superior Kerosene Oil. SKO dealers under the Public Distribution System are also called wholesale dealers {{6}}.
- **SL, SL1, SL2.** The supply location's samples. The lab tests the nozzle sample, the last tank lorry retention sample (TL1) and the matching supply location sample (SL1). If the nozzle sample fails and TL1 passes, TL2 and SL2 are tested as well {{2.5(G)}}.
- **SLC.** State Level Coordinator. Draws up the mobile lab programme and is told when a mobile lab sample fails {{7.2}} {{7.6.1}}.
- **Speaking order.** The reasoned decision the company officer issues after reading the reply to a show cause notice, preferably within 45 days of receiving it. It must set out the irregularities, the dealer's reply and the reasons the reply is or is not accepted {{8.5.8}}.
- **Stock variation.** The gap between the stock your books show and the stock in the tank, once the allowed 4% of tank stock and the listed evaporation, handling, shrinkage and temperature losses are taken into account {{5.1.11}}. See [stock variation](@stock-variation).
- **Supply location.** The oil company's storage point, depot or installation, from which tank lorries are filled. Chapter 3 sets its duties {{2.2.1}} {{3.1}}.
- **TL / TT.** Tank lorry, also written tank truck: the tanker. MDG uses both TL and TT for the vehicle and for its retention sample {{2.5}}, and Annexure 1 heads a column "tank truck receipt" {{annexure 1}}.
- **TLF.** The tank lorry filling point at the supply location. The supply location records the TLF point, the storage tank number and the release time on the invoice {{2.2.1}}.
- **Totaliser (totalizer).** The unit that keeps the dispensing unit's running total. A seal that allows the totaliser reading to be manipulated, a totaliser deliberately made non-working, or a non-working totaliser not reported, counts as tampering {{5.1.3}}. A critical irregularity {{8.2}}.
- **TVA.** Temperature Variation Allowance. Shown on the invoice as a credit where it applies, and counted in stock checks only for the quantity it was allowed on {{5.1.11}} {{annexure 10}}.
- **VTS.** The system that tracks a tank lorry on its way to your outlet. Supply locations must not fill a tank lorry without a working VTS, and its deviation alerts cover time and route {{3.2}}. The dealer should watch the lorry through the link provided and tell the supply location of any deviation before accepting it {{1.5(vii)}}. MDG does not spell out VTS; in the trade it is read as vehicle tracking system. See [tracking and route deviation](@tanker-vts).
- **Water finding paste.** The paste used to check each tank lorry compartment for water. For ethanol-blended petrol, MDG asks for a suitable paste {{1.2(vii)}}. See [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste).
- **Whatman 2.** The filter paper a dealer must keep to check petrol and make available to customers on demand. A message about it goes on the Customer Education board or poster {{1.4.1(a)}}. See [filter paper test](@density-filter-paper).
- **W&M.** Weights & Measures Department. Its seals sit on the metering unit and totaliser. It certifies the dip rod, verifies the 5-litre measure, and re-stamps a pump after recalibration {{1.4.2(a)}} {{1.4.2(b)}} {{1.4.2(d)}}. The Legal Metrology Act, 2009 sets the permissible limits for short delivery {{5.1.2(a)}}.
