## Why the direction matters

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the rules the oil companies set for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. They do not treat a gap in the tank as one thing. Two questions decide what happens: is the gap beyond the permissible limit, and does the tank hold more or less than the books show {{5.1.11}}. The answer to the second question decides whether your sales stop at once.

MDG does not define the words. In the trade, **positive** means the tank holds more than the books say, and **negative** means it holds less. The limit itself, 4% of tank stock plus the handling, shrinkage and TVA allowances, is explained on the [stock variation page](@stock-variation).

## The two cases, question by question

| Your question | Positive (more than the books) | Negative (less than the books) |
|---|---|---|
| Do my sales stop? | Yes, for all products, immediately | No. Sales and supplies to the outlet continue during the investigation |
| What happens to the samples? | They go to the lab | They are drawn and tested |
| What does the oil company want from me? | The clause calls for a study of the reasons and mentions no explanation from the dealer | An explanation from the dealer |
| What can make it worse? | A failed sample is handled as adulteration. A passed sample plus another irregularity, such as an unauthorised purchase, means action for that irregularity | Supply stops immediately if the oil company does not find the explanation satisfactory |

All of this is from {{5.1.11}}.

> **Note:** a positive variation beyond the limit shuts sales of every product at once, before any lab result comes back. A negative variation beyond the limit does not {{5.1.11}}.

## What the clause looks for behind extra fuel

The clause links a positive variation to two things: adulteration, and some other irregularity "like unauthorized purchase" {{5.1.11}}. MDG calls a purchase from a source the oil company has not authorised an unauthorised purchase {{5.1.6}}, and it bars a dealer from buying, selling or exchanging petroleum products with anyone except the principal oil company {{1.5(iii)}}. MDG tells oil companies to draw samples when stock variation is beyond the permissible limit {{2.2.2.2}} {{2.4}}. MDG does not say why it treats the two directions differently.

## What decides the final action

For a positive variation, the result depends on what is established:

| Finding | Class | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Sample fails | Adulteration, critical {{8.2}} | Termination at the first instance |
| Sample passes, unauthorised purchase established | Unauthorised purchase, critical {{5.1.6}} {{8.2}} | Termination at the first instance |
| Sample passes, nothing else established | Major {{8.3(iv)}} | 1st instance: 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies. 2nd: 30 days. 3rd: termination |

These are the minimum actions. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination, in any instance {{8.5.1}}. No action is taken until the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}. The count of first, second and third instance looks back five years, and it includes irregularities under the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}.

The clause for a positive variation mentions a study of the reasons. It does not mention a step where the dealer is asked to explain. A show cause notice is to be issued in all cases of irregularities {{8.5.6}}. The [show cause notice page](@inspection-show-cause-notice) has the timeline, and the [failed sample page](@sampling-failed) explains the lab side. For your own case, speak to an advocate or your oil company.

For a negative variation with no other irregularity, the clause describes the limit as "up to 4%" {{5.1.11}}. It does not say in that sentence whether the handling, shrinkage and TVA allowances also count. MDG also does not say how a suspension of supply, after an unsatisfactory explanation, fits with the 15-day and 30-day action for a variation beyond the limit {{8.3(iv)}}. Ask your oil company.

## Common causes of each, in practice

This list is general practice, not an MDG rule. MDG does not list causes of a gap. The only reasons it builds in are the allowances on the [stock variation page](@stock-variation).

| Cause | Usually shows up as |
|---|---|
| Temperature: fuel measured warm and cold takes up different volumes. MDG's TVA allowance is tied to temperature {{annexure 10}} | Either direction |
| A dip taken with the rod not straight, or before the tank has settled | Either direction |
| A pump delivering short measure: the meter shows more than the customer received, so extra fuel stays in the tank | Positive |
| A pump delivering excess: the tank gives out more than the meter shows | Negative |
| Testing litres not recorded: the quantity runs through the meter but is not a sale, so book and dip can disagree | Either direction |
| A leak in a tank or pipeline | Negative |
| A wrong meter reading or a wrong receipt entry | Either direction |

Three of these have an MDG rule behind them:

- The quantity used for daily nozzle testing goes into the stock register {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- If a pump delivers short or excess, the oil company's maintenance representative or the dealer is to arrange recalibration at once, with the Weights & Measures Inspector's help {{4.3(a)}}.
- A suspected loss of product from a tank leak is to be reported by the dealer to the oil company's controlling office {{4.3(c)}}.

## Keep the entries that explain a gap

MDG asks for a morning record of the pump meter readings and each tank's product and water dips {{1.3}}, and for entries in the stock register or on the stock display board after each tanker is decanted {{1.2(xiii)}}. The [daily stock register page](@stock-daily-register) shows an illustration layout.
