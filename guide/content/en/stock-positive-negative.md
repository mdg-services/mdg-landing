## Why the direction matters

Two things decide what happens: whether the gap is beyond the permissible limit, and whether the tank holds more or less than the books show {{5.1.11}}. MDG does not define the words. In the trade, **positive** means more than the books say, **negative** less.

## The two cases, question by question

| Your question | Positive (more than the books) | Negative (less than the books) |
|---|---|---|
| Do my sales stop? | Yes, for all products, immediately. The clause links extra stock to adulteration and to unauthorised purchase {{5.1.6}} | No. Sales and supplies continue during the investigation |
| What happens to the samples? | They go to the lab | They are drawn and tested |
| What does the oil company want from me? | A study to find the reasons. The clause mentions no explanation from the dealer | An explanation from the dealer |
| What can make it worse? | A failed sample brings action as for adulteration. A passed sample plus an established unauthorised purchase means action for that | Supply stops immediately if the explanation is not found satisfactory |

All of this is from {{5.1.11}}.

> **Note:** a positive variation beyond the limit shuts sales of every product at once, before any lab result comes back. A negative variation beyond the limit does not {{5.1.11}}.

## What decides the final action

With a positive variation, a failed sample brings action as for adulteration, which is critical {{8.2}}. A passed sample with an established unauthorised purchase is critical too {{5.1.6}} {{8.2}}. Otherwise, with the sample passing, a variation beyond the limit is major in either direction {{8.3(iv)}}. The limit and the ladder are on the [stock variation page](@stock-variation), the lab side on [failed samples](@sampling-failed). A show cause notice is to be issued in all cases of irregularities {{8.5.6}}: see [show cause notices](@inspection-show-cause-notice).

## Common causes, in practice

General practice, not an MDG rule.

| Cause | Usually shows up as |
|---|---|
| Temperature: warm fuel takes up more room | Either direction |
| A bad dip or a wrong entry | Either direction |
| A pump delivering short or excess measure | Positive if short, negative if excess |
| Testing litres not recorded | Either direction |
| A leak in a tank or pipeline | Negative |

Three have an MDG rule behind them: testing litres go in the stock register {{1.4.2(d)}}, a pump delivering short or excess is to be recalibrated at once, arranged by the dealer or the company's maintenance representative {{4.3(a)}}, and the dealer reports a suspected tank leak to the company's controlling office {{4.3(c)}}. See the [daily stock register](@stock-daily-register) for the entries that explain a gap.

General information, not legal advice. For your own notice, speak to an advocate or your oil company.
