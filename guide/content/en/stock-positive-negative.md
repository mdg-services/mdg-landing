## Why the direction matters

MDG 2024 does not treat a gap in the tank as one thing. It asks two questions: is the gap beyond the permissible limit, and is the tank over or under what the books show {{5.1.11}}. The answer to the second question decides whether your sales stop at once.

MDG does not define the words. In the trade, **positive** means the tank holds more than the books say, and **negative** means it holds less. The limit itself, 4% of tank stock plus the handling, shrinkage and TVA allowances, is explained on the [stock variation page](@stock-variation).

## Side by side

| | Positive variation | Negative variation |
|---|---|---|
| In the tank | More fuel than the books | Less fuel than the books |
| Samples | Drawn and sent to the lab | Drawn and sent for testing |
| Sales while it is looked into | Sales and supplies of all products suspended immediately | Sales and supplies to the outlet continue |
| What the clause asks for | A study to identify the reasons | The dealer's explanation |
| If it goes badly | Sample fails: action as for adulteration. Sample passes but another irregularity, such as unauthorised purchase, is established: action for that irregularity | Supply suspended immediately if the explanation is not found satisfactory |

All of this is in {{5.1.11}}.

> **Note:** a positive variation beyond the limit shuts sales of every product at once, before any lab result comes back. A negative variation beyond the limit does not {{5.1.11}}.

## What the clause looks for behind extra fuel

The clause names the two things it checks when a positive variation is beyond the limit: adulteration, and some other irregularity "like unauthorized purchase" {{5.1.11}}. Extra fuel in the tank may have come from somewhere other than your oil company. MDG calls a purchase from a source the oil company did not authorise an unauthorised purchase {{5.1.6}}, and bars a dealer from buying, selling or exchanging petroleum products with anyone except the principal oil company {{1.5(iii)}}.

Missing fuel does not point to fuel coming in. So the clause lets sales continue and asks for an explanation first. This is a plain reading of the clause. MDG does not state why it treats the two directions differently.

## Positive variation, step by step

1. The reconciliation shows the tank holds more than the books allow {{5.1.11}}.
2. Samples are drawn and sent to the lab. Oil companies are directed to draw samples when stock variation is beyond the permissible limit {{2.2.2.2}} {{2.4}}.
3. Sales and supplies of all products are suspended immediately {{5.1.11}}.
4. A study identifies the reasons for the variation {{5.1.11}}.

What follows depends on what is established:

| Finding | Class | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Sample fails | Adulteration, critical {{8.2}} | Termination at the first instance |
| Sample passes, unauthorised purchase established | Unauthorised purchase, critical {{5.1.6}} {{8.2}} | Termination at the first instance |
| Sample passes, nothing else established | Major {{8.3(iv)}} | 1st instance: 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies. 2nd: 30 days. 3rd: termination |

The clause for a positive variation mentions a study. It does not mention a step where the dealer is asked to explain. A show cause notice follows for any irregularity that is established {{8.5.6}}. The [show cause notice page](@inspection-show-cause-notice) has the timeline, and the [failed sample page](@sampling-failed) explains the lab side. For your own case, speak to your oil company or a lawyer.

## Negative variation, step by step

1. The reconciliation shows the tank holds less than the books allow {{5.1.11}}.
2. Samples are drawn and sent for testing {{5.1.11}}.
3. Sales and supplies to the outlet continue during the investigation {{5.1.11}}.
4. The dealer's explanation is called {{5.1.11}}.
5. If the explanation is not found satisfactory, supply is suspended immediately {{5.1.11}}.

For a negative variation with no other irregularity, the clause describes the limit as "up to 4%" {{5.1.11}}. It does not say in that sentence whether the handling, shrinkage and TVA allowances also count. MDG also does not say how the suspension of supply in step 5 fits with the 15-day and 30-day action for a variation beyond the limit {{8.3(iv)}}. Ask your oil company.

## Common causes of each, in practice

This list is general practice, not an MDG rule. MDG does not list causes of a gap. The only reasons it builds in are the allowances on the [stock variation page](@stock-variation).

| Cause | Usually shows up as |
|---|---|
| Temperature: fuel measured warm and cold takes up different volumes. MDG's TVA allowance is tied to temperature {{annexure 10}} | Either direction |
| A dip taken with the rod not straight, or before the tank has settled | Either direction |
| A pump delivering short measure: the meter shows more than the customer received, so extra fuel stays in the tank | Positive |
| A pump delivering excess: the tank gives out more than the meter shows | Negative |
| Testing litres not recorded: the quantity runs through the meter but is not a sale, so book and dip can disagree | Either direction |
| A leak in a tank or pipeline | Negative |
| A wrong meter reading or a wrong receipt entry | Either direction |

Three of these have an MDG rule behind them:

- The quantity used for daily nozzle testing must be recorded in the stock register {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- If a pump is delivering short or excess, the dealer or the company's maintenance representative should immediately arrange recalibration with the help of the Weights & Measures Inspector {{4.3(a)}}.
- Suspected loss of product through a tank leak is to be reported by the dealer to the oil company's controlling office {{4.3(c)}}.

## Keep the entries that explain a gap

MDG asks for a daily record of pump meter readings and of each tank's product and water dips, taken in the morning {{1.3}}, and for entries in the stock register after each tanker is decanted {{1.2(xiii)}}. The [daily stock register page](@stock-daily-register) shows an example layout.
