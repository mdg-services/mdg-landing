## One set of ranges, whichever company supplies you

MDG 2024 is one document for the dealers of the public-sector marketing companies {{cover page}}. Its density table has no column for any company {{7.6}}. See [MDG for every oil company](@mdg-every-oil-company).

| Fuel | Density at 15°C, kg/m³ | Separate figure per company? |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol (MS) | 720–775 | None {{7.6}} |
| Diesel (HSD), BS VI up to B7 | 810–845 | None {{7.6}} |
| Diesel with biodiesel, B8–B20 | 820–860 | None {{7.6}} |

A footnote to the 810–845 diesel figure says any relaxation for fuel processed from Assam crude may follow Government notices {{7.6}}. See [density by state](@density-by-state).

## Why the figures still differ

The range is the limit. The invoice figure is the 15°C density the supply location writes for the lot loaded for you {{2.2.1(iv)}}. Two things make the numbers you see differ, whatever the company:

- **The lot.** General knowledge, not MDG text: fuel made from different crudes at different refineries comes out at different densities, so invoice figures vary from lot to lot.
- **The sample's temperature.** General knowledge, not MDG text: warm fuel reads a lower density. Convert your reading to 15°C before you compare it with the invoice {{1.2(iv)}}. See [density and temperature](@density-temperature).

## Is there an "Indian Oil density chart"?

Not in MDG. It prints no chart for any company. It prints the 7.6 limits and expects every dealer to hold the ASTM density conversion table {{1.5(vi)}}. The tanker check names Table 53B {{1.2(iv)}}. The [15°C calculator](@tools-density-at-15) and the [temperature chart](@tools-density-chart) are helpers only; the printed table decides.

## The check at every pump

Each tanker is compared with its own invoice, within ±3.0 kg/m³ at 15°C, compartment by compartment {{1.2(iv)}} {{1.2(viii)}}.

For example (made-up figures): tanker A's invoice shows diesel at 832.0 and your converted reading is 833.5, a gap of +1.5, inside the limit. Tanker B's invoice shows 826.0 and your reading is 829.5, a gap of +3.5, outside the limit, although 829.5 sits inside 810–845. Being inside the range is not enough; the gap to the invoice counts too. If you doubt the quality, contact the supply location or the area field officer {{1.2(ix)}}. See the [tanker density check](@density-invoice-check).
