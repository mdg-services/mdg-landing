## What to do, in order

1. **Stop the unit.** Stop all sales through that dispensing unit at once {{1.4.2(a)}}. What counts as tampered is on [W&M and totalizer seals](@quantity-seals).
2. **Report it at once** to the controlling oil company office {{1.4.2(a)}}. MDG names no way to report. General practice, not an MDG rule: report in writing as well, and note the time.
3. **Leave the seal alone.** MDG treats a seal other than the W&M inspector's as tampering {{5.1.2(b)}}, so in practice do not fit one yourself. General practice, not an MDG rule: photograph the seal as found, with the time.
4. **Keep sales suspended** until the seals are replaced {{1.4.2(a)}}. MDG does not say who replaces them or sets a time limit. Ask your oil company.
5. **If the company's maintenance representative finds it broken,** he informs the company official and enters it in the pump maintenance report, which the dealer signs too {{4.3(a)}}. See [who fixes what](@company-breakdowns-leaks).

## What can follow

The unit is sealed, and samples of all products go to the lab {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}}. Tampered seals on the metering unit, and a tampered totalizer seal, are critical, with termination at the first instance {{8.2(ii)}} {{8.2(iii)}} {{8.2}}. W&M's opinion is final, and penal action follows on it even if the delivery was correct or in excess {{5.1.2(b)}}.

{{penalty-note}}

For example (a made-up outlet): the daily check at 7 AM shows no lead seal on the totalizer of unit 2. The dealer stops unit 2, tells the controlling office, notes the time and keeps the unit shut until new seals are fitted.

The notice that can follow: [show cause notices](@inspection-show-cause-notice).

General information, not legal advice. For your own notice, speak to an advocate or your oil company.
