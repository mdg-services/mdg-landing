## Who this covers

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. MDG 2024 is written for retail outlet (RO) and kerosene dealerships {{cover page}}, and its introduction counts about 6,400 SKO dealerships on 01.04.2024 {{introduction}}. Chapter 6 describes how the kerosene system works {{chapter 6}}:

- The oil companies appoint SKO dealers, also known as wholesale dealers, after running the Dealer Selection Process.
- State Governments appoint the retailers, the ration shops.
- The oil company makes PDS kerosene available at its depots or installations, and the dealers lift it.
- From the depot onward, the dealer is responsible for transport, under the guidance and supervision of the State Government.
- In a few places, such as hilly areas, supply is also delivered to the dealer.

Clause 8.10 speaks of "SKO-LDO dealerships". MDG does not spell out LDO. General knowledge, not MDG text: LDO usually means light diesel oil. Chapter 6 lists duties for SKO dealers only.

## The seven duties

Chapter 6 puts seven duties on the dealer. They fall into three groups.

### Who gets the kerosene, and at what price

- Sell blue-dyed kerosene to the people it is meant for {{chapter 6, item 1}}.
- Never charge more than the price fixed by the Government or the oil company {{chapter 6, item 4}}.

### Staying open and stocked

- Do not turn away a consumer on a working day, in working hours, when stock is available, unless the Government or the oil company has directed otherwise {{chapter 6, item 2}}.
- Do not keep the business premises, storage included, shut in working hours on a working day without the oil company's prior written permission {{chapter 6, item 3}}.
- Take all reasonable steps to have adequate stock at the premises and the storage at all times {{chapter 6, item 5}}.

### Showing and recording

- Put up a stock-cum-price board in a place where it is easy to see, at the business and at the storage {{chapter 6, item 6}}.
- Keep a proper daily account of purchase, sale and stock, at the premises or the tank lorry {{chapter 6, item 7}}.

Oil company officials inspect these dealerships. Irregularities lead to action under 8.10 {{chapter 6}}.

## What the oil company must do for kerosene

Chapter 3 sets out the oil company's side. See [oil company duties](@oil-company-duties). For kerosene:

- It releases kerosene only after visual, appearance, colour, density and flash point checks under its quality manual {{3.1(d)}}.
- It dyes PDS kerosene blue at the prescribed dosage {{3.1(g)}}.
- PDS kerosene travels in a separate tank lorry painted blue, never one used for MS or HSD {{3.1(l)}}.

## Irregularities and penalties

Clause 8.10 sorts kerosene irregularities into critical, major and minor {{8.10}}. Grouped by the kind of lapse:

| Kind of lapse | What 8.10 lists | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Meters and delivery | Short delivery where the Weights & Measures seals were tampered with | Critical |
| Meters and delivery | Totalizer seals found tampered with | Critical |
| Meters and delivery | Short delivery where the Weights & Measures seals are intact | Minor |
| Buying and selling | Unauthorised buying, selling or exchange of kerosene, or of anything that could stand in for it | Critical |
| Stock | A gap between physical and book stock beyond permissible limits | Critical |
| Price | Overcharging | Major |
| Rules | Not following Government regulations, and the company's operating guidelines or instructions on selling and distributing kerosene, if any | Major |

What each class leads to {{8.10}}:

| Class | Action listed in MDG | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Termination of the dealership at the first instance, for proven critical irregularities | {{8.10}} |
| Major | The dealer's allocation is reduced by half (50%) for 3 calendar months, once the State Government has given due approval | {{8.10}} |
| Minor | A warning letter | {{8.10}} |

Read these with four points in mind:

- Clause 8.5.1 says the actions in MDG 2024 are a minimum and the oil company can take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}.
- No action can follow until an irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}, and 8.10 itself speaks of proven critical irregularities {{8.10}}.
- When instances are counted over five years, irregularities under the previous guideline within those five years count too {{8.5.5}}. The appeal in 8.9 is for termination only {{8.9}}.
- Clause 8.10 carries the other provisions over "as applicable" and lists none {{8.10}}. MDG does not say how 8.5 and 8.9 apply to a kerosene dealership. Ask your oil company.

### Stock variation

Clause 8.10 sets out a procedure for a variation beyond permissible limits that an officer authorised only for search and seizure under the Kerosene Control Order, 1993 has established {{8.10}}:

1. Sales and supplies stop at once.
2. The dealer is asked to explain.
3. If the explanation is satisfactory, sales and supplies resume.
4. If it is not, the action is the one for unauthorised purchase or sale, which is graded critical.

Clause 8.10 gives no percentage. The limits in 5.1.11 are for MS and HSD {{5.1.11}}. See [stock variation](@stock-variation) for outlets.

### A worked example

For example, take a made-up dealer (an illustration, not a real case) with a monthly allocation of 20 kilolitres. Overcharging is established. The major-irregularity action, a 50% cut for 3 calendar months, leaves the dealer 10 kilolitres a month for those 3 months. That holds once the State Government has approved and the local Civil Supplies Authorities, who must be consulted, have agreed. If they object, a strong warning letter goes to the dealer instead {{8.10}}. Clause 8.5.1 calls the actions in MDG a minimum, so ask your oil company how it applies here {{8.5.1}}.

## The civil supplies check

The oil company must consult the local Civil Supplies Authorities before it acts against any kerosene dealership. MDG gives the reason: the company's action should not interrupt PDS kerosene supply to ordinary consumers. If those authorities object to an action that could mean suspending supplies or cutting the allocation, a strong warning letter goes to the dealer instead {{8.10}}.

## What MDG leaves open

- **Which duty falls in which class.** The seven duties and the lists of irregularities do not match one to one. Overcharging is named as major, and duty 4 is about price. For others, such as a closed godown or a missing stock-cum-price board, 8.10 names no class {{8.10}}. The second major item refers to Government regulations and the company's operating guidelines or instructions on kerosene, if any. MDG does not say whether the Chapter 6 duties count as those.
- **Matters MDG does not list.** For irregularities not covered, 8.5.4 says the oil company's competent authority takes suitable action after an enquiry, following natural justice {{8.5.4}}. Whether that reaches kerosene dealers depends on the point on provisions below.
- **The permissible limit for kerosene stock.** No figure is given.
- **Which petrol pump provisions apply.** Clause 8.10 says "as applicable" and lists none {{8.10}}. The show cause notice, the five-year count and the appeal against termination are written in 8.5 and 8.9. MDG does not say how they apply to a kerosene dealership. Ask your oil company.
- **The immediate suspension and the civil supplies check.** For a stock variation, sales and supplies stop at once. Separately, action is taken only in consultation with the Civil Supplies Authorities. MDG does not say how the two fit together.

## Compared with MDG 2012

Chapter 6 and clause 8.10 read the same in MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, last amended 30.11.2022), which MDG 2024 replaced, apart from spelling {{2012:chapter 6}} {{2012:8.10}}.
