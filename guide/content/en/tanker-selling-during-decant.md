## What MDG says

| Question | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Sales from the tank being filled | Stop before decantation begins and stay stopped until it is complete | {{1.2(vii)}} |
| Sales from other tanks or products | Not mentioned | none |
| After decanting | At least 50 litres through the pump, then the nozzle sample for the density record | {{1.2(xiv)}} |

MDG does not say where those 50 litres go or how long the tank waits before it sells again.

## Reading it literally

MDG's stop is tied to "the concerned underground tank", not to the whole outlet or to a particular dispensing unit {{1.2(vii)}}. So MDG neither allows nor bars sales from the other tanks while a lorry unloads. That silence is not a permission: other rules can still apply.

## What else may limit selling

The dealer must also follow the statutory rules on storage and sale of petroleum products {{1.5(i)}}, PESO rules {{1.5(ii)}} and the oil company's instructions {{1.5(v)}}. MDG does not list what they say about selling during unloading. Confirm with your oil company what applies at your outlet.

General practice, not an MDG rule: keep customer vehicles clear of the lorry, the hose and the tank's fill point while product is flowing.

## An example

For example (a made-up outlet with one petrol tank and one diesel tank): a petrol lorry arrives. Sales from the petrol tank stop before decantation and stay stopped until it is complete {{1.2(vii)}}. MDG says nothing about the diesel nozzles, so the dealer asks the oil company whether diesel sales may continue. After decanting, 50 litres go through the pump before the density sample {{1.2(xiv)}}.
