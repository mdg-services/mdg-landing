## What 3-tier sampling is for

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. Their sampling chapter has one aim: to show that the petrol and diesel an outlet sells is the product its oil company supplied {{2.1}}. Fuel is sampled at three points, and the lab compares the results.

The same comparison helps locate a problem. If a sample fails, the three sets can show whether the trouble began at the outlet, in the tank lorry or at the supply location {{2.1}}.

MDG uses the name "3-tier" {{2.1}} but never lists the three tiers in one place. This page takes them to be the three samples the lab tests and compares {{2.5(G)}}.

A sample counts as failed when its result and the reference sample's result are further apart than the test method allows, which MDG calls the "reproducibility/permissible limit" {{2.10}}. In plain terms, that is the gap a test method accepts between two results on the same fuel. MDG prints no figures for it.

## The three samples, side by side

| | Supply location sample | Tank lorry sample | Nozzle sample (RO sample) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drawn by | The supply location, at the filling point where lorries are loaded {{2.2.1(i)}} | The dealer or his representative {{2.2.2.1}} | An oil company official, mobile lab or other authorised sampler {{2.2.2.2}} |
| When | Every day, and again after the supply location switches storage tanks {{2.2.1(i)}} {{2.2.1(ii)}} | On each tank lorry received, before decanting, once the density check is within limits {{2.2.2.1}} {{2.3}} {{1.2(x)}} | At random, or when a trigger arises (see below) {{2.4}} |
| Size | At least 4 x 1 litre of MS and 2 x 1 litre of HSD {{2.2.1(i)}} | 2 x 1 litre of MS and/or 1 x 1 litre of HSD {{2.3}} | MS: 6 x 1 litre per tank. HSD: 3 x 1 litre per tank {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}} |
| Held by | The supply location {{2.2.1(iii)}} | The dealer. The transporter may take a second set {{2.3}} | Three equal sets: the dealer, the oil company's division, territory or regional office, and the lab {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}} |

In the lab's shorthand, TL1 is the latest tank lorry retention sample and SL1 is the supply location sample that goes with it {{2.5(G)}}. If the nozzle sample fails while TL1 passes, the lab also tests two further samples that MDG calls TL2 and SL2. It does not define them further {{2.5(G)}}.

The invoice is the link between your lorry and its supply location sample. The supply location is to note on it the density at 15°C, the filling point, the number of the storage tank the lorry was filled from and the time the lorry was released {{2.2.1(iv)}}. The tank named in the delivery documents tells the oil company which supply location sample is the match {{2.4.1}}.

## What you do on every tank lorry

- Check density and dips first. Then draw, seal, label and sign the tank lorry sample before you decant {{1.2(x)}} {{2.3}}.
- For each tank, keep the samples from its two latest loads. Where a product has more than one tank, mark the tank number on the container {{2.9.2}}.
- Ask your oil company in good time for containers, seals and labels {{2.6(B)}}.

The full steps are in [the tank lorry retention sample](@sampling-retention).

## When an inspector samples your pump

These people can draw samples at your outlet: [mobile labs](@sampling-mobile-lab), oil company officials, agencies the oil company has authorised, and any other authority the law empowers to do so {{2.2.2.2}}. Joint industry teams can draw samples during joint inspections, whatever quick tests such as density, stock variation or filter paper have shown {{2.4.3}}.

Random sampling by company officials covers at least 2% of the outlet network every month, in addition to the MDG samples {{2.4}}. Samples are also drawn when stock variation is past the permitted limit, when the density test or the filter paper test fails, during a special drive or campaign, and when there is a specific complaint against the outlet {{2.2.2.2}} {{2.4}}.

Samples are taken from the nozzles of the dispensing units on every MS and HSD tank {{2.2.2.2}}. For petrol the sample must come through a nozzle that delivers non-2T (not premixed) petrol. If every nozzle on a tank delivers 2T-premixed petrol, the sampler must take care that the sample has no 2T oil in it {{5.1 Note (ii)}}.

Officials bring their own aluminium containers and wooden boxes. They pay for what they collect and obtain a cash memo {{2.2.2.2}}, and the inspecting officer makes the payment to the dealer {{Chapter 2, General Notes (4)}}. Refusing to allow sampling or an inspection is a major irregularity {{5.1.8}} {{8.3}}. See [what happens at an inspection](@inspection) and [penalties](@penalties).

## What goes to the lab

For each tank sampled, the oil company's representative must make sure three kinds of sample reach the lab together {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}:

1. **The nozzle samples** drawn from that tank: 2 bottles for MS, 1 for HSD.
2. **Your tank lorry samples** from the tank's last two loads. They must be for the tank connected to the dispensing unit that was sampled.
3. **The supply location reference samples** from the tank that supplied you, as named in the delivery documents: 2 bottles for MS, 1 for HSD. If your tanks last received product on different days, the matching supply location samples for each delivery go too.

For MS, if the lab has no equipment for the octane number test, the second sample goes to a lab that does {{2.4.1}}.

The inspector does not open the sealed wooden box that holds your tank lorry samples. He checks the seal and labels on the outside, takes the box into his custody, and notes this in a joint memo that you or your representative acknowledge {{2.5(C)}}.

### An illustration

For example, an outlet has two MS tanks and one HSD tank, and an inspector draws nozzle samples from every tank {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}.

| Tank | Bottles drawn | With you | With the oil company's office | To the lab |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MS tank 1 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| MS tank 2 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| HSD tank | 3 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| **Total** | **15** | **5** | **5** | **5** |

Besides these 5 nozzle bottles, the lab also receives the tank lorry samples the outlet held for the last two loads of each tank, and the matching supply location samples.

## How the lab reads the result

1. The lab tests the nozzle sample, TL1 and SL1, and checks the three results against the reproducibility limits {{2.5(G)}}.
2. If the nozzle sample fails and TL1 passes, the lab also tests TL2 and SL2. Action against the dealer or the transporter rests on these results {{2.5(G)}}.
3. A decision waits until every relevant sample has been tested: the supply location sample if it is still within its retention period, the outlet's retained nozzle sample, and the dealer's tank lorry sample if he produced it at the inspection {{2.5(I)}}.
4. If the supply location sample is gone because it was already sent for lab testing earlier, the lab uses that earlier test report {{Chapter 2, General Notes (1)}}.

The tests are listed in Annexure 5. Petrol is tested to IS 2796, IS 17586 and IS 17021, and diesel to IS 1460 and IS 16531 {{2.10}} {{annexure 5}}. These Indian Standards are revised from time to time, so check the current version of each.

The density comparison uses two reference points: the figure on the dispatch document until the lorry is decanted, then the density of the mixed product in your tank, noted after at least 50 litres have gone through the pump {{2.10}}. See [reference density](@density-reference-10am).

When every sample passes, the oil company tells the dealer in writing. The retention samples held at outlets, oil company offices, dispatch locations and the transporter can then be disposed of {{2.5(F)}}. If one fails, read [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed).

## Containers, seals and labels

- Samples go into one-litre aluminium containers, each kept in a wooden box {{2.6(A)}}.
- For a tank lorry sample, the empty container is first rinsed with the lorry's own product {{2.3}}.
- The container and the box are both sealed, using plastic seals with random numbers, sealing wire and pliers {{2.7}}.
- Tank lorry samples carry the label in Annexure 3, and nozzle samples the label in Annexure 4 {{2.3}} {{2.4}}.
- The oil company provides containers, seals, wire, pliers and printed labels at no cost. The dealer has to ask for them in good time {{2.6(B)}} {{2.7}} {{2.8}}.
- The people drawing a sample share responsibility for checking the container before and after filling, and for the right label and seal number {{2.5(A)}}.

## How long samples are kept

| Where | Sample | How long |
|---|---|---|
| Supply location | Reference samples | At least 30 days {{2.9.1}} |
| Your outlet | Tank lorry samples | The two latest loads into each underground tank {{2.9.2}} |
| Your outlet | Your set of nozzle samples | Until testing and what follows it are finished {{2.9.2}} |
| Oil company's division, territory or regional office | Its set of nozzle samples | Until testing and what follows it are finished {{2.9.3}} |
| Transporter | Tank lorry sample, if he chooses to keep one | Until testing and what follows it are finished {{2.9.4}} |

## Time limits from sample to result

| Step | Preferred time |
|---|---|
| Samples reach the lab | Within 10 days of drawal {{2.10}} {{2.5(D)}} |
| Lab tests the samples | Within the next 15 days {{2.10}} |
| Oil company tells the dealer the result | Within 5 days of receiving the report {{2.10}} |
| Result reaches the dealer, pass or fail | Within 30 days of drawal {{2.5(F)}} |
| During special drives | Samples reach the lab within 15 days, testing done within 30 days of receipt {{2.10}} |

Most of these times are worded "preferably", although clause 2.10 also says the time frame is to be followed {{2.10}}. MDG says the time frames are there to keep the system moving and have no connection with the quality or result of the product {{2.5(L)}}.

> **Note:** if you cannot show your tank lorry samples when an inspector draws samples, a sample you produce later is not considered. The nozzle sample is then compared with the supply location sample alone, and any action is against the dealer only {{2.5(E)}}. One exception: if your tank lorry samples were already sent for testing earlier, the inspector notes this and the nozzle sample is compared with that earlier result {{Chapter 2, General Notes (3)}}.
