## What 3-tier sampling is for

MDG 2024 sets out to prove one thing: the petrol and diesel your outlet sells is the same product your oil company supplied to you {{2.1}}. To prove it, fuel is sampled at three points, and the lab compares the samples.

The comparison has a second use. When a sample fails, the three sets help show where the problem began: at the outlet, in the tank lorry, or at the supply location {{2.1}}.

MDG names its method "3-tier" {{2.1}} but does not list the three tiers in one place. The three below are the three samples the lab tests and compares {{2.5(G)}}.

MDG deems a sample failed when its results and the reference sample's results do not fall within the "reproducibility/permissible limit" of the test method {{2.10}}. In plain terms, that is the gap a test method allows between two results on the same fuel. MDG prints no figures for it.

## The three samples, side by side

| | Supply location sample | Tank lorry sample | Nozzle sample (RO sample) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drawn by | The supply location, from the tank lorry filling point {{2.2.1(i)}} | You or your representative {{2.2.2.1}} | An inspector {{2.2.2.2}} |
| When | Every day, and again after a storage tank is switched {{2.2.1(i)}} {{2.2.1(ii)}} | On receipt of each tank lorry, before decanting, once the density check is within limits {{2.2.2.1}} {{2.3}} {{1.2(x)}} | At random, or after a trigger (see below) {{2.4}} |
| Size | At least 4 x 1 litre of MS and 2 x 1 litre of HSD {{2.2.1(i)}} | 2 x 1 litre of MS and/or 1 x 1 litre of HSD {{2.3}} | MS: 6 x 1 litre per tank. HSD: 3 x 1 litre per tank {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}} |
| Held by | The supply location {{2.2.1(iii)}} | You. The transporter may take a second set {{2.3}} | Three equal sets: you, the oil company's division, territory or regional office, and the lab {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}} |

The lab's shorthand: TL1 is the last tank lorry retention sample, and SL1 is the supply location sample that matches it {{2.5(G)}}. If the nozzle sample fails and TL1 passes, the lab also tests two more samples MDG calls TL2 and SL2. MDG does not define them further {{2.5(G)}}.

The invoice ties your tank lorry to the right supply location sample. The supply location should show on it the density at 15°C, the filling point, the number of the storage tank the lorry was filled from, and the time the lorry was released {{2.2.1(iv)}}. The tank named in the delivery documents tells the oil company which supply location sample is the match {{2.4.1}}.

## Who draws nozzle samples, and when

These may draw samples at your outlet {{2.2.2.2}}:

- oil company officials
- mobile labs (see [what a mobile lab does](@sampling-mobile-lab))
- agencies authorised by the oil company
- any other authority the law empowers to do so

Joint industry teams can also draw samples during joint inspections, whatever the result of quick tests such as density, stock variation or filter paper {{2.4.3}}.

Random sampling by company officials covers at least 2% of the retail outlet network every month, over and above the MDG samples {{2.4}}. Samples are also drawn when {{2.2.2.2}} {{2.4}}:

- stock variation goes beyond the permissible limit
- the density test fails
- the filter paper test fails
- there is a special drive or campaign
- there is a specific complaint against the outlet

The sample comes from the nozzles of the dispensing units on all MS and HSD tanks {{2.2.2.2}}. For petrol it must come through a nozzle that delivers non-2T premixed petrol. If every nozzle on a tank delivers 2T premix, the inspector must take care that the sample is free from 2T oil {{5.1 Note (ii)}}.

Inspectors bring their own aluminium containers and wooden boxes, pay for the samples and obtain a cash memo {{2.2.2.2}}. Refusing to allow sampling is a major irregularity {{5.1.8}} {{8.3}}. See [what happens at an inspection](@inspection).

## What goes to the lab

For each tank sampled, the oil company's representative must see that three kinds of sample reach the lab together {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}:

1. **The nozzle samples** the inspector drew from that tank: 2 bottles for MS, 1 for HSD.
2. **Your tank lorry samples** of the last two loads for that tank. They must be for the tank connected to the dispensing unit that was sampled.
3. **The supply location reference samples** from the tank that supplied you, as named on the delivery documents: 2 bottles for MS, 1 for HSD. If your tanks last received product on different days, the matching supply location samples for each delivery are sent.

For MS, if the lab cannot test octane number, the second sample goes to a lab that can {{2.4.1}}.

Your tank lorry samples stay in their sealed wooden box. The inspector checks the outside seal and labels of the box, takes custody of it, and records this in a joint memo that you or your representative acknowledge {{2.5(C)}}.

## A worked example

For example, an outlet has two MS tanks and one HSD tank, and an inspector draws nozzle samples from every tank {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}.

| Tank | Bottles drawn | With you | With the oil company's office | To the lab |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MS tank 1 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| MS tank 2 | 6 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| HSD tank | 3 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| **Total** | **15** | **5** | **5** | **5** |

Along with the 5 nozzle bottles, the lab also receives the tank lorry samples you held for the last two loads on each of these tanks, and the matching supply location samples.

## How the lab reads the result

1. The lab tests the nozzle sample, TL1 and SL1 and compares the results against the reproducibility limits {{2.5(G)}}.
2. If the nozzle sample fails and TL1 passes, the lab also tests TL2 and SL2. Action against the dealer or the transporter follows from these results {{2.5(G)}}.
3. No decision is taken until the supply location sample (if still within its retention period), the "RO retention sample" and your tank lorry sample (if you produced it at the inspection) have all been tested {{2.5(I)}}.
4. If a supply location sample is no longer there because it was already sent for lab testing earlier, the lab uses that earlier test report {{Chapter 2, General Notes (1)}}.

Tests follow Annexure 5. Petrol is tested to IS 2796, IS 17586 and IS 17021, and diesel to IS 1460 and IS 16531 {{2.10}} {{annexure 5}}. For density, the reference is the figure in the dispatch document until the lorry is decanted. After that it is the density of the mixed product in your tank, recorded after at least 50 litres have gone through the pump {{2.10}}. See [reference density](@density-reference-10am).

If every sample passes, the dealer is told in writing and the retention samples at the outlet, the oil company's offices, the dispatch locations and the transporter can be disposed of {{2.5(F)}}. If one fails, read [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed).

## Containers, seals and labels

- Samples go in one-litre aluminium containers, kept in wooden boxes {{2.6(A)}}.
- Before filling, the empty container is rinsed with the same product {{2.3}}.
- Both the aluminium container and the wooden box are sealed, with random-numbered plastic seals, sealing wire and pliers {{2.7}}.
- Tank lorry samples are labelled as per Annexure 3 {{2.3}}. Nozzle samples use the label in Annexure 4 {{2.4}}.
- The oil company supplies containers, seals, wire, pliers and labels free of cost. You must ask for them in good time {{2.6(B)}} {{2.7}} {{2.8}}.
- Whoever draws a sample is jointly responsible for checking the container before and after filling, and for the correct label and seal number {{2.5(A)}}.

## How long samples are kept

| Where | Sample | How long |
|---|---|---|
| Supply location | Reference samples | At least 30 days {{2.9.1}} |
| Your outlet | Tank lorry samples | The last two loads for each underground tank {{2.9.2}} |
| Your outlet | Your set of nozzle samples | Until testing and the steps after it are finished {{2.9.2}} |
| Oil company's division, territory or regional office | Its set of nozzle samples | Until testing and the steps after it are finished {{2.9.3}} |
| Transporter | Tank lorry sample, if he opts to keep one | Until testing and the steps after it are finished {{2.9.4}} |

## Time limits from sample to result

| Step | Preferred time |
|---|---|
| Samples reach the lab | Within 10 days of drawal {{2.10}} {{2.5(D)}} |
| Lab tests the samples | Within the next 15 days {{2.10}} |
| Oil company tells the dealer the result | Within the next 5 days of receiving the report {{2.10}} |
| Whole chain, pass or fail | Within 30 days of drawal {{2.5(F)}} |
| During special drives | Samples reach the lab within 15 days, testing done within 30 days of receipt {{2.10}} |

MDG words most of these as "preferably", and clause 2.10 adds that the time frame is to be adhered to {{2.10}}. The time frames exist to streamline the system and have no bearing on the quality or result of the product {{2.5(L)}}.

## Your part on every tank lorry

- Check the density and dips, then draw, seal, label and sign the tank lorry sample before you decant {{1.2(x)}} {{2.3}}.
- Keep the samples of the last two loads for each tank, with the tank number marked on the container {{2.9.2}}.
- Ask your oil company in time for containers, seals and labels {{2.6(B)}}.

The full steps are in [the tank lorry retention sample](@sampling-retention).

> **Note:** if you cannot show your tank lorry samples when an inspector draws samples, a sample you produce later is not considered. The nozzle sample is then compared with the supply location sample alone, and any action is against the dealer only {{2.5(E)}}. One exception: if your tank lorry samples were already sent for testing earlier, the inspector notes this and the nozzle sample is compared with that earlier result {{Chapter 2, General Notes (3)}}.
