## Why the register matters

An inspector who arrives will ask for your recorded figures, and the rule on missing reference density turns on whether you have them {{5.1.9}}. The label on a sample drawn from your outlet carries the density from your register {{annexure 4}}. A mobile lab's report sets your tank's density after the last receipt beside the officer's reading {{annexure 12a}} {{annexure 13a}}.

## What the register must record

Annexure 1 is a ruled form. Its heading leaves room for a tank number, a product and a month, so as laid out one form covers one tank of one product for one month. The entries, grouped by the moment they are made, are these.

| Moment | What goes in | When | Clause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every morning | The opening density at 15°C, from a sample drawn at the pump nozzle, against that day's date | Before the first sale, by 10 AM | {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}} |
| A tanker arrives | The density reading of each compartment's sample, and the 15°C density printed on the invoice | Before decanting | {{1.2(viii)}} {{1.1(v)}} {{2.2.1(iv)}} |
| The load has gone into the tank | The 15°C density of the mixed product, from a nozzle sample drawn after at least 50 litres went through the pump | After each decanting | {{1.2(xiv)}} |

## What MDG leaves open

Ask your oil company about these. MDG does not answer them.

- **Temperature on the compartment readings.** MDG says the density and the temperature of each compartment's sample are recorded in the register {{1.2(viii)}}. The invoice entry and the after-decanting entry are stated at 15°C on the form, but the compartment entry is not {{annexure 1}}. The ±3.0 kg/m³ check is made on the 15°C figure {{1.2(iv)}}.
- **Several compartments and several loads.** The form does not say how its compartment and after-decanting entries are meant to be used when a tanker has many compartments, or when a tank takes several loads in a month {{annexure 1}}.
- **Who supplies it, and in what form.** MDG says the company provides the daily stock register {{1.3(ii)}}. It does not say who provides the density register, or whether it may be kept on a computer.

## An example

This is an illustration. The outlet and all figures are made up. Use your own readings.

Take a made-up outlet, with the register kept for Tank No. 2, petrol (MS). A tanker arrives on day 3 and another on day 6. All figures are density in kg/m³ at 15°C.

| Day | What was recorded | Figures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Morning reading | 743.5 |
| 2 | Morning reading | 743.5 |
| 3 | Morning reading, then a tanker with two compartments | Morning 743.0. Compartments 744.5 and 745.0 against an invoice density of 743.0. After decanting and 50 litres through the pump: 744.0 |
| 4 | Morning reading | 744.0 |
| 5 | Morning reading | 744.0 |
| 6 | Morning reading, then a tanker with one compartment | Morning 744.0. Compartment 743.0 against an invoice density of 742.0. After decanting: 743.5 |
| 7 | Morning reading | 743.5 |

On day 3, the two compartments read 1.5 and 2.0 above the invoice figure, so both are within ±3.0 kg/m³ {{1.2(iv)}}. On day 6, the single compartment reads 1.0 above the invoice figure, which is also within ±3.0 kg/m³ {{1.2(iv)}}.

Now suppose inspections on days 4 and 5. An inspector who arrives before 10 AM on day 4 asks for the day-3 after-decanting figure, 744.0, because a tanker came the day before {{5.1.9}}. An inspector who arrives after 10 AM on day 5, with no tanker that day, asks for that morning's figure, 744.0 {{5.1.9}}. See [the reference density rule](@density-reference-10am).

## Keeping the register

- **How long.** Keep it for the present financial year and the one before it {{5.1.12}}. The usual Indian financial year runs from April to March; MDG does not define it. For example, in October 2026 that means the register from April 2025 onwards.
- **If it is missing.** Not keeping a specified record is an irregularity. It is major if the records since the last inspection are missing {{8.3}}. It is minor if those are there but older records are not {{8.4}}. For the amounts, and the qualifiers that go with them, see [the penalties page](@penalties). For the other records MDG lists, see [the records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep).
- **Corrections.** MDG has no correction rule for the dealer's density register. The only correction rule sits in the mobile laboratory chapter and covers its Record Book and test reports: the old value is struck through and the new one written beside it, with name, designation and signature {{7.6.2}}. General practice, not an MDG rule: do the same in your register, and never overwrite.
