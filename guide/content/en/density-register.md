## Why the register matters

The rule on missing reference density asks for your recorded figures when an inspector arrives {{5.1.9}}. The label on a sample drawn from your outlet records the density "as recorded in the Density register" {{annexure 4}}. A mobile lab's report records your tank's density after the last receipt {{annexure 12a}} {{annexure 13a}}.

## The Annexure 1 columns, as printed

Annexure 1 is a ruled form. At the top are three blanks: Tank No., Product and Month. As laid out, the form covers one tank of one product for one month. Below the blanks are these columns.

| Column on the form | What goes in it | When | Clause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Date | The date of the entry | Each day you make an entry | – |
| Morning density | The opening density at 15°C, from a sample drawn at the pump nozzle | Every morning before the first sale, by 10 AM | {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}} |
| Tank truck receipt density: Compartment (four boxes) | The density reading of each compartment's sample | Each tanker, before decanting | {{1.2(viii)}} |
| Density at 15°C as per invoice | The density at 15°C printed on the invoice | Each tanker | {{1.1(v)}} {{2.2.1(iv)}} |
| Density at 15°C after decantation and after dispensing 50 ltrs (three boxes) | The density at 15°C of the mixed product, from a nozzle sample drawn after at least 50 litres went through the pump | After each decanting | {{1.2(xiv)}} |

## What MDG leaves open

Ask your oil company about these. MDG does not answer them.

- **The compartment boxes.** MDG says the density and temperature of each compartment's sample are recorded in the register {{1.2(viii)}}. The invoice column and the after-decant column say 15°C. The compartment heading does not {{annexure 1}}. The ±3.0 kg/m³ check is made on the 15°C figure {{1.2(iv)}}.
- **The extra boxes.** The form has four compartment boxes and three boxes under the after-decant heading. It does not label them or say what each box is for. It does not say what to do with a tanker of more than four compartments {{annexure 1}}.
- **The medium and the supply.** MDG says the company provides the daily stock register {{1.3(ii)}}. It does not say who provides the density register, or whether it may be kept on a computer.

## When entries are made

- **Every morning:** before the first sale, and by 10 AM at the latest {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}}.
- **On every receipt:** each compartment's reading {{1.2(viii)}}, and the invoice density, which has its own column {{annexure 1}}.
- **After every decanting:** once at least 50 litres have gone through the pump {{1.2(xiv)}}.

## A filled example

This example is an illustration. All figures are made up. Use your own readings.

Tank No. 2. Product: petrol (MS). In the table, C1 to C4 are the four compartment boxes. After 1 to After 3 are the three boxes under the after-decant heading. A dash means nothing was entered that day.

| Date | Morning | C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | Invoice | After 1 | After 2 | After 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 743.5 | – | – | – | – | – | – | – | – |
| 2 | 743.5 | – | – | – | – | – | – | – | – |
| 3 | 743.0 | 744.5 | 745.0 | – | – | 743.0 | 744.0 | – | – |
| 4 | 744.0 | – | – | – | – | – | – | – | – |
| 5 | 744.0 | – | – | – | – | – | – | – | – |
| 6 | 744.0 | 743.0 | – | – | – | 742.0 | 743.5 | – | – |
| 7 | 743.5 | – | – | – | – | – | – | – | – |

On day 3 a tanker with two petrol compartments arrives. The invoice density is 743.0. The 15°C figures for the two compartments are 744.5 and 745.0, which are 1.5 and 2.0 above the invoice, so both are within ±3.0 kg/m³ {{1.2(iv)}}. After decanting and 50 litres through the pump, the mixed-tank figure is 744.0.

On day 4, an inspector who arrives before 10 AM asks for the after-decant figure of day 3, which is 744.0, because a load came the day before {{5.1.9}}. An inspector who arrives after 10 AM on day 5, with no load that day, asks for that morning's figure, 744.0 {{5.1.9}}. See [the reference density rule](@density-reference-10am).

On day 6 a single-compartment tanker arrives. The invoice density is 742.0 and the compartment figure is 743.0, which is 1.0 above the invoice and within ±3.0 kg/m³ {{1.2(iv)}}. The after-decant figure is 743.5.

## Keeping the register

- **How long.** Keep it for the current and previous financial years {{5.1.12}}. The usual Indian financial year runs from April to March; MDG does not define it. For example, in October 2026 that means the register from April 2025 onwards.
- **If it is missing.** Not maintaining a specified record is an irregularity. It is major if records since the last inspection are missing {{8.3}}. It is minor if records since the last inspection are there but older ones are not {{8.4}}. For the other records MDG lists, see [the records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep).
- **Corrections.** MDG has no correction rule for the dealer's density register. The only correction rule in MDG sits in the mobile laboratory chapter and covers its Record Book and test reports: strike through the old value and write the new one alongside, with name, designation and signature {{7.6.2}}. General practice, not an MDG rule: do the same in your register, and never overwrite.
