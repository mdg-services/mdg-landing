## The daily toilet checks

The dealer checks these every day {{5.1.14(b)}}. Clause 1.6(vi) and point 7 of the circular repeat the duty {{1.6(vi)}} {{circular}}.

| Area | The dealer checks that | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Condition | The toilet stays clean all the time; water is available; the flush works wherever one is fitted | {{5.1.14(b)(i)}} {{5.1.14(b)(iv)}} {{5.1.14(b)(iii)}} |
| Light and door | The lighting is in order; the door has a latch that works | {{5.1.14(b)(ii)}} {{5.1.14(b)(v)}} |
| Signs and access | Signs are in place; every customer and transit traveller can use the toilet at all times, within the safety protocols | {{5.1.14(b)(vi)}} {{5.1.14(b)(vii)}} |
| Feedback | A “Darpan” QR code is displayed near the toilet | {{5.1.14(b)(viii)}} |

The protocol is displayed near the toilet, and a maintenance sheet is kept and displayed. MDG gives no sheet format: ask your oil company {{5.1.14(b)}}. See also [boards a petrol pump must display](@service-display-boards).

## Who can use the toilet

Every customer and every transit traveller, at all times, within the outlet's safety protocols {{5.1.14(b)(vii)}}. The text does not define either group, does not say the user has to buy fuel, and does not mention a charge.

**Court note, October 2026:** a court case in Kerala dealt with access to outlet toilets, and the position changed during 2025. Check the latest position with your oil company or an advocate.

## What happens at an inspection

Officials act on a dirty toilet, no water or a failed flush, a missing or failed latch, or a customer or transit traveller who cannot use it or complains of that. They photograph the toilet or pass the complaint on, and a letter listing the penalty is issued at once {{5.1.14(b)}}.

## The toilet penalty

### MDG 2024: the rule today

A toilet failure is a minor irregularity {{8.4(iii)(g)}}.

| Instance | Action |
|---|---|
| First | Warning-cum-guidance letter |
| Second | Rs 10,000 per irregularity |
| Third onwards | Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}} |

{{penalty-note}}

For how instances are counted, see [repeat offences](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

### An older figure you may see

Older guideline, replaced on 24.10.2024; not the rule today.

| | MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, as amended to 30.11.2022) |
|---|---|
| Class | Major irregularity {{2012:8.3(vii)}} |
| First instance | Rs 15,000 |
| Second instance | Rs 25,000 |
| Third instance onwards | The higher of Rs 35,000 or 45% of the monthly dealer margin (average of the last 6 months), plus suspension of sales and supplies for 7 days or until the toilet is fixed, whichever is later |

For example, under the old rule only: a 6-month average margin of Rs 2,00,000 makes 45% Rs 90,000, higher than Rs 35,000, so Rs 90,000 applies. News articles may quote even older figures; confirm with your oil company.
