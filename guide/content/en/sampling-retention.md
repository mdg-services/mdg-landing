## What the retention sample is for

It is the dealer's own copy of what was in the tank lorry. If an inspector later takes a nozzle sample that fails, the lab sets it beside the lorry sample and the supply location sample {{2.5(G)}}. Together they help show whether a problem began at the outlet, in transport or at the supply location {{2.1}}.

Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024), the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement, make the dealer and the transporter jointly responsible for correct sampling and safe keeping, "in their own interest" {{2.5(B)}}. A note in chapter 5 says the practice also protects innocent dealers {{5.1 Note (i)}}.

## On every tank lorry, in order

1. **Check the load first.** Compare the invoice details {{1.1}}. Confirm the density is within +/- 3.0 kg/m³ of the invoice density {{1.2(iv)}}, then dip every compartment and test each for water {{1.2(vii)}}. The sample is drawn once the density is within that limit {{2.3}}. If you doubt the product, call the supply location or the area field officer {{1.2(ix)}}.
2. **Get the container ready.** Before you take the sample, rinse the empty aluminium container with the lorry's own product {{2.3}}.
3. **Draw the sample.** From each compartment, first run off at least 20 litres. Then draw a bottom composite sample in which each compartment counts in proportion to the quantity it holds {{2.3}}. Fill 2 x 1 litre for MS and/or 1 x 1 litre for HSD {{2.3}}.
4. **Seal and label before unloading.** Seal the container and the wooden box, and fill in the Annexure 3 label {{2.3}} {{2.7}}. You or your representative and the lorry driver both sign it {{2.3}}.
5. **Mark the tank number** on the container if the product has more than one tank at your outlet {{2.3}} {{2.9.2}}.
6. **Then decant** {{1.2(x)}}.

## What the Annexure 3 label records

The label ties one sample to one delivery. It identifies the delivery (product, lorry, invoice, supply location and oil company), the outlet and the tank the load went into. It also holds the proof: when the sample was drawn, the density on the invoice beside the density found in the lorry, and the seal numbers of the container and the box. A short certificate says the containers were rinsed in the dealer's presence with the same product. You or your representative sign it with the outlet's seal or stamp, the place and the date, and the driver signs it too {{annexure 3}}.

The oil company prints these labels and supplies them to you at no cost {{2.8}}.

## Which samples to keep

For every MS tank and every HSD tank, keep the samples from its two latest loads {{2.3}} {{2.9.2}}.

| Product | Per load | For the last two loads of one tank |
|---|---|---|
| MS | 2 x 1 litre | 4 x 1 litre |
| HSD | 1 x 1 litre | 2 x 1 litre |

As an illustration, take an outlet with two MS tanks, where these are the first loads either tank has received.

- Monday: load A goes into tank 1.
- Tuesday: load B goes into tank 2.
- Wednesday: load C goes into tank 1.

After Wednesday the outlet holds loads A and C for tank 1 and load B for tank 2. That is three loads, or six 1-litre bottles. The tank number on each container tells them apart.

On Saturday load D goes into tank 1. Tank 1's two latest loads are now C and D, so load A drops out of the count. MDG does not say what to do with the older sample, so ask your oil company.

## If the driver will not sign

The lorry is not decanted. The dealer contacts the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3 Note (i)}}.

A second note to the same clause covers another case. If the dealer sees an unusual deviation by the lorry in time or route, he informs the supply location or the sales officer and decants only after receiving further instructions {{2.3 Note (ii)}}. See [vehicle tracking](@tanker-vts).

## The transporter's set

The transporter may ask for a second set of the same sample, labelled and signed by the dealer or his representative and the driver. The dealer hands it to the driver or the transporter's representative against payment. The transporter or driver also leaves a Rs 300 deposit for each sample container, and arranges his own safe keeping {{2.3}}.

MDG does not say how much the payment is. Ask your oil company if it comes up.

The transporter holds his sample until testing and what follows it are finished {{2.9.4}}. A transporter who is not also the dealer can ask for his sample to be tested {{2.5(J)}}. See [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed).

## When an inspector draws samples

- The inspector does not open the sealed wooden box to look at the samples inside. He checks the seal and labels on the outside, takes the box into his custody, and notes this in a joint memo that you or your representative acknowledge {{2.5(C)}}.
- If the lab later finds the inner container leaking or tampered with, MDG places the responsibility for its condition on the dealer or the transporter, whichever of them the sample belongs to, and the case is handled as clause 2.5(E) describes {{2.5(C)}}.

## If you cannot show the samples

If tank lorry samples were not kept, or are not produced at the time of sampling, the inspector records that fact. A sample the dealer produces later is not considered for testing. The nozzle sample is compared with the supply point sample, and any action is against the dealer only {{2.5(E)}}.

> **Note:** one case is different. If your tank lorry samples were already sent for testing earlier, the inspector notes this, and the nozzle sample is compared with the result already obtained for those samples {{Chapter 2, General Notes (3)}}.

A note in chapter 5 adds that a dealer whose samples are missing at an inspection is to be told to follow the procedure {{5.1 Note (i)}}. The list of irregularities in chapter 5 has no separate line for a missing retention sample. The consequence MDG states is the one above.
