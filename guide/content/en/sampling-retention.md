## What the retention sample is for

It is your own copy of what was in the tank lorry. If an inspector later draws a sample from your nozzle and it fails, the lab compares it with the lorry sample and the supply location sample {{2.5(G)}}. This helps show whether the problem began at the outlet, in transport or at the supply location {{2.1}}.

MDG puts the care of this sample on both the dealer and the transporter, "in their own interest" {{2.5(B)}}. It also says the practice is there to safeguard the interests of innocent dealers {{5.1 Note (i)}}.

## Step by step, on every tank lorry

1. **Check first.** Match the invoice details {{1.1}}, check that the density is within +/- 3.0 kg/m³ of the invoice density {{1.2(iv)}}, then check the dips and test each compartment for water {{1.2(vii)}}. MDG ties the sample to this: when the density check is within the permissible limit, you draw the retention sample {{2.3}}. If you doubt the quality of the product, contact the supply location or the area field officer {{1.2(ix)}}.
2. **Rinse the empty aluminium container** with the same product from the tank lorry, before drawing the sample {{2.3}}.
3. **Remove at least 20 litres** from each compartment {{2.3}}.
4. **Draw a bottom composite sample.** It must be proportionate to the quantity received in each compartment {{2.3}}.
5. **Fill the right amount:** 2 x 1 litre for MS and/or 1 x 1 litre for HSD {{2.3}}.
6. **Seal and label** the sample as per Annexure 3, before unloading. Both the aluminium container and the wooden box are sealed {{2.3}} {{2.7}}.
7. **Get both signatures.** The label is signed jointly by you or your representative and the tank lorry driver {{2.3}}.
8. **Mark the tank number** on the container if the product has more than one tank at your outlet {{2.3}} {{2.9.2}}.
9. **Now decant** {{1.2(x)}}.

## What the Annexure 3 label asks for

The label has fields for these details {{annexure 3}}:

- the supply location and the division, territory or region
- the outlet's name and address, the oil company and the product
- the source of the sample
- the tank lorry number and the invoice number
- the date and time the sample was drawn
- the density at 15°C, both as recorded on the invoice and as found in the sample from the lorry
- the number of the tank at your outlet where the product is decanted
- the seal numbers of the aluminium container and the wooden box
- a certificate that the containers were rinsed in your presence with the same product, signed by you or your representative with the outlet's seal or stamp, and the place and date
- the driver's name and signature, the transporter's name and the oil company

Fill it fully and legibly. The oil company prints these labels and supplies them to you free of cost {{2.8}}.

## Which samples to keep

Keep the samples of the **last two loads for each storage tank** of MS and HSD {{2.3}} {{2.9.2}}.

| Product | Per load | For the last two loads of one tank |
|---|---|---|
| MS | 2 x 1 litre | 4 x 1 litre |
| HSD | 1 x 1 litre | 2 x 1 litre |

For example, an outlet has two MS tanks, and these are the first loads either tank has received.

- Monday: load A goes into tank 1.
- Tuesday: load B goes into tank 2.
- Wednesday: load C goes into tank 1.

After Wednesday you hold loads A and C for tank 1, which are its last two loads, and load B for tank 2. That is three loads, or six 1-litre bottles. The tank number on each container tells them apart.

On Saturday load D goes into tank 1. The last two loads for tank 1 are now C and D, so load A is no longer one of them. MDG does not say what to do with an older sample, so ask your oil company.

## If the driver will not sign

Do not decant. Contact the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3 Note (i)}}.

A second note to the same clause covers another case. If you see an unusual deviation by the tank lorry in time or route, tell the supply location or the sales officer and decant only after you get further instructions {{2.3 Note (ii)}}. See [vehicle tracking](@tanker-vts).

## The transporter's set

The transporter can ask for another set of the same sample, labelled and signed by you and the driver. You hand it over against payment to the driver or the transporter's representative. The transporter or driver also pays you a deposit of Rs 300 per sample container. The transporter arranges his own safe keeping {{2.3}}.

MDG does not say how much the payment is. Ask your oil company if it comes up.

The transporter keeps his sample until testing and the steps after it are finished {{2.9.4}}. A transporter who is not also the dealer can ask for his sample to be tested {{2.5(J)}}. See [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed).

## When an inspector draws samples

- The inspector cannot open the sealed wooden box to check the tank lorry samples inside. He checks the outside seal and labels, takes custody of the box, and records it in a joint memo that you or your representative acknowledge {{2.5(C)}}.
- If the lab finds the inner container leaking or tampered, responsibility rests with the dealer or the transporter, depending on whose retention sample is being tested. The case is dealt with under clause 2.5(E) {{2.5(C)}}.
- The outside seal and labels of the wooden box are what the inspector checks, so they need to be intact when the box is handed over {{2.5(C)}}.

## If you cannot show the samples

When tank lorry samples are not retained or not made available at the time of sampling, the inspector records the fact. A tank lorry sample you produce later is not considered for testing. The result of the nozzle sample is compared with the supply point sample, and any action is against the dealer only {{2.5(E)}}.

> **Note:** one case is different. If your tank lorry samples were already sent for testing earlier, the inspector notes this, and the nozzle sample is compared with the result already obtained for those samples {{Chapter 2, General Notes (3)}}.

MDG also says that when the samples are found missing at inspection, the dealer has to be instructed to follow the procedure {{5.1 Note (i)}}. The list of irregularities in chapter 5 has no separate line for a missing retention sample. The consequence MDG states is the one above.
