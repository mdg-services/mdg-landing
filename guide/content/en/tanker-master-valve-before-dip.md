## What MDG says

The dealer releases the master valve levers so that product fills the pipelines. This comes before the dips are checked, and the dips are read with reference to the calibration certificate. MDG ties this to calibration: tank lorries are normally calibrated with the lines full {{1.2(iii)}}.

## Where it sits in the order

It is the third step in MDG's list, ahead of the certificate check {{1.2(v)}} and the dips {{1.2(vii)}}; the density check {{1.2(iv)}} sits between the valve step and the certificate. The whole order is on [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker), and the rod and certificate checks are on [dip rod and certificate](@tanker-dip-rod-and-certificate).

## Why it matters, in plain words

MDG's own words are short: lorries are normally calibrated with the lines full {{1.2(iii)}}. Plain reasoning, not MDG text: for a lorry calibrated with full lines, the certificate's figures assume full lines, so a dip read with empty lines can read short against the certificate. MDG says "normally", not "always", and points to the certificate {{1.2(iii)}}. MDG does not say how much difference empty lines make to a dip.

If the dip still reads short against the certificate, see [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water).

## What MDG does not say

- **How long to wait** after releasing the levers.
- **Which valves count** as master valves, or who moves the levers.
- **What to do if the lorry was not calibrated with full lines.** MDG says only to refer to the certificate. If it is unclear, ask the supply location or your oil company.
