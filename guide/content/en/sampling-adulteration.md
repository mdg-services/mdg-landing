## What counts as adulteration

MDG gives three parts, joined by "and/or". On its wording, any one can apply {{5.1.1}}.

1. **Foreign matter.** A foreign substance is put into the fuel illegally or without authority, so that it no longer meets its Indian Standard: IS 2796, IS 17586 and IS 17021 for petrol (MS), IS 1460 and IS 16531 for diesel (HSD). The standards are revised from time to time, so check the current version of each.
2. **A failed lab comparison.** The sample and the reference sample fall outside the test method's reproducibility or permissible limits. See [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed).
3. **Any other test** the competent authority issues from time to time for identifying adulteration.

## The penalty

Adulteration is a critical irregularity. The action is termination of the dealership at the first instance {{8.2}}.

{{penalty-note}}

Before termination is recommended or approved, the dealership's signatories or their nominees get a personal hearing, with one more chance if they miss the date, after which the case can go ahead without them {{8.6}}. See [the show cause notice](@inspection-show-cause-notice).

A dealer can appeal within 30 days of receiving the termination order. The appeal is for termination only {{8.9}}. See [appeals](@inspection-appeal).

## Who pays for the fuel

Fuel proven adulterated goes to the nearest refinery {{5.1.1}}.

| Where it is proven | Who pays | What the dealer receives |
|---|---|---|
| At the outlet | The dealer: all expenses, including moving and pumping the product, cleaning the tank, incidental charges and local levies {{5.1.1}} | The furnace oil value of the quantity that reaches the refinery {{5.1.1}} |
| By the transport contractor or crew | The contractor: all expenses, the loss from downgrading and any transit loss, besides action under the industry Transport Discipline Guidelines {{5.1.1}} | The full value of the product {{5.1.1}} |
