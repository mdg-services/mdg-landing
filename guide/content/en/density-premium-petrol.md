## What MDG 2024 lists for petrol

MDG's petrol table covers the BS VI grades MG, E5, E10, E12, E15 and E20 {{7.6}}. Its two columns give the same density. The lines for MG 91, MG 95 and MG 98 are about colour {{7.6}} and octane {{annexure 5}}, not density. The petrol mobile lab report also carries a single 720–775 {{annexure 12a}}.

| Line in MDG's petrol tables | MG, E5, E10, E12, E15 | E20 |
|---|---|---|
| Density at 15°C, kg/m³ {{7.6}} | 720–775 | 720–775 |
| Minimum octane number (RON), by grade {{annexure 5}} | MG 91: 91, MG 95: 95 | MG 95: 95, MG 98: 98 |

MDG never uses the word "premium" and does not say which grade is which brand.

## Power, XP95 and Speed

MDG names none of them {{7.6}}. General knowledge, not MDG text: oil companies sell their premium petrol under brand names such as these. Octane describes how well petrol resists knocking in an engine. It is a different test from density. Which MDG grade a brand matches, ask your oil company.

## A pump that sells both

Each lot is checked against its own invoice, whichever petrol it is:

1. Note the 15°C density the supply location wrote on the invoice {{2.2.1(iv)}}.
2. Read density and temperature on each compartment's sample, convert to 15°C and compare. The gap must be within ±3.0 kg/m³ {{1.2(iv)}} {{1.2(viii)}}. See the [invoice check](@density-invoice-check) and the [15°C calculator](@tools-density-at-15).
3. After decanting, the reference for that tank is its own mixed reading, taken after at least 50 litres have run through the pump {{1.2(xiv)}} {{2.10}}.

For example (made-up figures): one lot is invoiced at 746.0 and reads 747.5 after conversion, a gap of +1.5, inside the limit. Against another lot's invoice of 752.0 the same reading shows -4.5 and looks wrong. Compare only with the lot's own invoice.

## Normal petrol sold as branded

Selling normal petrol as branded fuel on purpose is a major irregularity {{5.1.10}} {{8.3}}; the penalty is on the [branded fuel page](@penalties-branded-fuel-overcharging). A branded sample must still meet at least the IS minimum for petrol {{annexure 5}}.
