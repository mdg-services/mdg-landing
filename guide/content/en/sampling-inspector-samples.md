## When a sample is drawn

- **Random checks.** Company officials sample at random, covering at least 2% of outlets every month, on top of the MDG samples {{2.4}}.
- **A trigger.** Stock variation past the permitted limit, a failed density test, a failed filter paper test, a special drive or a specific complaint {{2.2.2.2}} {{2.4}}.
- **A joint inspection.** A joint industry team can sample whatever the quick tests showed {{2.4.3}}.

## What the inspector takes

Samples come from dispensing-unit nozzles on every MS and HSD tank {{2.2.2.2}}. Each set is sealed and carries the Annexure 4 label {{2.4}}.

| Product | Bottles per tank | You keep | Oil company's office keeps | Lab gets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MS {{2.4.1}} | 6 x 1 litre | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| HSD {{2.4.2}} | 3 x 1 litre | 1 | 1 | 1 |

If a mobile lab's sample fails, the third set goes to the oil company for testing, not directly to the lab {{2.4.4}}.

For petrol, the sample should come only through a nozzle that delivers non-2T (not premixed) petrol. If every nozzle on the tank delivers 2T-premixed petrol, precautions are needed so the sample has no 2T oil {{5.1 Note (ii)}}.

For example, an outlet has two MS tanks and one HSD tank. The inspector draws 6 + 6 + 3 = 15 bottles. You keep 5, the oil company's office keeps 5, and 5 go to the lab {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}.

## What reaches the lab together

The oil company's representative sends the lab three kinds of sample for each tank {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}:

1. The nozzle samples from that tank: 2 bottles for MS, 1 for HSD.
2. Your tank lorry samples from that tank's last two loads.
3. The supply location samples from the tank named on your delivery documents: 2 bottles for MS, 1 for HSD.

If the lab cannot test MS for octane number, the second sample of each goes to one that can {{2.4.1}}.

The inspector does not open the sealed wooden box holding your tank lorry samples. He checks its seal and labels, takes the box into custody, and notes this in a joint memo that you or your representative acknowledge {{2.5(C)}}. If the lab later finds the inner container leaking or tampered with, the dealer or transporter who owns that sample is responsible for its condition, and the case is handled as set out in [the tank lorry retention sample](@sampling-retention) {{2.5(C)}} {{2.5(E)}}.

## If you refuse

Refusing or obstructing sampling or an inspection is a major irregularity {{5.1.8}}. The minimum action is a 15-day suspension of sales and supplies the first time, a 30-day suspension the second time, and termination of the dealership the third time {{8.3}}.
