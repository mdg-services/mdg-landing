## Before the first sale each morning

1. **Record the density by 10 AM.** Take a nozzle sample and write its 15°C density in your register before the first sale, by 10 AM at the latest {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}}. See [reference density](@density-reference-10am).
2. **Test every nozzle.** Use your Weights & Measures verified 5-litre measure and write the litres used in the stock register {{1.4.2(b)}} {{1.4.2(d)}}. MDG prints a band of ±10 ml to ±25 ml per 5 litres; the exact limit is in Legal Metrology law. Inside the limit sales go on, but recalibrate and re-stamp within 15 days; beyond it that nozzle stops {{5.1.2(a)}}. See [the 5-litre test](@quantity-5-litre-test).
3. **Dip every tank and write up the stock.** Record meter readings, product dips and water dips {{1.3}} {{1.4.1(b)}}. The normal gap between book and dip is 4% of tank stock, plus handling loss and, where they apply, shrinkage or TVA {{5.1.11}}. See [stock variation](@stock-variation).

## When a tanker arrives

4. **Match the invoice.** Each compartment's density, brought to 15°C, must be within ±3.0 kg/m³ of the invoice density {{1.2(iv)}}. After decanting, run at least 50 litres through the pump and record the density again {{1.2(xiv)}}. See [the tanker density check](@density-invoice-check).
5. **Keep your own sample.** Before decanting, draw, seal, label and sign it with the driver. Keep the samples of the last two loads for each tank {{2.3}} {{2.9.2}}. See [the retention sample](@sampling-retention).

## Through the day

6. **Show the boards and give the basics.** Display prices {{5.1.15}}, density, opening stock and complaint contacts {{5.1.17}}. Keep air, a telephone, a first aid box and a clean toilet ready {{5.1.14}}. Lapses here are minor {{8.4}}. See [display boards](@service-display-boards) and [customer service](@customer-service).
7. **Keep the records.** Sales register, density register, maintenance records, purchase invoices and sales bills: this and the previous financial year. Inspection record: the previous five years {{5.1.12}}. See [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep).

## When an inspector visits

8. **Let the visit and the sampling go ahead.** Refusing or obstructing is a major irregularity {{5.1.8}} {{8.3}}. A show cause notice is due within 30 days of the inspection, or of the test results if samples were drawn, and you have 10 days to reply {{8.5.6}} {{8.5.7}}. See [what an inspection covers](@inspection) and [the show cause timeline](@inspection-show-cause-notice).

## If an irregularity is found

9. **Know the three classes.** Critical means termination at the first instance {{8.2}}. Major usually means 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies, then 30 days, then termination {{8.3}}. Minor means a warning-cum-guidance letter, then Rs 10,000, then Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}}. See [which class is my lapse](@penalties-classes) and [the penalty ladders](@penalties).
10. **Expect a five-year look-back.** For a second or third instance, the oil company counts back five years from the date the irregularity is established, including the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).
