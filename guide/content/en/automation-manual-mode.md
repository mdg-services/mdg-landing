## The steps when the automation fails

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. They print no step list for a failure. Read together, clauses 1.5(viii) and 5.1.16 point to this order {{1.5(viii)}} {{5.1.16}}:

1. **Report it at once.** Tell the oil company's authorised official that the automation has stopped working {{1.5(viii)}}.
2. **Act on that official's direction** {{1.5(viii)}}.
3. **If the direction is manual mode, get it in writing first.** An email or a signed letter from a company official is the only form MDG admits {{5.1.16}}.
4. **Keep the written permission** on record for later reference {{5.1.16}}.
5. **Do not disable any part without permission.** A deliberate act by the dealership, its staff or anyone else that makes another part of the automation system stop working, fully or partly, without permission, is an irregularity too {{5.1.16(c)}}.

> **Note:** manual mode is not the problem. Manual mode without written permission is. A dispensing unit found in manual mode, or a tank gauge found off, with no email or signed letter behind it is what MDG treats as the irregularity {{5.1.16(a)}} {{5.1.16(b)}}.

## What counts as written permission

| How the permission reached you | Counts under MDG? |
|---|---|
| Email from a company official | Yes {{5.1.16}} |
| Signed letter from a company official | Yes {{5.1.16}} |
| Phone call | No. It is neither an email nor a signed letter. Ask the official to confirm by email |
| SMS or chat message | No. It is neither an email nor a signed letter. Ask for an email |

## What to keep

MDG asks only for a record of the written permission {{5.1.16}}. It also asks the dealer to keep the oil company's correspondence in a file at the outlet, ready to show at all times {{1.5(iv)}}.

General practice, not an MDG rule. When a company official allows manual mode, it helps to note down:

- the email or letter itself, printed or saved, with its date;
- which dispensing unit or device it covers;
- the time manual mode started and the time the automation came back;
- the name of the official who allowed it.

## If there is no permission

The three automation irregularities are major irregularities {{8.3(vii)}}:

| Instance | Action | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| First | Penalty of Rs 1,00,000 | {{8.3(vii)}} |
| Second | Penalty of Rs 2,00,000 and 7 days' suspension of sales and supplies | {{8.3(vii)}} |
| Third | Termination of the dealership | {{8.3(vii)}} |

- These amounts are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}.
- No action can follow until the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}.
- Second and third instances are counted over five years, and irregularities under the previous guideline within those five years count too {{8.5.5}}.

## A worked example

For example, at a made-up outlet (an illustration, not a real case), the automation stops at 9:00 AM on two of the six dispensing units. The manager calls the oil company's authorised official at once {{1.5(viii)}}. The official says to run those two units in manual mode until a technician comes. The manager asks for that by email and does not switch the two units until it arrives.

The email comes at 9:40 AM. The manager saves it, prints a copy for the outlet's file of company correspondence and notes the time manual mode began. At noon an inspector visits, sees the two units in manual mode and asks for the permission. The manager shows the email.

With no email, the same sight would be an irregularity under 5.1.16(a) {{5.1.16(a)}}.

## What MDG does not say

- **Who can give permission.** The clause says "competent authority", its note says "Company official", and no rank is named {{5.1.16}}.
- **How fast the company must reply or send a technician.** No time is set.
- **How long a permission lasts.** Not stated.
- **Whether sales must stop while you wait.** MDG says to report at once and follow the official's direction {{1.5(viii)}}. Ask your oil company.

For these, speak to your oil company. If a notice has already arrived, read [the show cause notice page](@inspection-show-cause-notice).
