## The steps when the automation fails

MDG prints no step list. In practice, clauses 1.5(viii) and 5.1.16 read together point to this order:

1. **Report it at once** to the oil company's authorised official {{1.5(viii)}}.
2. **Act on that official's direction** {{1.5(viii)}}.
3. **If the direction is manual mode, get it in writing first.** An email or a signed letter from a company official is the only form MDG admits {{5.1.16}}.
4. **Keep the written permission** on record {{5.1.16}}.

> **Note:** manual mode is not the problem. Manual mode, or a tank gauge left off, with no email or signed letter behind it is the irregularity {{5.1.16(a)}} {{5.1.16(b)}}.

## What counts as written permission

| How the permission reached you | Counts under MDG? |
|---|---|
| Email from a company official | Yes |
| Signed letter from a company official | Yes |
| Phone call | No. Ask the official to confirm by email |
| SMS or chat message | No. Ask for an email |

MDG asks only for a record of the written permission {{5.1.16}}. General practice, not an MDG rule, is to keep:

- the email or letter itself, with its date;
- which unit it covers, and when manual mode started and ended.

For example, at a made-up outlet the automation stops at 9:00 AM. The manager calls the authorised official {{1.5(viii)}}, asks for the manual mode instruction by email and switches the units only when it arrives. At noon an inspector asks for the permission and is shown the email {{5.1.16}}.

## If there is no permission

The first instance is a penalty of Rs 1,00,000 {{8.3(vii)}}.

## What MDG does not say

- **Who can give permission.** No rank is named; the clause says "competent authority" {{5.1.16}}.
- **Whether sales must stop while you wait.** Ask your oil company {{1.5(viii)}}.
