## Diesel density limits at a glance

| Diesel | Minimum, kg/m³ at 15°C | Maximum, kg/m³ at 15°C |
|---|---|---|
| BS VI diesel (HSD), up to B7 | 810 | 845 |
| Diesel blended with biodiesel, B8 to B20 | 820 | 860 |

Both rows are from {{7.6}}. MDG does not say which range your supply falls under, so ask your oil company. HSD means diesel: see [product names](@product-names). A footnote to the 810–845 row says any relaxation for fuel from Assam crude may be guided by Government notifications, and MDG prints no relaxed figure {{7.6}}. See [density by state and Assam](@density-by-state). For petrol: [petrol density](@density-petrol). Both fuels: [density range](@density-range).

## What a diesel reading means on a hot day

The limits are at 15°C, and a warm sample reads lower. Convert first, with the ASTM table or the [15°C calculator](@tools-density-at-15) {{1.2(iv)}} {{1.5(vi)}}. For example (made-up samples, worked with that calculator):

| Hydrometer reads | Sample temperature | Density at 15°C | Up to B7 (810–845) | B8–B20 (820–860) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 805.0 | 38°C | 821.3 | Inside | Inside |
| 815.0 | 15°C | 815.0 | Inside | Outside |
| 840.0 | 25°C | 846.9 | Outside | Inside |

The first row reads below 810 but is inside after conversion; the third reads 840 but converts above 845. A diesel exactly at 810 (15°C) reads about 792.0 at 40°C, and one at 845 about 827.7. The printed ASTM table decides if it differs in the last digit {{1.2(iv)}} {{1.5(vi)}}. For other temperatures use the [density temperature chart](@tools-density-chart).

## If your diesel reading is outside the limit

1. Take the density and the temperature from the same sample {{1.2(viii)}} and convert to 15°C. The [density checker](@tools-density-check) does it. If the figure surprises you, read both again (general practice, not an MDG rule).
2. For a tanker, more than 3.0 kg/m³ from the invoice is outside the limit {{1.2(iv)}}.
3. If you doubt the quality, MDG says to contact the supply location or the area field officer {{1.2(ix)}}. That step is written for a tanker being decanted. For your own tank, ask your oil company.

The lab tests on a diesel sample cover appearance, colour, density, distillation and viscosity, whatever the density result {{7.4}}. A sample that fails on density or any other test is acted on as established adulteration, and the mobile lab suspends sales and supplies {{7.4}}. Adulteration is a critical irregularity, with termination at the first instance {{8.2}}.

{{penalty-note}}

> **Note:** the hydrometer in your hand is not the lab. A higher or lower figure inside the range is not better or worse: see [high or low density](@density-high-or-low).
