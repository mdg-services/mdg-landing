## What an instance is

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the rules the oil companies apply to their dealers under the dealership agreement. Major and minor penalties in them step up with each instance: first, second, third. A repeat finding costs more than the first {{8.3}} {{8.4}}. A critical irregularity already means termination at the first instance {{8.2}}. The ladders are on [the penalty page](@penalties). This page covers how the count works and what follows a fine that is not paid.

> **Note:** every action on this page is a minimum. The competent authority of the oil company can take higher action, termination included, at the first instance or any later one {{8.5.1}}. None of it applies until the irregularity has been established {{8.5.2}}.

## The five-year count

To place a new finding on a ladder, the oil company counts back five years from the date the irregularity is established and looks at the earlier ones in that window. Irregularities under the previous guideline inside the window count as instances {{8.5.5}}. MDG separately asks the dealer to keep inspection records for the previous five years {{5.1.12}}.

Illustration: a made-up outlet is found without the reference density figure, and the irregularity is established on 9 March 2026, so the look-back runs from 9 March 2021. Each row assumes the earlier findings were the same irregularity.

| Earlier findings of the same irregularity | Inside the five years? | This finding is | Action (standard major ladder) |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | n/a | First instance | 15 days' suspension {{8.3}} |
| 2 February 2021 | No, more than five years back | First instance | 15 days' suspension {{8.3}} |
| 18 June 2023 (under MDG 2012) | Yes | Second instance | 30 days' suspension {{8.3}} |
| 18 June 2023 and 14 January 2024 (both under MDG 2012) | Both | Third instance | Termination {{8.3}} |

Missing reference density is a major irregularity in both MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) and MDG 2024 {{2012:8.3}} {{8.3}}, so the 2023 and 2024 findings count. MDG 2024 took effect on 24.10.2024, so both findings in the last two rows fall under the previous guideline. See [reference density by 10 AM](@density-reference-10am).

MDG words the cycle as running "starting from the date of first irregularity" and then restates it as a look-back over the previous five years {{8.5.5}}. This page follows the look-back. If your case turns on the exact start of the five years, ask your oil company.

## Several irregularities found together

When several irregularities are found together at one outlet, MDG says each is counted as an instance in its own class, critical, major or minor, and suitable action is taken for each irregularity and for its instance. It calls the result a compounding effect {{8.5.3}}.

Two statements in MDG bear on this. Clause 8.5.3 counts every irregularity found together on its own. Clause 8.4 says that one or more of the miscellaneous minor items count as one irregularity for taking action {{8.4}}. That group is every minor item except missing older records and a missing air facility, telephone or first aid box. MDG does not say how the two fit together.

MDG also gives no worked case for two findings of the same class on one day. Nor does it say whether an earlier irregularity of a different kind in the same class counts as an earlier instance, for example a missing complaint book followed years later by a dirty toilet, both minor. Every dated example on this page uses the same irregularity each time. How the oil company orders and counts such cases is for it to say, so ask your oil company rather than guess.

## Four kinds of suspension

"Suspended" can mean four main things.

| Kind | When | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Stop while a lab tests the fuel | Reference density missing at inspection, or positive stock variation beyond the limit: sales and supplies of all products stop at once and samples go to the lab | {{5.1.9}} {{5.1.11}} |
| One dispensing unit or nozzle stops | Seal, totaliser or fitting findings: the unit is suspended and sealed. Short delivery beyond the legal limit with seals intact: the nozzle stops until it is recalibrated and re-stamped | {{5.1.2(a)}} {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}} {{5.1.4}} |
| Penalty suspension | 15 or 30 days for most major irregularities, or 7 days with the second automation penalty | {{8.3}} |
| Suspension for an unpaid fine | From the 31st day, for 15 days, then another 15 days | {{8.7}} |

MDG has smaller stops too. When a nozzle's delivery is off but inside the permitted band, the finding is recorded and sales go on, and the dealer is advised to recalibrate and re-stamp within 15 days. If that is not done, the nozzle's sales are suspended until it is {{5.1.2(a)}}. When negative stock variation goes beyond the limits and the dealer's explanation is not found satisfactory, supply is suspended {{5.1.11}}.

## The 30-day rule for fines

Clause 8.7 deals with some major and minor irregularities where the action is only a fine and sales and supplies are not suspended. Such a fine is due within 30 days of the date of the company's notice {{8.7}}.

On the ladders, the fine-only steps are the Rs 10,000 and Rs 25,000 minor penalties {{8.4}} and the first automation penalty of Rs 1,00,000 {{8.3}}. The second automation step, Rs 2,00,000 with 7 days' suspension, carries a suspension, so it does not match that description. MDG does not say whether the 30-day rule also covers recovery of the differential price for normal fuel sold as branded fuel. Confirm with your oil company.

What follows an unpaid fine {{8.7}}:

1. From the 31st day, sales and supplies are suspended for 15 days.
2. The fine is still to be paid during that suspension. If it is not, the suspension is extended by another 15 days.
3. If the fine is still unpaid when the extended suspension ends, the dealership is terminated.

> **Note:** MDG does not say whether the 30 days count the day of the notice itself. The dates below take the notice date as day 0. Your oil company's count can differ by a day, so check the exact last day against your notice.

## An illustration with dates

Illustration: a made-up outlet is found on 5 September 2024 without a complaint book when a customer asked for it. That was before MDG 2024 took effect on 24.10.2024. Under MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) it was a minor irregularity, and the first-instance action was a warning-cum-guidance letter {{2012:8.4}}.

On 9 March 2026 the same finding is established again. The look-back runs from 9 March 2021, so the 2024 finding counts, although it was under the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}. This is the second instance of a minor irregularity: Rs 10,000 per irregularity {{8.4}}.

The company's notice of the Rs 10,000 fine is dated 1 June 2026. The fine carries no suspension, so the 30-day rule applies {{8.7}}.

| Day | Date | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 June 2026 | Notice of the fine |
| 1 to 30 | 2 June to 1 July | The fine falls due within this period. No suspension. |
| 31 | 2 July | If unpaid, sales and supplies are suspended for 15 days |
| 31 to 45 | 2 to 16 July | The fine is still to be paid in this period |
| 46 to 60 | 17 to 31 July | If still unpaid, the suspension is extended by another 15 days |
| After day 60 | After 31 July | If still unpaid, the dealership is terminated |

For the steps around a notice, see [the show cause notice process](@inspection-show-cause-notice). For a termination, see [the appeal after termination](@inspection-appeal). The appeal in MDG is for termination only, within 30 days of receiving the order {{8.9}}.
