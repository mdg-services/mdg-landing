## The five-year look-back

To place a new finding on a ladder, the oil company looks at the five years before the date the irregularity is established. Any earlier instance in that window counts, including one under the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}. The ladders are on [the penalties page](@penalties).

Illustration: a made-up outlet is found without the reference density figure, established on 9 March 2026, so the look-back starts from 9 March 2021. The irregularity is major in both MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) and MDG 2024 {{2012:8.3}} {{8.3}}, and every earlier finding is assumed to be the same irregularity.

| Earlier findings | Inside the five years? | This finding is | Action (standard major ladder) |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | n/a | First instance | 15 days' suspension {{8.3}} |
| 2 February 2021 | No, more than five years back | First instance | 15 days' suspension {{8.3}} |
| 18 June 2023 (under MDG 2012) | Yes | Second instance | 30 days' suspension {{8.3}} |
| 18 June 2023 and 14 January 2024 (both under MDG 2012) | Both | Third instance | Termination {{8.3}} |

{{penalty-note}}

MDG words the cycle as running "starting from the date of first irregularity" and then restates it as a look-back {{8.5.5}}. This page follows the look-back. If your case turns on the exact start, ask your oil company.

## Several irregularities found together

When several irregularities are found together at one outlet, each is counted as an instance in its own class, and each draws its own action. MDG calls this a compounding effect {{8.5.3}}. Separately, 8.4 counts the miscellaneous minor items as one irregularity when action is decided {{8.4}}. MDG does not say how the two fit, and gives no worked case. Ask your oil company how it would count yours.

A fine that is not paid is a different matter: see [unpaid fines](@penalties-unpaid-fines).
