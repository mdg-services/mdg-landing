## What an instance is

Major and minor penalties in MDG 2024 are built on instances: first, second, third. A repeat finding costs more than the first one {{8.3}} {{8.4}}. Critical irregularities already mean termination at the first instance {{8.2}}. The full ladders are in [the penalty table](@penalties). This page explains how the count works and what happens when a fine is not paid.

## The five-year count

When an irregularity is established, the oil company examines the records of the previous five years from that date. That tells it whether the present irregularity is the first, second or third instance. Irregularities under the previous MDG within those five years also count {{8.5.5}}.

MDG asks the dealer to keep inspection records for the previous five years {{5.1.12}}, the same span.

For example, an outlet is found without the reference density figure on 9 March 2026. The five-year look-back runs from 9 March 2021. Each row below counts the same irregularity.

| Earlier findings of the same irregularity | Inside the five years? | This finding is | Action (standard major ladder) |
|---|---|---|---|
| None | n/a | First instance | 15 days' suspension {{8.3}} |
| 2 February 2021 | No, more than five years back | First instance | 15 days' suspension {{8.3}} |
| 18 June 2023 (under MDG 2012) | Yes | Second instance | 30 days' suspension {{8.3}} |
| 18 June 2023 and 14 January 2024 (both under MDG 2012) | Both | Third instance | Termination {{8.3}} |

Missing reference density is a major irregularity in both MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) and MDG 2024 {{2012:8.3}} {{8.3}}, so the 2023 and 2024 findings count. MDG 2024 took effect on 24.10.2024, so both findings in the last two rows fall under the previous MDG. See [reference density by 10 AM](@density-reference-10am).

MDG 2024 first says the cycle runs "starting from the date of first irregularity" and then explains "in other words" that the records of the previous five years are examined. This page follows that second, plainer explanation {{8.5.5}}. If your case turns on the exact start of the five years, ask your oil company.

## Several irregularities found together

If two or more irregularities are found at the same outlet at the same time, three things happen {{8.5.3}}:

1. Each irregularity is counted as an instance against its own class: critical, major or minor.
2. The oil company takes suitable action for that irregularity and for that instance.
3. It does this for each irregularity, which MDG calls a compounding effect.

In plain words, the oil company does not pick the worst finding and drop the rest. A major and a minor found on the same day each get their own action. Minor items (a) to (h) are the exception: they count together as one irregularity {{8.4}}.

MDG does not print a worked case for two findings in the same class, for example two major irregularities on one day. It also does not say whether an earlier irregularity of a different kind in the same class counts as an earlier instance, for example a missing complaint book followed years later by a dirty toilet, both minor. Every dated example on this page uses the same irregularity each time. How the oil company orders and counts these cases is for it to say. Ask your oil company rather than guess.

## Four main kinds of suspension

"Suspended" can mean four different things.

| Kind | When | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Stop while a lab tests the fuel | Reference density missing at inspection, or positive stock variation beyond limits: sales and supplies of all products stop immediately and samples go to the lab | {{5.1.9}} {{5.1.11}} |
| One dispensing unit or nozzle stops | Seal, totaliser or fitting findings: the unit is suspended and sealed. Short delivery beyond the legal limit with seals intact: the nozzle stops until recalibrated and re-stamped | {{5.1.2(a)}} {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}} {{5.1.4}} |
| Penalty suspension | 15 or 30 days for most major irregularities, or 7 days with the second automation penalty | {{8.3}} |
| Suspension for an unpaid fine | From the 31st day, for 15 days, then another 15 days | {{8.7}} |

MDG has smaller stops too. If a nozzle's delivery is off but still within the permitted limit, it is recorded and sales continue, but the dealer is advised to recalibrate and re-stamp within 15 days. If that is not done, its sales are suspended until it is {{5.1.2(a)}}. If negative stock variation goes beyond the limits and the dealer's explanation is not found satisfactory, supply is suspended {{5.1.11}}.

## The 30-day rule for fines

Some penalties are only a fine, with no suspension attached. The fine must be paid within 30 days from the date of the company's notice {{8.7}}.

In the penalty table, the fine-only steps are the Rs 10,000 and Rs 25,000 minor penalties {{8.4}} and the first automation penalty of Rs 1,00,000 {{8.3}}. A step that already carries a suspension, such as the second automation penalty of Rs 2,00,000 plus 7 days, is outside that wording. MDG does not say in so many words whether the 30-day rule also covers recovery of the differential price for branded fuel. Confirm with your oil company.

What follows an unpaid fine {{8.7}}:

1. Sales and supplies are suspended from the 31st day for 15 days.
2. The fine should be paid within that suspension. If it is not, the suspension is extended for another 15 days.
3. If it is not paid within the extended suspension, the dealership is terminated.

> **Note:** MDG does not say whether the 30 days count the day of the notice itself. The dates below take the notice date as day 0. Your oil company's count can differ by a day, so check the exact last day against your notice.

## A worked example with dates

For example, an outlet is found on 5 September 2024 without a complaint book when a customer asked for it. That was before MDG 2024 took effect on 24.10.2024. Under MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) it was a minor irregularity, and the first-instance action was a warning-cum-guidance letter {{2012:8.4}}.

On 9 March 2026 the same finding is established again. The five-year look-back runs from 9 March 2021, so the 2024 finding counts, even though it was under the previous MDG {{8.5.5}}. This is the second instance of a minor irregularity: Rs 10,000 per irregularity {{8.4}}.

The company's notice of the Rs 10,000 fine is dated 1 June 2026. The fine carries no suspension, so the 30-day rule applies {{8.7}}.

| Day | Date | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 June 2026 | Notice of the fine |
| 1 to 30 | 2 June to 1 July | The dealer can pay. No suspension. |
| 31 | 2 July | If unpaid, sales and supplies are suspended for 15 days |
| 31 to 45 | 2 to 16 July | The fine should be paid within this period |
| 46 to 60 | 17 to 31 July | If still unpaid, the suspension is extended for another 15 days |
| After day 60 | After 31 July | If still unpaid, the dealership is terminated |

## What to keep on file

General practice, not an MDG rule: keep every inspection letter, notice and payment receipt in one dated file. Write the notice date on it and diary the 30th and 31st days. That file is also the record you need if the oil company asks how many earlier instances there are.
