## What counts as tampering

- Adding, removing, replacing or manipulating any part of the unit: a mechanism, gear, microprocessor chip, electronic part or the manufacturer's software {{5.1.4}}.
- Any fitting or gear likely to manipulate the delivery {{5.1.4}}.
- The manufacturer's independent opinion is obtained, and a decision is taken {{5.1.4}}.
- The unit stops selling and is sealed, and samples of all products go to the lab {{5.1.4}}. The finding is critical: termination at the first instance {{8.2(iv)}} {{8.2}}.

{{penalty-note}}

> **Note:** MDG does not say that a part replaced by the company's own representative, or one written in the job report, is exempt. The clause names no exception for repairs {{5.1.4}}. Ask your oil company before a unit is opened.

## Who repairs, who records

- The oil company arranges breakdown repair of its own equipment. The dealer handles it properly, does the day-to-day upkeep and tells the company promptly of any breakdown {{4.2(a)}}.
- The dealer keeps his own equipment in good working order {{4.2(b)}}.
- The Maintenance Job Report is the standard form for the dispensing units and other equipment the company provides {{4.1}}.

## What the job report records

On the Maintenance Job Report, the lines for repairs done, parts replaced and parts required are filled in at the visit, and the dealer and the company's maintenance representative both sign. There are three copies, and one stays at the outlet {{annexure 6}}. What to read before you sign is on [the fitter's job report page](@company-maintenance-job-report). Pump maintenance records are kept for the current and the previous financial year {{5.1.12(ii)}}.

General practice, not an MDG rule: take the outlet's signed copy before the visitor leaves, and check that every part he replaced is written in it.
