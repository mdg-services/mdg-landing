## The check, in order

1. **Release the master valve levers before checking dips** {{1.2(iii)}}. The reason is on [master valve before dip](@tanker-master-valve-before-dip).
2. **Ask the driver for the calibration certificate.** It mentions product level and proof level details {{1.2(v)}}. The invoice also lists the dip rod with its certificate among the details to match {{1.1(vi)}}.
3. **Check the rod's markings.** Look for the Weights & Measures markings at the bottom of the rod and at the proof level {{1.2(vi)}}.
4. **Verify the rod with a standard measuring tape.** MDG calls this an additional check {{1.2(vi)}}.
5. **Dip every compartment** with the rod certified by Weights & Measures that is provided with the lorry {{1.2(vii)}}. Test each compartment for water too: see [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste).
6. **Dip short against the certificate?** Make it up, record it and have both sides sign: see [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water) {{1.2(xi)}}.

## What the supply location must do

Only lorries with a valid calibration certificate and an explosives licence (from the Weights & Measures Department and PESO respectively) should be accepted for filling {{3.1(i)}}. That is the supply location's duty. The explosives licence is not on the dealer's list of receipt checks.

## What MDG does not say

- **No tolerance.** MDG gives no figure for how closely the rod must agree with the tape {{1.2(vi)}}.
- **No step for a failed check.** MDG does not say what to do if the driver cannot show the certificate, the markings are missing or the rod and the tape disagree. Ask your oil company.
- **No explanation of "proof level".** The certificate shows it.

The dips of your own underground tanks are a different matter: see [what a dip is](@stock-what-is-dip).
