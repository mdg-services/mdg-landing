## The three steps

MDG sorts every Chapter 5 irregularity into critical, major or minor {{8.1}}. For the minor class:

| Instance | Action | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| First | Warning-cum-guidance letter | {{8.4}} |
| Second | Rs 10,000 per irregularity | {{8.4}} |
| Third onwards | Rs 25,000 per irregularity | {{8.4}} |

{{penalty-note}}

The head of the Territory, Regional or Divisional office approves the action {{8.8(iii)}}. A fine with no suspension attached is due within 30 days of the company's notice {{8.7}}. See [unpaid fines](@penalties-unpaid-fines).

## What counts as one irregularity

| Counted together as one | Listed on their own |
|---|---|
| Toilet, housekeeping, the price display, the other displays (density, stock, zero sticker, nozzle names, contact numbers), the complaint book, driveway salesmen's uniform and badges, the customer service standard, and short delivery with the Weights & Measures seals intact | Earlier records missing while the records since the last inspection are kept {{8.4(i)}}; no air, telephone or first aid box {{8.4(ii)}} |

The first column is the group MDG counts as one irregularity when action is decided, however many are found together {{8.4}}. The line names only these eight kinds {{8.4}}. For which lapse sits where, see [critical, major or minor](@penalties-classes).

For example (made-up outlet): a warning-cum-guidance letter was issued two years ago for a missing price board. A new inspection finds an unclean toilet and a missing price board together. The two count as one irregularity at the second instance, so the second step above applies once, not twice {{8.4}} {{8.5.5}}. MDG gives no worked case; this follows its wording.

## What MDG 2024 leaves open

- How an air, telephone, first aid or earlier-records lapse counts when found with the group above: clause 8.5.3 counts irregularities found together each in its own class {{8.5.3}}, and MDG does not say how that fits the one-irregularity line.
- Whether the earlier instance must be the same lapse. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

For both, ask your oil company.
