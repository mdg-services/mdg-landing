## What the dealer must do every day

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. This page calls the daily nozzle check the "5-litre test". That name is ours, not MDG's. MDG asks for two separate things: a measure kept for pump testing {{1.4.2(b)}} and a daily check of every nozzle {{1.4.2(d)}}. The limits an inspector applies are stated per 5 litres {{5.1.2(a)}}. The nozzle-check clause does not name the measure, so if you are unsure how your oil company expects the check to be done, ask it.

- **Keep the measure.** A 5-litre measure verified by the Weights & Measures (W&M) Department, used only to test pumps at the outlet {{1.4.2(b)}}.
- **Test every nozzle, every day.** Every nozzle of every dispensing unit is checked for correct delivery before the day's sales begin {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- **Write down the testing quantity.** It goes in the stock register {{1.4.2(d)}}. See [the daily stock register](@stock-daily-register).
- **Report a variation at once.** Tell the W&M Department and the controlling oil company office immediately {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- **Keep the unit off.** W&M re-stamps. Until that is done and the delivery is corrected, the dealer should not run the unit {{1.4.2(d)}}.

The dealer or the oil company's maintenance representative should arrange recalibration straight away, with the W&M Inspector's help {{4.3(a)}}.

MDG gives no method for the test. It does not say how to read the measure, how long to run the nozzle or what to do with the test fuel. General practice, not an MDG rule: many dealers note the nozzle number and the result beside the testing quantity, so a variation can be shown later. Ask your oil company or the W&M office which method they expect.

## If an inspector finds a nozzle off

Clause 5.1.2(a) deals with short delivery when the W&M seals are intact. There are two outcomes.

| What the inspector finds | Sales | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery inside the permissible limit, which the note puts between ±10 ml and ±25 ml per 5 litres | Not suspended | The finding is recorded. The dealer is advised to recalibrate and re-stamp as early as possible, and within 15 days of the date the shortfall was seen. If that does not happen, sales from the unit or nozzle are suspended until it does {{5.1.2(a)}} |
| Short beyond the permissible limit set by the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 and its Rules | Suspended at once through that unit (on a dual unit or MPD, through that nozzle) | Recalibration and re-stamping come before sales restart {{5.1.2(a)}} |

If the seals are not intact, the case is different and far more serious. See [W&M and totalizer seals](@quantity-seals).

MDG points to the Legal Metrology Act and its Rules for the limit, and prints only the range in the note. That range is not one figure, and MDG does not say which figure inside it applies to which unit. It also does not say what a delivery off by less than 10 ml per 5 litres leads to. Read the current Act and Rules, and ask W&M or your oil company which limit applies to your unit.

## An illustration

Illustration, with made-up figures: on 10 November an inspector tests nozzle 2 against a 5-litre measure and finds it 15 ml short.

- 15 ml sits inside the ±10 ml to ±25 ml band in the note, so, reading the note as written, the finding is recorded and sales on nozzle 2 continue.
- The dealer is advised to have the unit recalibrated and re-stamped by 25 November, 15 days after the day it was observed.
- If nozzle 2 is still not re-stamped on 26 November, sales from it are suspended until it is.
- Had the nozzle been short beyond the Legal Metrology limit, sales through it would have stopped at once.

> **Note:** clause 1.4.2(d) tells the dealer to keep a unit off when a variation is found, until it is re-stamped and the delivery is corrected. The 15-day allowance sits in the note to 5.1.2(a) and covers what an inspector finds. MDG does not say how the two fit together for a variation the dealer finds in the morning test. Ask your oil company which rule it applies.

## What a finding costs

Short delivery with W&M seals intact is a minor irregularity {{8.4(iii)(h)}}.

- First instance: a warning-cum-guidance letter.
- Second instance: Rs 10,000 per irregularity.
- Third instance onward: Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}}.

Read those figures with MDG's own limits:

- The amounts are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}.
- Action needs the irregularity to be established first {{8.5.2}}.
- Instances are counted by looking back five years, and irregularities under the previous guideline within that period count too {{8.5.5}}. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).
- Clause 8.4 says the miscellaneous minor findings, short delivery with seals intact among them, count as one irregularity for taking action. Clause 8.5.3 says that when two or more irregularities are found together at one outlet, each counts as an instance in its own class and action is taken for each, with a compounding effect. MDG does not say how the two fit, so ask your oil company how it counts.

MDG does not say in so many words whether a finding inside the permissible band is also counted as this minor irregularity. Ask your oil company how it records these.

### MDG 2024 and the older MDG 2012 side by side

MDG 2024 (effective 24.10.2024) is the current text. MDG 2012, Ver. 7 as amended to 30.11.2022, is an older guideline, replaced on 24.10.2024. The 2012 column is here for comparison only. Confirm with your oil company how an old finding is being counted. See also [MDG 2012 against MDG 2024](@mdg-2012-vs-2024).

| | MDG 2024 (current) | MDG 2012, Ver. 7 (older guideline, replaced on 24.10.2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Class | Minor {{8.4(iii)(h)}} | Major {{2012:8.3}} |
| Shortfall inside the permissible limit | Recorded, sales go on, recalibration and re-stamping advised within 15 days {{5.1.2(a)}} | Sales stop until recalibrated and re-stamped, even inside the limit {{2012:5.1.2(a)}} |
| First instance | Warning-cum-guidance letter {{8.4}} | Rs 25,000 for each nozzle found short beyond the limit {{2012:8.3}} |
| Second instance | Rs 10,000 per irregularity {{8.4}} | Rs 50,000 for each such nozzle, plus 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies (within one year of the first) {{2012:8.3}} |
| Third instance | Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}} | Termination (within one year of the first) {{2012:8.3}} |

## Can a customer ask for a 5 litre check?

MDG 2024 sets out no customer test. It keeps the 5-litre measure for the dealer's pump testing at the outlet {{1.4.2(b)}}. The oil company supplies the customer messages and posters the dealer displays {{1.6(viii)}}, and what they say can differ from one oil company to another. Ask the dealer, and your oil company if you are unsure, whether a customer can request a check.

A customer who is not satisfied can ask for the complaint book, which the dealer must provide when a customer asks {{1.6(iv)}}, and can use the oil company contact name and number that the dealer must display prominently {{1.6(viii)}}. See [what a customer can ask for at the pump](@quantity-zero-before-delivery).
