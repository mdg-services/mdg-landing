## What the dealer must do every day

This page uses "5-litre test" for the daily nozzle check. That name is not MDG's wording. MDG requires the measure to be kept for pump testing {{1.4.2(b)}} and every nozzle to be checked daily {{1.4.2(d)}}, and the limits an inspector applies are stated per 5 litres {{5.1.2(a)}}. The nozzle-check clause does not name the measure, so ask your oil company if you are unsure how it expects the check to be done.

- **Keep the measure.** A standard 5-litre measure, duly verified by the Weights & Measures (W&M) Department, kept for exclusive use in pump testing at the outlet {{1.4.2(b)}}.
- **Test every nozzle, every day.** All nozzles of the dispensing units are checked for correct delivery before sales begin {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- **Record the testing quantity.** The quantity used for testing goes in the stock register {{1.4.2(d)}}. See [the daily stock register](@stock-daily-register).
- **Report any variation at once.** Tell the W&M Department and the controlling oil company office immediately {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- **Keep the unit off.** W&M does the re-stamping. Until it is done and the delivery is set right, the dealer must not operate the unit {{1.4.2(d)}}.

The dealer or the oil company's maintenance representative arranges the recalibration immediately, with the help of the W&M Inspector {{4.3(a)}}.

MDG gives no method for the test. It does not say how to read the measure, how long to run the nozzle or what to do with the test fuel. General practice, not an MDG rule: many dealers note the nozzle number and the result beside the testing quantity, so a variation can be shown later. Ask your oil company or the W&M office which method they expect.

## If an inspector finds a nozzle off

Clause 5.1.2(a) covers short delivery when the W&M seals are intact. Two outcomes are possible.

| What the inspector finds | Sales | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery within the permissible limit, which the note puts at between ±10 ml and ±25 ml per 5 litres | Not suspended | The finding is recorded. The dealer is advised to recalibrate and re-stamp at the earliest, and not later than 15 days from the date the short delivery was observed. If that is not done, sales from the unit or nozzle are suspended until it is {{5.1.2(a)}} |
| Delivery short beyond the permissible limit in the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 and its Rules | Suspended at once through that unit (on a dual unit or MPD, through that nozzle) | Recalibration and re-stamping must be done before sales restart {{5.1.2(a)}} |

If the seals are not intact, the case is different and far more serious. See [W&M and totalizer seals](@quantity-seals).

MDG points to the Legal Metrology Act and Rules for the limit and prints only the range in the note. That range is not one figure, and MDG does not say which figure inside it applies to which unit. It also does not say what a delivery off by less than 10 ml per 5 litres leads to. Ask W&M or your oil company for the limit that applies to your unit.

## A worked example

For example, on 10 November an inspector tests nozzle 2 against a 5-litre measure and finds it 15 ml short.

- 15 ml sits inside the band in the note, so the finding is recorded and sales on nozzle 2 continue.
- The dealer has until 25 November, 15 days from the date of observation, to get the unit recalibrated and re-stamped.
- If nozzle 2 is still not re-stamped on 26 November, sales from it are suspended until it is.
- Had the nozzle been short beyond the Legal Metrology limit, sales through it would have stopped at once.

> **Note:** clause 1.4.2(d) tells the dealer to keep a unit off when a variation is found, until it is re-stamped and set right. The 15-day allowance sits in the note to 5.1.2(a) and covers what an inspector finds. MDG does not say how the two fit together for a variation the dealer finds in the morning check. Ask your oil company which rule it applies. The stricter reading is 1.4.2(d).

## What a finding costs

Short delivery with W&M seals intact is a minor irregularity {{8.4(iii)(h)}}.

- First instance: a warning-cum-guidance letter.
- Second instance: Rs 10,000 per irregularity.
- Third instance onward: Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}}.

One or more of the miscellaneous minor irregularities found together count as one irregularity for taking action {{8.4}}. Instances are counted over five years, and irregularities under the previous MDG within that period count as instances {{8.5.5}}. The oil company may take higher action than the MDG minimum {{8.5.1}}. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

MDG does not say in so many words whether a finding inside the permissible band is also counted as this minor irregularity. Ask your oil company how it records these.

### MDG 2024 and MDG 2012 side by side

MDG 2024 (effective 24.10.2024) is the current text. MDG 2012, Ver. 7 as amended 30.11.2022, is replaced. Confirm with your oil company how an old finding is being counted. See also [MDG 2012 against MDG 2024](@mdg-2012-vs-2024).

| | MDG 2024 (current) | MDG 2012, Ver. 7 (replaced) |
|---|---|---|
| Class | Minor {{8.4(iii)(h)}} | Major {{2012:8.3}} |
| Finding within the permissible limit | Recorded, sales continue, recalibrate and re-stamp within 15 days {{5.1.2(a)}} | Sales stop until recalibrated and re-stamped, even within the limit {{2012:5.1.2(a)}} |
| First instance | Warning-cum-guidance letter | Rs 25,000 per nozzle found short beyond the limit |
| Second instance | Rs 10,000 per irregularity | Rs 50,000 per nozzle, and 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies (within one year of the first) |
| Third instance | Rs 25,000 per irregularity | Termination (within one year of the first) |

## Can a customer ask for a 5 litre check?

MDG 2024 does not set out a customer test. It keeps the 5-litre measure for pump testing at the outlet {{1.4.2(b)}}. IndianOil's Check and Fill display board, an oil company board and not MDG text, invites customers to verify the pump with the dealer's calibrated 5-litre measure. Other oil companies' boards may differ, so ask the dealer, and ask your oil company if you are unsure.

A customer who is not satisfied can use the complaint book, which must be available on demand {{1.6(iv)}}, and the oil company contact name and number the dealer must display {{1.6(viii)}}. See [what a customer can ask for at the pump](@quantity-zero-before-delivery).
