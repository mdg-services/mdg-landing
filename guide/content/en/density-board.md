## The number in plain words

A cubic metre is 1,000 litres. A density of 745 kg/m³ therefore means 1,000 litres of that fuel weigh 745 kg, so one litre weighs about 0.745 kg. The fuels sit in separate bands because diesel is heavier than petrol.

| Fuel | Normal range at 15°C (kg/m³) | One litre weighs about (kg) |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol (MS), every BS VI grade MDG names | 720–775 {{7.6}} | 0.720–0.775 |
| Diesel (HSD), BS VI up to B7 | 810–845 {{7.6}} | 0.810–0.845 |
| Diesel blended with biodiesel, B8–B20 | 820–860 {{7.6}} | 0.820–0.860 |

The last column is plain arithmetic, not an MDG figure. The standards behind each range are on the [density range page](@density-range); for litres to kilograms, use the [converter](@tools-litres-to-kg). A higher or lower number inside the range is not a better fuel: see [high or low density](@density-high-or-low).

## What MDG requires about it

- **Display.** Density is among the notices a pump must show; not showing it is a minor irregularity {{5.1.17}} {{8.4}}. MDG gives no layout. Your oil company issues the formats, and some carry density beside opening stock and price. See [the boards MDG requires](@service-display-boards).
- **Records.** Every morning before sales, by 10 AM {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}}, and after each tanker, once 50 litres have gone through the pump {{1.2(xiv)}}.
- **On arrival.** The tanker's reading must match the invoice within ±3.0 kg/m³ {{1.2(iv)}}.

Why 15°C? Warm fuel reads lower than cool fuel, so one fixed temperature keeps readings comparable. See [density and temperature](@density-temperature).

## If you want to ask about a pump's density

MDG does not give a customer a right to order a density test. The density register and instruments are for inspecting officials on demand {{1.5(v)}} {{1.5(vi)}}. A customer can ask for filter paper to check petrol {{1.4.1(a)}} and for the complaint book {{1.6(iv)}}. Some oil company customer posters invite a comparison with the dealer's recorded density; that offer is the poster's, not an MDG clause.

General practice, not an MDG rule:

1. Read the density board: the figure, the date, and that it is at 15°C.
2. Ask politely which reading it is and when it was taken.
3. For doubts about petrol, ask for the filter paper.
4. To complain, call the oil company contact displayed at the pump {{1.6(viii)}} {{5.1.17}}, or use the complaint book. See [what a customer can check](@service-customer-checks).
