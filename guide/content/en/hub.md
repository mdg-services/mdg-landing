## What MDG is

MDG is short for Marketing Discipline Guidelines. If you search for *petrol pump ke niyam* (पेट्रोल पंप के नियम), this is the document most people mean. It is the rulebook for running a petrol pump day to day: the quality of the fuel, the quantity delivered, the price charged, the records kept and the service a customer can expect {{1.6}} {{5.1.12}}.

The first MDG was written in 1981-82 {{introduction}}. The edition in force is **MDG 2024, Version 1, effective 24.10.2024** {{cover page}}. It replaced MDG 2012. See [what changed between the two](@mdg-2012-vs-2024).

MDG 2024 is written for the retail outlet (RO) and kerosene (SKO) dealerships of the public-sector oil companies {{cover page}}. Its introduction counts nearly 81,300 RO dealerships and 6,400 SKO dealerships in that network on 01.04.2024 {{introduction}}.

MDG is not the only rule a pump lives under. The text also tells the dealer to follow all statutory rules, the rules of the petroleum and explosives authority and the Government's order on MS and HSD malpractices {{1.5(i)}} {{1.5(ii)}} {{1.5(v)}}. A malpractice found by a government authority is treated as an irregularity under MDG too {{8.7}}.

## Who must follow it, and how it reaches you

MDG applies to the dealers of the public-sector marketing companies {{cover page}}. The handing-over form at the end of the document names three: IndianOil, Bharat Petroleum and Hindustan Petroleum. The oil companies have duties as well. Chapter 3 sets what they must do at their storage points, from quality checks to tracking tank lorries {{3.1}} {{3.2}}. See [oil company duties](@oil-company-duties).

The text ties action to your dealership agreement. The company may take higher action than the minimum in MDG, including termination, "in line with the provisions of the Agreement" {{8.5.1}}. How the agreement and MDG fit together in your own case is a question for your agreement and your lawyer, not for this guide.

You also sign for it. The last page of MDG 2024 is a form in which the dealer acknowledges receiving the document, and the 20.03.2025 service circular carries its own acknowledgement {{circular}}.

## The three classes of irregularity

Chapter 5 lists what counts as an irregularity. Chapter 8 sorts each one into a class {{8.1}}.

| Class | What it covers | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Critical {{8.2}} | Seven irregularities, from adulteration and tampered seals to unauthorised purchases and sales | Termination at the first instance |
| Major {{8.3}} | Seven irregularities, including refusing an inspection, missing reference density, missing records since the last inspection and overcharging | 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies, then 30 days, then termination. Selling normal fuel as branded fuel and automation lapses have their own ladders |
| Minor {{8.4}} | Missing air, telephone or first aid box, records from before the last inspection missing, display lapses, poor housekeeping, a dirty toilet, customer service gaps, short delivery with seals intact | A warning-cum-guidance letter, then Rs 10,000, then Rs 25,000 per irregularity |

The second and third instance are counted over five years {{8.5.5}}. The actions in MDG are minimums, so the company can act harder {{8.5.1}}. The full table is on the [penalties](@penalties) page.
