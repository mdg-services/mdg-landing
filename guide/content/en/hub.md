## What MDG is

Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) is the set of rules the public-sector oil companies lay down for their retail outlet (petrol pump) and kerosene dealers, under the dealership agreement {{cover page}} {{8.5.1}}. It binds the dealer through the dealership agreement. These are the oil companies' own rules for their dealers. When dealers say *petrol pump ke niyam* (पेट्रोल पंप के नियम), this is usually the document they mean.

It deals with the daily running of a pump: fuel quality, quantity, price, records and customer service {{1.6}} {{5.1.12}}.

The first MDG was framed in 1981-82 {{introduction}}. The edition in force is **MDG 2024, Version 1, effective 24.10.2024** {{cover page}}. It replaced MDG 2012. See [what changed between the two](@mdg-2012-vs-2024).

The introduction puts the network it is written for at nearly 81,300 RO dealerships and 6,400 SKO dealerships on 01.04.2024 {{introduction}}.

A pump answers to more than MDG. The text also expects the dealer to follow the statutory rules, the petroleum and explosives authority's rules and the Government's order on MS and HSD malpractices {{1.5(i)}} {{1.5(ii)}} {{1.5(v)}}. If a government authority establishes a malpractice, the oil company treats it as an irregularity under MDG too {{8.7}}.

## Who must follow it, and how it reaches you

MDG 2024 is for the dealers of the public-sector marketing companies {{cover page}}. The acknowledgement form at the end names IndianOil, Bharat Petroleum and Hindustan Petroleum. The oil companies have duties as well. Chapter 3 sets what they must do at their storage points, from quality checks to tracking tank lorries {{3.1}} {{3.2}}. See [oil company duties](@oil-company-duties).

The oil company may take higher action than the MDG amounts, up to termination, "in line with the provisions of the Agreement" {{8.5.1}}. How your agreement and MDG fit together in your own case is for you and your advocate to work out, not for this guide.

The last page of MDG 2024 is a form in which the dealer acknowledges receiving the document. The 20.03.2025 circular on service standards has its own acknowledgement format {{circular}}. That circular is a joint format for the dealers of all three oil companies, and it sets no penalty of its own {{circular}}.

## The three grades of irregularity

Chapter 5 says what counts as an irregularity. Chapter 8 puts each one into a grade, and the grade sets the first action {{8.1}}.

| Grade | First action in MDG |
|---|---|
| Critical {{8.2}} | Termination at the first instance |
| Major {{8.3}} | Usually a 15-day suspension of sales and supplies, then 30 days, then termination. Branded-fuel sales and automation lapses have their own ladders |
| Minor {{8.4}} | A warning-cum-guidance letter, then Rs 10,000, then Rs 25,000 per irregularity |

Where each topic sits:

- **Fuel quality and samples:** adulteration is critical {{8.2}}. Having no reference density to show at an inspection, or stock variation beyond the limits while the sample passes, is major {{8.3}}. See [density](@density), [samples and labs](@sampling) and [stock variation](@stock-variation).
- **Meters and quantity:** tampered seals, totaliser or dispensing-unit parts are critical {{8.2}}. Short delivery with the seals intact is minor {{8.4}}. See [meters and seals](@quantity).
- **Records:** records missing since the last inspection are major, and older missing records are minor {{8.3}} {{8.4}}. See [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep).
- **Price and brand:** overcharging, and selling normal fuel as branded fuel on purpose, are major {{8.3}}. See [branded fuel and overcharging](@penalties-branded-fuel-overcharging).
- **Facilities, displays and service:** minor {{8.4}}. See [customer facilities](@customer-service).
- **Automation and inspections:** automation lapses have a major ladder of their own, and refusing an inspection or a sample is major {{8.3}}. See [automated outlets](@automation) and [inspections](@inspection).

**Read the figures as minimums.** The oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}, but only after the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}. The second and third instance are counted over five years, and instances under the previous guideline count too {{8.5.5}}. An appeal under {{8.9}} lies only against termination. The full figures are on the [penalties](@penalties) page.

**When several irregularities turn up at one visit, the text points two ways.** For the minor items in 8.4(iii), it says one or more of them found together count as one irregularity for the purpose of action {{8.4}}. In 8.5.3 it says each irregularity found together is counted against its own grade and the actions compound {{8.5.3}}. The text does not say how the two fit. Ask your oil company how it counts a particular visit.
