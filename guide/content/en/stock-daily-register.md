## What MDG asks you to keep

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the rules the oil companies set for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. MDG 2024 gives no stock register form. The oil company supplies the daily stock register {{1.3(ii)}}. The entries MDG names are:

| Record | What it covers | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Daily stock register | One register for all petroleum products, supplied by the oil company | {{1.3(ii)}} |
| Morning meter and dip record | Pump meter readings, plus the product dip and water dip of every tank, taken in the morning | {{1.3}} |
| Water dip | Written every day as part of the stock register. If water is found, tell the controlling company office at once | {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| Nozzle testing litres | The litres used to test the nozzles go into the stock register | {{1.4.2(d)}} |
| Entries after a tanker | After decanting, entries go in the "stock register/stock display board". The clause does not list them. Writing down the receipt is the plain reading | {{1.2(xiii)}} |
| Opening stock | Shown on display each day, with the permitted selling prices | {{1.3(i)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| Density | Kept in a separate density register, in the Annexure 1 format | {{1.2(xiv)}} {{annexure 1}} |

The clause on the morning readings says they are taken "separately". It does not say whether that means apart from the stock register, or one tank at a time. Writing each tank's product dip and water dip on its own line, in a record that sits beside the register, fits either reading. Check whether your oil company's register already has these columns.

> **Note:** the oil company supplies the register. If its columns differ from the illustration layout further down, follow the company's register. The illustration only shows where each MDG entry could sit.

## When each entry is made

MDG does not fix an order for the morning entries. It does put the nozzle check and the morning density record before sales begin {{1.4.2(d)}} {{1.4.1(c)}}.

| When | Entry | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Every day, before sales begin | Every nozzle checked for correct delivery, and the testing litres written in the stock register | {{1.4.2(d)}} |
| Every morning | Pump meter readings, and the product and water dip of each tank | {{1.3}} {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| Every morning, by 10 AM at the latest | Density at 15°C from the pump nozzle, in the density register | {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}} |
| Each day | Opening stock of the day on display | {{1.3(i)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| Each tanker | Sales from the tank being filled stop until decantation is finished | {{1.2(vii)}} |
| After decanting | Entries in the stock register or on the stock display board. Then the density in the density register, once at least 50 litres have gone through the pump | {{1.2(xiii)}} {{1.2(xiv)}} |

If a compartment dips short or water is found, the short quantity is made up through the pump. MDG asks for the chamber-wise dip and the litres filled to be written on the back of every copy of the invoice {{1.2(xi)}}. See [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water) and [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker). The density entries are on the [density register page](@density-register).

## How the register feeds the stock check

The Annexure 9 method needs four figures for the period under inspection: the opening dip, the receipts, the sales as per meter and the closing dip {{annexure 9}}. In practice, the morning dips, the meter readings and the receipt entries are where those figures come from. The [stock variation page](@stock-variation) explains the limits, and the [shrinkage and TVA page](@stock-shrinkage-tva) walks through the sums with illustration figures.

General practice, not an MDG rule: the testing quantity runs through the meter but is not a sale. Recording it separately lets you explain the difference between the meter reading and the fuel actually sold.

## Keep it, and what happens if you do not

MDG asks you to keep the oil company's daily sales register and the density register for this financial year and the one before {{5.1.12}}. The clause says "daily sales register" here and "daily stock register" in clause 1.3. MDG does not say they are different books. Confirm with your oil company which book it means.

Not keeping the specified records is an irregularity {{5.1.12}}:

| Situation | Class | Minimum action |
|---|---|---|
| No records since the last inspection | Major {{8.3(v)}} | 1st instance: 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies. 2nd: 30 days. 3rd: termination |
| Records since the last inspection kept, but earlier records not available | Minor {{8.4(i)}} | Warning-cum-guidance letter first; Rs 10,000 per irregularity second; Rs 25,000 per irregularity third onwards |

These are the minimum actions. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination, in any instance {{8.5.1}}, and only after the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}. The count of first, second and third instance looks back five years and includes irregularities under the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}.

One more point on counting. Not displaying the opening stock sits in the miscellaneous group of minor irregularities. MDG 8.4 says that one or more irregularities in items a to h of that group count as one irregularity for action {{8.4}}. MDG 8.5.3 says that when two or more irregularities are found at the same time, each counts as an instance of its own class, with a compounding effect {{8.5.3}}. MDG does not say how the two work together when several minor irregularities are found at once. On a plain reading, the records item above is not one of items a to h. Ask your oil company how it counts.
