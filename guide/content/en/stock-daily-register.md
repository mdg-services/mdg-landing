## What MDG asks you to keep

MDG 2024 does not give a stock register form. It says the oil company provides the daily stock register {{1.3(ii)}}. The entries below are the ones MDG names.

| Record | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Daily stock register | A daily stock register for all petroleum products, provided by the company | {{1.3(ii)}} |
| Morning readings | A daily record of the pump meter readings and of the product and water dips of each tank, taken in the morning | {{1.3}} |
| Water dip | Recorded every day as part of the daily stock register. If water is found, report it to the controlling company office immediately | {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| Testing quantity | The quantity used to test the nozzles is recorded in the stock register | {{1.4.2(d)}} |
| Entries after decanting | Appropriate entries in the "stock register/stock display board" after a tanker is decanted. The clause does not list the entries. Recording the receipt is the plain reading | {{1.2(xiii)}} |
| Opening stock | The opening stock of the day, and the authorised selling prices, on display | {{1.3(i)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| Density | A separate density register in the Annexure 1 format | {{1.2(xiv)}} {{annexure 1}} |

The clause on the morning readings says they are taken "separately". It does not say whether that means apart from the stock register, or one tank at a time. Writing each tank's product dip and water dip on its own line, in a record that sits beside the register, fits either reading. Check whether your oil company's register already has these columns.

> **Note:** the oil company provides the register. If its columns differ from the example layout further down, follow the company's register. The example only shows where each MDG entry could sit.

## When each entry is made

MDG does not fix an order for the morning entries. It does say the nozzle check and the morning density record come before sales begin {{1.4.2(d)}} {{1.4.1(c)}}.

| When | Entry | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Every day, before sales begin | All nozzles checked for correct delivery, and the testing quantity recorded in the stock register | {{1.4.2(d)}} |
| Every morning | Pump meter readings, and the product and water dip of each tank | {{1.3}} {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| Every morning, by 10 AM at the latest | Density at 15°C from the pump nozzle, in the density register | {{1.4.1(c)}} {{5.1.9}} |
| Each day | Opening stock of the day on display | {{1.3(i)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| Each tanker | Sales from the tank being filled stopped until decantation is complete | {{1.2(vii)}} |
| After decanting | Entries in the stock register/stock display board. Then the density in the density register, after at least 50 litres have been dispensed through the pump | {{1.2(xiii)}} {{1.2(xiv)}} |

If a compartment dips short or water is found, MDG asks for the chamber-wise dip and the quantity filled through the pump to be recorded on the back of all the invoice copies {{1.2(xi)}}. See [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water) and [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker). The density entries are on the [density register page](@density-register).

## How the register feeds the stock check

The sample calculation in Annexure 9 needs four figures for the period under inspection: the opening dip, the receipts, the sales as per meter and the closing dip {{annexure 9}}. In practice, the morning dips, the meter readings and the receipts entries are where those figures come from. The [stock variation page](@stock-variation) explains the limits, and the [shrinkage and TVA page](@stock-shrinkage-tva) walks through the sums.

General practice, not an MDG rule: the testing quantity runs through the meter but is not a sale. Recording it separately lets you explain the difference between the meter reading and the fuel actually sold.

## Keep it, and what happens if you do not

MDG asks you to keep the daily sales register of the oil company and the density register for the current and previous financial year {{5.1.12}}. The clause uses the name "daily sales register" here and "daily stock register" in clause 1.3. MDG does not say they are different books. Confirm with your oil company which book it means.

Non-maintenance of specified records is an irregularity {{5.1.12}}:

| Situation | Class | Action |
|---|---|---|
| No records since the last inspection | Major {{8.3(v)}} | 1st instance: 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies. 2nd: 30 days. 3rd: termination |
| Records since the last inspection kept, but earlier records not available | Minor {{8.4(i)}} | Warning-cum-guidance letter first; Rs 10,000 per irregularity second; Rs 25,000 per irregularity third onwards |

See the [full list of records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep) and the [penalty overview](@penalties).
