## What MDG 2024 requires

Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) is the set of rules the oil companies apply to their petrol pump dealers under the dealership agreement. It binds the dealer through the dealership agreement.

Three places deal with the first aid box:

- **Clause 1.6(ix):** the dealer makes a first aid box available, stocked with the medicinal aids that are needed and valid {{1.6(ix)}}.
- **Clause 5.1.14(a):** among the facilities whose absence is an irregularity, it lists a first aid box with medicines that are within their expiry dates {{5.1.14(a)}}.
- **The 20.03.2025 circular, point 14:** availability of the Q&Q kit and first aid box. The inspector marks YES or NO {{circular}}.

MDG 2024 does not say what the box must contain, what size it is, where it is kept, or how often to check it. Ask your oil company.

## What counts as a valid box

Clause 1.6(ix) speaks of “valid” medicinal aids, and 5.1.14(a) of medicines within their expiry dates. So the box has to be there, and what is in it has to be in date {{1.6(ix)}} {{5.1.14(a)}}. A box that is present but holds expired medicines does not match the description.

## What to keep in the box

MDG 2024 sets no contents list. An oil company may give its dealers a list, and lists can differ between companies and change over time. Ask your oil company for its current list and keep to it.

General practice, not an MDG rule: first aid boxes at workplaces usually hold some of the following. This is a list of ordinary supplies, not medical advice.

- bandages and dressings, with gauze and cotton wool;
- adhesive plasters and tape;
- antiseptic solution and ointments;
- scissors, tweezers and safety pins;
- an eye wash bottle;
- a printed first aid instruction card.

Check the exact items and quantities with your oil company.

## Keeping the box valid

MDG says medicines must not be beyond their expiry dates. It does not say how often to check {{5.1.14(a)}}.

General practice, not an MDG rule: look at the expiry date printed on every medicine at a fixed interval, replace anything near expiry before it lapses, and keep the box where staff can reach it quickly.

## What a missing or expired box costs

| Route | Line in MDG | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Air, telephone and first aid box not provided | {{5.1.14(a)}} {{8.4(ii)}} | Minor |
| First aid box not available, as a customer service point. The circular ties point 14 to 5.1.14(a), and says action follows 5.1.18 or the clause that fits | {{circular}} {{5.1.18}} {{8.4(iii)(f)}} | Minor |

The first aid box appears under both lines. MDG does not say which one an inspector uses. The circular sets no penalty of its own {{circular}}.

The action is the same for a minor irregularity: a warning-cum-guidance letter the first time, Rs 10,000 per irregularity the second time, and Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third time {{8.4}}.

Read the amounts with these points:

- They are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}.
- The irregularity must be established before any action is taken {{8.5.2}}.
- Instances are counted over five years, and irregularities under the previous guideline within those five years count too {{8.5.5}}.
- Clause 8.4 says irregularities under lines (a) to (h) of 8.4(iii) count as one irregularity for taking action. That note does not name line 8.4(ii), the air, telephone and first aid line {{8.4}}. Clause 8.5.3 says that when two or more irregularities are found at the same visit, each is counted as an instance in its own class, with a compounding effect {{8.5.3}}. The text does not say how the two fit together. Ask your oil company how it counts a visit with more than one finding.
