## What MDG 2024 requires

Three places in the rules deal with the first aid box:

- **Clause 1.6(ix):** the dealer makes a First Aid Box available, with necessary valid medicinal aids {{1.6(ix)}}.
- **Clause 5.1.14(a):** non-provision of facilities, such as a first aid box with medicines not beyond expiry dates at the outlet {{5.1.14(a)}}.
- **The 20.03.2025 circular, point 14:** availability of the Q&Q kit and first aid box. The inspector marks YES or NO {{circular}}.

MDG 2024 does not say what the box must contain, what size it is, where it is kept, or how often to check it. Ask your oil company.

## What counts as a valid box

“Valid” in 1.6(ix) is paired with “not beyond expiry dates” in 5.1.14(a). So the box has to be there, and what is in it has to be in date {{1.6(ix)}} {{5.1.14(a)}}. A box that is present but holds expired medicines does not match the description.

## What goes in the box: IndianOil's list

IndianOil's specification sheet for a first aid kit (document HO/HSE/02, dated 30.05.2013) gives the list below. Read it with care:

- It is written for suppliers who make kits for IndianOil. It is **not MDG text**, and MDG does not adopt it.
- It is dated 2013. Ask your oil company for the list it wants today.
- BPCL and HPCL may use a different list.
- This is a list of contents, not medical advice.

The sheet describes a stainless steel box, 14 x 9 x 4 inches, with at least 6 compartments.

| Group | Items and quantity in IndianOil's list |
|---|---|
| Printed material | 1 first aid instruction book; 1 copy of the First Aid Leaflet issued by the Chief Adviser of Factories, Government of India; a list of the box's items pasted inside the box |
| Bandages and dressings | Roller bandages: 6 of 5 cm x 3 m (finger), 6 of 7.5 cm x 3 m (hand and feet), 3 of 10 cm x 3 m (body). 1 packet of 1 m x 1 m absorbent gauze for wounds. 6 pieces of 20 g absorbent cotton wool. 8 sterilised eye pads, each in a separate packet. 1 triangular bandage cloth. 5 band-aid strips. 1 roll of adhesive tape, 2.5 cm x 5 m |
| Solutions, powders and ointments | 100 ml mercurochrome solution. 100 ml antiseptic solution (Savlon or Dettol). 15 g Vellosulph powder. 20 g soda bicarbonate. 20 g boric acid. 20 g burn ointment. 20 g Soframycin ointment. 15 g povidone iodine ointment |
| Tools and containers | 500 cc polythene wash bottle for the eyes. 1 plastic kidney tray, 20 cm. 1 glass eye bath. 12 safety pins. 1 tourniquet with cotton belt and buckle. 1 pair of stainless steel dressing scissors, 12 cm. 1 stainless steel forceps or tweezers. 1 snake bite lancet. 1 plastic medicine glass. 1 scalpel |

The sheet prints three names that look like typing slips: “Bun Ointment”, “Providon Iodine” and “Scalpet”. The table reads them as burn ointment, povidone iodine and scalpel. Brand names (Savlon, Dettol, Soframycin, Vellosulph) are as the sheet prints them. They are not a recommendation. Check the exact items with your oil company.

## Keeping the box valid

MDG says medicines must not be beyond their expiry dates. It does not say how often to check {{5.1.14(a)}}.

General practice, not an MDG rule: look at the expiry date printed on every medicine at a fixed interval, replace anything near expiry before it lapses, and keep the box where staff can reach it quickly.

## What a missing or expired box costs

| Route | Line in MDG | Class |
|---|---|---|
| Air, telephone and first aid box not provided | {{5.1.14(a)}} {{8.4(ii)}} | Minor |
| First aid box not available, as a customer service point. The circular ties item 14 to 5.1.14(a), and says action follows 5.1.18 or the clause that fits | {{circular}} {{5.1.18}} {{8.4(iii)(f)}} | Minor |

The first aid box appears under both lines. MDG does not say which one an inspector uses.

The action is the same for a minor irregularity: a warning-cum-guidance letter the first time, Rs 10,000 per irregularity the second time, and Rs 25,000 per irregularity the third time onwards {{8.4}}. Line 8.4(ii) is separate from lines (a) to (h) of 8.4(iii), which count together as one irregularity {{8.4}}. If two or more irregularities are found at the same visit, 8.5.3 says each is counted as an instance against its class, with a compounding effect {{8.5.3}}. Instances are counted over five years {{8.5.5}}.
