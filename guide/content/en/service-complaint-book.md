## How a customer complains

The dealer keeps the book and a message that it exists {{1.6(iv)}}, and displays its own and the oil company's complaint contacts {{1.6(viii)}} {{5.1.17}}.

1. **Ask for the complaint book.** Look for the message saying it is available. The dealer must hand it over when you ask {{1.6(iv)}}.
2. **Write your complaint in it.** General practice, not an MDG rule: put the date and time, the pump and product, what went wrong and how to reach you. Take a photo of the page.
3. **If the dealer will not give the book,** that is itself an irregularity {{5.1.17}}. Go to step 4.
4. **Call the oil company person** whose name and number must be displayed at the outlet {{1.6(viii)}}.

MDG gives no national complaint number. Use the numbers displayed at the outlet or the oil company's official website. This guide prints no helpline numbers.

A complaint can also lead to sampling {{2.4}} {{2.2.2.2}} or, for the toilet, a letter listing the penalty, issued at once {{5.1.14(b)}}: see [samples and labs](@sampling) and [toilet rules](@service-toilet-rules).

## What it costs the dealer

- Complaint book not kept, or not handed over: minor {{8.4(iii)(c)}}.
- Contact board missing: minor {{8.4(iii)(b)}}.

The action is a warning-cum-guidance letter the first time, Rs 10,000 per irregularity the second time, and Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third time {{8.4}}.

{{penalty-note}}

See [penalties](@penalties) and [boards and notices](@service-display-boards).

For the dealer (general practice, not an MDG rule): keep the book within staff reach and the displayed contacts current.
