## What the rule says

MDG 2024 puts four duties on the dealer around complaints:

| The dealer must | Clause |
|---|---|
| Maintain a suggestion/complaint book | {{1.6(iv)}} |
| Make the book readily available to customers on demand | {{1.6(iv)}} |
| Display a message that the book is available, prominently | {{1.6(iv)}} |
| Display the dealer's name and telephone number, and the names and telephone numbers of the oil company people a customer should contact with a complaint | {{1.6(viii)}} |

The dealer must also display Customer Service Cell messages and Customer Education posters that the oil company provides {{1.6(viii)}}. Clause 5.1.17 adds the contact details of authorised persons to call in case of complaint, grievance or emergency {{5.1.17}}. The 20-point circular of 20.03.2025 checks the oil company contact as point 18 {{circular}}.

## How a customer complains

1. **Ask for the complaint book.** Look for the message saying it is available. The dealer must hand it over when you ask {{1.6(iv)}}.
2. **Write your complaint in it.** General practice, not an MDG rule: put the date and time, the pump and product, what went wrong, and how to reach you. Take a photo of the page.
3. **If the dealer will not give the book,** that is itself an irregularity {{5.1.17}}. Use step 4.
4. **Go to the oil company.** Call the oil company person whose name and number must be displayed at the outlet {{1.6(viii)}}.

### What about the complaint number?

MDG does not give one national number. It requires each outlet to display the right names and numbers {{1.6(viii)}}. For a helpline, check the oil company's official website. This guide does not print helpline numbers.

## What MDG does not say

MDG 2024 does not say what the dealer must do with an entry once written, who reads the book, or how fast anyone must respond. It requires only that the book is kept, shown and handed over {{1.6(iv)}}.

## What a complaint can lead to

- The oil company is meant to draw fuel samples when there is a specific complaint against an outlet, in addition to routine sampling {{2.4}} {{2.2.2.2}}.
- For the toilet, a customer's or traveller's complaint that it is not accessible is one of the situations that lead to a photograph, the complaint being shared, and a letter listing the penalty {{5.1.14(b)}}. See [petrol pump toilet rules](@service-toilet-rules).

Even then, the irregularity must be established before any action is taken against the dealer {{8.5.2}}.

## What it costs the dealer

| If this is missing | Defined in | Listed as minor in |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint book not kept, or not given on demand | {{5.1.17}} | {{8.4(iii)(c)}} |
| Contact details of authorised persons not displayed | {{5.1.17}} | {{8.4(iii)(b)}} |

The action is a warning-cum-guidance letter the first time, Rs 10,000 per irregularity the second time, and Rs 25,000 per irregularity the third time onwards {{8.4}}. Irregularities under lines (a) to (h) of 8.4(iii) count as one irregularity for taking action {{8.4}}. Instances are counted over five years {{8.5.5}}.

For example, an inspector finds no complaint book at the outlet and a blank oil company contact board. Both are under 8.4(iii), so they are one irregularity for action, and a first-time outlet gets one warning-cum-guidance letter {{8.4}}.

See [penalties](@penalties) for the full ladder and [boards and notices a petrol pump must display](@service-display-boards) for every notice on the list.

## For the dealer: keep it ready

- Keep the book where any staff member can find it at once, and make sure staff know it must be handed over on demand {{1.6(iv)}}. General practice, not an MDG rule.
- Keep the “book available” message where customers can see it {{1.6(iv)}}.
- Check that the displayed oil company names and numbers are current {{1.6(viii)}}.
