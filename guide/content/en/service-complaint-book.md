## What the rule says

Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) is the set of rules the oil companies apply to their petrol pump dealers under the dealership agreement. It binds the dealer through the dealership agreement.

MDG 2024 puts these duties on the dealer around complaints:

- **The book.** Keep a suggestion/complaint book, let customers have it readily when they ask, and display a prominent message that the book exists {{1.6(iv)}}.
- **The contacts.** Display the dealer's own name and telephone number, and the names and telephone numbers of the oil company people a customer should call with a complaint {{1.6(viii)}}.

The dealer also displays the Customer Service Cell messages and Customer Education posters that the oil company provides {{1.6(viii)}}. Clause 5.1.17 adds the contact details of the people to call about a complaint, grievance or emergency {{5.1.17}}. The 20-point circular of 20.03.2025 checks the oil company contact as point 18 {{circular}}.

## How a customer complains

1. **Ask for the complaint book.** Look for the message saying it is available. The dealer must hand it over when you ask {{1.6(iv)}}.
2. **Write your complaint in it.** General practice, not an MDG rule: put the date and time, the pump and product, what went wrong, and how to reach you. Take a photo of the page.
3. **If the dealer will not give the book,** that is itself an irregularity {{5.1.17}}. Use step 4.
4. **Go to the oil company.** Call the oil company person whose name and number must be displayed at the outlet {{1.6(viii)}}.

### What about the complaint number?

MDG does not give one national number. It requires each outlet to display the right names and numbers {{1.6(viii)}}. For a helpline, check the oil company's official website. This guide does not print helpline numbers.

## What MDG does not say

MDG 2024 does not say what the dealer must do with an entry once written, who reads the book, or how fast anyone must respond. It requires only that the book is kept, shown and handed over {{1.6(iv)}}.

## What a complaint can lead to

- The oil company is meant to draw fuel samples when there is a specific complaint against an outlet, in addition to routine sampling {{2.4}} {{2.2.2.2}}.
- For the toilet, a customer's or traveller's complaint that it cannot be accessed is one of the situations that lead to a photograph, the complaint being shared, and a letter listing the penalty {{5.1.14(b)}}. See [petrol pump toilet rules](@service-toilet-rules).

Even then, the irregularity must be established before any action is taken against the dealer {{8.5.2}}.

## What it costs the dealer

| If this is missing | Defined in | Listed as minor in |
|---|---|---|
| Complaint book not kept, or not given on request | {{5.1.17}} | {{8.4(iii)(c)}} |
| Contact details of the people to call not displayed | {{5.1.17}} | {{8.4(iii)(b)}} |

The action is a warning-cum-guidance letter the first time, Rs 10,000 per irregularity the second time, and Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third time {{8.4}}.

Read the amounts with these points:

- They are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}.
- The irregularity must be established before any action is taken {{8.5.2}}.
- Instances are counted over five years, and irregularities under the previous guideline within those five years count too {{8.5.5}}.
- Clause 8.4 says irregularities under lines (a) to (h) of 8.4(iii) count as one irregularity for taking action {{8.4}}. Clause 8.5.3 says that when two or more irregularities are found at the same visit, each is counted as an instance in its own class, and action is taken on each with a compounding effect {{8.5.3}}. The text does not say how the two fit together. Ask your oil company how it counts a visit with more than one finding.

Illustration, for a made-up outlet: an inspector finds no complaint book and a blank oil company contact board at the same visit. Under 8.4 both are lines of 8.4(iii), which count as one irregularity for taking action. Under 8.5.3, two findings at one visit are each counted as an instance, with a compounding effect. MDG does not say which reading the oil company follows, so ask it.

## For the dealer: keep it ready

- Keep the book where any staff member can find it at once, and make sure staff know it must be handed over on request {{1.6(iv)}}. General practice, not an MDG rule.
- Keep the “book available” message where customers can see it {{1.6(iv)}}.
- Check that the displayed oil company names and numbers are current {{1.6(viii)}}.
