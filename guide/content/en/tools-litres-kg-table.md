## kg to litres

A lower density puts more litres in every kilo. The figures are rounded.

| Density point (diesel / petrol) | Diesel: litres in 1 kg | Diesel: litres in 1 tonne | Petrol: litres in 1 kg | Petrol: litres in 1 tonne |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low end (810 / 720) | 1.23 | 1,235 | 1.39 | 1,389 |
| Middle (827.5 / 747.5) | 1.21 | 1,208 | 1.34 | 1,338 |
| High end (845 / 775) | 1.18 | 1,183 | 1.29 | 1,290 |

## How every figure is worked out

kg = litres × density ÷ 1,000, and litres = kg × 1,000 ÷ density. The litres-to-kg figures are exact, not rounded. Two checks: 1,000 × 827.5 ÷ 1,000 = 827.5 kg, and 5 × 845 ÷ 1,000 = 4.225 kg.

For example (a made-up load): the invoice shows diesel at 831.5 kg/m³ at 15°C, so 200 litres weigh 200 × 831.5 ÷ 1,000 = 166.3 kg. That sits between the middle column (165.5) and the high end (169), as it should.

> **Note:** a litre is a volume, and the figures use the density at 15°C. General knowledge, not MDG text: a warm litre holds a little less fuel, so it weighs a little less. See [density and temperature](@density-temperature).
