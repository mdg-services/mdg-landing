## The checklist

The point numbers are the circular's. The inspector marks each YES or NO {{circular}}.

| Area | What the inspector looks for | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Whole outlet | Adequately lit and clean; all required facilities adequate | {{1.6(x)}} |
| Dispensing units, pedestals, island, lube display (point 10) | Clean | {{circular}} {{5.1.17}} |
| Forecourt queue (point 4) and driveway entry and exit (point 11) | The queue is marshalled and regulated. The entry and exit are free of obstacles, clean and properly lit | {{circular}} {{5.1.17}} |
| Emblem, light poles, monolith, signage, fascia (point 12) | Clean | {{circular}} {{5.1.17}} |
| Sales room and bins (points 13 and 15) | The sales room is neat and clean. Utility bins and dustbins are clean and easy to reach | {{circular}} {{5.1.17}} |

MDG does not say how to marshal the queue or how many staff to use. The toilet has its own rules: see [toilet rules](@service-toilet-rules).

## What MDG leaves to the oil company

MDG does not define poor housekeeping, set a cleaning interval, or list standards. It points to the oil companies' housekeeping standards {{1.6(x)}}. Ask your oil company for its standard. See also [the 20 service points](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations).

## What poor housekeeping costs

Two lines can apply. Poor housekeeping itself is a minor irregularity {{5.1.17}} {{8.4(iii)(d)}}. The circular's points are acted on under 5.1.18, also minor {{circular}} {{5.1.18}} {{8.4(iii)(f)}}.

The action is a warning-cum-guidance letter the first time, Rs 10,000 per irregularity the second time, and Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third time {{8.4}}.

{{penalty-note}}

Lapses under a to h of the minor list count as one irregularity for taking action {{8.4}}.

## An example

For example (a made-up outlet): an inspector finds a full dustbin beside the air point and a dead light at the exit lane. The inspector may mark points 15 and 11 NO {{circular}}. If it is the first instance, the minimum is a warning-cum-guidance letter {{8.4}}.
