## What to do when a compartment is short or shows water

MDG 2024 gives one procedure for both problems. It applies when a compartment dips short against the calibration certificate, or when water is found with water finding paste (or a suitable paste for ethanol blends) {{1.2(xi)}}.

1. **Dip and test every compartment.** Use the Weights & Measures-certified dip rod that comes with the lorry. Check each compartment for water with the paste. Sales from the underground tank concerned stop before decantation starts and stay stopped until it is complete {{1.2(vii)}}.
2. **Make up the shortage.** The short dip, including the quantity lost by removing water, is made up by filling to the correct level through the dispensing pump {{1.2(xi)}}.
3. **Record it on the invoice.** Write the quantity filled on the back of all the invoice copies. Record it chamber by chamber: the observed dip and the quantity filled through the dispensing unit to reach the correct level {{1.2(xi)}}.
4. **Get both signatures.** The dealer or the dealer's representative and the tank lorry driver both sign the shortage {{1.2(xi)}}.
5. **Then decant.** Once quality and quantity are settled, decant into the correct underground tanks {{1.2(xi)}}.

If the outlet is dry when the lorry arrives, the shortage is measured by drawing product from another compartment of the lorry and using a calibrated measure {{1.2(xi)}}. The shortage is then recorded and signed in the same way.

> **Note:** MDG does not describe the mechanics, such as the hose, the measure or who pours. It also does not say who bears the cost of a shortage. Ask your oil company how it wants this done at your outlet before the first time it happens.

## Check the equipment before you trust the dip

A dip is only as good as the rod and the certificate behind it. MDG's list puts these four checks ahead of the dips.

| Check | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Master valve levers | Release them so product fills the pipelines on lorries that are normally calibrated with lines full, before you check dips | {{1.2(iii)}} |
| Calibration certificate | Ask the driver for it; it shows product level and proof level details | {{1.2(v)}} |
| Dip rod markings | Check the Weights & Measures markings at the bottom and at the proof level | {{1.2(vi)}} |
| Dip rod accuracy | As an extra check, verify the rod with a standard measuring tape | {{1.2(vi)}} |

## What the record can look like

MDG prints no format for this record. The clause asks for the observed dip and the quantity filled for each compartment, with both signatures. A layout like this holds the same facts.

| Compartment | Product | Observed dip | Quantity filled through the pump | Signed by dealer or representative and driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (example) | Petrol | The reading you measured | 35 litres (example) | Both |

## A worked example

This is an illustration, not a case from any outlet.

A petrol and diesel lorry arrives. The dealer's staff dip each compartment. Compartment 2 (petrol) sits below the correct level on the calibration certificate. They fill petrol through the dispensing pump until the compartment reaches the correct level. The pump meter shows 35 litres.

They write the observed dip and "35 litres" against compartment 2 on the back of every invoice copy. The dealer's representative and the driver both sign it. Only then is the petrol decanted into the correct underground tank.

## Water in your own tanks

Water is checked in your own tanks too, not only in the lorry.

- **Daily water dip.** The dealer keeps a daily record of the water dip, as part of the daily stock register. If water is detected, he reports it immediately to the controlling company office {{1.4.1(b)}}.
- **Morning record.** The daily record of pump meter reading and the product and water dips of each tank is taken in the morning, separately {{1.3}}.
- **Company visits.** The oil company's maintenance representative checks the underground tanks for water on every visit to the outlet. If water is detected, steps must be taken to remove it {{4.4}}.
- **Water getting in.** For seepage of water into a tank, the maintenance representative checks all tank openings and arranges corrective action, such as replacing packing and gaskets and any missing caps {{4.3(c)}}.
- **Ethanol-blended petrol.** After water is removed, the ethanol content is checked before release. See [water finding paste and the ethanol check](@tanker-water-finding-paste).

## Keep the invoice copies

MDG has no separate penalty for a missing short-dip record. The record sits on your invoice copies, though, and purchase invoices for the current and previous financial years are among the records MDG says you must keep {{5.1.12}}. Not keeping the specified records since the last inspection is a major irregularity. The action is 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies the first time, 30 days the second time and termination the third time {{8.3}}. If only older records are missing, it is a minor irregularity {{8.4}}.

If the driver refuses to sign the shortage record, MDG does not say what to do in this clause. A note to the sample-drawal clause says that if the driver refuses to sign, the lorry should not be decanted and the dealer should contact the supply location or sales officer {{2.3}}. General practice, not an MDG rule: call your supply location or sales officer before you decant. For the full receipt order, see [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).
