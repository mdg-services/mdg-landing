## What to do when a tank lorry is short or shows water

One procedure covers both problems: a dip that reads short against the calibration certificate, and water found with the paste. Dip and water-test every compartment first; the full order is on [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).

1. **Make the shortage good.** Run product through the dispensing pump until the level is right. Water taken out counts as part of the shortage {{1.2(xi)}}.
2. **Write it on the invoice.** On the back of every invoice copy, record each compartment's observed dip and the quantity pumped in {{1.2(xi)}}.
3. **Both sign.** The dealer or representative and the driver sign the shortage record {{1.2(xi)}}. If the driver will not sign, 1.2(xi) gives no step. The note on sample drawal says a lorry whose driver refuses to sign is not decanted; call the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3}}.
4. **Then decant** into the correct underground tanks {{1.2(xi)}}.

If the outlet is dry when the lorry arrives, measure the shortage by drawing product from another compartment into a calibrated measure {{1.2(xi)}}.

> **Note:** MDG does not describe the hose or the measure, or say who bears the cost of a shortage. Ask your oil company how it wants this done at your outlet.

## What the record can look like

MDG prints no format. It asks for the observed dip and the quantity pumped in for each compartment, plus both signatures. For example:

| Compartment | Product | Observed dip | Quantity pumped in | Signed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 (example) | Petrol | The reading you measured | 35 litres (example) | Dealer or representative, and driver |

Water in your own tanks is a daily check: see [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste). Purchase invoices for this and the previous financial year are among the records to keep {{5.1.12}}: see [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep).
