## What to do when a compartment is short or shows water

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. Clause 1.2(xi) uses one procedure for two problems: a dip that reads short against the calibration certificate, and water found with water finding paste (or the suitable paste for ethanol blends) {{1.2(xi)}}.

1. **Dip and test every compartment.** Use the Weights & Measures-certified rod that comes with the lorry, and test each compartment for water with the paste. Sales from the underground tank concerned stop before decantation starts and stay stopped until it is complete {{1.2(vii)}}.
2. **Make good the shortage.** Run product through the dispensing pump until the level is right. The shortage includes any quantity lost in taking the water out {{1.2(xi)}}.
3. **Write it on the invoice.** On the back of every invoice copy, record chamber by chamber the dip you observed and the quantity that went through the dispensing unit to bring the level right {{1.2(xi)}}.
4. **Get both signatures.** The dealer or the dealer's representative and the tank lorry driver both sign what was recorded {{1.2(xi)}}.
5. **Then decant.** Once quality and quantity are settled, the product goes into the correct underground tanks {{1.2(xi)}}.

If the outlet has no stock when the lorry arrives, the shortage is measured by taking product from another compartment of the same lorry into a calibrated measure {{1.2(xi)}}. It is recorded and signed in the same way.

> **Note:** MDG does not describe the mechanics, such as the hose, the measure or who pours. It also does not say who bears the cost of a shortage. Ask your oil company how it wants this done at your outlet before the first time it happens.

## Check the equipment before you trust the dip

A dip is only as good as the rod and the certificate behind it. Four checks come before the dips.

| Check | What it involves | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Master valve levers | On lorries calibrated with full pipelines, open them first so the lines fill before you dip | {{1.2(iii)}} |
| Calibration certificate | Ask the driver for it. It carries the product level and proof level details | {{1.2(v)}} |
| Dip rod markings | Look for the Weights & Measures markings at the foot of the rod and at the proof level | {{1.2(vi)}} |
| Dip rod accuracy | As a second check, compare the rod against a standard measuring tape | {{1.2(vi)}} |

## What the record can look like

MDG prints no format for this record. The clause asks for the observed dip and the quantity pumped in for each compartment, plus both signatures. A layout like this holds the same facts.

## A worked example

This is an illustration, not a case from any outlet.

A petrol and diesel lorry arrives. The dealer's staff dip each compartment. Compartment 2 (petrol) sits below the correct level on the calibration certificate. They run petrol through the dispensing pump into it until it reaches the correct level. The pump meter shows 35 litres.

They write the observed dip and "35 litres" against compartment 2 on the back of every invoice copy. The dealer's representative and the driver both sign. Only then is the petrol decanted into the correct underground tank.

## Water in your own tanks

Water is checked in your own tanks as well as in the lorry.

- **Daily water dip.** Record the water dip every day as part of the daily stock register. If water is detected, report it at once to the controlling company office {{1.4.1(b)}}.
- **Morning record.** Separately, the pump meter reading and the product and water dips of each tank are recorded in the morning {{1.3}}.
- **Company visits.** On every visit, the oil company's maintenance representative does a water dip check on the underground tanks. Steps are taken to remove any water found {{4.4}}.
- **Water seeping in.** The maintenance representative checks every tank opening and arranges the repair, such as new packing and gaskets or replacing any missing cap {{4.3(c)}}.
- **Ethanol-blended petrol.** After the water is removed, and before the tank is released, the ethanol content is checked {{4.3(c)}}. See [water finding paste and the ethanol check](@tanker-water-finding-paste).

## Keep the invoice copies

MDG 2024 lists no separate action for a missing short-dip record, and for an irregularity it does not list, the oil company's competent authority acts after an enquiry, following natural justice {{8.5.4}}. The record sits on your invoice copies, though, and purchase invoices for this financial year and the one before are among the records the guideline says to keep {{5.1.12}}.

MDG 2024 classes it as a major irregularity when the specified records since the last inspection are not kept. The action listed is a 15-day suspension of sales and supplies at the first instance, 30 days at the second, and termination at the third {{8.3}}. If the records since the last inspection are kept but older ones are missing, it is a minor irregularity {{8.4}}.

These are the minimum actions, and the oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}. An irregularity must be proved to exist before the oil company acts {{8.5.2}}. First, second and third instances are counted over five years, and irregularities under the previous guideline in that period count too {{8.5.5}}. For the full list of records, see [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep), and for how the levels work, see [penalties](@penalties).

If the driver will not sign the shortage record, clause 1.2(xi) does not say what to do. A note under the sample-drawal clause says the lorry is not to be decanted in that case, and the dealer is to contact the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3}}. For the full receipt order, see [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).
