## Who can inspect your pump

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers, applied through the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. This page explains what they say about inspections.

Clause {{2.2.2.2}} names who may draw samples. Clause {{8.7}} adds government authorities that check a dealership under other laws.

| Who | What MDG says |
|---|---|
| Oil company officers | Sales officers, inspection teams and senior officers carry out the company's random sampling {{2.4}}. |
| Mobile labs | They make surprise quality checks on petrol, diesel and lube samples at the outlet {{7.1}}. A mobile lab works for the whole industry and shows all three companies' logos. One company may also run its own {{7.2}}. |
| Joint industry teams | Officers the oil companies nominate to inspect together. They may take samples whatever the density, stock or filter paper results show {{2.4.3}} {{7.2}}. |
| Agencies the oil company authorises | They may draw samples {{2.2.2.2}}. |
| Any authority with the legal power | It may draw samples {{2.2.2.2}}. |
| Central and State Government authorities | They can check a dealership under other laws and control orders. What they establish as a "malpractice or irregularity" is treated by the oil company as one under MDG, and it acts once the authority advises it {{8.7}}. |

## How often outlets are visited

MDG fixes no number of visits for each outlet. It sets a minimum spread and some triggers.

- **Random checks.** Each month, company officers' random sampling must reach at least 2% of the whole outlet network, which means at least 2 outlets in every 100 {{2.4}}. This comes on top of the samples the dealer draws from each tanker {{2.2.2.1}} {{2.4}}.
- **Triggers.** Extra samples are drawn after a density failure, a filter paper failure, stock variation beyond the permitted limit, during a special drive or campaign, and on a specific complaint against the outlet {{2.2.2.2}} {{2.4}}.

## What a visit can cover

MDG gives inspectors no fixed script. The table below groups, by topic, the areas its rules reach, with the class MDG gives a failure. A class names the minimum action: the oil company may act more strongly, up to termination {{8.5.1}}, and nothing can be done against a dealer until the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}. Figures for each topic are on its own page.

| Topic | What the rule looks at | Class if it goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Density | The density figure for the moment of the visit, from your register {{5.1.9}}. See [the 10 AM rule](@density-reference-10am). | Major {{8.3}} |
| Product quality | Nozzle samples from every petrol and diesel tank {{2.2.2.2}}. See [failed samples](@sampling-failed). | Adulteration is critical {{8.2}} |
| Quantity and meters | Whether each nozzle delivers within the permitted limit per 5 litres {{5.1.2}}. See [the 5-litre test](@quantity-5-litre-test). | Minor if the W&M seals are intact {{8.4}} |
| Seals and fittings | W&M seals on the metering unit and totaliser, and any added fitting or part {{5.1.2}} {{5.1.3}} {{5.1.4}}. See [seals](@quantity-seals). | Critical {{8.2}} |
| Stock | Stock reconciliation against the permitted variation {{5.1.11}}. See [stock variation](@stock-variation). | Major if beyond the limit and the sample passes {{8.3}} |
| Records | The registers, bills and licences MDG lists {{5.1.12}}. See [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep). | Major if records since the last inspection are missing, minor if only older ones are {{8.3}} {{8.4}} |
| Sources and storage | Buying only from your own oil company, with no unauthorised storage or tank interconnection {{1.5(iii)}} {{5.1.5}} {{5.1.6}} | Critical {{8.2}} |
| Facilities and displays | The amenities and notices a customer sees, and housekeeping {{5.1.14}} {{5.1.15}} {{5.1.17}}. See [customer service](@customer-service). | Minor {{8.4}} |
| Service points | The 20 points marked YES or NO {{circular}}. See [the 20-point list](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations). | Minor {{8.4}} |
| Automation | Manual mode, or a switched-off ATG, without written authorisation {{5.1.16}}. See [automated outlets](@automation). | Major {{8.3}} |
| Testing equipment | Calibrated hydrometers and thermometers, the ASTM table and any other testing kit, in working order and ready on request {{1.5(vi)}} | Chapter 5 lists no irregularity for it |

> **Note:** refusing, obstructing or creating circumstances that stop an inspection or sampling is a major irregularity on its own {{5.1.8}} {{8.3}}. Let the visit happen, whatever else is wrong at the outlet.

## A visit, step by step

MDG sets no order for a visit. The steps below are our own sequence, built from the rules in the order they tend to matter, so the real day may differ.

1. **The inspector arrives.** You allow the inspection and the sampling {{5.1.8}}. The hour of arrival decides which density figure is asked for. Before 10 AM it is the previous morning's figure, or the after-tanker figure if a load came the day before. After 10 AM it is this morning's figure, or the after-tanker figure if a load has already come today {{5.1.9}}.
2. **Papers are asked for.** The records that the Government's anti-malpractice Order and company or State instructions require must be shown to inspecting officials on demand {{1.5(v)}}. The outlet file of inspection reports, oil company letters, test reports and invoice copies must be at hand at all times {{1.5(iv)}}.
3. **Equipment is asked for.** Hydrometers, thermometers, the ASTM table and other testing kit must work and be ready on request {{1.5(vi)}}.
4. **Samples are drawn if needed.** They come from the nozzles of the dispensing units connected to every petrol and diesel tank {{2.2.2.2}}. For petrol, six 1-litre samples are drawn from each tank: two stay with you, two go to the division, territory or regional office and two go to the lab. For diesel, three are drawn: one for each of the same three places {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}. The label follows the Annexure 4 format, and you or your representative sign it {{annexure 4}}. See [how sampling works](@sampling).
5. **The inspector pays for the samples.** They pay the cost and take a cash memo {{2.2.2.2}}. When a mobile lab comes, the outlet is paid for the quantity used in testing, and also for the retained samples if the sample fails {{7.3.1}}.
6. **Some findings stop sales at once.** Sales and supplies of all products stop if the right density figure is missing {{5.1.9}}, if positive stock variation is beyond the limit {{5.1.11}}, or after a density or filter paper failure {{2.5(K)}}. A tampered metering seal stops sales through that dispensing unit {{5.1.2}}. In each case samples are drawn and tested, and the penalty follows the result {{5.1.9}} {{5.1.11}} {{5.1.2}} {{2.5(K)}}.
7. **A toilet finding brings a letter on the spot.** If the toilet is dirty, has no water, has a flush or door latch that does not work, or is not open to all customers and transit travellers (or a customer complains of this), the officer photographs it or passes on the complaint, and issues a letter at once that lists the penalty under MDG {{5.1.14(b)}}.
8. **The 20 points are marked.** Each is recorded as YES or NO {{circular}}.

### If a mobile lab comes

A mobile lab takes nozzle samples of petrol and diesel from every tank and runs clinical tests there and then. If the sample is clean, nothing more is done on it {{2.4.4}}. If it fails, sales are suspended and samples go on for further testing {{2.5(K)}} {{7.4}}. The test report is made in five copies, one for the outlet. For a failed sample the report records the advice to suspend sales, and you sign every copy with your seal {{7.6.1}}.

## After the visit

- **Lab results.** Samples should preferably reach the lab within 10 days of being drawn. The lab should preferably test them within the next 15 days, and the oil company should preferably pass the result to you within 5 days of getting the report {{2.10}}. You should preferably have the result, pass or fail, within 30 days of drawal {{2.5(F)}}. In special drives the preferred limits are longer: 15 days to reach the lab and 30 days to test {{2.10}}. These time limits are there to keep the system moving and do not decide whether a sample passes {{2.5(L)}}.
- **No parallel sampling.** Once one agency has found a quality irregularity and started an investigation, no second round of sampling is done while it is pending {{2.5(M)}}.
- **The notice.** A show cause notice is due within 30 days of the inspection, or of the test results if samples were drawn {{8.5.6}}. See [the show cause timeline](@inspection-show-cause-notice).
- **The action.** Nothing can be done against the dealer until the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}. The actions printed in MDG are the minimum, and the company may act more strongly, up to termination, in line with the dealership agreement {{8.5.1}}. Repeat instances are counted over five years, and that count includes the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}. See [the penalty pages](@penalties).

## What to have ready

- The density register and the daily sales register for this financial year and the one before {{5.1.12}}.
- The inspection record for the previous five years, and the outlet file {{5.1.12}} {{1.5(iv)}}.
- Working hydrometers and thermometers, the ASTM table and the W&M-verified 5-litre measure {{1.5(vi)}} {{1.4.2(b)}}.
- The tank-lorry retention samples of the last two loads for each tank {{2.9.2}}.
- At an automated outlet, any email or signed letter that authorises manual mode or a switched-off ATG {{5.1.16}}.
