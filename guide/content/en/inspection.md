## Who can inspect your pump

MDG 2024 names several kinds of visitor. The list in {{2.2.2.2}} is of those allowed to draw samples. Clause {{8.7}} adds government authorities that can check a dealership under other laws.

| Who | What MDG says |
|---|---|
| Oil company officials | The Sales Officer, inspecting teams and senior-level officers do the company's random sampling {{2.4}}. |
| Mobile labs | They make surprise checks of MS, HSD and lube samples at the outlet. A mobile lab works for the whole oil industry and carries the logos of the three companies, though one company may also run its own {{7.1}} {{7.2}}. |
| Joint industry teams | Officers nominated by the oil companies who inspect together. They may collect samples whatever the density, stock or filter paper results show {{2.4.3}} {{7.2}}. |
| Agencies the company authorises | They may draw samples {{2.2.2.2}}. |
| Any authority with the legal power | It may draw samples {{2.2.2.2}}. |
| Central and State Government authorities | They can check a dealership under other laws and Control Orders. If they establish a "malpractice or irregularity", the oil company treats it as one under MDG and takes the prescribed action once the authority advises it {{8.7}}. |

## How often outlets are visited

The text does not fix a number of visits for each outlet. It sets a floor and some triggers:

- **Random sampling:** company officials' random sampling covers at least 2% of the total retail outlet network every month, which is at least 2 outlets in every 100 {{2.4}}. This is on top of the samples the dealer draws at every tanker receipt {{2.2.2.1}} {{2.4}}.
- **Triggers:** samples are also drawn after a density failure, a filter paper failure, stock variation beyond the permissible limit, during special drives or campaigns, and on a specific complaint against the outlet {{2.2.2.2}} {{2.4}}.

## What a visit can touch

MDG 2024 does not give inspectors a fixed script. These are the areas its rules cover, so these are the areas a visit can reach.

| Area | What the rule looks at | Class if it goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Reference density | The morning figure and the after-tanker figure in your register {{5.1.9}}. See [the 10 AM rule](@density-reference-10am). | Major {{8.3}} |
| Records | The specified registers, bills and licences {{5.1.12}}. See [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep). | Major if missing since the last inspection, minor if only older ones are missing {{8.3}} {{8.4}} |
| Delivery | Whether each nozzle delivers within the permitted limit, measured per 5 litres {{5.1.2}}. See [the 5-litre test](@quantity-5-litre-test). | Minor if the seals are intact {{8.4}} |
| Seals and fittings | W&M seals on the metering unit and totaliser, and any added fitting or part {{5.1.2}} {{5.1.3}} {{5.1.4}}. See [seals](@quantity-seals). | Critical {{8.2}} |
| Stock | Stock reconciliation against the permissible variation {{5.1.11}}. See [stock variation](@stock-variation). | Major if beyond limits and the sample passes {{8.3}} |
| Product quality | Nozzle samples from all MS and HSD tanks {{2.2.2.2}}. See [failed samples](@sampling-failed). | Adulteration is critical {{8.2}} |
| Sources and storage | Purchases only from the principal oil company, no unapproved storage or tank interconnection {{1.5(iii)}} {{5.1.5}} {{5.1.6}} | Critical {{8.2}} |
| Facilities and boards | Air, telephone, first aid box, toilet, price and density boards, complaint book {{5.1.14}} {{5.1.15}} {{5.1.17}} | Minor {{8.4}} |
| Service standards | The 20 points, marked YES or NO {{circular}}. See [the 20-point checklist](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations). | Minor {{8.4}} |
| Automation | Manual mode or a switched-off ATG without written authorisation {{5.1.16}}. See [automated outlets](@automation). | Major {{8.3}} |
| Testing equipment | Calibrated hydrometers and thermometers, the ASTM table, any other testing kit, in working order and available on demand {{1.5(vi)}} | Not listed as an irregularity in Chapter 5 |

> **Note:** refusing, obstructing or creating circumstances that stop an inspection or sampling is a major irregularity by itself {{5.1.8}} {{8.3}}. Let the visit happen, whatever else is wrong at the outlet.

## A typical visit, step by step

MDG does not set an order for a visit. The steps below follow the rules in the order they usually matter, so the real order on the day may differ.

1. **The inspector arrives.** You allow the inspection and the sampling {{5.1.8}}. The time of arrival decides which density figure they ask for: before 10 AM, the previous morning's figure (or the after-tanker figure if a load came the previous day); after 10 AM, this morning's figure (or the after-tanker figure if a load has come today) {{5.1.9}}.
2. **They ask for papers.** The records that the Government's malpractice-prevention Order and company or State instructions require must be produced to inspecting officials on demand {{1.5(v)}}. The file of inspection reports, oil company letters, test reports and invoice copies must be at the outlet for ready reference at all times {{1.5(iv)}}.
3. **They ask for equipment.** Hydrometers, thermometers, the ASTM table and other testing kits must be in good working condition and made available on demand {{1.5(vi)}}.
4. **They draw samples if needed.** Samples come from the nozzles of the dispensing units connected to all MS and HSD tanks {{2.2.2.2}}. For MS, six 1-litre samples are drawn from each tank: two stay with you, two go to the division, territory or regional office and two go to the lab. For HSD, three 1-litre samples are drawn: one stays with you, one goes to the division, territory or regional office and one goes to the lab {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}. The label follows the Annexure 4 format and carries your or your representative's signature {{annexure 4}}. See [how sampling works](@sampling).
5. **The inspector pays for the samples.** They pay the cost and obtain a cash memo {{2.2.2.2}}. For a mobile lab, the outlet is paid for the quantity used in testing, and for the retained samples too if the sample fails {{7.3.1}}.
6. **Some findings stop sales at once.** Sales and supplies of all products stop if the right density figure is missing {{5.1.9}} or positive stock variation is beyond limits {{5.1.11}}. A density or filter paper failure also stops sales and supplies of all products {{2.5(K)}}. A tampered metering seal stops sales through that dispensing unit {{5.1.2}}. In each case samples are drawn and tested, and the penalty follows the result {{5.1.9}} {{5.1.11}} {{5.1.2}} {{2.5(K)}}.
7. **A toilet finding gets a letter on the spot.** If the toilet is not clean, has no water, has a flush or latch that does not work, or is not open to all customers and transit travellers (or a customer complains of that), the inspector takes a photograph and issues a letter instantly, listing the penalty under MDG {{5.1.14(b)}}.
8. **The 20 points are marked.** Each is recorded as YES or NO {{circular}}.

### If a mobile lab comes

The mobile lab draws nozzle samples of MS and HSD from all tanks and runs clinical tests there. If the sample passes, no further action follows on the sample {{2.4.4}}. If it fails, sales are suspended and samples go for further testing {{2.5(K)}} {{7.4}}. The test report is made in five copies, one for the outlet. If the sample fails, the report records the advice to suspend sales and you sign all copies with your seal {{7.6.1}}.

## After the visit

- **Lab results.** Samples should reach the lab preferably within 10 days of drawal. The lab tests them preferably within the next 15 days, and the oil company passes the result to you preferably within 5 days of getting the report {{2.10}}. Results should reach you preferably within 30 days of drawal, pass or fail {{2.5(F)}}. During special drives the limits are longer: preferably 15 days to reach the lab and 30 days to test {{2.10}}. These limits keep the system moving. They do not change whether a sample passes {{2.5(L)}}.
- **No second team on the same case.** Once one agency has found a quality irregularity and started an investigation, no parallel sampling is done while it is pending {{2.5(M)}}.
- **The notice.** A show cause notice follows within 30 days of the inspection, or of the test results if samples were drawn {{8.5.6}}. See [the show cause timeline](@inspection-show-cause-notice).
- **The action.** An irregularity must be established before any action is taken {{8.5.2}}. The actions in MDG are the minimum, and the company may act more strongly, including termination, in line with the dealership agreement {{8.5.1}}. See [the full penalty table](@penalties).

## What to have ready

- The density register and the daily sales register for the current and previous financial years {{5.1.12}}.
- The inspection record for the previous five years and the outlet file {{5.1.12}} {{1.5(iv)}}.
- Working hydrometers and thermometers, the ASTM table and the W&M-verified 5-litre measure {{1.5(vi)}} {{1.4.2(b)}}.
- The last two tank-lorry retention samples for each tank {{2.9.2}}.
- At an automated outlet, any email or signed letter that authorises manual mode or a switched-off ATG {{5.1.16}}.
