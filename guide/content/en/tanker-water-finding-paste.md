## What MDG asks for

MDG 2024 mentions water finding paste in two places: when a tanker is received, and when a shortage from water is made up. It also asks for a daily water dip and an ethanol check, which are about water in the tank but do not name the paste.

| When | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Every tanker, before decanting | Check dips in all compartments and check each compartment for water with water finding paste | {{1.2(vii)}} |
| Ethanol-blended petrol | Use a suitable water finding paste | {{1.2(vii)}} |
| Water found in a compartment | Make up the shortage, including the water removed, and record it on the back of all invoice copies | {{1.2(xi)}} |
| Outlet tanks, every day | Record the water dip as part of the daily stock register; report water at once to the controlling company office | {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| Outlet tanks, every morning | Keep a daily record of the product and water dip of each tank, taken in the morning, separately | {{1.3}} |
| Every company maintenance visit | The company's maintenance representative checks the underground tanks for water; if found, steps are taken to remove it | {{4.4}} |
| After water is removed from an ethanol-blended tank | Check the ethanol content before release | {{4.3(c)}} |

Sales from the underground tank concerned must be stopped before decanting starts, and stay stopped until decantation is complete {{1.2(vii)}}.

## Petrol, diesel and ethanol blends: which paste?

MDG's wording covers every compartment of every lorry, so the water check applies to petrol and diesel alike {{1.2(vii)}}. In that clause, the only case where it asks for a "suitable" paste is ethanol-blended motor gasoline. Ask your oil company which of your products are ethanol-blended.

### Petrol

Use water finding paste in each petrol compartment. If the petrol is ethanol-blended, use a suitable paste {{1.2(vii)}}.

### Diesel

MDG sets no separate rule or paste for diesel. The same instruction applies: check each compartment for water with water finding paste {{1.2(vii)}}.

### Ethanol-blended petrol

MDG asks for a suitable paste and says no more {{1.2(vii)}}. It does not name a brand, a type or a colour change. It does not say how to apply the paste, how long to wait or how to read the result. We do not recommend any brand. Follow the maker's instructions and what your oil company tells you.

## Why an ordinary paste can mislead with ethanol blends

This part is general knowledge, not MDG text. MDG does not explain why it asks for a suitable paste.

Ethanol mixes with water, and plain petrol and diesel do not. In an ethanol blend, a small amount of water can stay dissolved in the fuel instead of settling at the bottom. A larger amount can separate out as a layer of water and ethanol, which is called phase separation. A paste made for water sitting at the bottom of plain fuel may not show that mixture the way you expect, so it can give a misleading result. For what to use at your outlet, ask your oil company.

## After water is found in an ethanol-blended tank

MDG does not set this out as one sequence. Reading the clauses together, this is how they fit.

1. **Report the water.** Tell the controlling company office immediately {{1.4.1(b)}}.
2. **The water is removed.** The company's maintenance representative checks the underground tanks for water on every visit, and steps are taken to remove any water found {{4.4}}. If water is seeping in, he also checks all tank openings and arranges corrective action, such as replacing packing and gaskets and any missing caps {{4.3(c)}}.
3. **Check the ethanol content.** Do it after the water is removed and before release {{4.3(c)}}.
4. **If it is not in the desired range,** stop sales and inform the controlling office {{4.3(c)}}.

MDG does not give the desired range in this clause, and Annexure 7 only points to the BIS specifications for E5 and E10 (IS 2796), E12 and E15 (IS 17586) and E20 (IS 17021) {{annexure 7}}. The clause also does not say who carries out the ethanol check at the outlet. Ask your oil company who does it and which range applies.

MDG also makes the dealer responsible for ensuring that the right quality of ethanol-blended product is sold {{3.1(f)}}.

## The daily water dip

MDG asks for the dip record but does not say how to take the dip. General practice, not an MDG rule: many dealers coat the end of the dip rod with water finding paste for this reading.

The company's maintenance report has a line for the water dip reading of the tank, in centimetres. It also has a line for the quality of the silica gel in the vent pipe of ethanol-blended petrol tanks {{annexure 6}}. General knowledge, not MDG text: silica gel in a vent pipe is there to keep moisture out of the tank.

## What MDG leaves open

- Which paste or brand to use, and for which product.
- How to apply the paste, how long to wait and how to read it.
- The desired ethanol range, and who does the ethanol check.

For all of these, ask your oil company. For the full order of checks on a tanker, see [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker). For what to do when water is found in a compartment, see [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water).
