## What MDG asks for

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. They mention water finding paste in two places: when a tanker is received, and when a shortage caused by water is made good. They also ask for a daily water dip and an ethanol check. Both concern water in the tank, but neither names the paste.

| Situation | What MDG asks for | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Each tanker, before decanting | Dip every compartment and test each one for water with water finding paste | {{1.2(vii)}} |
| Ethanol-blended petrol | A suitable paste for the water test | {{1.2(vii)}} |
| Water found in a compartment | The shortage, water included, is made good and written on the back of every invoice copy | {{1.2(xi)}} |
| Outlet tanks, daily | The water dip goes into the daily stock register, and any water is reported at once to the controlling company office | {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| Outlet tanks, mornings | A separate morning record of the product and water dip of each tank | {{1.3}} |
| Company maintenance visits | The company's maintenance representative checks the underground tanks for water, and steps are taken to remove what is found | {{4.4}} |
| After water is removed from an ethanol-blended tank | The ethanol content is checked before release | {{4.3(c)}} |

Sales from the underground tank concerned stop before decanting starts, and stay stopped until it is complete {{1.2(vii)}}.

## Petrol, diesel and ethanol blends: which paste?

The tanker clause covers every compartment of every lorry, so the water test applies to petrol and diesel alike {{1.2(vii)}}. The only place it asks for a "suitable" paste is ethanol-blended motor gasoline. Ask your oil company which of your products are ethanol-blended.

### Petrol

Test each petrol compartment with water finding paste. If the petrol is ethanol-blended, use a suitable paste {{1.2(vii)}}.

### Diesel

MDG sets no separate rule or paste for diesel. The same instruction applies: test each compartment for water with water finding paste {{1.2(vii)}}.

### Ethanol-blended petrol

MDG asks for a suitable paste and says no more {{1.2(vii)}}. It names no brand, type or colour change, and it does not say how to apply the paste, how long to wait or how to read the result. We do not recommend any brand. Follow the maker's instructions and what your oil company tells you.

## Why a suitable paste with ethanol blends (general background)

This part is general knowledge, not MDG text. MDG gives no reason for asking for a suitable paste.

Ethanol mixes with water, and plain petrol and diesel do not. In an ethanol blend, a small amount of water can stay dissolved in the fuel instead of settling at the bottom, and more can separate out as a layer of water and ethanol (this is called phase separation). A paste made for water lying at the bottom of plain fuel may not react to that mixture in the same way. For what to use at your outlet, ask your oil company.

## After water is found in an ethanol-blended tank

MDG does not set this out as one sequence. Putting the clauses side by side, this is how they fit.

1. **Report the water.** Tell the controlling company office at once {{1.4.1(b)}}.
2. **The water is removed.** The company's maintenance representative checks the underground tanks for water on every visit, and steps are taken to remove what is found {{4.4}}. Where water is seeping in, the representative also checks all tank openings and arranges the repair, such as new packing and gaskets or replacing any missing cap {{4.3(c)}}.
3. **Check the ethanol content.** Do it once the water is out and before the tank is released {{4.3(c)}}.
4. **If the content falls outside the desired range,** stop sales and tell the controlling office {{4.3(c)}}.

MDG does not give the desired range in this clause. Annexure 7 only points to the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) specifications: IS 2796 for E5 and E10, IS 17586 for E12 and E15, and IS 17021 for E20 {{annexure 7}}. Check the current version of each standard. The clause also does not say who carries out the ethanol check at the outlet. Ask your oil company who does it and which range applies.

Separately, the dealer is responsible for making sure that the ethanol-blended product sold to customers is of the right quality {{3.1(f)}}.

## The daily water dip

MDG asks for the dip record but does not say how to take the dip. General practice, not an MDG rule: many dealers coat the end of the dip rod with water finding paste for this reading.

The maintenance report that the oil company's representative completes includes the water dip reading of the tank (in cm) and the quality of the silica gel in the vent pipe of ethanol-blended petrol tanks {{annexure 6}}. General knowledge, not MDG text: silica gel in a vent pipe is there to keep moisture out of the tank.

## What MDG leaves open

- Which paste or brand to use, and for which product.
- How to apply the paste, how long to wait and how to read it.
- The desired ethanol range, and who does the ethanol check.

For all of these, ask your oil company. For the full order of checks on a tanker, see [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker). For what to do when water is found in a compartment, see [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water).
