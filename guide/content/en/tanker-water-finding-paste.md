## What MDG requires

| Situation | What MDG requires | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Each tanker compartment, before decanting | Dip it and test it for water with water finding paste | {{1.2(vii)}} |
| Ethanol-blended petrol | A suitable paste for that test | {{1.2(vii)}} |
| Outlet tanks, every day | Record the water dip daily in the stock register (the morning dips are recorded separately); report any water at once to the controlling company office | {{1.4.1(b)}} {{1.3}} |
| Company maintenance visit | The maintenance representative checks the underground tanks for water on every visit; steps are taken to remove any found | {{4.4}} |
| After water is removed from an ethanol-blended tank | Check the ethanol content before release; if it is outside the desired range, stop sales and tell the controlling office | {{4.3(c)}} |

Water found in a tanker compartment is handled on [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water).

MDG names no brand or type of paste and gives no way to apply it, no waiting time and no ethanol range. Ask your oil company. General practice, not an MDG rule: many dealers coat the end of the dip rod with paste for the daily dip.

## Why a suitable paste for ethanol blends

General knowledge, not MDG text: ethanol mixes with water, so in a blend some water can stay dissolved or separate as a water-and-ethanol layer (phase separation), and a paste made for plain fuel may not react the same way. MDG gives no reason.

## After water is found in an ethanol-blended tank

The steps are on [water in the underground tank](@company-water-in-tank): report the water at once {{1.4.1(b)}}, have it removed {{4.4}}, then check the ethanol content before release {{4.3(c)}}. If it is outside the desired range, stop sales and tell the controlling office {{4.3(c)}}. MDG does not give the desired range or say who does the check; ask your oil company. For the full tanker order, see [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).
