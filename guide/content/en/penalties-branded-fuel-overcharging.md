## The two irregularities side by side

Both are major irregularities {{8.3}}, but they have different ladders.

| | Normal fuel sold as branded fuel | Overcharging |
|---|---|---|
| Defined in | {{5.1.10}} | {{5.1.13}} |
| Products | Petrol (MS) and diesel (HSD) | Petrol, diesel, CNG and Auto LPG |
| First instance | Recovery of the differential price since the last inspection | Suspension of sales and supplies for 15 days |
| Second instance | Termination of the dealership | Suspension for 30 days |
| Third instance | n/a | Termination of the dealership |

The ladders are in {{8.3}}. Instances are counted over five years, and findings under the previous MDG within those years count {{8.5.5}}. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

## Selling normal fuel as branded fuel

The rule is one line: intentional sale of normal MS or HSD as branded fuel {{5.1.10}}.

- **"Branded fuel".** MDG does not define the term or list the grades. General knowledge, not MDG text: oil companies sell premium grades under their own brand names. Your oil company can tell you which grades at your outlet are branded.
- **"Intentional".** The word is in the rule. MDG does not say how intent is shown. As with every irregularity, it must be established before any action is taken {{8.5.2}}.
- **The money penalty.** The first instance is recovery of the differential price since the last inspection {{8.3}}. The period is tied to the date of the last inspection, not to a fixed number of days. MDG gives no formula and does not say how the quantity is worked out. Ask your oil company.
- **The second instance** is termination {{8.3}}. MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) had the same ladder {{2012:8.3}}, so a finding under it within the last five years counts as an earlier instance {{8.5.5}}.

## Overcharging

MDG names the irregularity as overcharging in the authorised selling prices of MS, HSD, CNG and Auto LPG {{5.1.13}}. A related duty sits in the customer service rules: the dealer should ensure quality product at correct quantity and correct price {{1.6(i)}}.

The text of 5.1.13 is only a heading. It gives no tolerance, no rounding rule and no method of proof. It also does not say how the authorised price reaches the dealer. If you are unsure which price is authorised for your outlet, ask your oil company.

The penalty is 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies, then 30 days, then termination {{8.3}}. Before any penalty, the irregularity must be established {{8.5.2}}, and a show cause notice follows within 30 days of the inspection {{8.5.6}}. See [the show cause notice process](@inspection-show-cause-notice).

Kerosene wholesale dealers have their own list. Overcharging is a major irregularity there too, with a different penalty {{8.10}}. See [kerosene dealers](@sko-dealers).

## The minor duty: display the prices

The dealer must display the authorised retail selling prices of the products sold at the outlet {{1.3(i)}} {{5.1.15}}. The March 2025 minimum service obligations circular repeats this: display the selling price of all products {{circular}}.

Not displaying prices is a minor irregularity {{8.4}}. The action is a warning-cum-guidance letter, then Rs 10,000, then Rs 25,000 per irregularity. It is one of the items (a) to (h) that count together as one irregularity {{8.4}}.

MDG also asks for the product name on each nozzle of the MPD. Missing names are minor too {{5.1.17}} {{8.4}}. See [display boards](@service-display-boards) and [the minimum service obligations](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations).

> **Note:** a clean price board does not protect against a charge of overcharging, and a correct charge does not excuse a missing board. They are separate irregularities in separate classes {{8.3}} {{8.4}}.

## Staying safe

From MDG:

- Display the authorised prices {{1.3(i)}} {{5.1.15}}.
- Show the product name on each nozzle {{5.1.17}}.
- Keep purchase invoices and sales bills for the current and previous financial years {{5.1.12}}. Missing records since the last inspection are a major irregularity. Missing older records are minor {{8.3}} {{8.4}}.
- Keep the complaint book available and the contact details displayed {{1.6(iv)}} {{1.6(viii)}}.

General practice, not an MDG rule:

- Keep the board current: check it against the pump display every time the price changes.
- Make sure every attendant knows which nozzle sells which grade.
- Note the date and time each price change was put in place.

## If you are a customer

Ask for the complaint book. The outlet must give it on demand {{1.6(iv)}}. Use the contact details displayed at the outlet {{1.6(viii)}}. This guide does not list helpline numbers.

General practice, not an MDG rule: your oil company's official website also lists how to complain. Write down the date, time, product, the price displayed and the price charged. See [the complaint book](@service-complaint-book).

## A worked example

For example, an outlet's last inspection was on 6 November 2024 and found nothing.

| Date | Finding | Instance | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 February 2025 | Normal diesel sold as branded fuel, established | First | Recovery of the differential price for the period since 6 November 2024 {{8.3}} |
| 9 March 2026 | The same irregularity established again, within five years of the first {{8.5.5}} | Second | Termination of the dealership {{8.3}} |

Overcharging works on the standard major ladder. If overcharging is established on 9 March 2026 and again on 15 July 2026, the first costs 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies and the second costs 30 days {{8.3}}.
