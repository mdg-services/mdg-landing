## The two irregularities side by side

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the rules the oil companies apply to their dealers under the dealership agreement. Both irregularities below are major {{8.3}}, but each has its own ladder.

| | Normal fuel sold as branded fuel | Overcharging |
|---|---|---|
| Defined in | {{5.1.10}} | {{5.1.13}} |
| Products | Petrol (MS) and diesel (HSD) | Petrol, diesel, CNG and Auto LPG |
| First instance | Recovery of the differential price since the last inspection | Sales and supplies suspended for 15 days |
| Second instance | Termination of the dealership | Suspension for 30 days |
| Third instance | n/a | Termination of the dealership |

The ladders are in {{8.3}}. Read them with these points:

- They are minimum actions. The oil company's competent authority can take higher action, termination included, at the first instance or any later one {{8.5.1}}.
- The oil company must establish the irregularity before it acts {{8.5.2}}.
- Instances are counted over five years, and findings under the previous guideline inside those years count {{8.5.5}}. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).
- A termination arising from MDG can be appealed within 30 days of receiving the order. The appeal in MDG is for termination only {{8.9}}.

## Selling normal fuel as branded fuel

MDG states this irregularity in a single line: the intentional sale of normal MS or HSD as branded fuel {{5.1.10}}.

- **"Branded fuel".** MDG does not define the term or list the grades. General knowledge, not MDG text: oil companies sell premium grades under their own brand names. Your oil company can tell you which grades at your outlet are branded.
- **"Intentional".** The word is in the rule. MDG does not say how intent is shown. As with every irregularity, the oil company must establish it first {{8.5.2}}.
- **The money penalty.** The first instance is recovery of the differential price since the last inspection {{8.3}}. The period is tied to the date of the last inspection, not to a fixed number of days. MDG gives no formula and does not say how the quantity is worked out. Ask your oil company.
- **The second instance** is termination {{8.3}}. MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) had the same ladder {{2012:8.3}}, so a finding under it within the last five years counts as an earlier instance {{8.5.5}}.

## Overcharging

The irregularity is charging above the authorised price of petrol (MS), diesel (HSD), CNG or Auto LPG {{5.1.13}}. A related duty sits in the customer service rules, which ask the dealer to ensure "quality product at correct quantity and correct price" {{1.6(i)}}.

Clause 5.1.13 states the irregularity in one line. It gives no tolerance, no rounding rule and no method of proof, and it does not say how the authorised price reaches the dealer. If you are unsure which price is authorised for your outlet, ask your oil company.

The penalty is a 15-day suspension of sales and supplies, then 30 days, then termination {{8.3}}. The irregularity must be established before any penalty {{8.5.2}}, and a show cause notice follows within 30 days of the inspection {{8.5.6}}. See [the show cause notice process](@inspection-show-cause-notice).

Kerosene wholesale dealers have their own list. Overcharging is a major irregularity there too, with a different penalty {{8.10}}. See [kerosene dealers](@sko-dealers).

## The minor duty: display the prices

The dealer must display the authorised retail selling prices of the products sold at the outlet {{1.3(i)}} {{5.1.15}}. The circular of 20.03.2025 on minimum service obligations lists the same duty: display the selling price of all products {{circular}}. That circular is a common format for the dealers of all three oil companies and sets no penalty of its own. MDG attaches the minor penalties to falling short of those obligations {{5.1.18}}.

Not displaying prices is a minor irregularity {{8.4}}. The action is a warning-cum-guidance letter, then Rs 10,000, then Rs 25,000 per irregularity, as minimum steps {{8.5.1}}. MDG 8.4 counts it with the other miscellaneous minor items as one irregularity when action is decided, while 8.5.3 counts irregularities found together each on its own {{8.4}} {{8.5.3}}. MDG does not say how the two fit. See [repeat offences and unpaid fines](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

MDG also asks for the product name on each nozzle of the MPD. Missing names are minor too {{5.1.17}} {{8.4}}. See [display boards](@service-display-boards) and [the minimum service obligations](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations).

> **Note:** price display and overcharging are separate irregularities in separate classes {{8.3}} {{8.4}}. Getting one right does not change the other.

## Duties that go with these rules

From MDG:

- Display the authorised prices {{1.3(i)}} {{5.1.15}}.
- Show the product name on each nozzle {{5.1.17}}.
- Keep purchase invoices and sales bills for this financial year and the one before {{5.1.12}}. Missing records since the last inspection are a major irregularity. Missing older records are minor {{8.3}} {{8.4}}.
- Keep the complaint book available and the contact details displayed {{1.6(iv)}} {{1.6(viii)}}.

General practice, not an MDG rule:

- Keep the board current: check it against the pump display every time the price changes.
- Make sure every attendant knows which nozzle sells which grade.
- Note the date and time each price change was put in place.

## If you are a customer

Ask for the complaint book. The outlet must give it on demand {{1.6(iv)}}. Use the contact details displayed at the outlet {{1.6(viii)}}. This guide does not list helpline numbers.

General practice, not an MDG rule: your oil company's own website also lists how to complain. Write down the date, time, product, the price displayed and the price charged. See [the complaint book](@service-complaint-book).

## An illustration

Illustration: the last inspection of a made-up outlet was on 6 November 2024 and found nothing. Both rows below show the minimum action {{8.5.1}}.

| Date | Finding | Instance | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 February 2025 | Normal diesel sold as branded fuel, established | First | Recovery of the differential price for the period since 6 November 2024 {{8.3}} |
| 9 March 2026 | The same irregularity established again, within five years of the first {{8.5.5}} | Second | Termination of the dealership {{8.3}} |

Overcharging works on the standard major ladder. In a second made-up outlet with no earlier findings, if overcharging is established on 9 March 2026 and again on 15 July 2026, the first costs 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies and the second costs 30 days {{8.3}}.
