## Which clause covers the ATG

| Clause | What it covers |
|---|---|
| 5.1.16(b) | The ATG switched off or non-operational without authorisation from the competent authority {{5.1.16(b)}}. It does not use the word deliberate |
| 5.1.16(c) | Deliberate action by the dealership, its staff or any other agency to make any other part of the automation system dysfunctional, fully or partly, without authorisation. It excludes dispensing units, MPDs and ATGs {{5.1.16(c)}} |

## What to do

MDG prints no step list. These are the clauses that bear on it, in the order a dealer meets them:

1. **Report it at once** to the oil company's authorised official, and **follow the direction given** {{1.5(viii)}}.
2. **If the ATG is to stay off or down, it needs authorisation.** Only an email or a signed letter from a company official counts, and you keep it on record {{5.1.16 Note}}. See [manual mode](@automation-manual-mode) for what does and does not count.

Who repairs what is on [breakdowns and leaks](@company-breakdowns-leaks).

## What it costs

An ATG switched off or non-operational without authorisation is major {{8.3(vii)}}. The first instance is a penalty of Rs 1,00,000.

## What MDG does not say

- **Who the competent authority is.** The clause says "competent authority" and its note says "Company official". No rank is named {{5.1.16}}.
- **Whose equipment the ATG is**, and so who repairs it.
- **How soon the company must attend,** or how long an ATG may stay off. Chapter 4 sets no time {{4.2(a)}}.

## An example

For example (a made-up outlet): the ATG shows no tank readings at 11 AM. The manager reports it at once to the authorised official {{1.5(viii)}} and asks him to confirm by email whether the outlet may keep operating with the ATG down. He files the reply {{5.1.16 Note}}. Asking for the email is general practice, not an MDG rule.
