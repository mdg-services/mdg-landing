If an article or forward quotes a fine you cannot find in Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024), it may be quoting MDG 2012. We read the two texts side by side. "MDG 2012" here means Version 7, effective 8 January 2013 and last amended on 30.11.2022 {{2012:cover page}}. "MDG 2024" means Version 1, effective 24.10.2024 {{cover page}}. Both are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. MDG 2012 is the older guideline, replaced on 24.10.2024.

## What changed, at a glance

| Topic | MDG 2024 (24.10.2024), in force | Older guideline, replaced on 24.10.2024 (MDG 2012, Ver. 7) |
|---|---|---|
| Unclean toilet | Minor. Warning-cum-guidance letter, then Rs 10,000, then Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}} | Major. Rs 15,000, then Rs 25,000, then the higher of Rs 35,000 and 45% of monthly margin, plus a suspension lasting 7 days or until the defect is fixed if that takes longer {{2012:8.3(vii)}} |
| Short delivery, W&M seals intact | Minor. Warning-cum-guidance letter, then Rs 10,000, then Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}} | Major. Rs 25,000 per nozzle, then Rs 50,000 per nozzle plus 15 days, then termination, the second and third within one year of the first {{2012:8.3(x)}} |
| Short delivery at the pump | Sales stop only beyond the legal limit. Within it, sales continue and recalibration is due in 15 days {{5.1.2(a)}} | Sales stop; recalibrate and re-stamp first, even within the permissible limit {{2012:5.1.2(a)}} |
| Wages | No wage penalty line in Chapter 8. The wage duty stays {{1.5(x)}} | Own penalty: 20%, then 30%, then 40% of monthly margin plus 15 days' suspension at the third instance {{2012:8.3(ix)}} |
| Customer service standards | New {{1.6(xii)}} {{5.1.18}}, and a minor item in {{8.4}} | No such clause |
| Toilet protocol | Eight points, including access for all customers and transit travellers at all times and a Darpan QR code {{5.1.14(b)}} | Seven points, the last being that the door is not kept locked {{2012:5.1.14(b)}} |
| Minor items grouped as one for action | Items a to h {{8.4}} (8.5.3 separately counts each irregularity found together: see below) | Items a to e {{2012:8.4}} |
| Automated outlets: authorisation | Email or signed letter only, and the dealer must keep the record {{5.1.16}} | Email or signed letter only {{2012:5.1.16}} |
| Appeals | Adds a committee of 2 EDs where an ED-rank authority terminated. SC/ST: a committee of two Directors {{8.9}} | ED (Retail) or a nominated ED. SC/ST: one Director {{2012:8.9}} |

All amounts above are minimum actions in both editions: the oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}} {{2012:8.5.1}}, and an irregularity must be established before any action {{8.5.2}} {{2012:8.5.2}}. The second and third instance are counted over five years, and the count includes instances under the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}. An appeal under {{8.9}} lies only against termination.

If you hold an old clause number: the toilet penalty moved from {{2012:8.3(vii)}} to {{8.4(iii)(g)}}. Short delivery with seals intact moved from {{2012:8.3(x)}} to {{8.4(iii)(h)}}. The automated outlet penalty moved from {{2012:8.3(viii)}} to {{8.3(vii)}}. The wage penalty at {{2012:8.3(ix)}} has no 2024 equivalent.

## The toilet penalty: two versions

**MDG 2024 (in force).** A dirty toilet is a minor irregularity {{8.4}}. The first instance brings a warning-cum-guidance letter, the second Rs 10,000 per irregularity, and the third and later Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}}. For the daily toilet checks themselves, see [toilet rules](@service-toilet-rules).

**Older guideline, replaced on 24.10.2024 (MDG 2012, Ver. 7).** It was a major irregularity {{2012:8.3(vii)}}:

- First instance: Rs 15,000.
- Second instance: Rs 25,000.
- Third instance onwards: the higher of Rs 35,000 and 45% of the dealer's average monthly margin over the last 6 months, plus a suspension of sales and supplies that lasts 7 days, or until the toilet defect is rectified if that takes longer.

Older articles and forwards may quote the second version. Confirm with your oil company which one applies to your case.

**Illustration (made-up figures):** an outlet's monthly dealer margin, averaged over six months, is Rs 2,00,000. Under the older guideline, 45% of that is Rs 90,000. As that is more than Rs 35,000, a third-instance fine would have been Rs 90,000, with a suspension of at least 7 days. Under MDG 2024, the third-instance action for the same finding is Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}}.

In both editions these are minimum amounts, higher action is possible up to termination {{8.5.1}}, and the irregularity must be established first {{8.5.2}}.

## Short delivery with seals intact

**MDG 2024 (in force).** Short delivery with W&M seals intact is a minor irregularity {{8.4}}. The first instance brings a warning-cum-guidance letter, the second Rs 10,000 per irregularity, and the third Rs 25,000 per irregularity {{8.4}}.

At the pump, sales through the unit or nozzle stop only if it delivers short beyond the limits in the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 and its Rules. Inside the permissible limit, which the text puts at between ±10 ml and ±25 ml per 5 litres, the finding is recorded and sales continue. Recalibration and re-stamping are then due within 15 days of the finding, or sales from that unit or nozzle are suspended until they are done {{5.1.2(a)}}.

**Older guideline, replaced on 24.10.2024 (MDG 2012, Ver. 7).** It was a major irregularity {{2012:8.3(x)}}:

- First instance: Rs 25,000 for each nozzle that delivers short by more than the Legal Metrology Act and Rules permit.
- Second instance, if within one year of the first: Rs 50,000 for each such nozzle plus 15 days' suspension.
- Third instance, if within one year of the first: termination.

At the pump, sales through a unit stopped as soon as it delivered short. It had to be recalibrated and re-stamped before sales resumed, even when the short or excess delivery was within the permissible limit {{2012:5.1.2(a)}}.

Short delivery with W&M seals tampered did not change. It is critical in both editions, with termination at the first instance {{2012:8.2}} {{8.2}}. See the [5-litre test](@quantity-5-litre-test) and [W&M and totaliser seals](@quantity-seals).

In both editions these are minimum amounts, higher action is possible up to termination {{8.5.1}}, and the irregularity must be established first {{8.5.2}}.

## Wages: the penalty is gone, the duty is not

The older guideline had a wage penalty of its own. It was a major irregularity: 20% of the monthly dealer margin (average of the last 3 months) the first time, 30% the second time, and from the third time 40% plus 15 days' suspension {{2012:8.3(ix)}}. These too were minimum amounts {{2012:8.5.1}}, and the irregularity had to be established first {{2012:8.5.2}}. MDG 2012 set out a wage standard in 1.5(x) and again in its 5.1.18, "Payment of wages". That standard was the higher of the company-notified and the state minimum wage, and it also covered e-payment, a list of benefits, insurance cover under PMSBY and PMJJBY, and record-keeping at the outlet {{2012:1.5(x)}} {{2012:5.1.18}}.

MDG 2024 states the duty more briefly. It asks the dealer to meet the statutory obligations that apply, minimum wages and other benefits among them, and it names the two insurance schemes PMSBY and PMJJBY {{1.5(x)}}. The same line is a note under 5.1.18 {{5.1.18}}. The e-payment rule, the list of benefits, the higher-of-two formula and the record-keeping line are not printed in the 2024 text, and Chapter 8 has no wage penalty line.

Do not read the missing penalty as the duty going away. The note sits under the customer service heading, and the text does not say which class a wage lapse falls in. MDG also lets the company act, after an enquiry, on matters it does not list {{8.5.4}}. Ask your oil company how it treats a wage lapse. See [staff wages and uniform](@service-staff-wages-uniform).

## Customer service standards: new in 2024

In MDG 2012, clause 5.1.18 was "Payment of wages". In MDG 2024 it is "Customer Service Standards". The dealer must keep the service standard set by the oil company's circulars, and a shortfall brings the action for minor irregularities {{5.1.18}}. The same duty now also sits in {{1.6(xii)}} and is one of the minor items in {{8.4}}. The circular of 20.03.2025 turned it into 20 points: see the [20-point checklist](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations). That circular is a common format for the dealers of all three oil companies, and it sets no penalty of its own {{circular}}.

## Toilet rules: what was added

- **Access.** The last point of the older protocol said the toilet door must not be kept locked {{2012:5.1.14(b)}}. Point vii of MDG 2024 says the toilet must be open to all customers and transit travellers at all times, subject to safety protocols {{5.1.14(b)}}.
- **Feedback.** MDG 2024 adds point viii: a "Darpan" QR code placed by the toilet so customers can give feedback {{5.1.14(b)}}.
- **What triggers a letter.** MDG 2024 adds a flush that is not working. In place of a locked door, it names a toilet found not accessible to any customer or transit traveller, or a complaint to that effect from one. The inspector photographs the toilet or the complaint is shared, and a letter listing the penalty goes out at once {{5.1.14(b)}}.
- **Unchanged.** Cleanliness at all times, lighting, water, a working latch, signage, the protocol displayed near the toilet and a maintenance sheet kept and displayed are in both editions.

## Other changes

- **Minor items grouped as one.** In MDG 2024, one or more of items a to h of 8.4(iii) found together count as one irregularity for the purpose of action {{8.4}}. In the older guideline that group ran from a to e, and the toilet and short delivery sat outside it as major items {{2012:8.4}}. MDG 2024 also says that when several irregularities are found together, each is counted against its own class and the actions compound {{8.5.3}}, and the older guideline carries the same line {{2012:8.5.3}}. The two lines pull in different directions, and the text does not say how they fit. Ask your oil company how it counts a visit with several findings.
- **Automated outlets.** MDG 2024 adds that the dealer must keep a record of the written authorisation (email or signed letter from a company official) {{5.1.16}}. See [manual mode authorisation](@automation-manual-mode).
- **Appeals.** A termination approved by an authority of ED rank in the State Office now goes to a committee of 2 EDs. SC/ST terminations go to a committee of two Directors, not counting the Director (Marketing) {{8.9}}. In MDG 2012 the appeal went to the ED (Retail) or a nominated ED, and for SC/ST to one Director, again not the Director (Marketing) {{2012:8.9}}. An appeal is for termination only. See [appeal against termination](@inspection-appeal).
- **Personal hearing.** The hearing before a critical termination is the same. Only the list of offices named changed: "Regional office" became "State Office (Retail)" {{2012:8.6}} {{8.6}}.
- **Clause numbers.** Safety equipment is {{1.6(xi)}} in MDG 2024. MDG 2012 printed two clauses numbered 1.6(x).

## What did not change

We compared the two texts word for word. Apart from the items above, the network figures in the introduction, the date of a government letter quoted in 5.1.1, and changes of spelling and layout, they read the same.

- The list of critical irregularities and their first action {{8.2}}.
- The ladders for the major items that remain in both editions, including the separate ladders for branded fuel and for automated outlets {{8.3}}, and the minor ladder {{8.4}}. The current figures are on the [penalties](@penalties) page.
- The reference density rule with its 10 AM time {{5.1.9}}, the stock variation limits {{5.1.11}}, the records list {{5.1.12}}, and the 3-tier sampling chapter with its timelines (Chapter 2).
- The show cause timeline: a notice within 30 days, 10 days to reply, and a speaking order preferably within 45 days {{8.5.6}} {{8.5.7}} {{8.5.8}}. Also payment of fines within 30 days {{8.7}} and the five-year count of instances {{8.5.5}}.
- The kerosene (SKO) dealer chapter and its penalties {{8.10}}.
- Annexures 1 to 13a. MDG 2024 only adds an acknowledgement form at the end.

## Old offences and the new edition

The five-year count in {{8.5.5}} is worded the same in both editions: irregularities under the previous guideline within five years count as instances. The 2024 text does not explain how a finding that was major under the older guideline is counted now that the toilet and short delivery are minor. It also does not set out how cases open on 24.10.2024 are handled. If your inspection was close to that date, or you have an earlier finding, ask your oil company which edition and which class it applied.
