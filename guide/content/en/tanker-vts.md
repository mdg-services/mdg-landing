## What the dealer has to do

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. For tank lorries they give the dealer a watching job and a hold rule. In order:

1. **Watch the lorry.** Follow the lorry carrying supplies for your outlet on VTS, through the link you are given {{1.5(vii)}}.
2. **Report a deviation straight away.** If you see one, tell the supply location immediately, before you accept the lorry {{1.5(vii)}}. The note to 2.3 names the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3}}.
3. **Write it down.** Record what you observed. Further action follows once the deviations you saw and the oil company officials' instructions are on record {{1.5(vii)}}.
4. **Hold the decanting.** Decant only after you have further instructions {{2.3}}.

> **Note:** The report comes before you accept the lorry, and MDG wants it immediate {{1.5(vii)}}. Tell the supply location as soon as you see the deviation.

A note to 2.3 adds that if the driver refuses to sign, the lorry is not to be decanted and the dealer contacts the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3}}. For the full order of checks, see [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).

## What VTS and the deviation report are

MDG uses VTS and GPS side by side and does not spell out the letters. It describes what the system does {{3.2}}:

- The system raises deviation reports on its own, and automatic alerts go to the officers who monitor VTS.
- The reports and alerts cover deviations in time and in route.
- Officers at the supply locations see them, and so does the dealer.
- Each one has to be looked into immediately.

| Who | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Supply location | Keeps the GPS running smoothly and does not fill a lorry whose VTS is missing or not working as prescribed. Its officers see the reports and alerts, which have to be looked into at once | {{3.2}} |
| Dealer | Follows the lorry on the VTS link. Tells the supply location of any deviation before accepting the lorry. Can see the deviation reports and alerts | {{1.5(vii)}} {{3.2}} |
| Oil company officials | The dealer records their instructions and further action follows. After an unusual deviation, the dealer decants only on getting further instructions | {{1.5(vii)}} {{2.3}} |

## Seeing the filling and riding along

For ex-MI supplies, dealers or their representatives are allowed to watch their lorry being filled at the supply location {{3.2}}. The dealer may also accompany the lorry to the outlet {{3.2}}. MDG does not spell out "ex-MI" or say which supplies it covers. Ask your oil company whether your supplies are included.

## A worked example

This is an illustration, not a real case.

For example, a diesel lorry is due at 3:00 PM. At 2:00 PM the link shows it standing for 30 minutes at a place off the usual route. The dealer calls the supply location right away, before the lorry reaches the outlet, and notes the time, what the link showed and whom the call went to.

When the lorry arrives, the dealer does not start decanting. The dealer waits for the oil company's instruction, notes it down and decants only after receiving it.

MDG sets no number of minutes or kilometres that counts as a deviation. The note to 2.3 speaks of an "unusual deviation" in time or route {{2.3}}. If something looks unusual on the link, report it.

## What MDG does not say

- **What counts as a deviation.** There is no time or distance limit in the text {{3.2}}.
- **What the link looks like.** MDG says only "the link provided" {{1.5(vii)}}. Ask your oil company how you get it.
- **What the instruction will be.** It depends on the oil company officials {{1.5(vii)}}. Follow what they tell you.
- **A penalty for not monitoring.** Chapter 5 does not list it as an irregularity. Anything not listed is handled by the oil company's competent authority, after an enquiry and following natural justice {{8.5.4}}. The actions MDG does list are a minimum {{8.5.1}}, and an irregularity must be proved to exist before the oil company acts {{8.5.2}}.
- **A format for your note.** General practice, not an MDG rule: a dated note with the time, what the link showed, whom you told and what they said covers it.

## Where this sits in the receipt procedure

Watching the lorry comes before the receipt procedure. The checks at your outlet start when it arrives, with the invoice and the seals {{1.1}} {{1.2(ii)}}. The VTS rules in 1.5(vii) and 3.2 read the same in MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, last amended 30.11.2022), which has been replaced {{2012:1.5(vii)}} {{2012:3.2}}. Confirm how VTS works for your supplies with your oil company.
