## What the dealer has to do

MDG 2024 gives the dealer a watching job and a stop rule. The steps, in order:

1. **Watch the lorry.** Follow the lorry that is carrying supplies for your outlet through VTS, using the link provided {{1.5(vii)}}.
2. **Report a deviation straight away.** If you see one, tell the supply location immediately, before you accept the lorry {{1.5(vii)}}. The note to 2.3 names the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3}}.
3. **Write it down.** Further action is taken after recording the deviations you observed and the instructions of the oil company officials {{1.5(vii)}}.
4. **Hold the decanting.** Decant only after you get further instructions {{2.3}}.

> **Note:** MDG puts the report before you accept the lorry, and asks for it immediately {{1.5(vii)}}. Tell the supply location as soon as you see the deviation.

Note (i) to 2.3 adds that if the driver refuses to sign, the lorry should not be decanted and the dealer should contact the supply location or sales officer {{2.3}}. For the full order of checks, see [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).

## What VTS and the deviation report are

MDG uses VTS and GPS side by side and does not spell out the letters. It describes what the system does {{3.2}}:

- The system generates deviation reports by itself and sends automatic alerts to the officers monitoring VTS.
- The reports and alerts cover time deviations and route deviations by tank lorries.
- They are viewed by officers at the supply locations and by the dealer.
- They have to be investigated immediately.

| Who | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Supply location | Responsible for smooth operation of the GPS. No filling of a lorry if VTS is not installed or not working in the prescribed manner. Officers at the supply location view the reports and alerts, which have to be investigated immediately | {{3.2}} |
| Dealer | Monitors the lorry through the VTS link. Tells the supply location of any deviation before accepting the lorry. Can view the deviation reports and alerts | {{1.5(vii)}} {{3.2}} |
| Oil company officials | Their instructions are recorded by the dealer, and further action follows. After an unusual deviation, the dealer decants on getting further instructions | {{1.5(vii)}} {{2.3}} |

## Seeing the filling and riding along

For ex-MI supplies, dealers or their representatives are allowed to witness the filling of their lorry at the supply location. The dealer may also accompany the lorry to the outlet {{3.2}}. MDG does not spell out "ex-MI" or say which supplies it covers. Ask your oil company whether your supplies are included.

## A worked example

This is an illustration, not a real case.

A diesel lorry is due at 3:00 PM. At 2:00 PM the link shows it standing for 30 minutes at a place that is not on the usual route. The dealer calls the supply location right away, before the lorry reaches the outlet. He writes down the time, what the link showed and whom he spoke to.

When the lorry arrives, he does not start decanting. He waits for the oil company's instruction, writes it down and decants only after he has it.

MDG sets no number of minutes or kilometres that counts as a deviation. The note to 2.3 speaks of an "unusual deviation" in time or route {{2.3}}. If something looks unusual on the link, report it.

## What MDG does not say

- **What counts as a deviation.** There is no time or distance limit in the text {{3.2}}.
- **What the link looks like.** MDG says only "the link provided" {{1.5(vii)}}. Ask your oil company how you get it.
- **What the instruction will be.** It depends on the oil company officials {{1.5(vii)}}. Follow what they tell you.
- **A penalty for not monitoring.** Chapter 5 has no item for it. Anything not listed is dealt with by the oil company's competent authority, after enquiry and in line with natural justice {{8.5.4}}.
- **A format for your note.** General practice, not an MDG rule: a dated note with the time, what the link showed, whom you told and what they said covers it.

## Where this sits in the receipt procedure

Watching the lorry comes before the receipt procedure. The checks at your outlet start when it arrives, with the invoice and the seals {{1.1}} {{1.2(ii)}}. The VTS rules in 1.5(vii) and 3.2 read the same in MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, last amended 30.11.2022), which has been replaced {{2012:1.5(vii)}} {{2012:3.2}}.
