## What the dealer does

1. **Watch the lorry** on VTS, through the link provided {{1.5(vii)}}.
2. **Report any deviation at once** to the supply location, before you accept the lorry {{1.5(vii)}}.
3. **Record** what you saw and the oil company officials' instructions; further action follows from them {{1.5(vii)}}. General practice, not an MDG rule: a dated note with the time, what the link showed and whom you told.
4. **After an unusual deviation, decant only on further instructions** {{2.3}}. The rest of the receipt order is on [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).

## What VTS covers

The system generates deviation reports and alerts on time and route, and both the supply location's officers and the dealer can see them {{3.2}}. A supply location must not fill a lorry whose VTS is missing or not working as prescribed {{3.2}}. For ex-MI supplies, dealers or their representatives may watch the lorry being filled, and a dealer may also accompany it to the outlet {{3.2}}. MDG does not define ex-MI, so ask your oil company if your supply is covered.

## An example

This is an illustration. A diesel lorry due at 3:00 PM shows at 2:00 PM standing 30 minutes off its usual route. The dealer calls the supply location at once, notes the time and whom the call went to, and decants only once the oil company's instruction arrives {{1.5(vii)}} {{2.3}}.

## What MDG does not say

MDG says only "the link provided" {{1.5(vii)}} and does not say how big a deviation must be to count. Ask your oil company how you get the link and what it treats as a deviation.
