## What decantation means

Decantation is emptying a tank lorry into your underground tanks. This page explains the Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024): the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. Clause 1.2, headed "Decanting of product", sets out what the dealer does before, during and after the transfer {{1.2}}.

Elsewhere MDG writes "TT" (as in "TT retention sample") {{2.5}} and "tank truck" (in the heading of the density record) {{annexure 1}} without saying what TT stands for. Read both as the tank lorry.

The order is part of the rule: the dealer settles quality and quantity first and decants only afterwards {{1.2(xi)}}.

Unauthorised product is a separate matter. MDG 2024 lists a lorry carrying unauthorised product that is found decanting at the outlet as a critical irregularity, with termination at the first instance {{5.1.7}} {{8.2}}. The listed actions are a minimum {{8.5.1}}, and an irregularity must be proved to exist before the oil company acts against a dealer {{8.5.2}}. MDG describes this irregularity in a single sentence, so ask your oil company if you are unsure what it covers.

> **Note:** sales from the underground tank you are about to fill stop before decanting begins and stay stopped until the transfer is complete {{1.2(vii)}}.

## First, match the invoice to the lorry

When the lorry arrives, check these invoice details against what is physically in front of you {{1.1}}.

| Invoice detail | Compare it with |
|---|---|
| Tank lorry number | The number on the vehicle |
| Seal or security lock number | The seal or lock fitted to the lorry |
| How many compartments there are, and the product and quantity in each | The compartments on the lorry |
| When the lorry left the supply point | When it reached your outlet. MDG does not spell out this comparison and sets no allowed gap; see [VTS and route deviation](@tanker-vts) |
| Density at 15°C as recorded on the invoice | Your own density check (step 4 below) |
| The dip rod and its calibration certificate, plus anything else the oil company has told you to check | The rod and certificate the driver hands over |

## The 14 steps, in the order MDG uses

The numbers below line up with the (i) to (xiv) of clause 1.2, so you can check each step against the document itself.

### Settle the lorry and check the seals

1. **Park, settle, secure.** Park on level ground and give the lorry 10 minutes to settle. Confirm earthing and bonding before anyone starts work on it. Keep a portable DCP-type fire extinguisher near the lorry, at a suitable distance. The discharge hoses must meet the safety requirements, and plastic or PVC hose pipes are ruled out in every case {{1.2(i)}}.
2. **Check the seals.** Look at the seal or security lock on the lorry's delivery manifold and on its manhole covers, and match each against the numbers written on the invoice {{1.2(ii)}}.
3. **Release the master valve levers before you dip.** On lorries that are normally calibrated with the pipelines full, this lets the product fill the lines first. MDG refers you to the calibration certificate on this {{1.2(iii)}}.

### Check quality and quantity

4. **Density against the invoice.** Convert your sample's density to 15°C with ASTM Table 53B (use the current table). The result has to sit within ±3.0 kg/m³ of the density on the invoice {{1.2(iv)}}. For example, with an invoice density of 745.0 kg/m³, a 15°C result anywhere from 742.0 to 748.0 is inside the limit. MDG does not say in words which sample this check reads. Step 8 is where density and temperature are measured and recorded compartment by compartment. See [the invoice density check](@density-invoice-check).
5. **Calibration certificate.** Ask the driver to hand it over. It carries the product level and proof level details {{1.2(v)}}.
6. **Dip rod.** Look for the Weights & Measures markings at the foot of the rod and at the proof level. As a second check, compare the rod against a standard measuring tape {{1.2(vi)}}.
7. **Dip and water test in every compartment.** Dip each compartment with the Weights & Measures-certified rod that travels with the lorry. Test each one for water with water finding paste, using a paste suited to ethanol-blended petrol where that applies. Sales from the tank that will receive the product stop before decanting begins and stay stopped until it ends {{1.2(vii)}}. See [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste).
8. **A sample from each compartment.** With the quantity known, draw off roughly 20 litres from each compartment's manifold, then fill a clear glass container for the quality check. Test each compartment's sample on its own: measure its density and temperature and write both in the register {{1.2(viii)}}.
9. **In doubt, ask.** If you doubt the product's quality, ask the supply location or the area field officer what to do {{1.2(ix)}}.

MDG asks for the bottom samples once the density check is within the limit {{2.3}}. It does not say in words what to do when the figure is outside ±3.0. Step 9 (doubt about quality: contact the supply location or the area field officer) is the nearest instruction, so follow what your oil company tells you.

### Samples, shortages and decanting

10. **Bottom samples for retention.** Once quality is settled and before any product goes into the tank, the dealer or representative draws bottom samples from the lorry under the three-tier sampling procedure {{1.2(x)}}. The amount is 2 × 1 litre for petrol and 1 × 1 litre for diesel. They are composite samples, blended in proportion to what each compartment holds, after at least 20 litres have been removed from each compartment {{2.3}}. Rinse the containers with the same product, seal them and label them as Annexure 3 describes. The dealer or representative and the driver both sign the label, and the dealer keeps the sample {{2.3}} {{annexure 3}}. See [retention samples](@sampling-retention).
11. **Short dip or water: top up, record, then decant.** If a compartment reads short against its calibration certificate, or the paste shows water, the shortfall (counting what is lost in taking the water out) is made good by running product through the outlet's dispensing pump until the level is right. On the back of every invoice copy, write the dip you observed and the quantity pumped in, compartment by compartment. The dealer or representative and the driver both sign. Only when quality and quantity are settled does the product go into the correct underground tanks {{1.2(xi)}}. See [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water).

### After the lorry is empty

12. **Empty the lorry fully** before you let it leave {{1.2(xii)}}.
13. **Make the stock entry**, in the stock register or on the stock display board {{1.2(xiii)}}.
14. **Density after decanting.** Run at least 50 litres through the pump to clear the product left in the line, then draw a sample at the nozzle. That sample represents the mixed product now in the tank. Write its density at 15°C in the density register, in the Annexure 1 format {{1.2(xiv)}} {{annexure 1}}. This reading is the reference density for that tank after the delivery {{2.10}}. See [reference density](@density-reference-10am) and [the density register](@density-register).

## When MDG says hold the lorry and call

- **The driver will not sign.** A note under the sample-drawal clause says the lorry is not to be decanted. The dealer contacts the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3}}.
- **The lorry shows an unusual deviation in time or route.** Tell the supply location or the sales officer, and decant only once you have further instructions {{2.3}}.
- **You doubt the quality.** Contact the supply location or the area field officer {{1.2(ix)}}.

MDG also expects you to follow the lorry before it arrives. The dealer tracks the lorry carrying the outlet's supply on VTS, using the link provided. If a deviation shows, the supply location is told straight away, before the lorry is accepted {{1.5(vii)}}. See [VTS and route deviation](@tanker-vts).

## What to have ready before the lorry arrives

- A calibrated hydrometer and thermometer, an ASTM density conversion table (the current one) and any other testing kit that applies, all in working order and available to inspecting authorities when they ask {{1.5(vi)}}.
- A clear glass container for the compartment samples {{1.2(viii)}}, and aluminium containers for the retention samples {{2.3}}.
- The density register in the Annexure 1 format {{annexure 1}} and the daily stock register {{1.3}}.
- A file at the outlet holding invoice copies and the oil company's letters, kept ready for reference {{1.5(iv)}}. Purchase invoices for this financial year and the one before are among the records MDG says to keep {{5.1.12}}.
- The DCP fire extinguisher for the lorry {{1.2(i)}}.

## If you learned this under MDG 2012

The receipt steps in 1.1 and 1.2 read the same in MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, last amended 30.11.2022) as in MDG 2024 {{2012:1.1}} {{2012:1.2}}. MDG 2012 has been replaced, so work from the 2024 text. Your oil company can advise further details from time to time, so confirm the current steps with it.
