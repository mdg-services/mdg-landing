## What decantation means

Decantation means emptying a tank lorry into your underground tanks. MDG 2024 sets out the checks before, during and after under the heading "Decanting of product" {{1.2}}. MDG also writes "TT" in places, such as 'TT retention sample' {{2.5}}, and "tank truck" in the heading of the density record {{annexure 1}}. It does not spell out TT. Read both as the tank lorry.

The order matters. MDG wants quality and quantity settled first, and the product decanted only after that {{1.2(xi)}}.

The stakes are high. A tank lorry carrying unauthorised product that is found decanting at your outlet is a critical irregularity. The action is termination at the first instance {{5.1.7}} {{8.2}}. MDG says no more about that irregularity, so ask your oil company if you are unsure what it covers.

> **Note:** sales from the underground tank you are about to fill must stop before decanting begins and stay stopped until decantation is complete {{1.2(vii)}}.

## First, match the invoice to the lorry

When the lorry arrives, compare these details on the invoice with what is physically there {{1.1}}.

| On the invoice | Check it against |
|---|---|
| Tank lorry number | The number on the lorry |
| Seal or security lock number | The seal or lock on the lorry |
| Number of compartments, and the product and quantity in each | The compartments on the lorry |
| Time the lorry left the supply point | The time it reached you. MDG gives no allowed gap; see [VTS and route deviation](@tanker-vts) |
| Recorded density at 15°C | Your own density check (step 4 below) |
| Dip rod with calibration certificate, and other details the company advises | The dip rod and certificate the driver hands over |

## The 14 steps, in MDG's order

The numbers below match MDG's own (i) to (xiv) in clause 1.2, so you can check each step against the text.

### Make the lorry safe and check the seals

1. **Park, settle, make safe.** Park on level ground and let the lorry settle for 10 minutes. Make sure earthing and bonding are in place before you start. Keep a DCP-type portable fire extinguisher near the lorry, at a suitable distance. Discharge hoses must meet the safety requirements, and plastic or PVC hose pipes must never be used {{1.2(i)}}.
2. **Check the seals.** Compare the seal or security lock on the lorry's delivery manifold and manhole covers with the numbers on the invoice {{1.2(ii)}}.
3. **Release the master valve levers.** Do this before you check dips, so product fills the pipelines on lorries that are normally calibrated with lines full. MDG points you to the calibration certificate for this {{1.2(iii)}}.

### Check quality and quantity

4. **Density against the invoice.** The density at 15°C, worked out with the ASTM Table 53B, must be within ±3.0 kg/m³ of the invoice density {{1.2(iv)}}. For example, if the invoice says 745.0 kg/m³, the 15°C figure from your sample should fall between 742.0 and 748.0. Use the ASTM table for the conversion itself. MDG does not say in words which sample this check reads; step 8 is where density and temperature are measured and recorded compartment by compartment. See [the invoice density check](@density-invoice-check).
5. **Calibration certificate.** Ask the driver for it. It shows the product level and proof level details {{1.2(v)}}.
6. **Dip rod.** Check the Weights & Measures markings at the bottom of the rod and at the proof level. As an extra check, verify the rod against a standard measuring tape {{1.2(vi)}}.
7. **Dips and water in every compartment.** Dip each compartment with the Weights & Measures-certified dip rod that comes with the lorry. Check each compartment for water with water finding paste, and use a suitable paste for ethanol-blended petrol. Sales from the underground tank concerned must be stopped before decanting starts {{1.2(vii)}}. See [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste).
8. **A sample from each compartment.** Once you know the quantity, drain about 20 litres from the manifold of each compartment, then draw a sample into a clear glass container. Check density and temperature compartment by compartment and record them in the register {{1.2(viii)}}.
9. **In doubt, ask.** If you doubt the quality, contact the supply location or the area field officer for instructions {{1.2(ix)}}.

MDG asks for the bottom samples once the density check is within the limit {{2.3}}. MDG does not say in words what to do when the figure is outside ±3.0. Step 9 (doubt about quality: contact the supply location or the area field officer) is the closest instruction, so follow what your oil company tells you.

### Samples, shortages and decanting

10. **Bottom samples for retention.** After quality is settled and before decanting, draw bottom samples from the lorry under the 3-tier sampling procedure {{1.2(x)}}. For petrol MDG asks for 2 × 1 litre, for diesel 1 × 1 litre. They are composite samples, taken in proportion to what each compartment holds, after removing at least 20 litres from each compartment {{2.3}}. The containers are rinsed with the same product, sealed and labelled as in Annexure 3. The dealer or representative and the lorry driver both sign the label, and the dealer keeps the sample {{2.3}} {{annexure 3}}. See [retention samples](@sampling-retention).
11. **Short dip or water: make it up, record it, then decant.** If a compartment dips short against the calibration certificate, or water shows on the paste, the shortage (including what is lost when water is removed) is made up by filling to the correct level through the dispensing pump. The quantity is written on the back of all the invoice copies, compartment by compartment, with the observed dip. The dealer or representative and the driver both sign. Only after quality and quantity are settled do you decant into the correct underground tanks {{1.2(xi)}}. See [short dip and water](@tanker-short-dip-water).

### After the lorry is empty

12. **Empty the lorry fully** before you release it {{1.2(xii)}}.
13. **Update the stock register** or the stock display board {{1.2(xiii)}}.
14. **Density after decanting.** Dispense at least 50 litres through the pump to push out what is in the line. Then take a sample from the nozzle and record the density at 15°C of the mixed product in the density register, in the format of Annexure 1 {{1.2(xiv)}} {{annexure 1}}. After decanting, this becomes your reference density for that tank {{2.10}}. See [reference density](@density-reference-10am) and [the density register](@density-register).

## When MDG says stop and call

- **The driver refuses to sign.** MDG's note under the sample-drawal clause says do not decant; contact the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3}}.
- **You notice an unusual deviation by the lorry in time or route.** Inform the supply location or sales officer, and decant only after you get further instructions {{2.3}}.
- **You doubt the quality.** Contact the supply location or the area field officer {{1.2(ix)}}.

You are also expected to watch the lorry on its way. The dealer monitors the lorry carrying his supply through VTS, using the link provided. If he sees a deviation, he informs the supply location immediately, before accepting the lorry {{1.5(vii)}}. See [VTS and route deviation](@tanker-vts).

## What to have ready before the lorry arrives

- A calibrated hydrometer and thermometer, the ASTM density conversion table and any other testing kits that apply, all in good working condition and available to inspectors {{1.5(vi)}}.
- A clear glass container for the compartment samples {{1.2(viii)}}, and aluminium containers for the retention samples {{2.3}}.
- The density register in the Annexure 1 format {{annexure 1}} and the daily stock register {{1.3}}.
- A file at the outlet with invoice copies and the oil company's letters {{1.5(iv)}}. Purchase invoices for the current and previous financial years are among the records MDG says you must keep {{5.1.12}}.
- The DCP fire extinguisher for the lorry {{1.2(i)}}.

## If you learned this under MDG 2012
