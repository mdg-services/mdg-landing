## What NANO asks of the dealer

MDG 2024 describes automated outlets as designed for "No automation, No Operation", called NANO {{1.5(viii)}}. That puts four jobs on the dealer:

1. **Keep the automation working.** The dealer is responsible for making sure it works properly at the outlet {{1.5(viii)}}.
2. **Report any malfunction immediately** to the authorised official of the oil company {{1.5(viii)}}.
3. **Then act as that official directs** {{1.5(viii)}}.
4. **Keep a power backup**, a generator or a UPS, so a power cut does not stop the automation or the electronic totaliser {{1.5(ix)}}.

> **Note:** MDG does not say in plain words that sales must stop when the automation fails. It says to report at once and follow the official's direction. Do not guess. Ask, and if the answer is "run it in manual mode", get that in writing first.

## The three automation irregularities

Clause 5.1.16 lists three things a dealer must not do at an automated outlet without authorisation from the competent authority {{5.1.16}}.

| Item | What the clause covers | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Manual mode | Any dispensing unit or MPD found working in manual mode, at an outlet where automation has been completed | {{5.1.16(a)}} |
| (b) Tank gauge | The ATG switched off or not working | {{5.1.16(b)}} |
| (c) Other parts | Any deliberate act by the dealership, its staff or any other agency that makes a component of the automation system stop working, wholly or partly. This item leaves out dispensing units and ATGs, which have their own items (a) and (b) | {{5.1.16(c)}} |

MDG writes "MPD" and "ATG" and does not spell either out. General knowledge, not MDG text: MPD usually means multi-product dispenser, and ATG usually means automatic tank gauge, the device that reads the level in a tank.

Touching the dispensing unit itself is a different matter. Adding, removing, replacing or manipulating any part of it, including a microprocessor chip, electronic parts or OEM software, is tampering with the dispensing unit {{5.1.4}}. That is a critical irregularity with termination at the first instance {{8.2}}. For tampering, MDG says the views and independent opinion of the original equipment manufacturer are obtained and a suitable decision is taken {{5.1.4}}.

## What counts as authorisation

- **Only an email or a signed letter from a company official** is admissible {{5.1.16}}.
- **The dealer keeps a record** of that written authorisation for future reference {{5.1.16}}.
- A phone call, an SMS or a chat message is neither an email nor a signed letter, so MDG does not admit it as authorisation {{5.1.16}}. Do not rely on one. If an official tells you something by phone, ask for it by email.
- MDG gives no period for keeping the record. It does ask for a file of correspondence received from the oil company, available at the outlet for ready reference at all times {{1.5(iv)}}. Sensible practice, not an MDG rule: keep the authorisation in that file.

See [what to do when the automation fails](@automation-manual-mode) for the steps.

## The penalty

The three automation irregularities are major irregularities {{8.3(vii)}}. They have their own ladder, not the usual 15-day and 30-day suspensions.

| Instance | Action | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| First | Penalty of Rs 1,00,000 | {{8.3(vii)}} |
| Second | Penalty of Rs 2,00,000 and suspension of sales and supplies for 7 days | {{8.3(vii)}} |
| Third | Termination of the dealership | {{8.3(vii)}} |

To decide whether an irregularity is the first, second or third, the oil company looks back five years from the date the irregularity is established {{8.5.5}}. These actions are the minimum. The oil company can act harder {{8.5.1}}. An irregularity must be established before any action is taken {{8.5.2}}.

A fine with no suspension must be paid within 30 days of the company's notice. If it is not, sales and supplies are suspended from the 31st day for 15 days {{8.7}}. See [repeat offences and unpaid fines](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

### A worked example

This is an illustration, not a real case.

An inspector finds one dispensing unit working in manual mode. The dealer has no email or signed letter allowing it. That is an irregularity under 5.1.16(a). If it is the first in five years, the penalty is Rs 1,00,000. If another is established within five years of the first, the penalty is Rs 2,00,000 and 7 days' suspension of sales and supplies. A third means termination.

Had the dealer held an email from a company official allowing manual mode for that unit, the unit would not fall under this irregularity. The clause covers manual mode "without proper authorization" {{5.1.16}}, and an email or signed letter is what MDG accepts as that authorisation.

## What MDG leaves open

- **Who the competent authority is.** 5.1.16 says "competent authority" and its note says "Company official". No rank or name is given {{5.1.16}}. Ask your oil company who can authorise.
- **How long an authorisation lasts.** Not stated.
- **Which parts make up the automation system.** Not listed.
- **How two of the three are counted.** When two or more irregularities are found together, each is counted as an instance against its class {{8.5.3}}. The penalty table lists 5.1.16 (a), (b) and (c) as one item {{8.3(vii)}}. MDG does not say whether, say, manual mode and a switched-off ATG found together count once or twice. Ask your oil company.
- **A separate penalty for having no power backup.** Chapter 5 has no item for it. For anything not specifically listed, the oil company's competent authority takes suitable action after enquiry, in line with natural justice {{8.5.4}}.

## Compared with MDG 2012

MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, last amended 30.11.2022), which has been replaced, had the same NANO and power backup clauses, the same three irregularities and the same Rs 1,00,000, Rs 2,00,000 plus 7 days, termination ladder {{2012:1.5(viii)}} {{2012:5.1.16}} {{2012:8.3}}. In 2012 the automation item sat at 8.3(viii). In 2024 it sits at 8.3(vii).

One addition: the 2024 clause says the dealer must keep a record of the written authorisation {{5.1.16}}. The 2012 clause only said that an email or signed letter was admissible {{2012:5.1.16}}.
