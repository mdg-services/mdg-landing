## What NANO asks of the dealer

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. For an automated outlet the key idea is NANO, "No automation, No Operation" {{1.5(viii)}}. In practice the dealer has four jobs:

1. **Keep the automation working** at the outlet {{1.5(viii)}}.
2. **Report any malfunction straight away** to the oil company's authorised official {{1.5(viii)}}.
3. **Follow the direction that official gives** {{1.5(viii)}}.
4. **Have a generator or UPS**, so that a power cut does not stop the automation or the electronic totaliser {{1.5(ix)}}.

> **Note:** MDG does not say in plain words that sales must stop when the automation fails. It says to report at once and follow the official's direction. Do not guess. Ask, and if the answer is "run it in manual mode", get that in writing first.

## When manual mode or a switched-off gauge becomes an irregularity

At an outlet where automation is complete, three things count as automation irregularities if there is no permission from the competent authority {{5.1.16}}.

| What happens at the outlet | Why it counts | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| A dispensing unit (MPD) is working in manual mode | Manual mode needs permission | {{5.1.16(a)}} |
| The tank gauge (ATG) is switched off or not working | The gauge may not be left off without permission | {{5.1.16(b)}} |
| The dealership, its staff or anyone else deliberately makes another part of the automation system stop working, fully or partly | Applies to every part other than the dispensing units and the ATG | {{5.1.16(c)}} |

MDG uses "MPD" and "ATG" without spelling them out. General knowledge, not MDG text: MPD usually means multi-product dispenser, and ATG usually means automatic tank gauge, the device that reads the level in a tank.

Interfering with the dispensing unit itself is covered by a different clause. Adding, taking out, replacing or manipulating any part of the unit, including a microprocessor chip, electronic parts or the maker's software, is treated as tampering {{5.1.4}}. Tampering is a critical irregularity, and the action is termination at the first instance {{8.2}}. For these cases MDG says the equipment's original maker is asked for its view before a decision is taken {{5.1.4}}.

## What counts as permission

- **An email or a signed letter from a company official.** MDG admits nothing else {{5.1.16}}.
- **The dealer keeps a record** of that written permission for later reference {{5.1.16}}.
- **A phone call, SMS or chat message** is neither an email nor a signed letter, so MDG does not admit it {{5.1.16}}. If an official tells you something by phone, ask for it by email.
- **How long to keep it** is not stated. MDG does ask for a file of the oil company's correspondence, at the outlet and ready to show at all times {{1.5(iv)}}. Sensible practice, not an MDG rule: keep the permission in that file.

See [what to do when the automation fails](@automation-manual-mode) for the steps.

## What the penalty is

The three automation irregularities are major irregularities {{8.3(vii)}}. They follow their own scale, not the usual 15-day and 30-day suspensions.

| Instance | Action | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| First | Penalty of Rs 1,00,000 | {{8.3(vii)}} |
| Second | Penalty of Rs 2,00,000 and 7 days' suspension of sales and supplies | {{8.3(vii)}} |
| Third | Termination of the dealership | {{8.3(vii)}} |

Read these amounts with four points in mind:

- They are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}.
- No action can follow until the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}.
- To tell a first instance from a second or third, the oil company looks back five years from the date the irregularity is established. Irregularities under the previous guideline within those five years also count {{8.5.5}}.
- A fine with no suspension is to be paid within 30 days of the company's notice. If it is not, sales and supplies are suspended from the 31st day for 15 days {{8.7}}. See [repeat offences and unpaid fines](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

### A worked example

For example, at a made-up outlet (an illustration, not a real case), an inspector finds one dispensing unit working in manual mode. The dealer holds no email or signed letter allowing it. That is an irregularity under 5.1.16(a). If it is the first in five years, the minimum penalty is Rs 1,00,000. If another is established within five years of the first, the minimum is Rs 2,00,000 and 7 days' suspension of sales and supplies. A third means termination. In each case the oil company may take higher action {{8.5.1}}.

If the dealer had held an email from a company official allowing manual mode for that unit, the unit would not fall under this irregularity. The irregularity is manual mode without permission, and an email or signed letter is the form of permission MDG admits {{5.1.16}}.

## What MDG leaves open

- **Who the competent authority is.** 5.1.16 says "competent authority" and its note says "Company official". No rank or name is given {{5.1.16}}. Ask your oil company who can give permission.
- **How long a permission lasts.** Not stated.
- **Which parts make up the automation system.** Not listed.
- **How findings made together are counted.** When two or more irregularities are found together at one outlet, each counts as an instance in its own class and each gets its own action, so the effect adds up {{8.5.3}}. The penalty entry lists 5.1.16 (a), (b) and (c) together as one item {{8.3(vii)}}. MDG does not say whether, for example, a unit in manual mode and a switched-off ATG found on the same day count once or twice. Ask your oil company.
- **A separate penalty for having no power backup.** Chapter 5 has no item for it. For matters it does not list, the oil company's competent authority takes suitable action after an enquiry, following natural justice {{8.5.4}}.

## Compared with MDG 2012

MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, last amended 30.11.2022), which MDG 2024 replaced, has the same NANO and power backup clauses, the same three irregularities and the same scale of Rs 1,00,000, then Rs 2,00,000 with 7 days' suspension, then termination {{2012:1.5(viii)}} {{2012:1.5(ix)}} {{2012:5.1.16}} {{2012:8.3}}. In MDG 2012 the automation item was 8.3(viii). In MDG 2024 it is 8.3(vii).

One addition: MDG 2024 tells the dealer to keep a record of the written permission {{5.1.16}}. The MDG 2012 clause only said that an email or signed letter would be admissible {{2012:5.1.16}}.
