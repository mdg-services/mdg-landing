## What counts as refusing

Clause 5.1.8 names three things: refusing sampling or an inspection, obstructing it, and creating circumstances that disable it {{5.1.8}}. It is one of the major irregularities {{8.3(i)}}. MDG gives no further definition of obstruction and no examples.

## Who may draw samples

| Who may draw samples | Clause |
|---|---|
| Oil company officials, mobile labs, agencies the oil company authorises, and any other authority the law empowers | {{2.2.2.2}} |
| For the company's random sampling: the Sales Officer, Inspecting Teams, Senior Level Officers and Mobile Labs | {{2.4}} |

Officials pay for the samples they take and obtain a cash memo {{2.2.2.2}}. See [who can inspect](@inspection-who) and [inspector samples](@sampling-inspector-samples).

## What it costs

| Instance | Minimum action | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| First | 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies | {{8.3}} |
| Second | 30 days' suspension | {{8.3}} |
| Third | Termination of the dealership | {{8.3}} |

{{penalty-note}}

See [critical, major or minor](@penalties-classes) for the class.

## If you doubt who the visitor is

MDG 2024 says nothing on what a dealer may do if he doubts a visitor's authority, and does not say that a visitor must show an identity card. General practice, not an MDG rule:

1. Ask at the start for the name, designation and oil company. The sample label has places for these {{annexure 4}}.
2. Note the time the visitor arrived.
3. If you disagree with something, say so and write it down.
4. Do not block or delay the inspection or the sampling while you do.

## What MDG 2024 leaves open

- What "obstruction" covers beyond the three words in 5.1.8.
- How a dealer should raise a doubt about a visitor.

For either, ask your oil company.
