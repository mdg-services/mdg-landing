## What the dealer does each day

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. On quantity they start from the dealer's general duty to give customers the right quality, the right quantity and the right price {{1.6(i)}}. Clause 1.4.2 then sets six specific duties, which fall across the day like this.

| When | What the dealer does | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Every day, before the first sale | Looks at the Weights & Measures (W&M) seal on the metering unit and the totalizer of each dispensing unit, and confirms the unit's W&M certificate is still valid | {{1.4.2(a)}} |
| Every day, before the first sale | Tests every nozzle for correct delivery and writes the quantity used for the test in the stock register | {{1.4.2(d)}} |
| Every day | Looks over the pump, hose and nozzle for leaks, and tells the oil company about any at once | {{1.4.2(e)}} |
| Kept at the outlet | A 5-litre measure verified by W&M, used for nothing but pump testing | {{1.4.2(b)}} |
| Kept at the outlet | A W&M stamp on every measure used to dispense bulk lube oil on the island or in service bays | {{1.4.2(c)}} |
| At every delivery | Meter returned to zero after the last delivery, zero shown to the customer by the attendant before the next one, and a message about it carried by each pump unit | {{1.4.2(f)}} |

Separately, each morning the dealer writes down the pump meter reading and the dip of each tank {{1.3}}.

Three of these duties have their own page: [the 5-litre test and nozzle check](@quantity-5-litre-test), [the W&M and totalizer seals](@quantity-seals) and [zero before every delivery](@quantity-zero-before-delivery).

## Short delivery: seals intact or seals tampered

Every quantity case starts with one question: are the W&M seals intact? The answer decides whether the case is minor or critical.

| | Seals intact | Seals tampered |
|---|---|---|
| MDG heading | Short delivery with seals intact {{5.1.2(a)}} | Metering-unit seals found tampered {{5.1.2(b)}} |
| Does the quantity matter? | Yes: the unit is delivering short | No: action follows even when the delivery is correct or in excess {{5.1.2(b)}} |
| Class | Minor {{8.4(iii)(h)}} | Critical {{8.2}} |
| Action | Warning-cum-guidance letter at the first instance, Rs 10,000 per irregularity at the second, Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third on {{8.4}} | Termination at the first instance {{8.2}} |
| Sales | Short beyond the legal limit: sales through that unit or nozzle stop until it is recalibrated and re-stamped. Short but inside the permissible band: the finding is recorded and sales go on, but recalibration and re-stamping are advised within 15 days, failing which sales stop {{5.1.2(a)}} | The unit stops selling, is sealed, and samples of all products go to the lab {{5.1.2(b)}} |

Read the figures with MDG's own limits:

- The amounts are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination, even at the first instance {{8.5.1}}.
- Action against a dealer needs the irregularity to be established first {{8.5.2}}.
- The second and third instances are counted by looking back five years, and irregularities under the previous guideline within those five years count too {{8.5.5}}. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).
- How several findings add up is not fully settled by the text. Clause 8.4 says the miscellaneous minor findings, which include short delivery with seals intact, count as one irregularity for taking action. Clause 8.5.3 says that when two or more irregularities are found together at one outlet, each counts as an instance in its own class and action is taken for each, with a compounding effect. MDG does not say how the two fit, so ask your oil company how it counts.

The full seals-intact rule, with the 15-day deadline, is on the [5-litre test page](@quantity-5-litre-test). MDG does not say in so many words whether a finding inside the permissible band also counts as the minor irregularity above. Ask your oil company how it records these.

## Tampering: the three critical findings

MDG 2024 puts three quantity findings in its critical class, and sets termination of the dealership at the first instance for each {{8.2}}.

- **Metering-unit seals tampered.** No seal at all, a seal that W&M's inspector did not emboss, or a sealing wire that has broken apart, each count as tampering {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- **Totalizer tampered.** This covers a seal that would let the reading be manipulated, a totalizer deliberately put out of action, and a dead totalizer that the dealer did not report to the oil company {{5.1.3}}.
- **Unauthorised fittings, parts or software.** A device found in a dispensing unit that could alter what it delivers is covered, and so is any part added, taken out, swapped or interfered with. The clause names chips, electronic parts and the manufacturer's software {{5.1.4}}.

For all three, the unit stops selling, is sealed, and samples of all products are drawn and sent to the lab {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}} {{5.1.4}}. Before an oil company recommends or approves termination for a critical irregularity, the dealership's signatories, or their nominees, get a personal hearing {{8.6}}. The [seals page](@quantity-seals) has the detail.

## Who fixes a pump that delivers wrong

- **Recalibration.** If a pump gives short or excess, the oil company's maintenance representative or the dealer should arrange recalibration straight away, with the W&M Inspector's help {{4.3(a)}}.
- **Re-stamping.** The Weights & Measures Department does it. Until it is done and the delivery has been corrected, the dealer should not run the unit {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- **A broken W&M seal found on a maintenance visit.** The oil company's representative passes it on to the company official concerned and writes it up in the maintenance report for the pump, which the dealer signs as well {{4.3(a)}}.
- **Breakdowns.** The oil company arranges breakdown maintenance on its own dispensing pumps, and the dealer must tell it promptly about any breakdown {{4.2(a)}}. See [what the oil company must fix](@company-breakdowns-leaks).

## Who checks a petrol pump

| Who | What they check | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| The dealer | Seals, certificate, every nozzle and leaks, every day | {{1.4.2}} |
| The oil company's maintenance representative | The maintenance report has questions on whether the metering unit's calibration was checked and found correct, and whether any additional unauthorised fitting is present | {{annexure 6}} |
| W&M | Re-stamps after recalibration, and its opinion on whether a seal was tampered is final | {{1.4.2(d)}} {{5.1.2(b)}} |
| Oil company officers and government authorities | Both can check the outlet. If an authority establishes an irregularity, the oil company treats it as one under MDG and acts on that authority's advice | {{8.7}} |

## One morning, worked through

Illustration, with made-up details: at 6:40 AM, before the first sale, the manager at an imaginary outlet sees that the sealing wire on the totalizer of unit 3 has been cut in two.

- **What MDG tells the manager to do:** stop sales through unit 3 at once, tell the controlling oil company office immediately, and keep the unit closed until the seals are replaced {{1.4.2(a)}}. The clause does not name other units.
- **How MDG classes the cut wire:** a broken wire counts as tampering {{5.1.2(b)}}, which is critical once it is established {{8.2}} {{8.5.2}}.

> **Note:** MDG does not say that reporting a broken seal first changes how the finding is classed. The daily check and the immediate report are the dealer's duties under 1.4.2(a). Ask your oil company what follows in your case.

## What to have ready

- A valid W&M certificate for every dispensing unit {{1.4.2(a)}}.
- The W&M-verified 5-litre measure, used for pump testing only {{1.4.2(b)}}.
- Stamped lube measures {{1.4.2(c)}}.
- A message about zero on every pump unit {{1.4.2(f)}}.
- Power backup, a generator or UPS, so that an electronic totalizer keeps working in a power cut {{1.5(ix)}}.

MDG does not say how long a W&M certificate lasts or how it is renewed. Ask your oil company or the local W&M office.
