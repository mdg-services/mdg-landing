## What the circular says and does not say

Point 9 asks for digital payment to be available and enabled, naming credit or debit card, loyalty machine and QR code as examples {{circular}}. The inspector marks it YES or NO. A deviation is acted on under 5.1.18 or the clause that fits {{circular}}.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is it required? | Yes, as point 9 of the circular {{circular}} |
| Which methods must an outlet have? | Card, loyalty machine and QR code are examples; the circular does not say which are a must |
| How long may a machine be down? | Not said |
| Bank or network fault? | Not said |
| Cash fallback, or a customer charge? | Not said |

Where the text is silent, ask your oil company. General practice, not an MDG rule: note the date and time a machine stops, when you told the bank or the oil company, and keep the reply.

## What a NO costs

Under 5.1.18 the action is a warning-cum-guidance letter the first time, Rs 10,000 per irregularity the second time, and Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third time {{8.4}}.

{{penalty-note}}

A NO on a circular point is acted on under the customer service standard line {{8.4(iii)(f)}}, unless a more specific clause fits. A lapse under any of a to h, such as housekeeping or a dirty toilet, counts as one irregularity with it for taking action {{8.4}}. See [how minor irregularities are counted](@penalties-minor-ladder).

## An example

For example (a made-up outlet): at 4 PM the inspector finds the card machine switched off and no other digital option on offer. Point 9 is marked NO. If this is the first instance, the minimum action is a warning-cum-guidance letter {{8.4}}.

The other 19 points are in [the 20 service obligations](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations).
