## What the 20 points are

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers, applied through the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. Clause 5.1.18 asks the dealer to keep a minimum standard of customer service, as oil company circulars set it from time to time {{5.1.18}} {{1.6(xii)}}.

A circular dated 20.03.2025 turns that into 20 points. It is a common format for the dealers of all three oil companies, and each company sends it to its dealers on its own letterhead. It is published on iocl.com. It sets no penalty of its own and sends the inspector back to clause 5.1.18, or to whichever clause fits the item {{circular}}.

At an inspection the inspecting authority checks that all 20 are in place and practised, and records each one as **YES** or **NO** {{circular}}. "From time to time" means later circulars can add to or change what is expected, so check what your oil company has sent.

## The 20 points in plain words

The point numbers are the circular's, so you can match them to your copy. The groups and the wording are ours. A clause column that shows only "Circular" means the circular is the only source we cite for that point.

### Products, staff and the forecourt

| Point | What you need in place | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No product runs out. The circular says "always" and lists no exception. | {{circular}} |
| 2 | Each dispensing unit has staff of the kinds the circular names (CA, DSM or CSA) on duty for the whole working day. | {{circular}} |
| 3 | After each delivery the meter returns to zero, and a message about this is shown on each pump unit. The attendant shows the customer the zero before starting and the closing reading at the end. | {{1.4.2(f)}} {{5.1.17}} {{circular}} |
| 4 | Staff guide vehicles and keep the forecourt queue in order. The circular says no more than this. | {{circular}} |
| 8 | Staff are quick to serve and polite throughout the day. | {{1.6(iii)}} |

## What a NO can cost

The circular sets no penalty of its own. Under clause 5.1.18 a shortfall draws the penalty for minor irregularities, and "customer service standard" is one of the minor irregularities in 8.4 {{5.1.18}} {{8.4}}.

| Instance | Action MDG lists, per irregularity |
|---|---|
| First | Warning-cum-guidance letter |
| Second | Rs 10,000 |
| Third and later | Rs 25,000 |

Four things go with these figures:

- **Minimum, not a ceiling.** The amounts are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination, in line with the dealership agreement {{8.5.1}}. The irregularity must be established before any action {{8.5.2}}.
- **Counting instances.** Second and third instances are counted over five years from the date an irregularity is established, and the count includes irregularities under the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}. See [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).
- **Found together.** The text points two ways. Clause 8.4 says that when several of the minor items lettered a to h are found together, they count as one irregularity for deciding action. Customer service standards and the toilet are among those items {{8.4}}. Clause 8.5.3 says that when two or more irregularities are found at the same time, each counts as an instance in its own class, each gets action, and the effect compounds {{8.5.3}}. MDG does not say how the two fit together, so ask your oil company how it counts.
- **The clause that fits.** The circular lets the company act under 5.1.18 or under the clause that fits the item {{circular}}. For the points it ties to other clauses, that means 5.1.14(a) for air and the first aid box, 5.1.14(b) for the toilet and 5.1.17 for housekeeping and displays. Price display has its own clause, 5.1.15. These are minor irregularities too {{8.4}}. For point 20 MDG names no specific penalty, so ask your oil company which clause it would use.

## Acknowledge the circular

The circular says the 20 points are to be passed to every dealership, with the acknowledgement kept in the records {{circular}}.

- The oil company sends a letter on its letterhead that lists the 20 points. It asks you to confirm receipt to your controlling office, in the format that comes with it {{circular}}.
- That confirmation is a short letter. It records the outlet, its customer code and the date of the circular, and carries the dealer's name, signature, date and place {{circular}}.
- MDG asks for oil company letters to be kept in a file at the outlet, ready for reference at all times {{1.5(iv)}}. This circular is one of them.

General practice, not an MDG rule: keep your signed acknowledgement with the circular, and ask your controlling office for a copy if you cannot find it.

## A related duty in the same clause

A note under 5.1.18 adds that the dealer must meet all applicable legal duties towards staff, including minimum wages and other benefits such as PMSBY and PMJJBY {{5.1.18}} {{1.5(x)}}. See [staff wages and uniform](@service-staff-wages-uniform).
