## The two seals and the daily check

W&M puts a seal on the metering unit and another on the totalizer unit of a dispensing unit. Each is a sealing wire closed with a lead seal that the W&M inspector has embossed {{5.1.2(b)}}. In plain words, the totalizer is the counter that keeps the running total of everything the unit has delivered. That explanation is ours, not MDG's wording.

Every day, the dealer must check two things on each dispensing unit: that the W&M seal on the metering unit and the totalizer is correct, and that the unit's W&M certification is valid {{1.4.2(a)}}.

If a seal is found tampered, the clause lists three steps {{1.4.2(a)}}:

1. Stop sales through that dispensing unit forthwith.
2. Report the matter immediately to the controlling oil company office.
3. Keep sales suspended until the seals are replaced.

MDG does not say how long a W&M certificate lasts or who renews it. Ask your oil company or the local W&M office.

## When MDG treats a seal as tampered

| What is found | Treated as | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| The seal itself is missing | Tampered | {{5.1.2(b)}} |
| A different seal, not the one the W&M inspector embossed | Tampered | {{5.1.2(b)}} |
| The sealing wire is broken and not in one piece | Tampered | {{5.1.2(b)}} |
| Any other situation that could allow the delivery, the quantity or the totalizer to be manipulated | May also be treated as tampering | {{5.1.2(b)}} |
| A totalizer seal that allows the totalizer reading to be manipulated | Tampered | {{5.1.3}} |
| The totalizer deliberately made non-functional | Tampered | {{5.1.3}} |
| A totalizer that is not working and not reported to the oil company | Tampered | {{5.1.3}} |

## Who decides, and why the quantity does not matter

For seals and the totalizer, the views and opinion of the W&M authorities are obtained, and the opinion W&M gives is final {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}}. MDG does not say who asks for it. Based on that opinion, penal action follows even if the delivery is found correct or excess {{5.1.2(b)}}.

So a pump can deliver exactly what it should and the dealer can still be penalised. That is how MDG is written: the tampering is the irregularity, and a correct delivery does not cure it.

> **Note:** MDG says nothing that softens this for a dealer who finds a broken seal and reports it first. The daily check and the immediate report are the dealer's duty under 1.4.2(a). If a seal is damaged, report it to the oil company straight away, before any inspection, and ask what follows.

## Fittings, chips and software

Clause 5.1.4 goes beyond seals. It covers any mechanism, fitting or gear found fitted in a dispensing unit that is likely to manipulate the delivery. It also deems the addition, removal, replacement or manipulation of any part of the unit to be tampering, including a mechanism, a gear, a microprocessor chip, electronic parts or the manufacturer's software {{5.1.4}}.

Here the views and independent opinion of the original equipment manufacturer are obtained, and a suitable decision is taken {{5.1.4}}. MDG does not say who asks the manufacturer or who decides.

The clause has no exception for repairs. The oil company attends breakdowns on its dispensing pumps {{4.2(a)}}, and its maintenance report records the repairs and the parts replaced and carries the dealer's signature {{annexure 6}}. Ask your oil company before anyone other than its maintenance representative opens a unit.

## Keep the totalizer working

A totalizer that stops working must be reported. Not reporting it counts as tampering {{5.1.3}}. MDG also requires a power backup, a generator or UPS, which it calls important for the proper working of an electronic totalizer and of automated outlets {{1.5(ix)}}.

## What happens after a finding

All three findings are critical irregularities {{8.2}}. In each, sales from the dispensing unit are suspended, the unit is sealed, and samples of all products are drawn and sent to the lab {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}} {{5.1.4}}.

| Step | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Established first | An irregularity must be established before any action is taken | {{8.5.2}} |
| Action | Termination of the dealership at the first instance | {{8.2}} |
| Notice | A show cause notice within 30 days of the inspection, or within 30 days of the test results if samples were drawn. The dealer has 10 days to reply | {{8.5.6}} {{8.5.7}} |
| Hearing | Before termination is recommended or approved, the signatories to the dealership or their nominees get a personal hearing | {{8.6}} |
| Order | A speaking order, preferably within 45 days of the reply | {{8.5.8}} |
| Appeal | Within 30 days of receiving the termination order | {{8.9}} |

This is general information. It is not advice on a particular case. For a notice that has been served, speak to the oil company and to a lawyer. See [show cause notices](@inspection-show-cause-notice) and [appeals against termination](@inspection-appeal).

## A worked example

For example, an inspector finds that the lead seal on the totalizer of unit 2 is not the one W&M embossed. The unit is tested and delivers exactly 5 litres.

- The seal counts as tampered, because it is a different seal {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- W&M's views and opinion are obtained, and its opinion is final {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- The correct delivery does not change the outcome {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- Sales from unit 2 are suspended, the unit is sealed, and samples of all products are drawn and sent to the lab {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- The finding is critical, and the action is termination at the first instance {{8.2}}.

## When the seals are intact

A pump found delivering short with its W&M seals intact is a different case. It is a minor irregularity {{8.4(iii)(h)}}. See [the 5 litre test](@quantity-5-litre-test) and [the quantity rules overall](@quantity).
