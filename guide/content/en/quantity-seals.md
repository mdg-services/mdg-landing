## The two seals and the daily check

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. Under them, the Weights & Measures (W&M) Department fits one seal on the metering unit and another on the totalizer unit of a dispensing unit. Each seal is a sealing wire fastened with a lead seal that carries the W&M inspector's emboss {{5.1.2(b)}}. In plain words, the totalizer is the counter that keeps the running total of everything the unit has delivered. That explanation is ours, not MDG's wording.

Every day, on each dispensing unit, the dealer confirms two things: the W&M seal on the metering unit and the totalizer is correct, and the unit's W&M certificate is valid {{1.4.2(a)}}.

If a seal is found tampered, the unit stops selling at once, the controlling oil company office is told immediately, and the unit stays closed for sales until new seals are fitted {{1.4.2(a)}}.

MDG does not say how long a W&M certificate lasts or who renews it. Ask your oil company or the local W&M office.

## What MDG treats as a tampered seal

On the metering-unit and totalizer seals, MDG deems these to be tampering:

- **No seal at all** on the unit {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- **A seal that W&M's inspector did not emboss**, put on in place of the right one {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- **A sealing wire that has broken apart** {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- **Any other situation** that could let the delivery, the quantity or the totalizer be manipulated, which MDG says can count as tampering too {{5.1.2(b)}}.

On the totalizer itself, tampering also covers:

- a seal that allows the reading to be manipulated {{5.1.3}};
- a totalizer that has been deliberately put out of action {{5.1.3}};
- a totalizer that has stopped working and was not reported to the oil company {{5.1.3}}.

## Who decides, and why the quantity does not matter

For seals and the totalizer, W&M's views and opinion are obtained, and the opinion W&M gives is final {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}}. MDG does not say who asks for it. Action then follows even when the delivery is found correct or in excess {{5.1.2(b)}}.

So a pump can deliver exactly what it should and the dealer can still face action. In MDG the tampering is the irregularity, and a correct delivery does not remove it.

> **Note:** MDG does not say that reporting a broken seal first changes how the finding is classed. The daily check and the immediate report are the dealer's duties under 1.4.2(a). Ask your oil company what follows in your case.

## Fittings, chips and software

Clause 5.1.4 reaches past the seals to the inside of the unit. A mechanism, fitting or gear found in a dispensing unit that could be used to alter what it delivers is covered. So is any change to the unit itself: parts added, taken out, swapped or interfered with, including a microprocessor chip, electronic parts and the manufacturer's software {{5.1.4}}.

In these cases MDG calls for the equipment manufacturer's independent view before a decision is made {{5.1.4}}. It does not say who asks the manufacturer or who decides.

The clause has no exception for repairs. The oil company arranges breakdown maintenance on its dispensing pumps {{4.2(a)}}, and its maintenance report has places to record repairs and parts replaced and carries the dealer's signature {{annexure 6}}. Ask your oil company before anyone other than its maintenance representative opens a unit.

## Keep the totalizer working

A totalizer that stops working must be reported, and not reporting it counts as tampering {{5.1.3}}. MDG also asks for a power backup, a generator or UPS, and calls it important for an electronic totalizer and for automated outlets to work properly {{1.5(ix)}}.

## When the seals are intact

A pump found delivering short with its W&M seals intact is a different case, and a minor irregularity {{8.4(iii)(h)}}. See [the 5 litre test](@quantity-5-litre-test) and [the quantity rules overall](@quantity).

## What happens after a finding

All three findings are critical irregularities {{8.2}}. For each, sales from the dispensing unit are suspended, the unit is sealed, and samples of all products are drawn and sent to the lab {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}} {{5.1.4}}.

| Step | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Established first | No action against a dealer until the irregularity is established | {{8.5.2}} |
| Action | Termination of the dealership at the first instance | {{8.2}} |
| Notice | A show cause notice goes out within 30 days of the inspection, or within 30 days of the test results where samples were drawn. The dealer has 10 days from the date it is issued to reply | {{8.5.6}} {{8.5.7}} |
| Hearing | Before termination is recommended or approved, the dealership's signatories, or their nominees, get a personal hearing | {{8.6}} |
| Order | A speaking order, preferably within 45 days of receiving the reply | {{8.5.8}} |
| Appeal | An appeal under 8.9 is open only against termination, within 30 days of receiving the order | {{8.9}} |

See [show cause notices](@inspection-show-cause-notice) and [appeals against termination](@inspection-appeal).

## An illustration

Illustration, with made-up details: an inspector finds that the lead seal on the totalizer of unit 2 at an imaginary outlet is not the one W&M embossed. The unit is tested and delivers exactly 5 litres.

- The seal counts as tampered, because it is a different seal {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- W&M's views and opinion are obtained, and its opinion is final {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- The correct delivery does not change the outcome {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- Sales from unit 2 are suspended, the unit is sealed, and samples of all products are drawn and sent to the lab {{5.1.2(b)}}.
- Once the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}, the finding is critical, and the action MDG sets is termination at the first instance {{8.2}}.
