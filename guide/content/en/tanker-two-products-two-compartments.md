## Receiving it, step by step

The order is that of [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker), applied one compartment at a time.

1. **Match the invoice.** The number of compartments, and the product and quantity in each {{1.1(iii)}}.
2. **Dip and water-test every compartment**, and stop sales from the underground tank that will receive the product {{1.2(vii)}}.
3. **Density, compartment by compartment.** At each manifold run off about 20 litres, then read and record that compartment's density and temperature on its own. Each 15°C figure is compared with the invoice density, within ±3.0 kg/m³ {{1.2(iv)}} {{1.2(viii)}}. See [invoice density check](@density-invoice-check).
4. **Retention samples.** Once density is within the limit, draw bottom composites in proportion to the quantity received in each compartment, after removing at least 20 litres from each: 2 × 1 litre of MS and/or 1 × 1 litre of HSD. The driver and you both sign the label {{1.2(x)}} {{2.3}}. See [retention samples](@sampling-retention).
5. **Decant each product into its correct underground tank** {{1.2(xi)}}. If a product has more than one tank, the tank number goes on the sample container {{2.3}}.
6. **After decanting**, run at least 50 litres through the pump and record the 15°C density, kept for each tank and product {{1.2(xiv)}} {{annexure 1}}.

## An example

An illustration with made-up figures:

| Compartment | Product on the invoice | Quantity | Goes into |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Petrol | 6,000 litres | Petrol tank |
| 2 | Petrol | 6,000 litres | Petrol tank |
| 3 | Diesel | 8,000 litres | Diesel tank |

Each compartment gets its own dip, water test and density reading. The two petrol compartments together make up the MS samples, and the diesel compartment the HSD sample {{2.3}}.

## What MDG says about mixed loads, and what it leaves out

MDG bars mixed loads in tank lorries with a common manifold. That is a supply location rule, not a dealer check {{3.1(j)}}, and MDG does not say what a dealer does if such a lorry arrives.

MDG leaves two points open:

- Whether sales from the first tank may restart before the whole lorry is empty. It says sales from the concerned tank stop until decantation is complete {{1.2(vii)}}. See [selling during unloading](@tanker-selling-during-decant).
- Which invoice figure a compartment is compared with when the lorry carries two products. General practice, not an MDG rule: use the density printed for that compartment's product.

Ask your oil company about both.
