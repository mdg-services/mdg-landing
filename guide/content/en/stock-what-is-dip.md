## Two dips, two questions

General knowledge, not MDG text: a dip rod is lowered to the bottom of a tank and lifted out, and the wet mark shows how deep the liquid is.

- **Product dip:** the height of fuel, in cm. Not litres yet.
- **Water dip:** the height of water at the bottom, recorded every day {{1.4.1(b)}}. MDG's maintenance report asks for it in cm {{annexure 6}}. General practice: water finding paste on the rod shows the water line; MDG names the paste for tanker compartments {{1.2(vii)}}. See [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste).

To get litres, look the height up in that tank's own calibration chart. That is general practice, not an MDG rule. Every tank has its own chart, and between two lines the usual way is a straight-line estimate: see the [dip chart calculator](@tools-dip-chart). Never use another tank's chart.

## When MDG asks for a dip

| When | Which dip | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Every morning | Product dip and water dip of each tank, taken separately and recorded | {{1.3}} |
| Every day | Water dip, as part of the stock register. Water found goes to the controlling company office at once | {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| A tanker arrives | Every compartment dipped with the lorry's certified rod, after checking the rod's markings, plus a water paste test | {{1.2(vi)}} {{1.2(vii)}} |
| Dip short, or water | The shortage is made up through the dispensing pump and written on the back of the invoice | {{1.2(xi)}} |
| The stock check | The opening dip and the closing dip start and end the sum | {{annexure 9}} {{5.1.11}} |
| A mobile lab sample fails | Each dispensing unit's meter reading and the dip of every tank of that product go into the test report | {{7.6.1(b)}} |

## How the dip feeds the stock register

Each morning's dip, turned into litres, is the opening stock. With receipts and meter sales it gives the book stock, and the next morning's dip shows how far the tank differs from the books {{annexure 9}}. For example (made-up tank): a morning dip of 142 cm is looked up in that tank's chart, and the litres are written beside it in the register.

What is written each morning is on the [daily stock register](@stock-daily-register) page, and the limit on [stock variation](@stock-variation). Our [register formats](@tools-registers) have columns for each tank's dips.
