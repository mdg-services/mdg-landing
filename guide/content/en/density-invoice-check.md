## What the invoice tells you

The supply location writes the density at 15°C on the invoice. It also writes the filling point, the storage tank number the lorry was filled from, and the time the lorry was released {{2.2.1(iv)}}. It checks the density at the filling point every two hours and whenever it switches tanks {{3.1(h)}}.

When the lorry arrives, the dealer matches the invoice with the lorry on six items. The recorded density at 15°C is one of them {{1.1(v)}}. The rest of the receipt checklist is on the page about [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).

## The check, step by step

The order below puts the sample step after the dips and water checks, as 1.2(viii) does.

1. **Note the invoice density.** It is the figure your reading is compared with {{1.1(v)}} {{1.2(iv)}}.
2. **Finish the dip and water checks.** The sample step comes after them {{1.2(vii)}} {{1.2(viii)}}. See [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste).
3. **Drain about 20 litres** of product from the manifold of each compartment. Then fill a clear glass container from that compartment {{1.2(viii)}}.
4. **Read the density and the temperature** of each compartment's sample. Each compartment is checked individually {{1.2(viii)}}.
5. **Convert to 15°C** using the ASTM table (53B) {{1.2(iv)}}.
6. **Compare with the invoice density.** The 15°C figure must be within ±3.0 kg/m³ {{1.2(iv)}}.
7. **Record each compartment's reading** in the register {{1.2(viii)}}.
8. **Go on** to the bottom sample for retention, which MDG ties to the density check being within limits {{2.3}} {{1.2(x)}}. Decant only after quality and quantity are ascertained {{1.2(xi)}}. See [retention samples](@sampling-retention).

## If a compartment is outside ±3.0 kg/m³

MDG says one thing here. When you doubt the quality of the product, contact the supply location or the concerned area field officer for further instructions {{1.2(ix)}}. The bottom-sample step applies when the density check is within limits {{2.3}}. MDG gives no other steps for a compartment that is outside.

General practice, not an MDG rule: treat a compartment outside ±3.0 kg/m³ as a doubt. Keep it out of the tank and call the supply location before you decant it. MDG's own sequence is quality and quantity first, then decanting {{1.2(xi)}}. Confirm with your oil company how it wants this handled.

If the driver refuses to sign the sample label, MDG says the lorry should not be decanted. Contact the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3}}.

## A worked example

This example is an illustration. The figures are made up. Your invoice and your table give the real ones.

A tanker brings petrol in three compartments. The invoice shows a density at 15°C of 743.0 kg/m³. You sample each compartment, write down the hydrometer reading and the temperature, and use the ASTM table to get the 15°C figure. Suppose the table gives these figures:

| Compartment | 15°C figure from the table | Difference from the invoice (743.0) | Within ±3.0? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 744.5 | +1.5 | Yes |
| 2 | 745.0 | +2.0 | Yes |
| 3 | 746.5 | +3.5 | No |

Compartments 1 and 2 meet the check. Compartment 3 is 0.5 beyond the limit. You record all three readings {{1.2(viii)}}. If you doubt the quality of compartment 3, 1.2(ix) tells you to contact the supply location or the area field officer for instructions {{1.2(ix)}}.

> **Note:** the example starts from the 15°C figures on purpose. Never convert by eye. Take the observed density and temperature to the ASTM table.

## How to calculate density of petrol at 15°C

MDG gives no formula, and you do not need one. You read the density on a hydrometer and the temperature on a thermometer. You look both up in the ASTM table and read off the density at 15°C. MDG names Table 53B for this {{1.2(iv)}}. It also requires you to have calibrated hydrometers and thermometers and the ASTM table in working order {{1.5(vi)}}.

We do not print a conversion here. Use the table, or the [15°C density calculator](@tools-density-at-15), which converts a reading and a temperature for you.

## Why the invoice figure matters later

Until the tanker is decanted, the reference density for any sample is the density recorded in the dispatch document {{2.10}}. The supply location writes that density at 15°C on the invoice {{3.1(h)}}. After decanting, the reference becomes the mixed-tank density you record once 50 litres have gone through the pump {{1.2(xiv)}} {{2.10}}. See [the reference density rule](@density-reference-10am).

The label on your tank lorry retention sample has two density lines: the density as recorded in the invoice and the density of the sample collected from the lorry {{annexure 3}}.
