## What the invoice tells you

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. Under them, the supply location writes the 15°C density on the invoice. It also writes the filling point, the number of the storage tank the lorry was filled from and the time the lorry was released {{2.2.1(iv)}}. The supply location checks density at the filling point every two hours, and again whenever it switches tanks {{3.1(h)}}.

When the lorry arrives, the dealer matches the invoice against the lorry on six points. The recorded 15°C density is one of them {{1.1(v)}}. The rest of the receipt checklist is on the page about [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker).

## The density check, step by step

MDG puts the sampling step after the dip and water checks {{1.2(vii)}} {{1.2(viii)}}. The steps below cover the density part only.

1. **Note the invoice density.** Your reading is compared with this figure {{1.1(v)}} {{1.2(iv)}}.
2. **Finish the dip and water checks first.** See [water finding paste](@tanker-water-finding-paste).
3. **Run off about 20 litres** at the manifold of each compartment, then fill a clear glass container from that compartment {{1.2(viii)}}.
4. **Read density and temperature** on each compartment's sample. Each compartment is taken on its own {{1.2(viii)}}.
5. **Convert to 15°C** with the ASTM table (53B) {{1.2(iv)}}.
6. **Compare with the invoice density.** The 15°C figure must be within ±3.0 kg/m³ {{1.2(iv)}}.
7. **Record each compartment's reading** in the register {{1.2(viii)}}.

After that comes the bottom sample kept for retention, which MDG ties to the density check being within limits {{2.3}} {{1.2(x)}}. The product is decanted only once quality and quantity are settled {{1.2(xi)}}. See [retention samples](@sampling-retention).

## If a compartment is outside ±3.0 kg/m³

MDG says one thing about this. If you doubt the quality of the product, contact the supply location or the area field officer concerned for instructions {{1.2(ix)}}. The bottom-sample step applies when the density check is within limits {{2.3}}. MDG gives no other steps for a compartment that falls outside.

General practice, not an MDG rule: treat a compartment outside ±3.0 kg/m³ as a doubt. Keep it out of the tank and call the supply location before decanting. This fits MDG's own order, which settles quality and quantity first and decants afterwards {{1.2(xi)}}. Confirm with your oil company how it wants this handled.

If the driver refuses to sign the sample label, MDG says the lorry is not to be decanted. Contact the supply location or the sales officer {{2.3}}.

## An example

This is an illustration. The outlet and the figures are made up. Your invoice and your table give the real ones.

A tanker brings petrol in three compartments. The invoice shows a density at 15°C of 743.0 kg/m³. You sample each compartment, write down the hydrometer reading and the temperature, and use the ASTM table to get the 15°C figure. Suppose the table gives these figures:

| Compartment | 15°C figure from the table | Difference from the invoice (743.0) | Within ±3.0? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 744.5 | +1.5 | Yes |
| 2 | 745.0 | +2.0 | Yes |
| 3 | 746.5 | +3.5 | No |

Compartments 1 and 2 meet the check. Compartment 3 is 0.5 beyond the limit. You record all three readings {{1.2(viii)}}. If you doubt the quality of compartment 3, the route MDG gives is to contact the supply location or the area field officer for instructions {{1.2(ix)}}.

> **Note:** the example starts from the 15°C figures on purpose. Never convert by eye. Take the observed density and temperature to the ASTM table.

## How to calculate density of petrol at 15°C

MDG gives no formula, and you do not need one. Read the density on a hydrometer and the temperature on a thermometer, look both up in the ASTM table and read off the 15°C density. MDG names Table 53B for this {{1.2(iv)}}. It also requires calibrated hydrometers and thermometers and the ASTM table, all in working order {{1.5(vi)}}.

We do not print a conversion here. Use the table, or the [15°C density calculator](@tools-density-at-15), which converts a reading and a temperature for you.

## Why the invoice figure matters later

Until the tanker is decanted, the reference density for any sample is the density in the dispatch document {{2.10}}. The supply location writes that density at 15°C on the invoice {{3.1(h)}}. After decanting, the reference becomes the mixed-tank density you note once 50 litres have gone through the pump {{1.2(xiv)}} {{2.10}}. See [the reference density rule](@density-reference-10am).

The label on a tank lorry retention sample carries two density lines: the density as recorded in the invoice, and the density of the sample drawn from the lorry {{annexure 3}}.
