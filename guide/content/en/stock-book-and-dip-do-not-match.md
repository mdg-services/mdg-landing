## What to re-check, in this order

The order is general practice, not an MDG rule. Each row is an entry MDG asks for.

| Step | Re-check | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The morning pump meter readings, and the product dip and water dip of each tank, taken separately | {{1.3}} {{1.4.1(b)}} |
| 2 | Every load entered after decanting. MDG says "appropriate entries" and lists none | {{1.2(xiii)}} |
| 3 | Short-dip make-up litres: dip and quantity filled, chamber by chamber, on the back of the invoice copies | {{1.2(xi)}} |
| 4 | Litres used to test the nozzles, written in the stock register | {{1.4.2(d)}} |
| 5 | The allowances: 4% of tank stock, handling loss on litres sold (with evaporation for petrol), shrinkage or TVA only where they apply | {{5.1.11}} |
| 6 | The Annexure 9 comparison: opening dip plus receipts, less the shrinkage volume where it applies, less meter sales, against the closing dip, within ±4% of the dip | {{annexure 9}} |

Steps 5 and 6 are explained on [stock variation](@stock-variation), [four percent of what](@stock-four-percent-of-what) and the [calculator](@tools-stock-variation). The entries in steps 1 to 4 are on the [daily stock register](@stock-daily-register) page.

## If the gap is still there

What follows depends on whether the tank holds more or less than the books {{5.1.11}}: see [positive vs negative](@stock-positive-negative).

## What MDG does not say

- **How make-up litres enter the stock register.** It asks only for the invoice record {{1.2(xi)}}.
- **The order of checking.** The table's order is ours.

## An example

For example (a made-up outlet, ignoring the allowances): the opening dip is 20.000 KL, the entered receipts are 12.000 KL and meter sales are 25.000 KL, so the book closing stock is 7.000 KL. The closing dip reads 13.000 KL, a gap of 6.000 KL against a 4% band of 0.520 KL. Step 2 finds a 6.000 KL compartment that was never entered. With receipts at 18.000 KL, the book closing stock is 13.000 KL, matching the dip {{annexure 9}}.
