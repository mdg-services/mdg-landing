## What stock variation is

Stock variation is the gap between the fuel your books say a tank holds and the fuel the dip shows. In practice, the two seldom match exactly.

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the rules the oil companies set for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. MDG 2024 lets a normal gap pass and says when a gap becomes an irregularity {{5.1.11}}.

The gap is judged only after the allowances below are counted {{5.1.11}}. Annexure 9 shows the book-side method: opening dip, plus receipts, minus the shrinkage allowance where it applies, minus sales as per the meter, then compare the result with the closing dip {{annexure 9}}. The [shrinkage and TVA page](@stock-shrinkage-tva) walks through it with illustration figures.

This rule covers MS and HSD. Kerosene dealers have a separate rule, mentioned in the questions below and on the [kerosene dealers page](@sko-dealers).

## The allowances you can count

| Allowance | Figure | Counted |
|---|---|---|
| Normal operating variation | 4% of tank stock | Yes {{5.1.11}} |
| Petrol (MS): evaporation and handling loss | 0.75% of the litres sold, up to a 600 KL annual average; 0.60% on litres beyond that | Yes {{5.1.11}} |
| Diesel (HSD): handling loss | 0.25% of the litres sold, up to a 600 KL annual average; 0.20% on litres beyond that | Yes {{5.1.11}} |
| Shrinkage allowance and temperature variation allowance (TVA), MS and HSD | A rupee amount per KL, which Annexures 8 and 10 turn into a percentage of the net price | Only where and when it applies {{5.1.11}} |

The 4% is not the whole allowance. MDG asks for it and the other factors to be counted together {{5.1.11}}. In the Annexure 9 method, the 4% is worked on the closing dip stock {{annexure 9}}.

### How the 600 KL line works

The handling loss is not one flat rate. The higher rate covers sales "up to an annual average of 600 KLs", and the lower rate covers the quantity above it {{5.1.11}}. Read plainly, the higher rate applies to the first 600 KL and the lower rate only to the part above. Illustration, with made-up quantities:

| Quantity sold in the illustration | MS allowance | HSD allowance |
|---|---|---|
| 500 KL | 500 x 0.75% = 3.75 KL | 500 x 0.25% = 1.25 KL |
| 800 KL | 600 x 0.75% + 200 x 0.60% = 4.5 + 1.2 = 5.7 KL | 600 x 0.25% + 200 x 0.20% = 1.5 + 0.4 = 1.9 KL |

This table only shows how the two rates split. MDG prints no worked sum for the handling loss. It does not say over what period the 600 KL is counted, how the annual average is arrived at, or how the allowance is spread over the days an inspector examines. Ask your oil company how its officer works it out.

## When the gap is beyond the limit

What happens depends on the direction of the gap {{5.1.11}}. MDG does not define "positive" and "negative". In the trade, positive means the tank holds more than the books, and negative means it holds less.

| Your question | Positive (more than the books) | Negative (less than the books) |
|---|---|---|
| Do my sales stop? | Yes, for all products, at once | No. Sales and supplies to the outlet continue during the investigation |
| Are samples taken? | Yes, and they go to the lab | Yes, and they are tested |
| What does the oil company do next? | Looks into the reasons for the gap | Asks the dealer to explain |
| When does it get worse? | A failed sample is handled as adulteration. If the sample passes but another irregularity, such as an unauthorised purchase, is established, the action is for that irregularity | Supply stops at once if the oil company does not find the explanation satisfactory |

The [positive vs negative page](@stock-positive-negative) walks through each case.

> **Note:** a positive variation beyond the limit stops sales of all products at once, before any lab result comes back {{5.1.11}}.

## What it costs

These actions are the minimum MDG sets. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination, in any instance {{8.5.1}}.

| What is found | Class | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Variation beyond the limit, sample passes, nothing else established | Major {{8.3(iv)}} | 1st instance: 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies. 2nd: 30 days. 3rd: termination of the dealership |
| Positive variation, sample fails | Adulteration, critical {{8.2}} | Termination at the first instance |
| Sample passes but an unauthorised purchase is established | Unauthorised purchase, critical {{5.1.6}} {{8.2}} | Termination at the first instance |

No action is taken until the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}. To decide whether a case is the first, second or third instance, the oil company looks at the five years before the date of the irregularity, and that count includes irregularities under the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}. After an inspection, a show cause notice is to be issued within 30 days, or within 30 days of the test results where samples were drawn {{8.5.6}}. The [show cause notice page](@inspection-show-cause-notice) has the timeline. For a notice about your own outlet, speak to an advocate or your oil company.

## Keep the records the check is built from

The reconciliation is only as good as the entries behind it. The records MDG names for this check are:

- The daily stock register that your oil company supplies, covering every petroleum product you sell {{1.3(ii)}}.
- A morning record of the pump meter readings and of each tank's product and water dips {{1.3}}.
- The quantity used for testing the nozzles, written in the stock register {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- Entries in the stock register or on the stock display board after each tanker is decanted {{1.2(xiii)}}.
- The opening stock of the day, shown on display {{1.3(i)}}. Not displaying it is a minor irregularity {{5.1.17}} {{8.4}}.

Keep the oil company's daily sales register and the density register for this financial year and the one before {{5.1.12}}. The [daily stock register page](@stock-daily-register) has an illustration layout. To run the sums yourself, see the [stock variation calculator](@tools-stock-variation).

## What MDG leaves to the oil company

- **How the allowances are combined.** The clause lists the 4% and the other factors. The annexures show only the shrinkage and TVA sums. There is no single worked example that puts them all together.
- **The annual average.** MDG gives the 600 KL line but not the period it is counted over, or how the average is worked out for an inspection period.
- **A negative variation.** The clause describes the limit as "up to 4%". It does not say in that sentence whether handling loss, shrinkage and TVA are also counted.
- **Words.** "Positive" and "negative" are not defined.

Where MDG is silent, confirm with your oil company before relying on any figure.

## Same as MDG 2012

MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022), the older guideline replaced on 24.10.2024, has the same clause, the same figures and the same Annexures 8 to 10 {{2012:5.1.11}}. The action for a variation beyond the limit with a passing sample (15 days, 30 days, termination) is also the same {{2012:8.3}}. MDG 2024 is the current version.
