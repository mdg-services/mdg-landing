## What stock variation is

In practice, the fuel in a tank seldom matches exactly what your books say should be there. That gap is stock variation. MDG 2024 does not ban a gap. It allows a normal one, and it says where the gap becomes an irregularity {{5.1.11}}.

The clause asks for a stock reconciliation, with the variation worked out only after the allowances below are taken into account {{5.1.11}}. Annexure 9 shows the method: start from the opening dip, add the receipts, take off the shrinkage allowance where it applies, take off the sales as per meter, and compare that book figure with the closing dip {{annexure 9}}. The step-by-step is on the [shrinkage and TVA page](@stock-shrinkage-tva).

This clause covers MS and HSD. Kerosene dealers have their own rule, mentioned in the questions below.

## The allowances MDG lets you count

| Allowance | Figure | Counted |
|---|---|---|
| Normal operating variation | 4% of tank stock | Yes {{5.1.11}} |
| Evaporation and handling loss, MS | 0.75% of the quantity sold, up to an annual average of 600 KL; 0.60% on the quantity beyond 600 KL | Yes {{5.1.11}} |
| Handling loss, HSD | 0.25% of the quantity sold, up to an annual average of 600 KL; 0.20% on the quantity beyond 600 KL | Yes {{5.1.11}} |
| Shrinkage allowance and temperature variation allowance (TVA), MS and HSD | An allowance in rupees per KL, turned into a percentage of the net price in Annexures 8 and 10 | Only where and when it applies {{5.1.11}} |

The 4% is not the whole allowance. MDG asks for the operating variation and the factors in the table to be taken into account together {{5.1.11}}.

MDG says "4% of tank stock". Annexure 9 works the 4% out on the closing dip stock {{annexure 9}}.

### How the 600 KL line works

The handling loss is not one flat percentage. The clause gives the higher rate on the quantity sold "up to an annual average of 600 KLs" and the lower rate on the additional quantity beyond it {{5.1.11}}. Read plainly, the higher rate applies to the first 600 KL and the lower rate only to the part above it. For example:

| Quantity sold in the example | MS allowance | HSD allowance |
|---|---|---|
| 500 KL | 500 x 0.75% = 3.75 KL | 500 x 0.25% = 1.25 KL |
| 800 KL | 600 x 0.75% + 200 x 0.60% = 4.5 + 1.2 = 5.7 KL | 600 x 0.25% + 200 x 0.20% = 1.5 + 0.4 = 1.9 KL |

This table shows only how the two rates split. MDG prints no worked sum for the handling loss. It does not say over what period the 600 KL is counted, how the annual average is arrived at, or how the allowance is spread over the days an inspector examines. Ask your oil company how its officer works it out.

## When the gap is beyond the limit

What MDG does depends on the direction of the gap {{5.1.11}}. MDG does not define "positive" and "negative". In the trade, positive means the tank holds more than the books, and negative means it holds less.

| | Positive (more than the books) | Negative (less than the books) |
|---|---|---|
| Samples | Drawn and sent to the lab | Drawn and sent for testing |
| Sales | Sales and supplies of all products suspended at once | Sales and supplies to the outlet continue during the investigation |
| Next step | A study to find the reasons | The dealer's explanation is called |
| If it goes badly | Sample fails: action as for adulteration. Sample passes but another irregularity such as unauthorised purchase is established: action for that irregularity | Supply is suspended immediately if the explanation is not found satisfactory |

The [positive vs negative page](@stock-positive-negative) walks through each case.

> **Note:** a positive variation beyond the limit stops sales of all products at once, before any lab result comes back {{5.1.11}}.

## What it costs

| What is found | Class | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Variation beyond the limit, sample passes, nothing else established | Major {{8.3(iv)}} | 1st instance: 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies. 2nd: 30 days. 3rd: termination of the dealership |
| Positive variation, sample fails | Adulteration, critical {{8.2}} | Termination at the first instance |
| Sample passes but an unauthorised purchase is established | Unauthorised purchase, critical {{5.1.6}} {{8.2}} | Termination at the first instance |

When an irregularity is established, the previous five years are examined to decide whether it is the first, second or third instance. Irregularities under the previous MDG within those five years count as instances {{8.5.5}}. No action is taken until the irregularity is established {{8.5.2}}. The actions in MDG are the minimum, and the oil company can take higher action {{8.5.1}}. After an inspection a show cause notice follows within 30 days, or within 30 days of the test results where samples were drawn {{8.5.6}}. See the [show cause notice page](@inspection-show-cause-notice) for the timeline. For a notice about your own outlet, speak to your oil company or a lawyer.

## Keep the records the check is built from

The reconciliation is only as good as the entries behind it. MDG asks for:

- A daily stock register provided by the company, for all petroleum products {{1.3}}.
- A daily record of pump meter readings and of the product and water dips of each tank, taken in the morning {{1.3}}.
- The quantity used for testing the nozzles, recorded in the stock register {{1.4.2(d)}}.
- Entries in the stock register and on the stock display board after each tanker is decanted {{1.2(xiii)}}.
- The opening stock of the day on display {{1.3(i)}}. Not displaying it is a minor irregularity {{5.1.17}} {{8.4}}.

Keep the daily sales register and the density register for the current and previous financial year {{5.1.12}}. The [daily stock register page](@stock-daily-register) has an example layout. To run the sums yourself, see the [stock variation calculator](@tools-stock-variation).

## What MDG leaves to the oil company

- **How the allowances are combined.** The clause lists the 4% and the other factors. The annexures show only the shrinkage and TVA sums. There is no single worked example that puts them all together.
- **The annual average.** MDG gives the 600 KL line but not the period it is counted over, or how the average is worked out for an inspection period.
- **A negative variation.** The clause words the limit as "up to 4%". It does not say in that sentence whether handling loss, shrinkage and TVA are also counted.
- **Words.** "Positive" and "negative" are not defined.

Where the text is unclear, confirm with your oil company before relying on any figure.

## Same as MDG 2012

MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) has the same clause, the same figures and the same Annexures 8 to 10 {{2012:5.1.11}}. The action for a variation beyond the limit with a passing sample (15 days, 30 days, termination) is also the same {{2012:8.3}}. MDG 2024 is the current version.
