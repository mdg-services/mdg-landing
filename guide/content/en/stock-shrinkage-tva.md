## Two rupee allowances, one method

Both are rupees per KL. MDG turns each into a percentage of the net price and takes that share of your receipts off book stock before comparing it with the dip, only where and when the allowance applies {{5.1.11}}.

| | Shrinkage allowance | TVA (temperature variation allowance) |
|---|---|---|
| Shown as | Annexure 8: a line in the price build-up | Annexure 10: a credit on each invoice |
| Applied to | All of that product received | Only litres on which the invoice allowed TVA |
| Revised | MDG does not say | Generally every quarter |

All of this is from {{annexure 8}} and {{annexure 10}}.

## From rupees to a percentage

Divide the allowance per KL by the net price per KL. The net price leaves out commission and taxes, and for shrinkage the allowance itself {{annexure 8}} {{annexure 10}}. For example, with made-up figures:

- **Shrinkage:** Rs 300 on Rs 60,000 per KL is 0.50%, so 100 KL received takes 500 litres off book stock.
- **TVA:** Rs 165 on Rs 55,000 per KL is 0.30%, applied to the litres where the invoice allowed TVA.

> **Note:** Annexures 8 and 10 print sample figures dated 1998 (shrinkage from 1.4.98, TVA April to June 1998), not current rates. Ask your oil company which allowance applies to you.

## Annexure 9: using the percentage

In our words, the steps are below, and TVA follows the same ones {{annexure 9}} {{annexure 10}}. The example is made up: MS with shrinkage at 0.50%.

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| 1. Start with the opening dip stock | 18.000 KL |
| 2. Add the receipts | 90.000 KL |
| 3. Take off the shrinkage (or TVA) volume: the percentage times the receipts (for TVA, only the receipts it was allowed on) | 0.50% of 90.000 = 0.450 KL |
| 4. Take off the sales as per meter. That leaves the closing stock in the books | Sales 100.200 KL, so 18.000 + 90.000 - 0.450 - 100.200 = 7.350 KL |
| 5. Compare it with the closing dip: the book figure should fall within plus or minus 4% of the dip figure | Dip 7.200 KL, so the range is 6.912 to 7.488 KL: inside |

The example leaves out handling loss, because Annexure 9 has no step for it {{5.1.11}}. Ask your oil company how its officer adds it. See also [stock variation](@stock-variation), the [calculator](@tools-stock-variation) and [positive vs negative](@stock-positive-negative).

## What MDG does not say

- **Where the shrinkage figure appears.** TVA is on the invoice, but for shrinkage the annexure shows only a price build-up. Ask your oil company.
- **Which annexure is meant.** Annexure 9 points to "Annexure I" for the percentage. Annexure 1 is the density record, and the shrinkage method is in Annexure 8 {{annexure 9}}.
