## The short version

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the rules the oil companies set for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. Under them, both allowances are set as money, in rupees per KL {{annexure 8}} {{annexure 10}}. MDG asks one question of that money: how many litres is it worth? The answer is a percentage, and MDG takes that share of your receipts off the book stock before the book is compared with the dip. That is how shrinkage and temperature losses are taken into account {{5.1.11}}.

Think of a bill with a rupee credit on it. To know what the credit is worth in fuel, you divide it by the price of a KL.

They count only for the outlets and periods to which the allowance applies {{5.1.11}}. The [stock variation page](@stock-variation) has the other allowances that make up the limit.

## What the two allowances are

| | Shrinkage allowance | TVA |
|---|---|---|
| Worked out in | Annexure 8 | Annexure 10 |
| Shown as | A line in the price build-up | A credit on each invoice, which reduces the invoiced total |
| Applied to | All of that product received over the inspected period | Only the quantity received in that period on which the oil company allowed TVA in the invoices |
| Set for | Each product separately | Each product separately, and a product can have none |
| Revised | MDG does not say | Generally every quarter, based on average temperature and similar factors |

All of this is from {{annexure 8}} and {{annexure 10}}. MDG does not define shrinkage or temperature variation beyond their names. General practice, not an MDG rule: fuel takes up a little more room when warm and a little less when cold, which is why density is quoted at 15°C.

## From rupees to a percentage

Annexure 8 shows the method for shrinkage: divide the allowance per KL by the net price per KL. The net price leaves out commission, taxes and the allowance itself {{annexure 8}}.

Illustration, with made-up figures that are not a real price or rate: if the net price of MS is Rs 60,000 per KL and the shrinkage allowance is Rs 300 per KL, the share is 300 divided by 60,000, which is 0.50%.

The annexure then says to apply that percentage to all of the product received over the inspected period {{annexure 8}}. At 0.50%, 100 KL of MS received gives 0.5 KL, which is 500 litres.

> **Note:** the sample figures printed in Annexures 8 and 10 are dated 1998. The shrinkage sample runs from 1.4.98, and the TVA sample covers April to June 1998. They are not current allowances, and the figures on this page are made up. Ask your oil company for the allowance that applies to your outlet.

## TVA: the same idea

Annexure 10 repeats the method for TVA {{annexure 10}}. The oil company gives TVA as a credit on each invoice. To find the volume it stands for, divide the TVA per KL by the net price per KL. The net price leaves out commission and taxes.

Illustration, with made-up figures: TVA of Rs 165 per KL on a net price of Rs 55,000 per KL is 165 divided by 55,000, which is 0.30%.

The notes to the annexure add three points {{annexure 10}}:

1. Use the percentage only on litres actually received in the inspected period where the oil company allowed TVA on the invoice.
2. TVA is generally revised each quarter.
3. The calculation steps are the same as in Annexure 9.

## Annexure 9: using the percentage

Annexure 9 sets out the stock check. In our words, the steps are {{annexure 9}}:

1. Start with the stock shown by the opening dip.
2. Add the receipts.
3. Take off the shrinkage (or TVA) volume, which is the percentage times the receipts.
4. Take off the sales as per the meter. What is left is the closing stock as the books show it.
5. Compare it with the closing stock as the dip shows it. The book figure should fall within plus or minus 4% of the dip figure.

Where the annexure says how to get the shrinkage percentage, it points to "Annexure I" {{annexure 9}}. Annexure 1 is the density record, and the percentage method is shown in Annexure 8. MDG does not say which one is meant, so ask your oil company.

### Example 1 (illustration): MS with shrinkage at 0.50%

All figures are made up. The method follows the annexure.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Opening dip stock | 18.000 KL |
| Receipts | 90.000 KL |
| Shrinkage volume, 0.50% of 90.000 KL | 0.450 KL |
| After shrinkage, 18.000 + 90.000 - 0.450 | 107.550 KL |
| Sales as per meter | 100.200 KL |
| Closing stock in the books, 107.550 - 100.200 | 7.350 KL |
| Closing stock by the dip | 7.200 KL |
| 4% of the dip figure | 0.288 KL, so the range is 6.912 to 7.488 KL |
| Result | The book figure of 7.350 KL is inside the range |

Now take the same sums with a closing dip of 6.600 KL. Four per cent of the dip figure is 0.264 KL, so the range is 6.336 to 6.864 KL. The book figure of 7.350 KL is outside the range. The book is 0.750 KL (750 litres) above the dip, so the tank holds less than the books. In this simple check, that is a negative variation beyond the 4%.

### Example 2 (illustration): HSD with TVA at 0.30%

All 70 KL received carried TVA on the invoices. All figures are made up.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Opening dip stock | 22.000 KL |
| Receipts | 70.000 KL |
| TVA volume, 0.30% of 70.000 KL | 0.210 KL |
| After TVA, 22.000 + 70.000 - 0.210 | 91.790 KL |
| Sales as per meter | 86.500 KL |
| Closing stock in the books, 91.790 - 86.500 | 5.290 KL |
| Closing stock by the dip | 5.350 KL |
| 4% of the dip figure | 0.214 KL, so the range is 5.136 to 5.564 KL |
| Result | The book figure of 5.290 KL is inside the range |

The dip is 0.060 KL (60 litres) above the book. That is a small positive variation, inside the range.

If only 50 KL of the 70 KL had carried TVA on the invoice, the TVA volume would be 0.30% of 50 KL, which is 0.150 KL, because the percentage applies only to the quantity on which TVA was allowed {{annexure 10}}.

Both examples show the annexure's method only. They leave out the evaporation and handling loss from clause 5.1.11, because Annexure 9 has no step for it {{5.1.11}}. What happens when the result is outside the range is on the [positive vs negative page](@stock-positive-negative). The [stock variation calculator](@tools-stock-variation) runs these sums for you.

## What MDG does not say

- **Current rates.** The annexures carry sample figures dated 1998, not today's allowances.
- **Where to find the shrinkage allowance.** TVA is on the invoice. For shrinkage, the annexure shows only the price build-up. Ask your oil company where the figure appears for your outlet.
- **Both at once.** MDG does not say whether shrinkage and TVA can apply to the same product at the same time.
- **The rest of the limit.** The 4% test in Annexure 9 does not show the evaporation and handling allowances. Ask your oil company how its officer adds them.

## Same as MDG 2012

Annexures 8, 9 and 10 in MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022), the older guideline replaced on 24.10.2024, carry the same figures and the same method {{2012:5.1.11}}.
