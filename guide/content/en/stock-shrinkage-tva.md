## The short version

Both allowances are set as money, in rupees per KL {{annexure 8}} {{annexure 10}}. MDG asks one question of that money: how many litres is it worth? The answer is a percentage, and MDG takes that share of your receipts off the book stock before the book is compared with the dip. That is how shrinkage and temperature losses are taken into account {{5.1.11}}.

Think of a bill with a rupee credit on it. To know what the credit is worth in fuel, you divide it by the price of a KL.

They count only in the cases and at the locations where, and for the time when, the allowance applies {{5.1.11}}. The [stock variation page](@stock-variation) has the other allowances that make up the limit.

## What the two allowances are

| | Shrinkage allowance | TVA |
|---|---|---|
| Worked out in | Annexure 8 | Annexure 10 |
| Shown as | A line in the price build-up | A credit in each invoice, deducted from the total invoiced amount |
| Applied to | The total receipts of the product during the period under inspection | Only the quantity received in that period on which the oil company allowed TVA in the invoices |
| Revised | MDG does not say | Generally every quarter, based on average temperature and similar factors |
| In MDG's illustration | MS and HSD | MS nil; HSD only |

All of this is from {{annexure 8}} and {{annexure 10}}. MDG does not define shrinkage or temperature variation beyond their names. General practice, not an MDG rule: fuel takes up a little more room when warm and a little less when cold, which is why density is quoted at 15°C.

## Annexure 8: from rupees to a percentage

The annexure takes the allowance in rupees per KL and divides it by the net price per KL. The net price leaves out commission, taxes and the allowance itself {{annexure 8}}.

| Rupees per KL | MS | HSD |
|---|---|---|
| Ex-storage point | 21,673.14 | 9,202.83 |
| Road freight | 56.34 | 78.25 |
| Toll charges | 5.00 | 5.00 |
| **Net price** | **21,734.48** | **9,286.08** |
| Shrinkage allowance | 135.92 | 36.61 |
| **Allowance as a share of net price** | **0.63%** | **0.39%** |

For MS: 135.92 divided by 21,734.48 is 0.63%. For HSD: 36.61 divided by 9,286.08 is 0.39%. The annexure prints the percentages to two decimal places.

The annexure says this percentage is applied to the total receipts of the product during the period under inspection {{annexure 8}}. At 0.63%, 100 KL of MS received gives 0.63 KL, which is 630 litres.

> **Note:** the rupee figures in Annexures 8 and 10 are from 1998. Annexure 8 shows rates effective from 1.4.98, and Annexure 10 shows April to June 1998. They are part of the illustration, not current allowances. Ask your oil company for the figures that apply to your outlet.

## Annexure 10: the same idea for TVA

| Rupees per KL | HSD |
|---|---|
| Ex-storage point | 8,792.29 |
| Road freight | 18.25 |
| State surcharge | 54.55 |
| **Net price** | **8,865.09** |
| TVA | 38.40 |
| **TVA as a share of net price** | **0.43%** |

38.40 divided by 8,865.09 is 0.43%. The annexure leaves octroi, toll tax, sales tax and commission out of the net price. TVA for MS is nil in this illustration {{annexure 10}}.

The notes to the annexure add three points {{annexure 10}}:

1. The percentage is applied only to the quantity actually received during the period under inspection for which the oil company allowed TVA in the invoices.
2. TVA is generally revised every quarter.
3. The calculation method is the same as Annexure 9.

## Annexure 9: using the percentage

Annexure 9 sets out the stock check as a short list of steps {{annexure 9}}:

| Step | What it is |
|---|---|
| A | Stock as per the opening dip |
| B | Plus the receipts |
| C | Less the shrinkage (or TVA) volume: the percentage times the receipts |
| D | A + B - C |
| E | Less the sales as per meter |
| F | Closing stock as per book: D - E |
| G | Closing stock as per the dip |
| Test | F compared with G, allowing plus or minus 4% of G |

The line for C says the percentage is "arrived at as per Annexure I". Annexure 1 is the density record. The percentage is worked out in Annexure 8, so the reference looks like a slip for Annexure 8. If in doubt, ask your oil company.

### Example 1: MS with shrinkage at 0.63%

The figures are made up. The method follows the annexure.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| A, opening dip stock | 20.000 KL |
| B, receipts | 100.000 KL |
| C, 0.63% of 100.000 KL | 0.630 KL |
| D, A + B - C | 119.370 KL |
| E, sales as per meter | 112.500 KL |
| F, closing stock as per book (D - E) | 6.870 KL |
| G, closing stock as per dip | 6.700 KL |
| 4% of G | 0.268 KL, so the range is 6.432 to 6.968 KL |
| Result | F is 6.870 KL, inside the range |

Now take the same sums with a closing dip of 6.050 KL. Four per cent of G is 0.242 KL, so the range is 5.808 to 6.292 KL. F is 6.870 KL, outside the range. The book is 0.820 KL (820 litres) above the dip, so the tank holds less than the books. In this simple check, that is a negative variation beyond the 4%.

### Example 2: HSD with TVA at 0.43%

All 80 KL received carried TVA on the invoices.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| A, opening dip stock | 25.000 KL |
| B, receipts | 80.000 KL |
| C, 0.43% of 80.000 KL | 0.344 KL |
| D, A + B - C | 104.656 KL |
| E, sales as per meter | 99.800 KL |
| F, closing stock as per book (D - E) | 4.856 KL |
| G, closing stock as per dip | 4.900 KL |
| 4% of G | 0.196 KL, so the range is 4.704 to 5.096 KL |
| Result | F is 4.856 KL, inside the range |

The dip is 0.044 KL (44 litres) above the book. That is a small positive variation, inside the range.

If only 60 KL of the 80 KL had carried TVA on the invoice, C would be 0.43% of 60 KL, which is 0.258 KL, because the percentage applies only to the quantity on which TVA was allowed {{annexure 10}}.

Both examples show the annexure's method only. They leave out the evaporation and handling loss from clause 5.1.11, because Annexure 9 has no line for it {{5.1.11}}. What happens when the result is outside the range is on the [positive vs negative page](@stock-positive-negative). The [stock variation calculator](@tools-stock-variation) runs these sums for you.

## What MDG does not say

- **Current rates.** The annexures show 1998 figures as illustrations.
- **Where to find the shrinkage allowance.** TVA is on the invoice. For shrinkage, the annexure shows only the price build-up. Ask your oil company where the figure appears for your outlet.
- **Both at once.** MDG does not say whether shrinkage and TVA can apply to the same product at the same time.
- **The rest of the limit.** The 4% test in Annexure 9 does not show the evaporation and handling allowances. Ask your oil company how its officer adds them.

## Same as MDG 2012

Annexures 8, 9 and 10 in MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) carry the same figures and the same method {{2012:5.1.11}}.
