## What a mobile lab is

A mobile lab is a testing unit that visits outlets to check petrol, diesel and lube samples by surprise {{7.1}}. The State Level Coordinator (SLC) draws up the programme of visits and coordinates with the other oil companies, which nominate officers to join the mobile lab's inspections as a joint industry team {{7.2}}.

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for their dealers under the dealership agreement. They say a mobile lab works for the oil industry as a whole, so it carries the logos of all three oil companies, and an individual oil company may also run mobile labs for its own outlets {{7.2}}. Mobile labs are among those allowed to draw samples at your outlet {{2.2.2.2}}.

## What happens at your outlet

1. The mobile lab takes samples of MS and HSD from the nozzles of the dispensing units on your premises, covering every tank {{2.4.4}} {{7.3}}.
2. It runs the clinical tests that apply to mobile labs {{2.4.4}}. These are appearance, colour, density at 15°C and distillation for both fuels, plus kinematic viscosity at 40°C for diesel {{7.4}}.
3. No further action is taken on a passing sample {{2.4.4}}, and the balance of the samples goes back to the outlet {{7.6}}.
4. The outlet is paid for the sample used in testing, and for the retained samples too if a sample fails. Keep the cash receipts {{7.3.1}}.

The limits for these tests are set in clause 7.6, by the Indian Standards for each fuel: IS 2796, IS 17586 and IS 17021 for petrol, and IS 1460 and IS 16531 for diesel {{7.6}}. These standards are revised from time to time, so check the current version of each. For the density figures see [the density range](@density-range). Chapter 7 sets out tests only for MS and HSD, although mobile labs also check lube samples {{7.1}}.

The clinical tests run whatever the density result. Where the product fails in density or in any clinical test, chapter 7 says the oil company is to take action "for established adulteration" {{7.4 Note (i)}}. Chapter 7 does not say how that fits with the chapter 2 testing of reference samples that follows a failure (see below). Confirm with your oil company.

## Your density record counts here too

Mobile lab reports put the density of your tank after the last receipt next to the density the officer observes, with a 3.0 kg/m³ maximum variation between the two {{annexure 12a}} {{annexure 13a}}. The density MDG tells you to record in your [density register](@density-register) after decanting {{1.2(xiv)}} is the natural match for that figure, but the report itself does not name the register.

When the reference density is missing at a mobile lab visit, the lab first runs its clinical tests at the outlet. A passing sample leads the officer to recommend action for the missing record. A failing one means samples go to a stationary lab, and the officer recommends action that includes stopping the sales and supplies of all products at once {{5.1.9}}. See [reference density](@density-reference-10am).

## If a sample fails

1. In the test report (Annexure 12 for MS, Annexure 13 for HSD) the officer notes the meter reading on every dispensing unit and the dip reading in each tank of that product, and advises the dealer to suspend sales of it {{7.6.1(b)}}.
2. That advice is recorded in the report's remarks, and the dealer's signature and seal go on every copy {{7.6.1(c)}}.
3. Samples are drawn as at any inspection: six 1-litre bottles per MS tank and three per HSD tank {{2.4.4}} {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}. They carry the Annexure 11 label {{7.5}}.
4. The three sets travel differently from an ordinary inspection {{2.4.4}}:

| Set | Goes to |
|---|---|
| First set | The dealer, to retain |
| Second set | The oil company, to retain |
| Third set | The oil company, for testing, not straight to the lab |

5. The mobile lab passes the samples to the oil company's representative, preferably within 5 days. It also phones the supply location at once so that the depot sample can be kept {{2.4.4}}.
6. The mobile lab officer tells the SLC straight away about the failure and the action taken {{7.6.1(d)}}. The SLC and the oil company then arrange to collect and test the related reference samples under chapter 2 {{7.6}}.

From there the process is the one in [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed).

### How wide is the suspension?

The text describes the suspension in three different ways:

- the dealer is advised to suspend sales of the product concerned {{7.6.1(b)}}
- the mobile lab suspends sales and supplies when a sample fails in the lab test {{7.4 Note (ii)}}
- on a clinical test failure by a mobile lab, an oil company officer suspends sales and supply of all products, after informing the Territory, Divisional or Regional Manager {{2.5(K)}}

Confirm with your oil company which one it applies to your outlet.

## The test report

The report is made on the Annexure 12 and 12a forms for MS, and the Annexure 13 and 13a forms for HSD {{7.6.1(a)}}. Five copies are made: for the outlet, the oil company's divisional, territory or regional office, the SLC, the oil company's technical or quality control department, and the mobile lab {{7.6.1(a)}}.

Reports are serially numbered. A result that is already entered is never overwritten. An editorial correction strikes through the old value and writes the new one beside it, with name, designation and signature {{7.6.2}}.

## An illustration

For example, a mobile lab arrives at an outlet with two MS tanks and one HSD tank. It draws a nozzle sample from each. Both MS samples pass. The HSD sample fails a clinical test.

- The balance of the MS samples goes back to the outlet {{7.6}}.
- In the Annexure 13 report the officer records the meter reading of every dispensing unit and the dip of the HSD tank, and advises the dealer to suspend HSD sales. The dealer signs all five copies with the outlet's seal {{7.6.1}}.
- Three 1-litre HSD samples are drawn. The dealer keeps one. The other two go to the oil company {{2.4.2}} {{2.4.4}}.
- The officer phones the supply location at once and informs the SLC {{2.4.4}} {{7.6.1(d)}}.

> **Note:** refusing to let a mobile lab draw samples or inspect is a major irregularity {{5.1.8}} {{8.3}}. For a major irregularity MDG sets a 15-day suspension of sales and supplies the first time, a 30-day suspension the second time, and termination the third time {{8.3}}. These are the lowest actions: the oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}, and the irregularity has to be established first {{8.5.2}}. The count of instances runs over five years and includes the previous guideline {{8.5.5}}. The appeal route in MDG is for termination only {{8.9}}.
