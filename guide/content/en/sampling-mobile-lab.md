## What a mobile lab is

A mobile lab is a testing unit that visits outlets to check the quality of MS, HSD and lube samples by surprise {{7.1}}. The State Level Coordinator (SLC) draws up the programme of visits. The SLC also arranges with the other oil companies to nominate officers for joint industry team inspections with the mobile lab {{7.2}}.

A mobile lab works on behalf of the oil industry and carries the logo of all three oil companies. An individual oil company can also run mobile labs for its own outlets {{7.2}}. Mobile labs are among those allowed to draw samples at your outlet {{2.2.2.2}}.

## What happens at your outlet

1. The mobile lab draws nozzle samples of MS and HSD for all the tanks, from the dispensing units on your premises {{2.4.4}} {{7.3}}.
2. It runs the clinical tests that apply to mobile labs on those samples {{2.4.4}}.
3. If a sample passes, no further action is taken on it {{2.4.4}}. The balance of the samples is returned to the outlet {{7.6}}.
4. You are paid for the quantity of sample consumed in testing, and for the retained samples too if a sample fails. Keep the cash receipts {{7.3.1}}.

The tests are:

| Test | MS | HSD |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Yes | Yes |
| Colour (visual) | Yes | Yes |
| Density at 15°C | Yes | Yes |
| Distillation | Yes | Yes |
| Kinematic viscosity at 40°C | No | Yes |

{{7.4}} The limits are in the tables in clause 7.6 {{7.6}}. For the density limits see [the density range](@density-range). Chapter 7 sets out tests only for MS and HSD, although mobile labs also check lube samples {{7.1}}.

The clinical tests are done whatever the density result. If the product fails in density or in any clinical parameter, MDG says the oil company is to take action for established adulteration {{7.4 Note (i)}}. Chapter 7 does not say how this fits with the chapter 2 testing of reference samples that follows a failure (see below). Confirm with your oil company.

## Your density record counts here too

The mobile lab's test report has lines for the dealer's tank density after the last receipt, the density the officer observed, and the variation between them. The report prints a maximum variation of 3.0 kg/m³ {{annexure 12a}} {{annexure 13a}}. The first line matches the after-decanting figure MDG has you record in your [density register](@density-register) {{1.2(xiv)}}. The report does not itself name the register as the source.

If your reference density is not available when the mobile lab inspects, the lab does clinical testing at the outlet. If the sample passes, the officer recommends action for the missing record. If it fails, samples are drawn and sent to a stationary lab, and the officer recommends action including immediate suspension of sales and supplies of all products {{5.1.9}}. See [reference density](@density-reference-10am).

## If a sample fails

1. The mobile lab officer records the meter readings of all dispensing units and the dip readings of the tank(s) of that product in the test report (Annexure 12 or 13). The dealer is advised to suspend sales of that product {{7.6.1(b)}}.
2. That instruction goes in the Remark column. You sign every copy of the report with the outlet's seal {{7.6.1(c)}}.
3. Samples are drawn as for any inspection: six 1-litre samples per MS tank and three per HSD tank {{2.4.4}} {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}. Their label is the one in Annexure 11 {{7.5}}.
4. The sets are handled differently from an ordinary inspection:

| Set | Goes to |
|---|---|
| First set | You, to retain |
| Second set | The oil company, for retention |
| Third set | The oil company, for testing. It does not go directly to the lab |

{{2.4.4}}

5. The mobile lab hands the samples to the oil company's representative, preferably within the next 5 days. It also informs the supply location by telephone straight away, so that the depot sample can be retained {{2.4.4}}.
6. The mobile lab officer informs the SLC immediately about the failure and the action taken {{7.6.1(d)}}. The SLC and the oil company then arrange to collect and test the related reference samples under chapter 2 {{7.6}}.

From there the process is the one in [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed).

### How wide is the suspension?

MDG words this in three ways, and does not reconcile them:

- the dealer is advised to suspend sales of the concerned product {{7.6.1(b)}}
- sales and supplies are suspended by the mobile lab when a sample fails in the lab test {{7.4 Note (ii)}}
- on a clinical test failure by a mobile lab, an oil company officer suspends sales and supply of all products, after informing the Territory, Divisional or Regional Manager {{2.5(K)}}

Confirm with your oil company which one it applies to your outlet.

## The test report

The report for MS follows Annexure 12 and 12a, and the one for HSD follows Annexure 13 and 13a {{7.6.1(a)}}. Five copies go to {{7.6.1(a)}}:

- the retail outlet
- the oil company's Divisional, Territory or Regional office
- the State Level Coordinator
- the oil company's Technical or Quality Control department
- the mobile lab

Reports are serially numbered. No correction is made over a result that has been entered. For an editorial correction, the original value is struck through and the new value written beside it with name, designation and signature {{7.6.2}}.

## A worked example

For example, a mobile lab arrives at an outlet with two MS tanks and one HSD tank. It draws a nozzle sample from each. Both MS samples pass. The HSD sample fails a clinical test.

- The balance of the MS samples is returned to the outlet {{7.6}}.
- In the Annexure 13 report the officer records the meter reading of every dispensing unit and the dip of the HSD tank, and advises the dealer to suspend HSD sales. The dealer signs all five copies with the outlet's seal {{7.6.1}}.
- Three 1-litre HSD samples are drawn. The dealer keeps one. The other two go to the oil company {{2.4.2}} {{2.4.4}}.
- The officer phones the supply location at once and informs the SLC {{2.4.4}} {{7.6.1(d)}}.

> **Note:** refusing to let a mobile lab draw samples or inspect is a major irregularity: 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies the first time, 30 days the second time, termination the third time {{5.1.8}} {{8.3}}.
