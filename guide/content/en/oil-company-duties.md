## What MDG puts on the oil company

Chapter 3 opens with the company's main duty: to supply product of correct quality and quantity and to give excellent customer service {{chapter 3}}. The duties fall in four places.

| Where | What the company does | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Storage point (depot) | Quality checks, water dips, records, samples, density checks, ethanol and kerosene dye checks | {{3.1(a)}} to {{3.1(h)}} |
| Filling point and lorry | Accepts only calibrated, licensed lorries, checks for leaks, no mixed loads, separate lorries for PDS kerosene | {{3.1(i)}} to {{3.1(l)}} |
| On the road | Seals the lorry, keeps GPS working, lets the dealer see the filling (ex-MI supplies) | {{3.2}} |
| For the dealer | Free sample kit, training, repair of its own equipment | {{2.6(B)}} {{3.3}} {{4.2(a)}} |

## At the depot: quality checks

| Check | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Quality control | Checks as laid down in the Industry Quality Control Manual (IQCM) at every stage of handling, so the product released meets the standard specifications | {{3.1(a)}} |
| Water | Water dips of storage tanks, with remedial measures where needed | {{3.1(b)}} |
| Records | Quality checks recorded in the standard formats of the IQCM | {{3.1(c)}} |
| Density | Appearance, colour and density from the tank lorry filling point checked every two hours and at every tank switch-over. Records kept | {{3.1(h)}} |
| Samples | Drawn every morning and at each switch-over, kept at least 30 days, each with a number or batch number that also appears in the delivery documents. This includes Refinery Dispatch Units supplying outlets directly | {{3.1(e)}} |
| Daily draw | At least 4 x 1 litre of MS and 2 x 1 litre of HSD from the filling point. Drawn again after a tank switch-over, once the earlier product has been flushed from the pipeline | {{2.2.1(i)}} {{2.2.1(ii)}} |
| Ethanol | Blended as per the government guidelines and within the prescribed limits. Presence checked before release | {{3.1(f)}} |
| Kerosene | Kerosene (SKO) released only after visual, appearance, colour, density and flash point checks. Kerosene released under the PDS is dyed blue at the prescribed dosage | {{3.1(d)}} {{3.1(g)}} |

## At the filling point and on the lorry

| Duty | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Papers | Only tank lorries with a valid calibration certificate from Weights & Measures and an explosives licence from PESO are accepted for filling | {{3.1(i)}} |
| Leaks | Lorry fitness checked; leaks checked after filling and before dispatch | {{3.1(j)}} |
| Mixed loads | Not allowed in lorries with a common manifold | {{3.1(j)}} |
| Kerosene lorries | PDS kerosene goes in a separate lorry, never one used for MS or HSD. Those lorries are painted blue | {{3.1(l)}} |
| Seals | Lorry sealed or locked as per industry practice. Seal or lock numbers recorded on the invoice | {{3.2}} |
| GPS | The supply location keeps GPS running smoothly. No lorry is filled if VTS is not installed or not working in the prescribed manner | {{3.2}} |
| Deviation alerts | Reports are system-generated. Alerts reach the officers monitoring VTS. Officers at the supply location and the dealer can both see them. Time and route deviations must be investigated at once | {{3.2}} |
| Malpractice | Action in line with the Industry Transport Discipline Guidelines | {{3.1(k)}} |

For what the dealer then does with the lorry, see [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker) and [tank lorry VTS](@tanker-vts).

### Watching the filling

For ex-MI supplies, the dealer or a representative may witness the filling of the lorry at the supply location and may travel with it to the outlet. The outlet should also monitor the lorry that carries its supply {{3.2}}. MDG does not spell out "ex-MI". General knowledge, not MDG text: MI usually means marketing installation, the oil company's depot or terminal. Ask your oil company which of your supplies this covers.

## Free kit for samples

- The company must buy and place enough sample containers, random-numbered plastic seals, ISI-marked stainless steel sealing wire and sealing pliers at the outlet, free of cost {{2.6(B)}}.
- When an oil company representative or a mobile lab takes a container from the dealer, they must replace the empty standard container {{2.6(B)}}.
- Sample labels as per Annexure 3 are printed and supplied in sufficient numbers, free of cost {{2.8}}.
- The company may add its own seals or holograms {{2.7}}.
- The dealer must ask the company for this material in time {{2.6(B)}}. MDG gives no number of days.

See [retention samples](@sampling-retention) for what the dealer does with them.

## Training

Oil companies should keep making efforts to train RO dealers and their staff on running the outlet, including safe handling of petroleum products, customer service and courteous behaviour {{3.3}}. The wording asks for effort. It sets no number of sessions.

## A worked example

This is an illustration, not a real case.

A diesel lorry reaches an outlet at 5:00 PM. The dealer reads the invoice and the delivery papers. They show the lorry number, two compartments, the seal numbers, the density at 15°C with the depot tank number, a time of release and a sample number {{1.1}} {{3.1(h)}} {{3.2}} {{2.2.1(iv)}} {{3.1(e)}}. The dealer compares the seal numbers with the seals on the lorry {{1.2(ii)}}. The sample number links the delivery to the sample the depot keeps for at least 30 days {{3.1(e)}}.

If the tank number or the seal numbers were missing from the invoice, MDG does not say what the dealer then does. General practice, not an MDG rule: raise it with the supply location before decanting. Where the dealer is in doubt about the quality of the product, MDG says to contact the supply location or the concerned Area Field Officer for instructions {{1.2(ix)}}.

## What to ask for

- A complete invoice: density at 15°C with the tank number, seal or lock numbers, sample number in the delivery papers, filling point and time of release.
- The VTS link, so you can follow the lorry {{1.5(vii)}}.
- Containers, seals, wire, pliers and labels, before you run out.
- A replacement container each time an oil company representative or a mobile lab takes one.
- Repair of the company's own equipment. See [breakdowns and leaks](@company-breakdowns-leaks).

## What MDG does not say

- **A penalty for the company.** Chapter 8 deals with dealers and kerosene dealers. It has no table for company lapses {{chapter 8}}.
- **How a dealer complains.** General practice, not an MDG rule: write to your controlling office, say what is missing and keep a copy.
- **How much training.** MDG sets no number of sessions {{3.3}}.
- **A deadline for supplying the sample kit.** The dealer asks "in time" {{2.6(B)}}.
