## What MDG puts on the oil company

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. Chapter 3 starts by making the oil company responsible for the quality and quantity of what it supplies, and for the standard of its customer service {{chapter 3}}. A dealer meets those duties in four places.

| Where you meet it | What the oil company does | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| At the depot | Tests quality, takes water dips, keeps records and samples | {{3.1}} |
| On the tank lorry | Seals it, tracks it by GPS, puts the numbers on the invoice | {{3.2}} |
| At your outlet | Supplies the sample kit free, repairs its own equipment | {{2.6(B)}} {{4.2(a)}} |
| For you and your staff | Trains | {{3.3}} |

## Quality checks at the depot

| Topic | What the oil company does | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Product tests | Runs the tests in the oil industry's quality control manual (IQCM) at each step of handling, so that what leaves the depot meets the product specification. Records them on the manual's standard formats | {{3.1(a)}} {{3.1(c)}} |
| Water in storage tanks | Takes water dips and fixes any problem found | {{3.1(b)}} |
| Density at the filling point | Checks appearance, colour and density every two hours and at every tank change, and keeps records | {{3.1(h)}} |
| Ethanol in petrol | Blends within the prescribed limits and per government guidelines. Checks that ethanol is present before release | {{3.1(f)}} |
| Kerosene | Releases kerosene only after visual, appearance, colour, density and flash point checks. Dyes kerosene that goes out under the PDS blue, at the prescribed dosage | {{3.1(d)}} {{3.1(g)}} |

### Samples the depot keeps

- A sample is drawn each morning and at each tank change, and kept for at least 30 days {{3.1(e)}}.
- Each sample gets a number, and that number also appears in the delivery documents {{3.1(e)}}. This lets a delivery be tied to its sample.
- Refinery dispatch units that supply outlets directly do the same {{3.1(e)}}.
- The daily draw is at least four 1-litre samples of MS and two of HSD from the filling point. It is drawn again after a tank change, once the earlier product has cleared the pipeline {{2.2.1(i)}} {{2.2.1(ii)}}.

## On the tank lorry

| Topic | What the oil company does | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Lorry papers | Fills only lorries with a valid calibration certificate and an explosives licence, from the Weights & Measures Department and PESO respectively | {{3.1(i)}} |
| Leaks and loads | Checks the lorry's fitness, checks for leaks after filling and before dispatch, and allows no mixed loads in lorries with a common manifold | {{3.1(j)}} |
| Kerosene lorries | Uses a separate lorry for PDS kerosene, never one used for MS or HSD, painted blue | {{3.1(l)}} |
| Seals | Seals or locks the lorry as industry practice requires and records the seal or lock numbers on the invoice | {{3.2}} |
| GPS | Keeps GPS running. Does not fill a lorry whose VTS is missing or not working as prescribed | {{3.2}} |
| Deviation alerts | The system raises time and route alerts. Officers monitoring VTS get them, and so do the supply location and the dealer. Each deviation must be looked into at once | {{3.2}} |
| Malpractice in lorries | Action under the Industry Transport Discipline Guidelines | {{3.1(k)}} |

For what the dealer does with the lorry on arrival, see [receiving a tanker](@receiving-a-tanker) and [tank lorry VTS](@tanker-vts).

### Watching the filling

For ex-MI supplies, the dealer or a representative may watch the lorry being filled at the supply location and may travel with it to the outlet. The outlet should also follow the movement of the lorry carrying its supply {{3.2}}. MDG does not spell out "ex-MI". General knowledge, not MDG text: MI usually means marketing installation, the oil company's depot or terminal. Ask your oil company which of your supplies this covers.

## Free kit for samples

- The oil company places enough sample containers, numbered plastic seals, ISI-marked stainless steel sealing wire and sealing pliers at the outlet, free of cost {{2.6(B)}}.
- When an oil company representative or a mobile lab takes a container from the dealer, they replace the empty standard container {{2.6(B)}}.
- Sample labels in the Annexure 3 format are printed and supplied in sufficient numbers, free of cost {{2.8}}.
- The oil company may add seals or holograms of its own {{2.7}}.
- The dealer must ask for this material in time {{2.6(B)}}. MDG gives no number of days.

See [retention samples](@sampling-retention) for what the dealer does with them.

## Training

MDG asks oil companies to keep making efforts to train dealers and their staff. The topics it names are running the outlet, handling petroleum products safely, serving customers and behaving courteously {{3.3}}. It asks for effort and sets no number of sessions.

## A worked example

For example, at a made-up outlet (an illustration, not a real case), a diesel lorry arrives at 5:00 PM. The dealer reads the invoice and the delivery papers. They show the lorry number, two compartments, the seal numbers, the density at 15°C with the depot tank number, a time of release and a sample number {{1.1}} {{3.1(h)}} {{3.2}} {{2.2.1(iv)}} {{3.1(e)}}. The dealer compares the seal numbers with the seals on the lorry {{1.2(ii)}}. The sample number links the delivery to the sample the depot keeps for at least 30 days {{3.1(e)}}.

If the tank number or the seal numbers were missing from the invoice, MDG does not say what the dealer then does. General practice, not an MDG rule: raise it with the supply location before decanting. If the dealer is in doubt about the quality of the product, MDG says to contact the supply location or the concerned Area Field Officer for instructions {{1.2(ix)}}.

## What to ask for

- A full invoice: density at 15°C with the tank number, seal or lock numbers, the sample number in the delivery papers, the filling point and the time of release.
- The VTS link, so you can follow the lorry {{1.5(vii)}}.
- Containers, seals, wire, pliers and labels, before you run out.
- A replacement container each time an oil company representative or a mobile lab takes one.
- Repair of the company's own equipment. See [breakdowns and leaks](@company-breakdowns-leaks).

## What MDG does not cover

- **A penalty for the company.** The action chapter deals with dealers and kerosene dealers and has no table for company lapses {{chapter 8}}.
- **How a dealer complains.** General practice, not an MDG rule: write to your controlling office, say what is missing and keep a copy.
- **How much training.** No number of sessions is set {{3.3}}.
- **A deadline for supplying the sample kit.** The dealer asks "in time" {{2.6(B)}}.
