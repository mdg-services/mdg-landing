## What decides the penalty

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the rules the oil companies apply to their dealers under the dealership agreement. Chapter 8 says what the oil company does when an irregularity is found. Two things decide the action: the class of the irregularity, critical, major or minor {{8.1}}, and whether it is the first, second or third instance.

This page sorts the irregularities by topic, in our own words, with the class of each and a link to the page that explains it. The three ladders come first and appear only once.

## The three ladders, lightest first

| Class | First instance | Second instance | Third instance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minor {{8.4}} | Warning-cum-guidance letter | Rs 10,000 per irregularity | Rs 25,000 per irregularity, from the third instance onwards |
| Major, standard ladder {{8.3}} | Sales and supplies suspended for 15 days | Suspended for 30 days | Termination of the dealership |
| Major, normal fuel sold as branded fuel {{8.3}} | Recovery of the differential price since the last inspection | Termination | n/a |
| Major, automation breach {{8.3}} | Rs 1,00,000 | Rs 2,00,000 and 7 days' suspension | Termination |
| Critical {{8.2}} | Termination | n/a | n/a |

> **Note:** these steps and amounts are the minimum. The oil company's competent authority can go further where it sees the need, up to termination at the first instance or any later one, in line with the dealership agreement {{8.5.1}}.

Read every figure with these points:

- **Proof comes first.** The oil company must establish the irregularity before it acts against a dealer {{8.5.2}}.
- **Five-year count.** The oil company looks back five years from the date the new irregularity is established to decide the instance. Irregularities under the previous guideline within those five years count {{8.5.5}}. See [repeat offences and unpaid fines](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).
- **Findings made together.** Irregularities found together at one outlet are each counted as an instance in their own class, and each draws its own action. MDG calls this a compounding effect {{8.5.3}}. Separately, 8.4 says that one or more of the miscellaneous minor items, marked "counted together" below, count as one irregularity when action is decided {{8.4}}. MDG does not say how these two statements fit when such items are found along with other findings. Ask your oil company how it counts your case.
- **Appeal.** A termination arising from MDG can be appealed within 30 days of receiving the order. The appeal in MDG is for termination only {{8.9}}. See [the appeal against termination](@inspection-appeal).

Two minor items are outside the "counted together" group: missing older records, and a missing air facility, telephone or first aid box.

The older guideline, MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, last amended 30.11.2022, replaced on 24.10.2024), ranked a missing clean toilet and short delivery with seals intact as major, each with its own money ladder {{2012:8.3}}. MDG 2024 ranks both as minor {{8.4}}. An amount from the older guideline is not today's penalty. Confirm the current position with your oil company.

## Automation

In an automated outlet, three things are irregular when done without authorisation from the competent authority: running a dispensing unit in manual mode, switching off the automatic tank gauge or leaving it out of action, and deliberately disabling any other part of the automation system {{5.1.16}}. The class is major, on its own automation ladder {{8.3}}. Only an email or a signed letter from a company official counts as authorisation, and the dealer keeps it on record {{5.1.16}}. See [automated outlets](@automation) and [manual mode](@automation-manual-mode).

## Inspections and samples

Refusing a sample or an inspection, obstructing it, or creating circumstances that stop it is a major irregularity on the standard ladder {{5.1.8}} {{8.3}}. See [what happens at an inspection](@inspection).

## Rules that sit beside every penalty

- **Stops come before the penalty.** Some findings halt sales at once while a lab tests the fuel. A missing reference density and a positive stock variation beyond the limit stop all products {{5.1.9}} {{5.1.11}}. A seal, totaliser or fitting finding stops the dispensing unit {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}} {{5.1.4}}. These stops are not the penalty on the ladders.
- **Irregularities MDG does not name.** The competent authority of the oil company acts after an enquiry, following the principles of natural justice {{8.5.4}}.
- **Notice and reply.** A show cause notice follows within 30 days of the inspection, or of the test results if samples were drawn {{8.5.6}}. The dealer has 10 days from the date of issue to reply {{8.5.7}}. The oil company then passes a reasoned speaking order, preferably within 45 days of receiving the reply {{8.5.8}}. See [the notice process](@inspection-show-cause-notice).
- **Hearing before termination for a critical irregularity.** Before termination is recommended or approved, the dealership's signatories or their nominees are heard in person {{8.6}}.
- **Government checks count too.** If a central or state government authority establishes a malpractice or irregularity during its own check, MDG takes it as one under the guideline, and the oil company acts once it receives that authority's advice {{8.7}}.
- **Unpaid fines.** Where the action is a fine alone, the fine is due within 30 days of the company's notice. If it stays unpaid, sales and supplies are suspended from the 31st day for 15 days, then for another 15 days, and after that the dealership is terminated {{8.7}}. See [the unpaid-fine timeline](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

## An illustration

For example, an inspector visits a made-up outlet on 10 March 2026. It has had no irregularity in the last five years. The inspector finds three things on the same day.

| Finding | Class | Instance | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| No density record for the time of the visit {{5.1.9}} | Major | First | Sales and supplies of all products stop and samples go to the lab. If they pass: 15 days' suspension {{8.3}}. If they fail: the action for adulteration, termination {{8.2}}. |
| Toilet not clean {{5.1.14(b)}} | Minor | First | Warning-cum-guidance letter {{8.4}} |
| Selling prices not displayed {{5.1.15}} | Minor | First | The same letter. Both minor items are in the group that 8.4 counts as one irregularity. |

The density finding is major and the other two are minor, so each class draws its own action {{8.5.3}}. If the samples pass, the outlet faces 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies and a warning-cum-guidance letter. Samples were drawn, so the show cause notice is due within 30 days of the test results {{8.5.6}}.

One question stays open. MDG 8.4 counts the toilet and the price board as one irregularity, while 8.5.3 counts every irregularity found at the same time as its own instance. MDG does not say how the two fit together if either item is found again later, so ask your oil company how it would count it.

Had the toilet and the price board been the only findings, the wording of 8.4 would count them as one irregularity, and its first-instance action is the warning-cum-guidance letter, which carries no suspension {{8.4}}.
