## The three classes at a glance

MDG 2024 sorts the irregularities of Chapter 5 into three classes: critical, major and minor {{8.1}}. The class decides the penalty. The penalty also depends on whether this is the first, second or third instance in the last five years (see [how repeat offences are counted](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid)).

| Class | First instance | Second instance | Third instance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical {{8.2}} | Termination of the dealership | n/a | n/a |
| Major, most items {{8.3}} | Suspension of sales and supplies for 15 days | Suspension for 30 days | Termination |
| Major, normal fuel sold as branded fuel {{8.3}} | Recovery of the differential price since the last inspection | Termination | n/a |
| Major, automation breach {{8.3}} | Penalty of Rs 1,00,000 | Rs 2,00,000 and suspension for 7 days | Termination |
| Minor {{8.4}} | Warning-cum-guidance letter | Rs 10,000 per irregularity | Rs 25,000 per irregularity, third instance onwards |

> **Note:** these are the minimum actions. The oil company can take higher action, up to termination at the first instance, in line with the dealership agreement {{8.5.1}}.

## Critical irregularities: termination at the first instance

Termination at the first instance is the action for all seven of these {{8.2}}. Each is defined in Chapter 5.

| Irregularity | Defined in | What MDG means |
|---|---|---|
| Adulteration of petrol or diesel | {{5.1.1}} | A foreign substance put in so that the fuel fails the BIS specification, or a lab result outside the permitted test limits against the reference sample. See [what happens when a sample fails](@sampling-failed). |
| Metering unit seals tampered | {{5.1.2(b)}} | A missing seal, a different seal from the one embossed by the Weights & Measures inspector, or a broken sealing wire counts as tampered. The W&M authorities' opinion is final, and action follows even if the delivery was correct or in excess. |
| Totaliser seal tampered or totaliser made non-working | {{5.1.3}} | Includes a seal that allows the reading to be manipulated, deliberately stopping the totaliser, or not reporting a totaliser that has stopped working. |
| Unauthorised fittings in a dispensing unit, or tampering with it | {{5.1.4}} | Any gear, chip or electronic part that can manipulate delivery. Adding, removing, replacing or interfering with any part, including the manufacturer's software, counts as tampering. |
| Unauthorised storage facility or interconnection | {{5.1.5}} | A storage facility inside the licensed area that the oil company has not approved, a storage facility outside the premises connected to the outlet, or an unauthorised connection between tanks inside the premises. |
| Unauthorised purchase or sale | {{5.1.6}} | Buying products from a source the oil company has not authorised, or selling petrol or diesel other than through the outlet's own dispensing units. |
| Tank lorry with unauthorised product | {{5.1.7}} | A tank lorry carrying unauthorised product found decanting into the outlet's tanks. |

For seal and fitting findings, sales from that dispensing unit are suspended, the unit is sealed, and samples of all products go to the lab {{5.1.2(b)}} {{5.1.3}} {{5.1.4}}. Read more on [W&M and totaliser seals](@quantity-seals).

Before the oil company recommends or approves termination for a critical irregularity, it gives the signatories to the dealership, or their nominees, a personal hearing {{8.6}}. See [the show cause notice process](@inspection-show-cause-notice) and [the appeal against termination](@inspection-appeal).

## Major irregularities

### The standard ladder: 15 days, 30 days, termination

For these five, the first instance means suspension of sales and supplies for 15 days, the second 30 days, and the third termination of the dealership {{8.3}}.

| Irregularity | Defined in | What MDG means |
|---|---|---|
| Refusing sampling or inspection | {{5.1.8}} | Refusing, obstructing, or creating circumstances that stop samples being drawn or the inspection being done. |
| No reference density at inspection | {{5.1.9}} | The density figure that should have been recorded for the time of the inspection is not available: the morning figure (due by 10 AM) or the figure after a tanker is unloaded. If the lab sample fails, the action is the one for adulteration. See [reference density by 10 AM](@density-reference-10am). |
| Stock variation beyond limits, sample passing | {{5.1.11}} | The variation is outside the permitted limit but the sample passes quality tests. If a positive variation comes with a failed sample, the action is the one for adulteration. See [stock variation](@stock-variation). |
| Records not maintained since the last inspection | {{5.1.12}} | The specified records since the last inspection are not kept. See [records to keep](@inspection-records-to-keep). |
| Overcharging | {{5.1.13}} | Charging above the authorised selling price of MS, HSD, CNG or Auto LPG. See [overcharging](@penalties-branded-fuel-overcharging). |

### Two major irregularities with their own ladder

**Normal fuel sold as branded fuel** {{5.1.10}}. The first instance is recovery of the differential price since the last inspection. The second is termination {{8.3}}. Details in [branded fuel and overcharging](@penalties-branded-fuel-overcharging).

**Automation breaches** {{5.1.16}}. This covers running an automated outlet in manual mode without authorisation, switching off the automatic tank gauge (or leaving it non-operational) without authorisation, and deliberately disabling any other part of the automation system without authorisation. The penalty is Rs 1,00,000 at the first instance, Rs 2,00,000 plus 7 days' suspension at the second, and termination at the third {{8.3}}. Only an email or signed letter from a company official counts as authorisation {{5.1.16}}. See [automated outlets](@automation).

## Minor irregularities: a letter first, then Rs 10,000 and Rs 25,000

The action is a warning-cum-guidance letter at the first instance, Rs 10,000 per irregularity at the second, and Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third instance onwards {{8.4}}.

| Irregularity | Defined in | What MDG means |
|---|---|---|
| (i) Older records missing | {{5.1.12}} | Records since the last inspection are kept, but earlier records the rules require are not available. |
| (ii) No air, telephone or first aid box | {{5.1.14(a)}} | Air with a calibrated gauge, a telephone, and a first aid box with medicines within their expiry date. |
| (iii)(a) Selling prices not displayed | {{5.1.15}} | The authorised retail selling prices must be displayed at the outlet. |
| (iii)(b) Required displays missing | {{5.1.17}} | Density, opening stock of the day, the zero-before-delivery sticker, the product name on each nozzle, and the contact details for complaint, grievance or emergency. |
| (iii)(c) Complaint book | {{5.1.17}} | Not maintained, or not given when the customer asks. |
| (iii)(d) Poor housekeeping | {{5.1.17}} | MDG names it without a detailed test. It says the outlet must be kept illuminated and clean to the housekeeping standards the oil companies lay down {{1.6(x)}}. |
| (iii)(e) Driveway salesmen | {{5.1.17}} | Not in uniform or not wearing badges. |
| (iii)(f) Customer service standards | {{5.1.18}} | Falling short of the minimum service obligations. See [the 20-point list](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations). |
| (iii)(g) No clean toilet | {{5.1.14(b)}} | See [toilet rules](@service-toilet-rules). |
| (iii)(h) Short delivery with W&M seals intact | {{5.1.2(a)}} | See [the 5-litre test](@quantity-5-litre-test). |

One or more irregularities among (iii)(a) to (h) count as **one** irregularity when the action is decided {{8.4}}. A dirty toilet, a missing price board and a missing complaint book found together are charged once, not three times. MDG names only (a) to (h) for this. It does not say the same for items (i) and (ii), so ask your oil company how it counts those.

MDG 2012 (Ver. 7, 30.11.2022) treated a missing clean toilet and short delivery with seals intact as major irregularities with money ladders of their own {{2012:8.3}}. MDG 2024 made both minor {{8.4}}. The old and new ladders differ, so an amount quoted from the old guidelines is not the current penalty. Confirm the current position with your oil company.

## How instances are counted

The second and third instance are counted over five years. When an irregularity is established, the oil company looks back five years from that date to decide whether it is the first, second or third instance. Irregularities under the previous MDG within those five years count as instances {{8.5.5}}.

When two or more irregularities are found at the same outlet at the same time, each is counted as an instance against its own class, and each gets the action for its own instance. MDG calls this a compounding effect {{8.5.3}}. The full rules, with dates, are in [repeat offences and unpaid fines](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).

## Rules that apply to every penalty

- **Proof comes first.** Every irregularity must be established before any action is taken {{8.5.2}}.
- **Stops come before the penalty.** Some findings stop sales at once while a lab tests the fuel: missing reference density and positive stock variation beyond limits stop all products {{5.1.9}} {{5.1.11}}, and tampering findings stop the dispensing unit {{5.1.2(b)}}. That stop is not the penalty in the table.
- **Irregularities not in the table.** The oil company's competent authority acts after an enquiry, following the principles of natural justice {{8.5.4}}.
- **Notice and reply.** A show cause notice comes within 30 days of the inspection, or of the test results if samples were drawn {{8.5.6}}. The dealer has 10 days from the date of issue to reply {{8.5.7}}. A reasoned speaking order follows, preferably within 45 days of the reply {{8.5.8}}. See [the notice process](@inspection-show-cause-notice).
- **Other authorities count too.** A malpractice or irregularity established by a central or state government authority during its own checks is also taken as one under MDG, and the oil company acts on that authority's advice {{8.7}}.
- **Unpaid fines.** Where the action is only a fine, with no suspension, the fine is due within 30 days of the company's notice. If it is left unpaid, sales and supplies are suspended from the 31st day for 15 days. If it is still unpaid, the suspension is extended for another 15 days, and after that the dealership is terminated {{8.7}}. See [the unpaid-fine timeline](@penalties-repeat-and-unpaid).
- **Appeal.** Termination under MDG can be appealed within 30 days of receiving the order {{8.9}}.

## A worked example

For example, an inspector visits an outlet on 10 March 2026. The outlet has had no irregularity in the last five years. The inspector finds three things on the same day.

| Finding | Class | Instance | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| No density figure in the register for the time of the visit {{5.1.9}} | Major | First | Sales and supplies of all products stop at once and samples go to the lab. If the samples pass: 15 days' suspension {{8.3}}. If they fail: the action for adulteration, which is termination {{8.2}}. |
| Toilet not clean {{5.1.14(b)}} | Minor (g) | First | Warning-cum-guidance letter {{8.4}} |
| Selling prices not displayed {{5.1.15}} | Minor (a) | Counted with the toilet | The same letter. Items (a) and (g) count as one irregularity {{8.4}}. |

The findings fall in two classes, so each class gets its own action {{8.5.3}}. If the samples pass, the outlet faces 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies and one warning-cum-guidance letter. The show cause notice is due within 30 days of the test results, because samples were drawn {{8.5.6}}.

Had the toilet and the price board been the only findings, the outlet would have received the letter alone, with no suspension.
