## What "failed" means

A sample is deemed failed when the test results of the sample under scrutiny and the reference sample do not fall within the reproducibility or permissible limit of the test method {{2.10}}. See [how 3-tier sampling works](@sampling) for the three samples being compared.

MDG's definition of adulteration is wider than adding something to the fuel. It has three parts, joined by "and/or" {{5.1.1}}:

1. A foreign substance is put into MS or HSD illegally or without authority, so the fuel no longer meets the BIS specifications: IS 2796, IS 17586 and IS 17021 for MS, and IS 1460 and IS 16531 for HSD.
2. The sample under scrutiny and the reference sample do not fall within the reproducibility or permissible limits of the test method.
3. Any other requirement the competent authority issues from time to time to identify adulteration.

On the wording of the definition, a failed lab comparison (part 2) can meet it on its own.

## What starts a lab test

Samples are drawn at random and in the situations listed in [3-tier sampling](@sampling). Some quick checks at the outlet lead straight to the lab:

- If the density test, the filter paper test or a mobile lab's clinical test fails, an oil company officer suspends sales and supply of all products after informing the Territory, Divisional or Regional Manager. Samples are drawn and sent for testing on priority {{2.5(K)}}.
- Positive stock variation beyond the permissible limit: samples are drawn and sales and supplies of all products are suspended immediately {{5.1.11}}.
- Missing reference density at inspection: sales and supplies of all products are suspended immediately and samples of all products are drawn {{5.1.9}}.

## How the lab decides

1. The lab tests the nozzle sample (RO sample), the last tank lorry retention sample (TL1) and the matching supply location sample (SL1), and compares the results against the reproducibility limits {{2.5(G)}}.
2. If the nozzle sample fails and TL1 passes, the lab tests TL2 and SL2 as well. MDG does not define those two further. Action against the dealer or transporter follows from the results {{2.5(G)}}.
3. A decision is taken only after the supply location sample (if still within its retention period), the "RO retention sample" and your tank lorry sample (if you produced it at the inspection) have all been tested {{2.5(I)}}.
4. If everything passes, the dealer is told in writing and the retention samples can be disposed of {{2.5(F)}}.

If you could not show your tank lorry samples, the nozzle sample is compared with the supply point sample, and any action is against the dealer only {{2.5(E)}}. See [the tank lorry retention sample](@sampling-retention).

## The time limits

| Step | Preferred time |
|---|---|
| Samples reach the lab | Within 10 days of drawal {{2.10}} {{2.5(D)}} |
| Lab tests the samples | Within the next 15 days {{2.10}} |
| Oil company gives the dealer the result, pass or fail | Within 5 days of receiving the report, and about 30 days from drawal in all {{2.10}} {{2.5(F)}} |
| Dealer asks for a re-test of his retained sample | Within 10 days of receiving the lab report {{2.5(H)}} |
| Show cause notice, when samples were drawn at inspection | Within 30 days of the test results {{8.5.6}} |
| Dealer's reply to the notice | 10 days from the date the notice is issued {{8.5.7}} |
| Speaking order | Preferably within 45 days of receiving the reply {{8.5.8}} |

The time frames for sampling and testing are there to streamline the system. MDG says they have no bearing on the quality or result of the product {{2.5(L)}}. Two of the steps carry a dealer's own deadline: the 10-day re-test window {{2.5(H)}} and the 10-day reply to a show cause notice {{8.5.7}}. An appeal against termination has its own 30-day window {{8.9}}.

> **Note:** the 10 days to ask for a re-test run from the day you receive the lab report. Write down that date.

## Your right to a re-test

If a sample fails, you can ask for your retained sample to be tested again {{2.5(H)}}:

- The request must be made within 10 days of receiving the lab report.
- Your sample and the counter sample the oil company holds are tested at any PSU laboratory of your choice in the same or a nearby state, or at FTL Noida.
- Testing is done in the presence of the field officer and you, after the samples are verified.
- The competent authority decides on the results of all three samples. The decision is decisive and binding on all.
- You bear the cost.

MDG does not say which three samples it means, or how to submit the request. Ask your oil company.

A transporter who is not also the dealer has a similar right. The oil company tests his retained sample, preferably in the presence of the field officer, the dealer and the transporter, and compares it with the dealer's tank lorry sample and the supply location sample. The decision is binding. The transporter bears the cost {{2.5(J)}}.

## What happens to the fuel

Proven adulterated MS or HSD is sent to the nearest refinery, as per a 21-12-2001 directive of the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas {{5.1.1}}.

| Where the adulteration is proven | Who pays | What the dealer receives |
|---|---|---|
| At the outlet | All costs of transport, pumping, tank cleaning, incidental charges and local levies are recovered from the dealer {{5.1.1}} | The cost of furnace oil, for the quantity actually received at the refinery {{5.1.1}} |
| By the transport contractor or crew | All expenses are recovered from the contractor, along with the loss from downgrading and any transit loss. Action under the TDG applies as well {{5.1.1}} | The full value of the product {{5.1.1}} |

## When the sample passes

A passing sample does not always end the matter. Other findings still count.

| Situation | Sample passes | Sample fails |
|---|---|---|
| Reference density not available | Major irregularity: 15 days' suspension of sales and supplies, 30 days the second time, termination the third time {{5.1.9}} {{8.3}} | Action as for adulteration {{5.1.9}} |
| Positive stock variation beyond limits | Action for any other irregularity found, such as unauthorised purchase {{5.1.11}}. Stock variation with a passing sample is a major irregularity {{8.3}} | Action as for adulteration {{5.1.11}} |

See [reference density](@density-reference-10am) and [positive and negative stock variation](@stock-positive-negative).

## Penalty, notice and appeal

Adulteration is a critical irregularity. The action is termination at the first instance {{8.2}}. The actions in MDG are the minimum, and the oil company can act higher where it thinks fit {{8.5.1}}. Every irregularity must be established before any action is taken {{8.5.2}}.

After the test, the oil company sends a show cause notice within 30 days of the results, along with the reports it relies on {{8.5.6}}. You have 10 days to reply {{8.5.7}}. For a critical irregularity leading to termination, the signatories to the dealership or their nominees get a personal hearing before termination is recommended or approved {{8.6}}. A dealer can appeal within 30 days of receiving the termination order {{8.9}}. See [the show cause notice](@inspection-show-cause-notice) and [appeals](@inspection-appeal).

One more rule: when one agency has found a quality irregularity and started its investigation, no parallel investigation by drawing samples is carried out while it is pending {{2.5(M)}}.
