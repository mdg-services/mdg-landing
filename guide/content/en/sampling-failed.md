## What "failed" means

A sample counts as failed when its result and the reference sample's result are further apart than the test method allows. MDG calls this limit the "reproducibility/permissible limit" {{2.10}}. In plain terms, it is the gap a test method accepts between two results on the same fuel. MDG prints no figures for it. For the three samples that are compared, see [how 3-tier sampling works](@sampling).

MDG's definition of adulteration reaches further than adding something to the fuel. It has three parts, which are alternatives joined by "and/or" {{5.1.1}}:

1. A foreign substance is added to MS or HSD illegally or without authority, so that the fuel no longer meets the Indian Standards for it: IS 2796, IS 17586 and IS 17021 for MS, and IS 1460 and IS 16531 for HSD, as amended. Indian Standards are revised from time to time, so check the current version of each.
2. A lab comparison puts the sample and the reference sample outside the reproducibility or permissible limits of the test method.
3. The competent authority issues some other requirement from time to time for identifying adulteration.

On its wording, a failed lab comparison (part 2) can meet the definition on its own.

## What sends a sample to the lab

Samples are drawn at random and in the situations listed in [3-tier sampling](@sampling). Three findings at the outlet lead to a suspension of sales and supplies, and to samples going to the lab:

- A failed density test, filter paper test or mobile lab clinical test: an oil company officer suspends sales and supply of all products after informing the Territory, Divisional or Regional Manager. Samples are drawn and sent for priority testing {{2.5(K)}}.
- Positive stock variation beyond the permitted limit: samples are drawn and sales and supplies of all products are suspended at once {{5.1.11}}.
- No reference density at inspection: sales and supplies of all products are suspended at once, and samples of all products are drawn {{5.1.9}}.

## How the lab decides

1. The lab tests three samples: the nozzle sample (RO sample), TL1 (the latest tank lorry retention sample) and SL1 (the supply location sample that goes with it). It checks the results against the reproducibility limits {{2.5(G)}}.
2. If the nozzle sample fails but TL1 passes, TL2 and SL2 are tested too. MDG does not define those two further. Action against the dealer or the transporter rests on the results {{2.5(G)}}.
3. A decision waits until all the relevant samples have been tested: the supply location sample (if it is still within its retention period), the outlet's retained nozzle sample, and the dealer's tank lorry sample (if he produced it at the inspection) {{2.5(I)}}.
4. If everything passes, the dealer is told in writing and the retention samples can be disposed of {{2.5(F)}}.

If you could not show your tank lorry samples, the nozzle sample is compared with the supply point sample, and any action is against the dealer only {{2.5(E)}}. See [the tank lorry retention sample](@sampling-retention).

## Your right to ask for a re-test

If a sample fails, the dealer can ask for his retained sample to be tested again {{2.5(H)}}:

- The request has to be made within 10 days of receiving the lab report.
- His sample and the counter sample the oil company holds are tested at a PSU laboratory he picks, in the same or a neighbouring state, or at FTL Noida.
- The testing is done in the presence of the field officer and the dealer, after the samples are verified.
- The competent authority decides on the results of all three samples. The decision is binding on all.
- The dealer bears the cost.

MDG does not say which three samples it means, or how the request is to be made. Ask your oil company.

A transporter who is not also the dealer has a similar right. The oil company tests his retained sample, preferably with the field officer, the dealer and the transporter present. The result is compared with the dealer's tank lorry sample and with the supply location sample, the decision is binding on all, and the transporter bears the cost {{2.5(J)}}.

## The time limits

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Samples delivered to the lab | Preferably within 10 days of drawal {{2.10}} {{2.5(D)}} |
| Lab completes its testing | Preferably in the 15 days after that {{2.10}} |
| Oil company tells the dealer the result, pass or fail | Preferably within 5 days of receiving the report, and about 30 days from drawal in all {{2.10}} {{2.5(F)}} |
| Dealer asks for a re-test of his retained sample | Within 10 days of receiving the lab report {{2.5(H)}} |
| Show cause notice, when samples were drawn at an inspection | Within 30 days of the test results {{8.5.6}} |
| Dealer's reply to the notice | 10 days from the date the notice is issued {{8.5.7}} |
| Speaking order | Preferably within 45 days of receiving the reply {{8.5.8}} |

The sampling and testing times are there to keep the system moving. MDG says they have no connection with the quality or result of the product {{2.5(L)}}. The dealer's own deadlines are the 10-day re-test request {{2.5(H)}} and the 10-day reply to a show cause notice {{8.5.7}}. An appeal against termination has its own 30-day window {{8.9}}.

> **Note:** the 10 days for a re-test request run from the day the dealer receives the lab report {{2.5(H)}}. The 10 days to reply to a notice run from the date the notice is issued {{8.5.7}}.

## What happens to the fuel

Product proven to be adulterated goes to the nearest refinery, under a directive of the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas dated 21-12-2001 {{5.1.1}}.

| Where the adulteration is proven | Who pays | What the dealer receives |
|---|---|---|
| At the outlet | The dealer bears all the expenses of moving the product, cleaning the tank and related charges, including local levies {{5.1.1}} | The furnace oil value of the quantity that actually reaches the refinery {{5.1.1}} |
| By the transport contractor or crew | The contractor bears all the expenses, plus the loss from downgrading and any transit loss. Action under the Transport Discipline Guidelines (TDG) applies as well {{5.1.1}} | The full value of the product {{5.1.1}} |

## When the sample passes

A passing sample does not always end the matter, because other findings still count.

| Situation | Sample passes | Sample fails |
|---|---|---|
| Reference density not available | Major irregularity {{5.1.9}} {{8.3}} | Action as for adulteration {{5.1.9}} |
| Positive stock variation beyond the limit | Action for any other irregularity found, such as unauthorised purchase {{5.1.11}}. Stock variation with a passing sample is itself a major irregularity {{8.3}} | Action as for adulteration {{5.1.11}} |

For these two major irregularities MDG sets a 15-day suspension of sales and supplies the first time, a 30-day suspension the second time, and termination of the dealership the third time {{8.3}}. These are the lowest actions: the oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}, and the irregularity has to be established first {{8.5.2}}. The second and third instances are counted over five years, and irregularities under the previous guideline count as instances {{8.5.5}}. The appeal route in MDG is for termination only {{8.9}}.

See [reference density](@density-reference-10am) and [positive and negative stock variation](@stock-positive-negative).

## Penalty, notice, hearing and appeal

Adulteration is a critical irregularity, and MDG's action for it is termination at the first instance {{8.2}}. The actions in MDG are the minimum, and the oil company may take higher action where it considers it necessary {{8.5.1}}. Action against a dealer comes only after the irregularity has been established {{8.5.2}}.

When samples were drawn at an inspection, the show cause notice is to be issued within 30 days of the test results, along with the reports and documents it is based on {{8.5.6}}. The dealer has 10 days from the date of issue to reply {{8.5.7}}. Before termination for a critical irregularity is recommended or approved, the dealership's signatories or their nominees get a personal hearing. If they do not attend on the appointed date, they get one more chance, after which the case may proceed without them {{8.6}}. A dealer can appeal against a termination within 30 days of receiving the order {{8.9}}. The appeal covers terminations only {{8.9}}. See [the show cause notice](@inspection-show-cause-notice) and [appeals](@inspection-appeal).

MDG also says that once one agency has found a quality irregularity and begun its investigation, no parallel investigation by drawing samples is carried out while that one is pending {{2.5(M)}}.
