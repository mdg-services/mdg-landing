## What "failed" means

A sample fails when its result and the reference sample's are further apart than the test method allows, which MDG calls the "reproducibility/permissible limit". MDG prints no figures for it {{2.10}}. On its wording, that can meet MDG's definition of adulteration {{5.1.1}}: see [adulteration, penalty and who pays](@sampling-adulteration).

## How the lab decides

1. The lab tests the nozzle sample against TL1, the latest tank lorry sample, and SL1, the supply location sample that goes with it. If the nozzle sample fails and TL1 passes, it tests TL2 and SL2 too. MDG does not define those two further {{2.5(G)}}.
2. A decision waits until every relevant sample is tested {{2.5(I)}}.
3. If all pass, the dealer is told in writing and the retention samples can be disposed of. The result, pass or fail, should reach the dealer, preferably within 30 days of the sample being drawn {{2.5(F)}}.

See [how 3-tier sampling works](@sampling).

## Your right to ask for a re-test

- After a failed result, ask within 10 days of receiving the lab report {{2.5(H)}}.
- Your retained sample and the oil company's counter sample are tested at a PSU lab you pick in the same or a neighbouring state, or at FTL Noida, with the field officer and you present {{2.5(H)}}.
- The competent authority decides on all the results, and the decision is binding on all {{2.5(H)}}.
- You bear the cost {{2.5(H)}}.

MDG does not say how the request is made. Ask your oil company.

## The deadlines that follow

Where samples were drawn at an inspection, the show cause notice is due within 30 days of the test results {{8.5.6}}. The reply, the speaking order and any appeal then run as set out on the [show cause notice](@inspection-show-cause-notice) and [appeal](@inspection-appeal) pages.

A passing sample can still leave other findings to answer: see [reference density](@density-reference-10am), [positive and negative stock variation](@stock-positive-negative) and [penalties](@penalties).

General information, not legal advice. For your own notice, speak to an advocate or your oil company.
