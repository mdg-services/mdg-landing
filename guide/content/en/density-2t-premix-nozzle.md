## Which nozzle, for which sample

General knowledge, not MDG text: 2T premixed petrol is petrol with two-stroke engine oil already mixed in. MDG does not define it.

| Sample | Through which nozzle | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Official sample from an MS tank, drawn through a nozzle | Only one delivering non-2T premixed MS. If all nozzles on that tank deliver 2T premix, precautions keep the sample free from 2T oil | {{5.1 Note (ii)}} |
| Your morning density, before sales begin | The pump nozzle. MDG names no nozzle and does not mention 2T | {{1.4.1(c)}} |
| Your density after a tanker, once at least 50 litres have gone through the pump | The pump nozzle. MDG names no nozzle here either and does not mention 2T | {{1.2(xiv)}} |

Who may draw official nozzle samples, and how many samples they take, is on [inspector samples](@sampling-inspector-samples). Where a product has two tanks, see [pumps with more than one tank](@sampling-more-than-one-tank).

## What MDG leaves open

- **Whether the note covers your own sample.** It speaks of drawing samples from an MS tank through a nozzle and does not say who draws {{5.1 Note (ii)}}.
- **What the precaution is** when all nozzles give 2T premix.
- **Which nozzle you should use** for your own reading.

General practice, not an MDG rule: if one nozzle on the tank gives plain petrol, draw your own sample from it, as an official sample would be; if every nozzle gives 2T premix, ask your oil company how it wants the sample taken, and keep the answer with the [density register](@density-register).

## An example

For example (a made-up outlet): MS tank 1 feeds three nozzles. Two deliver plain petrol and one delivers 2T premix. An official sample from tank 1 goes through a plain-petrol nozzle {{5.1 Note (ii)}}. Had all three delivered 2T premix, precautions would be needed to keep the sample free from 2T oil.

Your own readings still go in the register each morning and after each tanker {{1.4.1(c)}} {{1.2(xiv)}}: see [the reference density rule](@density-reference-10am).
