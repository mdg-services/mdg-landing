## What a customer can check and ask for

MDG 2024 does not hand customers a list of rights. It lists what the dealer must provide, and the items in this table are ones a customer can ask to see. Dealers can use the same table as a checklist.

| What you can check or ask for | What the dealer must do | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Zero before fuel flows | The attendant shows the zero setting to the customer before the start of each delivery. The meter is brought back to zero after each delivery | {{1.4.2(f)}} |
| A message about zero on the pump | A message to this effect is displayed on each pump unit | {{1.4.2(f)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| The closing reading | The final closing meter reading is shown after delivery (item 3 of the circular) | {{circular}} |
| Filter paper to test petrol | Whatman 2 filter paper is kept and made available to customers on demand, with a message on the Customer Education Board or poster | {{1.4.1(a)}} |
| The complaint book | It is kept, readily available on demand, with a prominent message that it exists | {{1.6(iv)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| Who to contact | The dealer's name and telephone number, and the name and number of the oil company personnel to contact about complaints, are shown prominently | {{1.6(viii)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| The price | The authorised retail selling price of each product is exhibited (item 19 of the circular) | {{5.1.15}} {{circular}} |
| Density and opening stock | Both are displayed | {{5.1.17}} {{1.3}} |

The contact numbers are on the board at the outlet, so none are printed here.

## Zero before delivery, step by step

The order matters, so here it is as a list.

1. After the previous delivery, the pump meter is brought back to zero {{1.4.2(f)}}.
2. Before the next delivery starts, the pump attendant shows the zero setting to the customer {{1.4.2(f)}}.
3. The fuel is delivered.
4. After the delivery, the final closing meter reading is shown {{circular}}.

The circular also asks that every dispensing unit is manned by staff during working hours (item 2) {{circular}}.

If a meter does not return to zero, the pump maintenance report lists "not resetting to zero" among the possible pump complaints {{annexure 6}}. The dealer must promptly inform the oil company of any breakdown of the company's pump equipment {{4.2(a)}}.

## Filter paper

MDG asks the dealer to keep Whatman 2 filter paper to check the quality of petrol (MS), and to give it to customers on demand if they want to satisfy themselves {{1.4.1(a)}}. See [the filter paper test](@density-filter-paper).

The circular's list also names the Q&Q kit (item 14) {{circular}}. Neither MDG nor the circular says what is in it. Ask your oil company whether it covers your filter paper and 5-litre measure.

## The 5 litre check on request

MDG 2024 does not give customers a test of their own. It keeps a W&M-verified 5-litre measure at the outlet for pump testing {{1.4.2(b)}}. IndianOil's Check and Fill display board, which is an oil company board and not MDG text, invites customers to verify the pump with the dealer's calibrated 5-litre measure. Other oil companies' boards may differ. The dealer's own daily test is explained on the [5 litre test page](@quantity-5-litre-test).

## What each failure costs the dealer

| If this is missing | Class | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| The message about zero on a pump | Minor | {{8.4(iii)(b)}} |
| Zero or the closing reading not shown | Customer service standard: minor penalties apply | {{5.1.18}} {{8.4(iii)(f)}} |
| The complaint book, or not giving it when asked | Minor | {{8.4(iii)(c)}} |
| Density, opening stock, the product name on each nozzle of a multi-product dispenser, or the complaint and emergency contacts | Minor | {{8.4(iii)(b)}} |
| Retail selling prices | Minor | {{8.4(iii)(a)}} |
| Filter paper available on demand | Not named in MDG's list of irregularities. Matters not listed are dealt with by the oil company after enquiry | {{8.5.4}} |

Minor irregularities carry a warning-cum-guidance letter at the first instance, Rs 10,000 per irregularity at the second and Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third on {{8.4}}. One or more of the miscellaneous items found together count as one irregularity for taking action {{8.4}}. Instances are counted over five years {{8.5.5}}. The circular tells inspectors to mark each point YES or NO, and a shortfall leads to action under 5.1.18 or the clause that fits {{circular}}. See [how the minimum service obligations are checked](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations).

For example, an inspector finds no zero message on pump 4, and the complaint book is not produced when asked for. Both are miscellaneous minor items, so they count as one irregularity. If it is the first in five years, the action is a warning-cum-guidance letter.

## When it is more than a missing sticker

Cheating on quantity is a different class of case. Tampered W&M seals, a tampered totalizer and unauthorised fittings are all critical, with termination at the first instance {{8.2}}. The duty to check the seals every day is the dealer's {{1.4.2(a)}}. Read [W&M and totalizer seals](@quantity-seals), or the overview of [quantity rules](@quantity).

> **Note:** the actions above are minimums, and the oil company can take higher action {{8.5.1}}. The dealer must also keep the customer service standard set in the oil company's circulars from time to time {{1.6(xii)}}. Check what your oil company has issued.
