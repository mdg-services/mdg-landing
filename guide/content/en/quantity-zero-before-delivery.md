## What a customer can check and ask for

The Marketing Discipline Guidelines 2024 (MDG 2024) are the oil companies' rules for dealers under the dealership agreement. They bind the dealer through the dealership agreement. They give customers no list of rights. They list what the dealer must provide, and the items in this table are ones a customer can ask to see. Dealers can use the same table as a checklist.

| What you can check or ask for | What the dealer must do | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Zero before fuel flows | The attendant shows the customer the zero setting before each delivery, and the meter goes back to zero after each one | {{1.4.2(f)}} |
| A zero message on the pump | Every pump unit carries a message about it | {{1.4.2(f)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| The closing reading | The final closing meter reading is shown once the delivery ends (item 3 of the circular) | {{circular}} |
| Filter paper to test petrol | Whatman 2 paper is kept and given to a customer who asks, and the Customer Education Board or poster carries a message about it | {{1.4.1(a)}} |
| The complaint book | It is kept, handed over whenever a customer asks, with a prominent message that it exists | {{1.6(iv)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| Who to contact | The dealer's name and phone number, and the name and number of the oil company contact for complaints, are displayed prominently | {{1.6(viii)}} {{5.1.17}} |
| The price | The authorised retail selling price of each product is on display (item 19 of the circular) | {{5.1.15}} {{circular}} |
| Density and opening stock | Both are on display | {{5.1.17}} {{1.3}} |

The contact numbers are on the board at the outlet, so none are printed here.

The circular dated 20.03.2025 gives a standard format for the letter each oil company is to issue to its own dealers on its letterhead. It lists 20 minimum service obligations, and it sets no penalty of its own: a shortfall is dealt with under MDG 5.1.18 or the clause that fits {{circular}} {{5.1.18}}.

## Zero before delivery, step by step

The order matters, so here it is as a list.

1. After the last delivery, the pump meter goes back to zero {{1.4.2(f)}}.
2. Before the next delivery starts, the attendant shows the customer the zero setting {{1.4.2(f)}}.
3. The fuel is delivered.
4. Once it ends, the final closing meter reading is shown {{circular}}.

The circular also asks that every dispensing unit has staff on it during working hours (item 2) {{circular}}.

The pump maintenance report has a place to note a meter that does not return to zero, among its possible pump complaints {{annexure 6}}. The dealer must tell the oil company promptly about any breakdown of the company's pump equipment {{4.2(a)}}.

## Filter paper

MDG asks the dealer to keep Whatman 2 filter paper to check the quality of petrol (MS), and to give it to a customer who wants to check the petrol personally {{1.4.1(a)}}. See [the filter paper test](@density-filter-paper).

The circular's list also names the Q&Q kit (item 14) {{circular}}. Neither MDG nor the circular says what is in it. Ask your oil company whether it covers your filter paper and 5-litre measure.

## The 5 litre check on request

MDG 2024 gives customers no test of their own. It keeps a W&M-verified 5-litre measure at the outlet for the dealer's pump testing {{1.4.2(b)}}. Customer messages and posters come from the oil company {{1.6(viii)}}, and what they say can differ between oil companies, so ask the dealer, and your oil company if you are unsure, whether a customer can request a check. The dealer's own daily test is explained on the [5 litre test page](@quantity-5-litre-test).

## What a miss costs the dealer

| If this is missing | How MDG treats it | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| The zero message on a pump | Minor irregularity | {{8.4(iii)(b)}} |
| Zero or the closing reading not shown | Customer service standard: the minor-irregularity action applies | {{5.1.18}} {{8.4(iii)(f)}} |
| The complaint book, or not handing it over when asked | Minor irregularity | {{8.4(iii)(c)}} |
| Filter paper on demand | Not named in MDG's lists of irregularities. Matters not listed are dealt with by the oil company after an enquiry | {{8.5.4}} |

Missing price, density, opening-stock or contact displays are minor irregularities too {{8.4(iii)(a)}} {{8.4(iii)(b)}}. See [the display boards page](@service-display-boards).

For a minor irregularity the action is a warning-cum-guidance letter the first time, Rs 10,000 per irregularity the second time and Rs 25,000 per irregularity from the third time on {{8.4}}. Read those figures with MDG's own limits:

- The amounts are the minimum. The oil company may take higher action, up to termination {{8.5.1}}.
- Action needs the irregularity to be established first {{8.5.2}}.
- Instances are counted by looking back five years, and irregularities under the previous guideline within that period count too {{8.5.5}}.
- Clause 8.4 says the miscellaneous minor findings count as one irregularity for taking action. Clause 8.5.3 says that when two or more irregularities are found together at one outlet, each counts as an instance in its own class and action is taken for each, with a compounding effect. MDG does not say how the two fit, so ask your oil company how it counts.

The circular tells inspectors to mark each point YES or NO, and a shortfall leads to action under 5.1.18 or the clause that fits {{circular}}. See [how the minimum service obligations are checked](@inspection-minimum-service-obligations).

Illustration, with made-up details: an inspector finds no zero message on pump 4 at an imaginary outlet, and the complaint book is not produced when asked for. Both fall in the miscellaneous group that 8.4 says counts as one irregularity for taking action, so on that wording these are one irregularity. Read with 8.5.3, they could be counted as two. MDG does not settle which applies, so confirm with your oil company how it would count them.

## When it is more than a missing sticker

Cheating on quantity is a different class of case. Tampered W&M seals, a tampered totalizer and unauthorised fittings are all critical, and the action is termination at the first instance once the irregularity is established {{8.2}} {{8.5.2}}. Checking the seals every day is the dealer's duty {{1.4.2(a)}}. Read [W&M and totalizer seals](@quantity-seals), or the overview of [quantity rules](@quantity).

> **Note:** the actions above are minimums, and the oil company can take higher action {{8.5.1}}. The dealer must also meet the customer service standard set in the oil company's circulars from time to time {{1.6(xii)}}. Check what your oil company has issued.
