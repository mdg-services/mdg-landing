## Who MDG says signs which paper

| Paper | Who signs | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Tank lorry retention sample label, when a tanker arrives | Dealer / his representative, with the tank lorry driver | {{2.3}} {{annexure 3}} |
| Label of samples drawn by a company or joint team | Dealer / his representative; in a joint inspection, the team members sign too | {{2.4.3}} {{annexure 4}} |
| Joint memo when the tank lorry sample box is taken into custody | Acknowledged by dealer / representative | {{2.5(C)}} |
| Mobile lab sample label | Dealer / Dealer's representative | {{annexure 11}} |
| Mobile lab test report | The clause asks for the dealer's signature with seal on all copies after a failed sample; the form has a line for Dealer / Dealer's Representative | {{7.6.1(c)}} {{annexure 12a}} {{annexure 13a}} |
| Pump maintenance job report, when a Weights & Measures seal is found broken | The company's maintenance representative records it; the report should be signed by the dealer as well | {{4.3(a)}} |

## What a signature says, and what MDG leaves open

The signer of an Annexure 4 label certifies that the empty containers were rinsed in his presence and that one of the three sets was handed to him. The label also takes the signer's name {{annexure 4}}.

MDG does not define "representative". It gives no authority format and does not say whether a manager or staff member qualifies. Ask your oil company in writing.

General practice, not an MDG rule: keep a signed letter naming the manager, with your inspection papers.

For example (a made-up outlet): the dealer is away and the manager signs the Annexure 4 label as the dealer's representative. The label records his name {{annexure 4}}. MDG does not say whether he counts as a representative.
