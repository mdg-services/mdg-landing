## Petrol pump me density kitni honi chahiye?

The limits, all at 15°C {{7.6}}:

| Fuel | Density at 15°C, kg/m³ | Standard to check |
|---|---|---|
| Petrol (MS): grades MG, E5, E10, E12, E15 (BS VI) | 720–775 | IS 2796 and IS 17586 |
| Petrol (MS): grade E20 (BS VI) | 720–775 | IS 17021 |
| Diesel (HSD): BS VI up to B7 | 810–845 | IS 1460 |
| Diesel blended with biodiesel: B8 to B20 | 820–860 | IS 16531 |

Standards are revised from time to time, and MDG says a change in a BIS specification or test method applies at once {{7.6}}. Check the current edition of each standard, or ask your oil company, before you rely on a figure.

A footnote to the 810–845 diesel figure says any relaxation for fuel made from Assam crude may follow Government of India notifications. MDG gives no relaxed figure, so ask your oil company which range applies to you {{7.6}}.

## Your own test is not this range

The range is the specification a lab tests a sample against {{7.6}} {{annexure 5}}. The checks you make yourself are different:

- **On receipt:** each compartment, converted to 15°C, within ±3.0 kg/m³ of the invoice density {{1.2(iv)}}.
- **Every morning and after every load:** your own density goes in the register {{1.4.1(c)}} {{1.2(xiv)}}.
- **At an inspection:** samples from your outlet are compared with your own recorded density {{2.10}}.

See [the density rules in one place](@density) and [the invoice check](@density-invoice-check). To bring a reading to 15°C, use the ASTM table or the [15°C calculator](@tools-density-at-15).
