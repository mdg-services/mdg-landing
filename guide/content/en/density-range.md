## Petrol pump me density kitni honi chahiye?

Petrol: 720 to 775 kg/m³ at 15°C. BS VI diesel up to B7: 810 to 845 kg/m³ at 15°C. Diesel blended with biodiesel, B8 to B20: 820 to 860 kg/m³ at 15°C {{7.6}}. These are the limits MDG 2024 prints in its clinical test tables.

| Fuel | Grade as MDG prints it | Density at 15°C, kg/m³ | Standard MDG cites |
|---|---|---|---|
| Petrol (MS, motor gasoline) | MG, E5, E10, E12, E15 (BS VI) | 720–775 | IS 2796 / IS 17586 |
| Petrol (MS, motor gasoline) | E20 (BS VI) | 720–775 | IS 17021 |
| Diesel (HSD) | BS VI, up to B7 | 810–845 | IS 1460 |
| Diesel blended with biodiesel | B8 to B20 | 820–860 | IS 16531 |

E5 to E20 are petrol blended with ethanol {{annexure 7}}. MDG says motor spirit and motor gasoline are one and the same {{7.6}}. Petrol is the everyday name. The same figures are repeated in the list of tests for MS and HSD samples and in the mobile lab test reports {{annexure 5}} {{annexure 12a}} {{annexure 13a}}.

MDG ties the limits to the latest revision of each standard. If a BIS specification or test method changes, MDG says the change applies immediately {{7.6}}. If you are unsure whether a limit has changed since MDG 2024 was issued, ask your oil company.

## Your own test is not this range

The range is what a lab tests a sample against. A mobile lab runs clinical tests on the nozzle sample: for petrol, appearance, colour, density at 15°C and distillation. For diesel it adds kinematic viscosity at 40°C {{7.4}}. The tests run whatever the density result is. If the product fails on density or on any other clinical test, the oil company acts for established adulteration {{7.4}}.

The checks you make yourself are different.

- **On receipt:** each compartment, converted to 15°C, within ±3.0 kg/m³ of the invoice density. This step in MDG does not mention the range {{1.2(iv)}}.
- **Every morning and after every load:** record your own density in the register {{1.4.1(c)}} {{1.2(xiv)}}.
- **At an inspection:** samples from your outlet are compared with your own recorded density {{2.10}}. A mobile lab's report lists a maximum variation of ±3.0 kg/m³ between your tank's density after the last receipt and the density the officer observes {{annexure 12a}} {{annexure 13a}}.

**For example (made-up figures):** your petrol tank's last recorded density after a load is 744.0. A mobile lab officer reads 759.0 from the nozzle. The reading of 759.0 is inside 720–775. It is also 15.0 above your record, and the printed maximum variation is 3.0. Being inside the range does not remove that gap from the report.

> **Note:** the mobile lab report carries both the range and the variation line. A reading outside the range does not meet the specification {{7.4}}. MDG does not say in words what follows if the variation is above the printed maximum. Ask your oil company how it treats that line.

## Why density changes with temperature

This is a general explanation, not MDG text. Fuel expands as it warms. The same litre then weighs less, so a hydrometer reads lower on a hot day and higher on a cold morning. That is why every limit above says "at 15°C", and why you note the temperature of each sample.

Read the hydrometer, read the thermometer, then convert to 15°C with the ASTM density conversion table that MDG requires you to keep {{1.5(vi)}}. MDG does not print that table, and we do not give a conversion here. The [15°C calculator](@tools-density-at-15) converts a reading and a temperature for you.

## Colour of petrol grades

The same table lists the colour of each petrol grade. Colour is judged by eye {{7.6}}.

| Grade | MG, E5 to E15 column | E20 column |
|---|---|---|
| MG 91 | Orange | Not listed |
| MG 95 | Red | Red |
| MG 98 | Not listed | Green |

For diesel, the table only asks that the colour is reported {{7.6}}.

## Diesel from Assam crude

A footnote to the 810–845 figure says that a relaxation of the density range, and its time frame, if any, for fuel processed from Assam crude may be guided by notifications of the Government of India issued from time to time {{7.6}}. MDG gives no relaxed figure. If your supply may be affected, ask your oil company which range applies to you.
