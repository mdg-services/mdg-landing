## Who is responsible, stage by stage

Both the container and the wooden box carry numbered plastic seals and wire {{2.7}}, and the Annexure 3 label records both sets of seal numbers {{annexure 3}}.

| Stage | What MDG says | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Day to day | Dealer and transporter are jointly responsible for correct sampling and safekeeping, in their own interest | {{2.5(B)}} |
| Inspector takes your box | He checks the outer seal and labels, takes custody without opening it, and records this in the joint memo you or your representative acknowledge | {{2.5(C)}} |
| Lab finds the inner container leaking or tampered with | Responsibility for its condition rests with the dealer or transporter whose sample it is, and the case is treated as under 2.5(E) | {{2.5(C)}} |
| Samples not shown at inspection | The fact is recorded, a sample produced later is not considered, the nozzle sample is compared with the supply point sample, and action is against the dealer only | {{2.5(E)}} |

For the full routine, see [the tank lorry retention sample](@sampling-retention).

For example (a made-up outlet): the inspector checks the box's outer seal and takes it. At the lab, the aluminium container inside, from your own retention sample, is found leaking. MDG puts responsibility for the container's condition on you and treats the case as under 2.5(E) {{2.5(C)}}.

## What MDG does not say

- **A remedy.** No re-sealing, no replacement, no one to report to and no time limit for a broken seal or a leaking container.
- **An already-broken outer seal.** MDG says only that the inspector's check is recorded {{2.5(C)}}.

Ask your oil company. General practice, not an MDG rule: when each load arrives, compare the seal numbers on your two latest samples for each tank with their labels, and write to your oil company at once if anything is damaged; read the joint memo before you acknowledge it.
