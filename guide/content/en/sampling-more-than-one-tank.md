## What is tracked tank by tank

| Record | What it carries for each tank | Clause |
|---|---|---|
| Your tank lorry sample | The tank that received the load, written on the container; the Annexure 3 label has a line for it. You keep the last two loads of each tank | {{2.3}} {{2.9.2}} {{annexure 3}} |
| Your density record | The Annexure 1 format is headed by tank number, product and month | {{annexure 1}} |
| The inspector's samples | Taken from the nozzles of units on all tanks: 6 x 1 litre per petrol tank, 3 x 1 litre per diesel tank | {{2.2.2.2}} {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}} |
| The inspector's label | The dispensing unit number and the tank number | {{annexure 4}} |

Steps on a tank lorry: [the tank lorry retention sample](@sampling-retention). The record itself: [the density register](@density-register).

## What reaches the lab for each tank

The three kinds of sample the lab gets are on [what the inspector takes](@sampling-inspector-samples). Two things depend on the tank:

1. Your lorry samples used are those of the tank joined to the unit he sampled {{2.4.1(ii)}} {{2.4.2(ii)}}.
2. If your tanks last received product on different days, the depot's reference sample for each of those supplies is needed {{2.4.1(iii)}} {{2.4.2(iii)}}.

That is why the tank number on your containers and records must be right.

For example (a made-up outlet): two petrol tanks and two diesel tanks. An inspector draws 6 + 6 + 3 + 3 = 18 samples, and you keep 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 6 {{2.4.1}} {{2.4.2}}. Your own lorry samples are 4 containers per petrol tank and 2 per diesel tank, 12 in all {{2.3}} {{2.9.2}}.

## What MDG does not say

The Annexure 1 format is headed by tank number {{annexure 1}}, but MDG's morning density clause says only "from the pump nozzle" {{1.4.1(c)}}. It does not say which nozzle serves which tank. General practice, not an MDG rule: write the tank number beside each reading, use a unit joined to the tank that received the load for the after-delivery reading, and ask your oil company if unsure.
