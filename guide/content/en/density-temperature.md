## What happens when fuel warms up

Warm fuel takes more room than cool fuel. A cubic metre of it therefore weighs a little less, and the hydrometer sinks deeper and shows a smaller number. The fuel is the same; only the reading moved.

For example (made-up samples, worked out with the [15°C calculator](@tools-density-at-15)): each fuel below has one fixed density at 15°C, but the hydrometer shows a different figure as the sample warms.

| Fuel | Sample temperature | Hydrometer reads (kg/m³) | Density at 15°C (kg/m³) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Petrol | 20°C, cool morning | 748.7 | 753.1 |
| Petrol | 27°C | 742.5 | 753.1 |
| Petrol | 35°C, hot afternoon | 735.3 | 753.1 |
| Diesel | 20°C | 828.4 | 831.9 |
| Diesel | 32°C | 820.0 | 831.9 |
| Diesel | 38°C | 815.8 | 831.9 |

The printed ASTM table decides if it differs from the calculator, and it may differ in the last digit {{1.5(vi)}}. Do not turn these rows into a rule of thumb. Use the table.

## Why MDG fixes 15°C

One fixed temperature lets any two readings be compared, whenever and by whoever they were taken. MDG states density at 15°C wherever it matters: the invoice {{2.2.1(iv)}}, the tanker check {{1.2(iv)}}, the density after decanting {{1.2(xiv)}}, the morning reading {{1.4.1(c)}} and the ranges for each fuel {{7.6}}.

A raw hydrometer reading can sit outside a range that is stated at 15°C. For example, a petrol at the bottom of the petrol range, 720 at 15°C, reads about 697 at 40°C. Convert first, then compare.

## At the pump

1. Put the hydrometer and the thermometer in the same sample and read both {{1.2(viii)}}.
2. Convert to 15°C with ASTM Table 53B or the [15°C calculator](@tools-density-at-15) {{1.2(iv)}} {{1.5(vi)}}.
3. Compare or record: a tanker against its invoice, within ±3.0 kg/m³ ({{1.2(iv)}}, see the [invoice check](@density-invoice-check)); the morning reading in the register, by 10 AM ({{5.1.9}}, see the [10 AM rule](@density-reference-10am)).

> **Note:** a wrong thermometer spoils the answer too. In the calculator, a petrol read at 742.5 gives 753.1 at 27°C but 755.8 at 30°C, a 2.7 gap from 3°C of error alone, most of the ±3.0 allowance.

For what a hydrometer would read at other temperatures, see the [temperature chart](@tools-density-chart).
