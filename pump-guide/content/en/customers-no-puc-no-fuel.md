## The short answer

As on 25 November 2026, two official orders limit fuel to vehicles with a valid Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate: Delhi's own notification, and a direction for the whole National Capital Region (NCR). Neither source we opened covers the rest of India.

## Delhi: the 22 April 2026 notification

Delhi's notification, issued in the Lieutenant Governor's name through the Environment Department, directs that fuel (petrol, diesel, CNG, LPG) is dispensed to motor vehicles only upon production of a valid PUC certificate {{Delhi Gazette 23 Apr 2026}}. It names the oil companies, Indraprastha Gas, all other petrol, diesel and CNG pumps and retail outlets, and Delhi's departments and traffic police as bodies that must ensure strict compliance {{Delhi Gazette 23 Apr 2026}}.

It recites a rule in the Central Motor Vehicles Rules: after one year from first registration, a vehicle must carry a valid PUC certificate and produce it on demand {{Delhi Gazette 23 Apr 2026}}.

## The NCR: CAQM Direction No. 101

The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) approved Direction No. 101, which envisages dispensing fuel only upon production of a valid PUCC from 1 October 2026 in the entire NCR {{PIB 15 May 2026}}. CAQM later recorded it as dated 15 May 2026, and its enforcement sub-committee reviewed how ready the NCR governments are to install number plate recognition systems at fuel and CNG stations to enforce it {{CAQM press release 30 Sep 2026}}.

## At the counter

In practice, a pump in these areas asks for the certificate before filling, and a customer without one renews it at a PUC centre and returns.

## What we could not confirm

In the orders we opened, we found no penalty set for a pump and no rule for other states. Read the full text of Direction No. 101 on the CAQM site before acting on a detail not stated here. For legal questions, ask an advocate.
