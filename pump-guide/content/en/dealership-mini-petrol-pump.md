## What do the rules say about selling petrol and diesel?

The Motor Spirit and High Speed Diesel Control Order 2005 says only a dealer or an oil company may be in the business of selling them {{MS and HSD Control Order 2005, cl 3(4)}}, and that a dealer of an oil company delivers or sells them only from an authorised retail pump outlet {{MS and HSD Control Order 2005, cl 3(7)}}. Buying fuel from a source the oil companies have not authorised is a malpractice {{MS and HSD Control Order 2005, cl 2(p)}}.

The Petroleum Rules 2002 add the safety licence. No one may store petroleum except under a licence, apart from small exempt quantities {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 116}}. A service station is premises specially prepared for fuelling motor vehicles {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 2(xxiv)}}, and its tank and pump are licensed in Form XIV {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV}}. None of these rules has a smaller "mini" category. The only portable pump in them is a stand-in: a licensed station may use one for up to a month while its permanent pump is out of order, after telling the licensing authority in writing {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV cond 17}}.

## What small formats are legal?

| Format | What the source says |
|---|---|
| Mobile diesel dispenser | Diesel to heavy vehicles, machinery and equipment; needs a PESO licence and a contract with an oil company {{PESO mobile dispenser SOP}} |
| Hindustan Petroleum's doorstep diesel scheme | Only for start-ups recognised by DPIIT, who register as resellers and sign an agreement with the oil company {{HPCL mobile dispenser page}} |
| Rural retail outlet | A dealership in a rural area, off highways and outside municipal limits, chosen through the oil company's advertisement {{BPCL DSG 2023, cl 1(ii)}} |

Petrol was not part of the doorstep delivery change {{PIB 20 Nov 2019}}. More on these: [doorstep diesel](@customers-doorstep-diesel) and [rural retail outlets](@dealership-rural-outlet).

## How to check a "mini petrol pump scheme" offer

The oil companies' own notice describes fake messages that say a dealership is approved and ask for a "registration charge" into a bank account not in the company's name {{Oil companies fraud notice}}. It says they have appointed no agent, do not use Gmail, Yahoo or WhatsApp, and take payments only through the official dealer selection portal {{Oil companies fraud notice}}. Report such offers to the police and the nearest oil company office {{Oil companies fraud notice}}.
