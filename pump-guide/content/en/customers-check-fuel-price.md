## The short answer

As on 22 November 2026, the price changes by the oil company's decision, not the pump's. Three public sector oil companies began daily revision of petrol and diesel prices across the country from 16 June 2017 {{PIB 8 Jun 2017}}. The simplest check is the board at the pump.

## What the pump must display

For pumps under the public sector guideline, the dealer must display the opening stock and authorised selling prices {{MDG 2024, 1.3}}. Not displaying the authorised retail selling prices of petrol and diesel is a listed violation {{MDG 2024, 5.1.15}}.

## Where to check officially

| Where | What you get |
|---|---|
| The board at your pump | The selling prices {{MDG 2024, 5.1.15}} |
| PPAC daily table | Petrol and diesel in Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata, with a date of revision for each day {{PPAC metro price table}} |
| Oil company website | HPCL links a map of selling prices at important locations {{HPCL price page}}; BPCL's Petro Prices page carries a "View Prices" button; we have not checked what it shows {{BPCL petro prices page}} |

For Indian Oil outlets, ask the pump or look on the company's own channels. Check the date shown beside a price before you rely on it.

## When does it change?

Revision is daily {{PIB 8 Jun 2017}}. We found no official statement of the hour, so we do not give one. In PPAC's table posted on 20 November 2026, Delhi's petrol and diesel prices were the same on each row from 15 to 20 November {{PPAC metro price table}}.

## Why prices differ by place

Excise duty is a fixed amount a litre and most states levy VAT as a percentage {{PIB 19 Sep 2020}}. See [the price build-up page](@money-price-build-up).
