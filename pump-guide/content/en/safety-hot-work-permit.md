Can a welder start work at a pump once the owner says yes? The Rules ask for more. All below is as on 26 November 2026.

## What is hot work?

Hot work is welding, burning, soldering, brazing, sand blasting, chipping with spark-producing tools, some power tools, non-flameproof electrical equipment and engines. Any other work that can heat enough to ignite inflammable gases also counts {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 2}}.

## What the Rules ask at a pump

- No fire, furnace or other source of heat or light that can ignite vapour in a service station, except in places the licensing authority authorises {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 117}}.
- Work runs under an experienced, responsible supervisor with safety training {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 118}}.
- Everyone working there must avoid any act that tends to cause fire or explosion {{Form XIV, condition 18}}.

## Tank and electrical repairs

Before a tank is repaired it is cleared of all petroleum and flammable vapour. When it is opened, no lamp, cable, fan or other article that can ignite vapour comes near the manhole until a competent person certifies it gas-free in writing. The licensee keeps that certificate at least three months {{Form XIV, condition 8}}.

A flameproof apparatus is not opened until all electrical supply to it is cut off {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 113}}. Flame or heat work in a zone 1 area is allowed only after a competent person tests the area with an approved gas tester and certifies it safe, and it must stay safe while the work lasts {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 113}}. See [flameproof electrical fittings](@equipment-flameproof-fittings).

## Where does the work permit come from?

For a refinery, the Rules say no maintenance or repair, and no entry into a confined space, except under a written permit as per OISD standard 105. A competent person inspects first and issues it for a limited period {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 171}}. For pipelines, no maintenance, repair or hot work is allowed unless an experienced engineer first inspects the section and issues a written permit, as rule 98 reads after the March 2024 amendment {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 98}}.

We found no rule that repeats this for a pump. OISD-STD-105, Work Permit System, is in OISD's list at its August 2023 edition {{OISD standards list}}. An oil company may ask for a permit under its own rules; ask yours for its form. See also [OISD standards](@safety-oisd-standards) and [smoking at a pump](@safety-smoking).

For your own case, ask the oil company, PESO or a safety officer.
