## What the rules say about smoking

Three places in the Petroleum Rules 2002 matter to a pump.

1. **Rule 11, the general rule.** Unless the rules expressly provide otherwise, nobody may smoke, and no matches, fires, lights or articles capable of causing ignition of petroleum are allowed in proximity to a place where petroleum is stored or handled {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 11}}.
2. **Rule 117, for a service station.** The rules define a service station as premises specially prepared for the fuelling of motor vehicles {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 2(xxiv)}}. Nobody may smoke in one except in places specifically authorised by the licensing authority {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 117(1)}}. No fire, furnace or other source of heat or light capable of igniting flammable vapour is allowed there, again except in places specially authorised {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 117(3)}}.
3. **The pump's licence (Form XIV).** Petroleum may not be put into a vehicle while its engine is running {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 15(a)}}. People connected with the vehicle may not smoke while it is refuelled {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 15(b)}}.

## During a tanker delivery

When the underground tank is filled from a tank lorry, no artificial light capable of igniting flammable vapour may be near the lorry, and no person engaged in the transfer may smoke {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 11}}. More on this is on the page about [unloading a tanker](@safety-tanker-unloading).

## What the sources do not say

- Who may be authorised to smoke inside a service station, and where, is for the licensing authority. We found no list of such places.
- We did not open the Petroleum Act 1934 or any OISD standard, so this page gives no penalty and no standard's wording.
- We could not read the Cigarettes and Other Tobacco Products Act 2003 on an official site that day, so this page does not say whether it applies at a pump.

## What to do

In practice, do not smoke or light anything anywhere on the forecourt, and follow the outlet's signs. A phone is a separate question, covered on the page about [mobile phones at a pump](@safety-mobile-phone). Signs are covered in [safety signs](@safety-signage). This is practice, not a rule we can cite.
