Petrol gives off vapour. A vapour recovery system catches it instead of letting it into the air. Where it is required depends on the city, the pump's monthly sale and when the pump was built, as on 9 November 2026.

## What are Stage I and Stage II?

In plain words, Stage I sends the vapour from the underground tank back into the tanker while it unloads. Stage II catches the vapour at the nozzle while a vehicle's tank is filled.

## Where is a vapour recovery system required?

| Which pumps | What is required | Source |
|---|---|---|
| All pumps in Delhi | Stage I and Stage II devices; time extended on 28 September 2018 | {{Supreme Court order 14 Mar 2023, quoting NGT OA 147 of 2016}} |
| All pumps in Delhi and NCR | Directed by the NGT and Supreme Court; CPCB reported Stage IB and II installed at all of them (8 outlets in its table had stopped selling petrol) | {{CPCB report to NGT, Jan 2021}} |
| Existing pumps selling more than 300 KL a month, in 46 cities of a million people or more | CPCB directions of 12 and 22 February 2016 | {{CPCB report to NGT, 13 Aug 2021}} |
| Pumps selling more than 100 KL a month in such cities, and more than 300 KL a month in cities of 1 to 10 lakh | CPCB's recommendation to the NGT; revised directions of 18 September 2020 set timelines, revised again in July 2021 | {{CPCB report to NGT, 13 Aug 2021}} |
| New pumps | A city above 1 lakh people and sale potential of 300 KL petrol a month; or a city above 10 lakh and 100 KL a month | {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, D.1 and D.2}} |

## What must a pump keep in order?

1. Stage II nozzles need a flexible cover flap or another way to cover the filling tank {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, D.3}}.
2. The oil marketing companies are responsible for maintaining installed systems and keeping records {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, D.4}}.
3. The dispenser is linked to the system. If it stops, it must be working again within 24 hours, and in any case within 72 hours, or petrol sales stop {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, D.5}}.

In practice, tell your oil company the day a fault shows.

## What is not required everywhere?

The directions and guidelines we opened name pumps by city size, monthly petrol sale and date of building. They do not name every pump in India, so ask your oil company or the State Pollution Control Board whether yours is covered. Newer orders may exist.
