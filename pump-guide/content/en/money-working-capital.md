Working capital is the cash a pump keeps moving: it buys fuel first, sells it over the following days, and is paid later for part of what it sells. Figures are as on 14 November 2026.

## Why is cash needed before sales come in?

The pump must buy a tanker load before any of it is sold. The June 2023 guideline makes the candidate responsible for the working capital to operate the outlet, with the amount indicated in the location's advertisement {{Selection brochure June 2023, cl.8(C)}}. In practice the oil company is paid before or on delivery, but terms differ, so ask your company.

## What ties cash up?

In practice, four things:

1. **Stock in the tank.** Fuel paid for but not yet sold.
2. **Customer credit.** Fuel given on credit is cash not yet received.
3. **Card settlement.** Card sales usually reach the bank a day or more later; ask your bank.
4. **A price change.** The same load costs more when the price rises.

## A worked example, made up

These numbers are made up. The tank holds 20 kilolitres (KL) and the price is Rs 100 a litre. That is not today's price: replace it with the price on your own invoice.

| Step | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| One full load | 20,000 litres x Rs 100 | Rs 20,00,000 |
| Price rises Rs 2 a litre | 20,000 x Rs 2 | Rs 40,000 more for the same load |
| Credit sales not yet paid | Rs 1,00,000 given on credit | Rs 1,00,000 not in hand |

The example shows how cash is held up; it says nothing about any pump's results. The commission is a separate payment ({{PPAC dealer commission table}}; see [commission](@money-commission)).

## What does the guideline say about help?

For SC/ST locations under the Corpus Fund Scheme, the guideline provides working capital assistance for 7 days of sales and at most 18 KL each of petrol and diesel at first {{Selection brochure June 2023, cl.9}}. The assistance and its interest are recovered in monthly instalments from the 13th month {{Selection brochure June 2023, cl.9}}. Other locations are not covered by that clause.

For a loan, ask a bank. For how to treat it in accounts, ask a chartered accountant. For payment terms, ask the oil company. This page gives no loan or tax advice.
