## What did the Government decide?

On 27 June 2002 the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas announced that cell phones should be switched off at retail outlets, because they are a potential ignition source for flammable vapours that may be present {{PIB 27 Jun 2002}}.

The release adds that OISD examined the issue and advised that phones be switched off at petrol pumps, CNG and LPG stations, with a caution notice at the outlet {{PIB 27 Jun 2002}}. Oil companies were asked to brief staff and put up signs {{PIB 27 Jun 2002}}. This is a 2002 decision. As on 18 November 2026 we did not find a newer one on the sites listed below.

## What do the licence rules say?

The Petroleum Rules 2002 do not use the words mobile phone. Rule 11 says that, unless the rules provide otherwise, nobody may smoke, and no matches, fires, lights or articles capable of causing ignition of petroleum are allowed near a place where petroleum is stored or handled {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 11}}.

A petrol pump's licence (Form XIV) adds two conditions at the pump:

1. Petroleum is not put in a vehicle while its engine is running {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 15(a)}}.
2. People connected with the vehicle may not smoke while it is refuelled {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 15(b)}}.

## What the sources do not say

- The MDG 2024 text we read says nothing about customers' phones {{MDG 2024, clause 1.6(vii)}}.
- We found no fine for a customer who uses a phone. Ask the oil company or an advocate.
- We read no OISD standard text, so we cannot say what it requires.

## What to do

In practice, put the phone away while you fill, and follow the outlet's notice and its staff. This is practice, not a rule we can cite.
