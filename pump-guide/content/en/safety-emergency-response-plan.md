This page lists what the rules say a pump must have ready, report and display, as on 25 November 2026. It is not a fire drill or legal advice.

## What must be ready for a fire?

- Two tins or drums of dry sand and two portable foam or dry chemical extinguishers for each pump, kept ready for immediate use {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 20}}.
- A DCP-type extinguisher near the tank lorry while it unloads {{MDG 2024, clause 1.2(i)}} (public sector oil companies).
- No smoking in a service station {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 117(1)}}, and no fire, furnace or light that can ignite vapour {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 117(3)}}.

See [fire extinguishers](@safety-fire-extinguishers) and [smoking](@safety-smoking).

## What about a leak?

Care must be taken to stop petroleum escaping into any drain, sewer or public road {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 19}}. More in [spill rules](@safety-spill).

## Who must be told?

| Event | Who is told |
|---|---|
| Accident, fire or explosion with loss of life or serious injury to person or property | At once, the nearest Magistrate or police station officer, and the Chief Controller of Explosives {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 22}} |
| Notice of accident under section 27 of the Act | Forthwith to the Chief Controller by telephone, fax, email or other electronic means, and to the nearest police station {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 200(1)}}; {{Petroleum Amendment Rules 2024, rule 200}} |

Until the Chief Controller's visit or word, wreckage and debris are left untouched, except to rescue or recover people {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 200(2)}}. The visit is to be made not later than seven days from the notice {{Petroleum Amendment Rules 2024, rule 200}}.

## What must be displayed?

- Contact details of the authorised persons to call in a complaint, grievance or emergency: MDG 2024 lists their non-display as a minor irregularity {{MDG 2024, clause 5.1.17}} (public sector oil companies).
- An extract of the main rules and the licence conditions, in a conspicuous place {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 134}}.

For emergencies the national number is 112 {{MHA ERSS page}}.

## Is there a written emergency plan standard?

OISD lists OISD-STD-227, Emergency Response and Preparedness in Oil & Gas Industry, edition August 2023 {{OISD standards list}}. Its title does not say it is for retail outlets. Ask your oil company whether it applies.
