A spill is a fire risk first and a pollution problem second. What a pump must do is set by its PESO licence and, for newer pumps, by CPCB's guidelines, as on 15 November 2026. No step below makes a site safe; follow your oil company's instructions.

## A small spill at the nozzle

The licence conditions that apply here:

1. No fuel goes into a vehicle with its engine running, and no one smokes during refuelling {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 15}}.
2. Petroleum must not escape into a drain, sewer or public road {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 19}}.
3. Each pump keeps two tins or drums of dry sand and two foam or dry chemical extinguishers ready {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 20}}.

In practice: stop the dispenser, keep vehicles from driving through the puddle, cover it with dry sand, sweep the used sand into a closed container, and do not hose it into a drain.

## A spill while a tanker unloads

No light that could ignite vapour may be near the tanker during the transfer, and no one smokes {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 11}}. In practice, stop the unloading, close the valves and keep customers away until it is clear.

## A suspected tank or pipeline leak

CPCB's guidelines for new pumps say a major spill is more than 1 barrel, 165 litres. The oil company reports it to the State Pollution Control Board, PESO and the district administration within 24 hours, and the tank stays out of use until PESO and the Board accept the repair {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, A.3}}. For a leak that reaches soil or groundwater, fuel is removed from the tank at once and PESO's anti-explosion measures are followed {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, B.3}}. These guidelines are for new pumps; ask your oil company what applies to yours.

## Who must be told

- An accident or fire with loss of life or serious injury to a person or property goes at once to the nearest Magistrate or police station and to PESO's Chief Controller of Explosives {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 22}}.
- A letter of particulars follows to the Chief Controller within 24 hours, and wreckage stays untouched unless it must move to rescue people {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 200}}.
- In practice, also call the fire service and your oil company's officer.

OISD's standard for storage, handling and dispensing at retail outlets is listed on its site as OISD-STD-225, October 2010 {{OISD standards list}}. We did not read its text; ask your oil company.
