A pump beside a national highway needs the road authority's permission to connect to the road, as well as the PESO licence. Figures are as on 15 November 2026, from the MoRTH circular of 26 June 2020. MoRTH amends these norms, so check the latest circular first.

## Who gives permission, and for which roads?

The power to permit access to a national highway lies with the Highway Administration under the Control of National Highways (Land and Traffic) Act 2002 {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, para 2.2}}. "Fuel station" here covers petrol and diesel outlets, CNG outlets, EV charging stations and bio fuel pumps {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, para 2.3}}. These norms are for national highways. For a state highway, ask the state PWD and the oil company which rules apply.

## How is it applied for?

1. Send a self-certified proposal to the authority the highway is entrusted to: NHAI project director, NHIDCL general manager or deputy general manager, or the executive engineer of the state PWD's national highway wing {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, para 2.3}}.
2. After the processing and provisional fees are paid, the application is handled in the online system only {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, para 2.5.1}}.
3. The whole online process is meant to finish within 45 days, not counting time taken to answer queries {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, para 2.5.2}}.
4. Build the outlet and its access within 12 months of provisional permission, then apply online for final permission {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, para 2.6}}.

## What does it cost?

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Processing fee, non-refundable | Rs 10,000 |
| Provisional permission, non-refundable | Rs 20,000 |
| Bank guarantee, valid three years | Rs 2,50,000 |
| One-time licence fee | Rs 2,50,000 |

All from {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, para 2.11}}. Renewal of the permission after 15 years needs the processing fee only {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, para 2.11}}.

## Distance and plot norms

| Distance from | Rural stretch | Urban or hilly stretch |
|---|---|---|
| Intersection or median gap | 300 m | 100 m |
| Toll plaza or railway crossing | 1000 m | 1000 m |
| Start of ROB approach | 200 m | 200 m |
| Another fuel station | 300 m undivided road, 1000 m divided | 300 m |

Source for the table: {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, Appendix-I}}. Start of a flyover approach: 300 m on both. Rural stretches also need a 100 m acceleration lane and a 70 m deceleration lane; urban and hilly roads may skip them {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, Appendix-I}}. A town of 20,000 people or more (census 2011) counts as urban {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, Appendix-I}}. 

Minimum plot, frontage by depth: 35 m by 35 m on rural plain stretches, 30 m by 30 m urban, 20 m by 20 m hilly {{MoRTH circular 26.06.2020, Appendix-I}}. A relaxation needs the approval of the Minister for Road Transport and Highways {{NHAI Chapter 14 note, para 1c}}.
