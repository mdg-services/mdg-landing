Fuel reaches your vehicle in three steps. The tanker empties into a buried tank, a pump draws the fuel out, and the dispenser measures it into the vehicle. Each part has its own rules and its own checker, as on 9 November 2026.

## How does one fill work, from tanker to vehicle?

Steps 1 and 5 follow the public sector oil companies' guideline (MDG 2024); the rest follow the licence conditions.

1. Before unloading, the dealer checks the tanker's seals and the product, and stops sales from the receiving tank until it is unloaded {{MDG 2024, 1.2}}.
2. The fuel enters the tank through a close-coupled, electrically continuous hose {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 9}}.
3. The tank is gas-tight and outside any building. Its fill and dip pipes go nearly to the bottom, and it has its own vent pipe at least 4 metres high {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, conditions 1, 2, 4 and 5}}.
4. A pump draws the fuel out. The pipe between tank and pump runs underground {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 12}}.
5. The dispenser's meter counts the litres. Before each delivery it goes back to zero, and the attendant shows the zero to the customer {{MDG 2024, 1.4.2}}.
6. The fuel flows through a sound hose with a secure tap and nozzle into the vehicle {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 13}}. The engine must be off, and nobody in or with the vehicle may smoke {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 15}}.

## What are the parts, and who checks each one?

| Part | What it does | Who checks it |
|---|---|---|
| Buried tank | Holds the fuel; water-pressure tested before first use and after a major repair {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 126}} | PESO ([tanks](@equipment-tanks)) |
| Dispenser's electrics | Motor, switches and wiring need the Chief Controller of Explosives' approval {{PESO SOP on Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 102}} | PESO |
| Meter | Counts litres; re-verified every 24 months {{LM (General) Rules 2011, rule 27}} {{G.S.R. 905(E), 18 Dec 2025}} | State Legal Metrology ([measurement](@equipment-metrology)) |
| Hose and nozzle | New pumps: automatic cut-off nozzle and a breakaway on each hose {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, A.4 and A.5}} | Oil company, PESO |
| Fire gear | At least two tins or drums of dry sand and two portable extinguishers for each pump {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 20}} | PESO ([safety](@safety)) |
| Vapour recovery | Catches petrol vapour at some pumps | CPCB, oil company ([vapour recovery](@equipment-vrs)) |

## Do petrol and diesel nozzles differ in colour or size?

We did not find an Indian rule or standard that fixes nozzle colours or sizes for petrol and diesel. The public sector oil companies' guideline asks the dealer to display the product name on each nozzle of a multi-product dispenser {{MDG 2024, 5.1.17}}. For nozzle specifications, ask your oil company.
