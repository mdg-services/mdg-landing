The stock sits underground, so it is read through the tank. Two methods are in use, as on 17 November 2026.

## What is dipping?

Dipping is the manual method. A dip rod goes down the tank's dip pipe, which is carried nearly to the bottom {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 4}}. You read how high the fuel wets the rod, then use the tank's dip chart to turn that height into litres. Water settles under the fuel, so a dip with water-finding paste also shows water.

The public sector guideline uses the dip rod in three places {{MDG 2024, paras 1.2 and 1.3}}:

- A tank lorry's rod must carry the Weights and Measures mark, and the dealer checks dips in every compartment and checks for water with the paste.
- Sales from that underground tank are stopped before decanting starts.
- A daily record of product and water dips of each tank is kept, taken in the morning.

## What is an automatic tank gauge?

An automatic tank gauge (ATG) is a probe fixed inside each tank, joined by cable to a console in the office. One oil company's 2017 tender described a system that measures product level, water level, temperature and density, with equipment approved by PESO and calibrated against the dip chart and dip rod at commissioning {{BPCL ATG tender 25 Jul 2017}}. That is a 2017 purchase document.

## What do the rules ask for?

| Point | Source |
|---|---|
| Automation with auto gauging to alert on a tank leak at all new outlets, with a PESO-approved system | {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, A.10}} |
| Overfill alarm through automation at all retail outlets | {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, A.11}} |
| Daily volume-balance report, records kept; manual gauging once a month compared with the ATG | {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, B.1 and B.2}} |
| ATG off or not working without company authorisation is an irregularity (public sector) | {{MDG 2024, para 5.1.16}} |

The CPCB points were written for new pumps; ask your oil company and State Pollution Control Board whether they reach an older one. In practice, a dip and the ATG should agree closely; when they do not, check the probe, the chart and the dip first. For reading a dip, see [what is dip](/guides/stock-variation/what-is-dip/).
