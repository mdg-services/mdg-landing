The answer is one line in the Constitution, as on 26 November 2026. For what a pump pays GST on, see [GST at a petrol pump](@running-gst).

## What does the Constitution say?

The 101st Amendment of 8 September 2016 brought in GST, and its Article 279A(5) said the GST Council shall recommend the date on which GST is levied on petroleum crude, high speed diesel, motor spirit (commonly known as petrol), natural gas and aviation turbine fuel {{101st Amendment Act 2016, art 279A(5)}}. So these five are inside GST in principle, but not in practice until that date. The Petroleum Ministry put it the same way in a written reply in the Rajya Sabha {{PIB, Petroleum Ministry, 26 Jun 2019}}.

## Which taxes stay in the meantime?

The amendment rewrote two entries of the Seventh Schedule. The Union keeps duties of excise on these goods, and the States keep taxes on their sale, outside inter-State trade {{101st Amendment Act 2016, s.17}}. In practice, the price of petrol carries central excise and State VAT; see [the price build-up](@money-price-build-up) and [prices by state](@money-price-differs-by-state).

## Why does it need the Council?

The Council is chaired by the Union Finance Minister and has a Minister from each State Government as a member {{101st Amendment Act 2016, art 279A(2)}}; the Petroleum Ministry adds that States and Union Territories with a legislature are all represented {{PIB, Petroleum Ministry, 26 Jun 2019}}. A decision needs at least three-fourths of the weighted votes: the Centre has one-third of the votes and all States together two-thirds {{101st Amendment Act 2016, art 279A(9)}}. So neither the Centre nor the States alone can set the date. The sources we opened give no reason for the delay.

## What is the position today?

The latest government statement we could open is the Rajya Sabha reply of 26 June 2019 {{PIB, Petroleum Ministry, 26 Jun 2019}}. We could not open a newer Parliament answer, and found no date recommended. Any change would come as a Council recommendation, so check the Council's and CBIC's sites. Ask a chartered accountant how it affects your pump.
