People often ask whether every petrol pump must stay open 24 hours, or from what time to what time a pump runs. In the papers we read, the answer is: the rules fix no number of hours. They ask that the hours the pump keeps are shown to customers. This page uses the public sector oil companies' guideline (MDG 2024); a private company's pump should ask for its own. As on 21 November 2026.

## What do the guidelines say about working hours?

| Point | What it says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Display | The outlet's working hours should be prominently displayed | {{MDG 2024, 1.6}} |
| Holidays | Holiday notices should be displayed well in time | {{MDG 2024, 1.6}} |
| Free air | Provided to customers during the operating hours of the outlet | {{MDG 2024, 1.6}} |
| HPCL list | Display of working hours is a mandatory facility at every retail outlet | {{HPCL retail outlets brochure}} |

None of these sets an opening or closing time, and none says 24 hours. We found no clause on hours in BPCL's dealer selection brochure either.

## Can a pump sell at night?

Yes, the licence does not bar it. The PESO licence allows petroleum to be supplied to a vehicle between sunset and sunrise if the pump and the vehicle are well lit, and no light that can ignite vapour is inside the hazardous area {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 14}}. Filling the underground tanks from a tanker at night is a separate matter, allowed only on the terms endorsed on the licence {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 10}}.

## So who decides a pump's hours?

The sources do not say. They ask the pump to show its hours; they do not say what the hours are. In practice a pump keeps the hours its outlet runs, and some stay open round the clock; no document we read promises this for any pump. To know one outlet's timing, read its board or ask the oil company. If there is no board, you can raise it through the complaint number shown at the pump; see [customer rights](@customers).
