## The short answer

As on 22 November 2026, a pump's licence (Form XIV, Petroleum Rules 2002) bars filling a container that is not clamped or fitted to a motor vehicle {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 16}}. For diesel there is an exception: filling petroleum Class B into an approved container of up to 200 litres {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 16(iv)}}. Petrol has no such exception; see [petrol in a bottle](@customers-petrol-in-bottle).

## Is diesel Class B?

The rules sort petroleum by flash point; Class B is 23 degrees C and above but below 65 degrees C {{PESO Petroleum Rules SOP}}. PESO describes high speed diesel as Petroleum Class B {{PESO parking SOP}}.

## What the licence says for diesel

| Point | Source |
|---|---|
| Class B may be filled into an approved container of up to 200 litres | {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 16(iv)}} |
| No vehicle with its engine running within 4.5 metres of the container and the dispensing pump | {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 16(iv)}} |

## Which container?

A diesel container over 5 litres must be of a type approved by the Chief Controller {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 4(1)}}. Containers for Class B are of steel or iron {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 6(1)}}, with an air space of at least 5 per cent {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 6(2)}}. The rule does not mention plastic. So a bottle or plastic can is not what the rule describes.

## Keeping diesel at home

For storage, PESO says no licence is needed for Class B kept non-bulk up to 2,500 litres, in receptacles not exceeding 1,000 litres {{PESO Petroleum Rules SOP}}. This is about storing, not buying.

## What the rules do not settle

This page covers the Petroleum Rules only. A local authority may have its own order, and the pump and its oil company decide how they apply the licence. We have not checked any local order. In practice, ask the pump first. For legal questions, ask an advocate.
