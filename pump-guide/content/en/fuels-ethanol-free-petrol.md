## What has the Ministry said?

On 10 July 2026 the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas published a Q&A on the Ethanol Blended Petrol Programme {{PIB Q&A 10 Jul 2026}}. One question was why consumers do not get the choice of pure petrol, E10 or E20. The Ministry's reply is about supply: India operates over one lakh retail outlets, and maintaining several grades of base petrol across refineries, terminals, depots, pipelines and outlets would add handling cost and complicate inventory {{PIB Q&A 10 Jul 2026}}.

## What blend is sold now?

The Ministry's table shows blending of 10.0 per cent in 2021-22 and 20 per cent for the ethanol supply year 2025-26, November to June 2026 {{PIB Q&A 10 Jul 2026}}. As on 24 November 2026, PPAC's home page shows 20.00 per cent for November 2025 to September 2026 {{PPAC home page, ethanol blending}}.

## What about older vehicles?

The Ministry says that in some vehicles there may be a 3 to 5 per cent reduction in fuel economy {{PIB Q&A 10 Jul 2026}}. It also says a manual that reads "E10 compatible" reflects the fuel standard when the vehicle was certified {{PIB Q&A 10 Jul 2026}}. For your own vehicle, ask its maker or dealer.

## Premium petrol

The Ministry calls premium fuels niche products, sold in limited quantities at a significant price premium because performance-enhancing additives are blended in; they are not separate nationwide base fuel streams {{PIB Q&A 10 Jul 2026}}. That passage does not say whether a premium grade contains ethanol, so we make no claim either way. See [premium petrol](@fuels-premium-petrol).

## What we could not confirm

We found no primary source naming any outlet or grade as ethanol-free, so this page lists none. If you need to know what a pump sells, ask the oil company that owns it.
