Fire gear at a pump is set mainly by the PESO licence. Figures are as on 15 November 2026, from the Petroleum Rules 2002 as published on PESO's site.

## What does the licence require?

Form XIV is the licence for a pump's tanks. Its condition 20 says the licensee must provide for each pump, kerb side or portable, at least two tins or drums of dry sand and two portable foam type or dry chemical type fire-extinguishers, kept ready at a convenient location {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 20}}.

That is the licence minimum per pump. We found no official count for the whole outlet in the rules. Where the outlet has more, the figure comes from the oil company's checklist, so ask the company.

Example, made up: an outlet with four pumps counts the minimum per pump, so eight extinguishers and eight tins or drums of sand under this condition.

Other duties in the licence and rules:

- No smoking in a service station except in places the licensing authority authorizes {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 117(1)}}.
- No fire, furnace or other source of heat or light that could ignite vapour, except in authorized places {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 117(3)}}.
- Where auto LPG or CNG is dispensed, the licences under the gas rules must also be complied with {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 21}}.
- A fire with loss of life or serious injury to person or property is reported at once to the nearest Magistrate or police station officer {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 22}}.

## Which types, and why not water?

The licence names foam type and dry chemical type. The BIS product manual lists the types made under IS 15683 : 2018: water based, foam based, powder, carbon dioxide, clean agent and water mist, in sizes such as foam 2 to 9 litres, powder 1 to 12 kg and carbon dioxide 2 to 5 kg {{BIS product manual IS 15683}}. The extinguisher is rated for a class of fire, and the manufacturer declares the rating {{BIS product manual IS 15683}}. In practice, check that the label says it suits oil fires. A water jet on burning petrol usually spreads it.

## Who checks them, and how often?

We found no official interval in the sources we opened. OISD-STD-225, "Storage, Handling & Dispensing at Petroleum Retail Outlets", is dated October 2010 on OISD's list {{OISD standards list}}. OISD has a Purchase of Standards page for the text. The oil company's inspection checklist and the PESO inspector set the checks in practice. Ask the company for the current list.
