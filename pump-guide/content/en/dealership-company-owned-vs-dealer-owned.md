Three short names describe who owns a petrol pump and who runs it. This page uses the public sector selection brochure of June 2023 (hosted on Bharat Petroleum's site) and a petroleum ministry circular, as on 15 November 2026. Nothing here is a promise of a dealership.

## What do CODO, DODO and COCO mean?

| Type | Land and building | Who runs it | How it is chosen |
|---|---|---|---|
| DODO, dealer owned dealer operated | Dealer develops the land and structure; the company provides pump, tank, automation, signage | Dealer | Draw of lots |
| CODO, company owned dealer operated | Company takes the land on lease or purchase and fully develops it | Dealer | Bidding, or draw of lots for Corpus Fund sites |
| COCO, company owned company operated | Company | Company, through its own officers | No dealer is selected |

The first two rows come from {{OMC dealer selection brochure Jun 2023}}, the last from {{MoPNG circular 6 Sep 2006}}. The brochure writes "corporation owned" and calls these sites A, CC or CFS; the short form CODO is common usage.

## How do the fees differ?

The brochure sets these figures, which hold only for the public sector companies:

- A dealer-owned site pays a non-refundable fixed fee of Rs 5 lakh (rural) or Rs 15 lakh (regular), including GST, within 15 days of receiving the NOC {{OMC dealer selection brochure Jun 2023}}.
- A company-owned site chosen by bidding has a minimum non-refundable bid of Rs 10 lakh (rural) or Rs 30 lakh (regular), including GST {{OMC dealer selection brochure Jun 2023}}.
- The refundable security deposit does not depend on site type; it depends on category, for example Rs 5 lakh for a regular outlet in the open category {{OMC dealer selection brochure Jun 2023}}.

Check the current brochure and the advertisement for each location, since amounts can change.

## Why is a COCO outlet not a dealership?

The ministry's circular says permanent COCO outlets are run by the oil company's own officers, and that temporary COCO outlets, run only until a dealer is appointed, should be phased out preferably within a year {{MoPNG circular 6 Sep 2006}}. The circular is from 2006, so ask the oil company how it runs COCO outlets today.

A COCO service provider cannot also become a dealer without first surrendering that appointment {{OMC dealer selection brochure Jun 2023}}.

Dealerships come only through the oil companies' own advertisements. Paying anyone else for one is a sign of fraud, and MDG Services does not sell or arrange dealerships. Ask an advocate or chartered accountant before committing money or land.
