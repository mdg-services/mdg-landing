Unloading, or decanting, a tanker is governed by the Petroleum Rules, and the oil company's guideline adds quality and quantity checks. The checks below are from the public sector oil companies' guideline (MDG 2024); a private company's pump should ask for its own. As on 19 November 2026.

## What do the Petroleum Rules require?

- **Place.** A tank vehicle may be unloaded only at a place inside licensed premises that is approved for it. The exception is an accident or breakdown, with precautions against fire and supervision {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 76(1)}}.
- **Attendance.** The vehicle must be attended by a person who knows the rules until valves are shut and the filling pipe and faucets are closed {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 76(2)}}.
- **Engine and battery.** Engine stopped and battery isolated before unloading {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 79}}.
- **Wheels.** Secured by brakes or scotching {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 80}}.
- **Hoses and earthing.** Sound, electrically continuous hoses or metal pipes only {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 78(3)}}.
- **No ignition.** Nobody unloading may smoke or carry matches, lighters or anything that can ignite {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 31}}.
- **Right product.** Choose the correct hose and avoid contaminating one class of petroleum with another {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 81(1)}}.
- **Night.** Approved electric lighting only {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 83}}, with fire-fighting facilities and personnel ready {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 32}}.

## What does the oil company's guideline add?

The guideline tells the dealer to do these, here shortened {{MDG 2024, 1.2}}:

1. Park on level ground and allow 10 minutes to settle. Ensure earthing and bonding, and keep a DCP fire extinguisher near the tanker.
2. Check the seals and locks on the delivery manifold and manhole covers against the invoice.
3. Check the density at 15 degrees C is within 3.0 kg per cubic metre of the invoice density.
4. Dip every compartment with the Weights and Measures certified dip rod, and test for water with water finding paste.
5. Stop sales from the tank being filled until unloading ends.
6. Draw bottom samples for the retention sample. If the driver refuses to sign, do not decant, and call the supply location or sales officer {{MDG 2024, 2.3}}.
7. Decant into the correct underground tank, make sure the tanker is fully emptied, and enter the stock register.
8. Dispense at least 50 litres, then take a nozzle sample and record the density.

A short dip or water found is made up through the dispensing pump and noted on the invoice copies, signed by dealer and driver {{MDG 2024, 1.2}}. See [density checks](@running-density-check), [earthing](@safety-earthing) and [spills](@safety-spill).

## What is not covered here?

OISD lists a standard for storage, handling and dispensing at petroleum retail outlets, OISD-STD-225, dated October 2010, but its text was not read for this page. Ask your oil company which OISD documents apply to your pump.
