This page explains the nozzle in plain words and states only what the rules we opened say, as on 15 November 2026.

## What are the parts of a nozzle?

1. The spout, which goes into the vehicle's tank.
2. The lever, squeezed to open the valve.
3. The hose from the dispenser, which a pump's licence says must be sound and fitted with a secure nozzle {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 13}}.
4. The breakaway coupling, a hose joint that parts if a vehicle drives off with the nozzle in place {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, A.5}}.

## How does the automatic cut-off work?

In plain words: air is drawn in through a small hole near the spout's tip while the tank fills. When rising fuel covers the hole, a pressure change snaps the nozzle's valve shut. In practice, topping up after the click can cause a spill.

The rule: all dispensing units of new pumps shall have auto cut-off nozzles that stop fuel when the customer's tank is full {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, A.4}}. BIS has a committee panel on automatic nozzles at petrol filling stations; we found no published Indian standard number for it {{BIS MED 38 panel page}}.

## Why do petrol and diesel nozzles differ?

In practice, the diesel spout is usually wider, so a petrol car's narrower filler neck does not take it easily. We found no rule or standard that fixes spout sizes or colours in the sources we opened, so ask your oil company.

## Where is a vapour-recovery nozzle used?

Where the rules require Stage II vapour recovery, the nozzle must have a flexible cover flap or another way to cover the filling tank {{CPCB new pump guidelines 7 Jan 2020, D.3}}. See the [vapour recovery page](@equipment-vrs).

## Who checks nozzles?

- The dealer checks every nozzle's delivery and looks for leaks in the hose and nozzle daily, under the public sector oil company guideline {{OMC dealer guidelines 2024, 1.4.2(d) and (e)}}.
- Petrol and diesel dispensers are among the measures verified by Legal Metrology Government Approved Test Centres, with a fee of Rs 5,000 for each nozzle as on 15 November 2026 {{Legal Metrology GATC Amendment Rules 2026, G.S.R. 346(E)}}.
