This page explains the parts of a dispenser in plain words, as on 17 November 2026.

## What is a fuel dispenser, in the rules?

The Legal Metrology rules call it a measuring system for refuelling motor vehicles, small boats and small aircraft {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}. The smallest system has a meter, a gas elimination device, a filter and a pumping device {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}.

## The four parts, one by one

1. **Pump.** It pushes fuel from the underground tank through the dispenser. When the dispenser has its own pump, a gas elimination device must sit just before the meter inlet {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}.
2. **Meter.** It measures the liquid passing through, remembers the volume and displays it. It has at least a sensor (the measurement transducer), a calculator and an indicating device {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}.
3. **Pulser.** In practice, this is the name used at pumps for the part that turns the meter's movement into electrical pulses. The Rules do not use the word. They say the sensor's signals may be pulses, each standing for a small fixed volume, and that the dispenser must check the sensor is present and working {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}.
4. **Display.** The calculator turns the signals into volume, and into price where a price display is fitted.

## What the rules say about the display

- The dispenser must have a way to reset the volume display to zero; the figures must be at least 10 mm high, and the price figures at least 4 mm {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}.
- After the nozzle is replaced, the next delivery is blocked until the display is reset to zero {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}.
- An electronic dispenser may take no more than 500 ms between a measured value and the value shown {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}.
- It must be possible to interrupt a delivery {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}.
- After a power failure, an interrupted delivery cannot be continued if the failure lasted more than 15 seconds {{LM (General) Rules 2011, 8th Sch Pt IV}}.

## Who checks the dispenser?

Dispensers are verified every 24 months for petrol and diesel {{G.S.R. 905(E), 18 Dec 2025}}. A Government Approved Test Centre may verify them at Rs 5,000 for each nozzle as on 17 November 2026 {{G.S.R. 346(E), 8 May 2026}}.
