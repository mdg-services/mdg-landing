## What counts as tampering?

As on 24 November 2026, these rules are from the guideline for public sector oil company dealers (MDG 2024). A private company's dealer should ask that company for its own rules.

1. Any mechanism, fitting or gear in the dispensing unit that is likely to manipulate delivery {{MDG 2024, clause 5.1.4}}.
2. Addition, removal, replacement or manipulation of any part, including a mechanism, gear, microprocessor chip, electronic parts or OEM software {{MDG 2024, clause 5.1.4}}.
3. A Weights & Measures seal that is missing, replaced by a different seal, or has a broken wire {{MDG 2024, clause 5.1.2(b)}}.

For a seal, the Weights & Measures opinion is final, and penal action can follow even if delivery is found correct or excess {{MDG 2024, clause 5.1.2(b)}}. For a dispenser fitting, the original equipment manufacturer's opinion is taken {{MDG 2024, clause 5.1.4}}.

## What happens to the pump?

Sales through that unit are suspended, the unit is sealed, and samples of all products go to a lab {{MDG 2024, clause 5.1.4}}. These irregularities are classed as critical, and termination at the first instance is the stated action {{MDG 2024, clause 8.2}}. For how termination works see [the agreement termination page](@dealership-agreement-termination).

## What does the law say?

The Legal Metrology Act 2009 punishes whoever tampers with or alters any weight or measure to deceive {{LM Act 2009, s.26}}; the term includes a measuring instrument {{LM Act 2009, s.2(w)}}. Tampering with or removing an official stamp is punished by imprisonment of not less than six months {{LM Act 2009, s.44(1)}}.

## What can a customer do?

In practice, note the date, pump, nozzle and amount, and keep the receipt. Write it in the complaint book {{MDG 2024, clause 1.6(iv)}} and use the routes on [the short delivery page](@customers-short-delivery). A customer cannot open a dispenser; leave that to the oil company and Legal Metrology. Ask an advocate for legal questions.
