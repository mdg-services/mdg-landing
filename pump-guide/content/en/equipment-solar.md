## What the oil companies report

As on 22 November 2026, we could open published counts from two public sector oil companies. Each one is the company's own figure, for its own network and its own date.

| Company | Reported count | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| HPCL | 22,353 outlets with solar panels, 94 per cent of its network | Annual report 2024-25 | {{HPCL Annual Report 2024-25}} |
| HPCL | 95 per cent of the network complete | 31 March 2026 | {{HPCL investor presentation Q4 FY26}} |
| BPCL | 12,244 of 23,642 outlets solarised | Report for 2024-25 | {{BPCL BRSR 2024-25}} |

HPCL says it installed panels at 4,735 outlets in that year alone {{HPCL Annual Report 2024-25}}, and lists 100 per cent solarisation of its outlets as a goal {{HPCL investor presentation Q4 FY26}}. BPCL's count is a little over half of its outlets. It also reports solar lighting at 7,563 outlets as a separate figure {{BPCL BRSR 2024-25}}.

## Why the figures differ

The two companies report on different dates, on networks of different size, and they do not say what size of plant counts as "solarised". So do not add the numbers up to get a national figure. We have not quoted a count for Indian Oil, or any total for all companies. We could not open a primary source for either today. A Parliament answer or a press release with a newer all-company count may exist; if you need one, search the petroleum ministry's replies on sansad.in.

## What this means for a dealer

BPCL says it offers its dealers subsidies as an incentive to solarise {{BPCL BRSR 2024-25}}. It gives no amount in that report. In practice, whether a pump can fit panels, who pays and how the power is used are matters between the dealer and the oil company. We give no cost or saving figure, because none of the sources we opened states one.
