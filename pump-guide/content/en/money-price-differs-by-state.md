## Why does the price change at a state border?

The Central Government levies excise duty on petrol and diesel, and state governments levy VAT or sales tax. Excise duty is a fixed amount per litre; most states charge VAT as a percentage of the price {{PIB 19 Sep 2020}}. Each state decides its own rate and adds its own cesses, so the tax on a litre differs. For the other parts of the price see [the price build-up](@money-price-build-up).

## How much do state taxes differ?

These are the petrol rows from PPAC's table of actual state taxes, which PPAC says is based on details from the oil companies (the PPAC page footer showed 23 November 2026) {{PPAC state tax table}}:

| State or UT | Petrol: VAT or sales tax and levies |
|---|---|
| Andaman and Nicobar Islands | 1% |
| Lakshadweep | 10% VAT |
| Tamil Nadu | 13% plus Rs 11.52 a litre |
| Delhi | 19.40% VAT |
| Goa | 21.5% VAT plus 0.5% green cess |
| Maharashtra | 25% VAT plus Rs 5.12 a litre additional tax |
| Telangana | 35.20% VAT |

Several states charge a percentage or a fixed amount a litre, whichever is higher, and add cesses; the full list of every state and diesel is on the PPAC page {{PPAC state tax table}}. A state's rate alone does not give its price.

## What do the prices look like?

PPAC's table of metro prices, posted 20 November 2026, shows these prices in rupees a litre {{PPAC metro RSP table}}:

| City | Petrol | Diesel |
|---|---|---|
| Delhi | 102.12 | 95.20 |
| Mumbai | 111.21 | 97.83 |
| Chennai | 107.77 | 99.55 |
| Kolkata | 113.51 | 99.82 |

These are the prices on that date, not a forecast, and we do not rank states.
