This page says what the Petroleum Rules 2002 state, as amended on 4 March 2024, as on 23 November 2026. It gives no design figures. A licensed electrician and your oil company decide what is fitted at your pump. It is not legal advice.

## What is a hazardous area?

An area is hazardous where petroleum with a flash point below 65 degrees C, or any flammable gas or vapour in a concentration capable of ignition, is likely to be present {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 103}}. It is split into three zones by how often vapour is expected:

| Zone | Vapour is expected |
|---|---|
| 0 | continuously {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 104}} |
| 1 | under normal operating conditions {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 104}} |
| 2 | only under abnormal conditions or failure of equipment {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 104}} |

If there is a question about the zone, the Chief Controller of Explosives decides {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 104}}. The Fourth Schedule gives the extent for a service station: the pit and the inside of a dispenser are zone 1, and the outdoor area around it zone 2 {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Fourth Schedule, Part C}}. The distances are in the Schedule on the PESO site.

## Why do fittings differ by zone?

The rules allow only specified apparatus in each zone {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 106}}:

- **Zone 0:** no electric apparatus, except approved intrinsically safe apparatus where it cannot be avoided.
- **Zone 1:** flameproof, pressurised, powder-filled, oil-immersed, increased-safety, intrinsically safe or encapsulated apparatus, conforming to the IS/IEC 60079 parts the rule lists {{G.S.R. 154(E), 4 Mar 2024}}.
- **Zone 2:** non-sparking apparatus conforming to IS/IEC 60079-15, or any type allowed in zone 1 {{G.S.R. 154(E), 4 Mar 2024}}.

Selection follows the equipment protection level in IS 16724:2018 / IEC 60079-14 {{G.S.R. 154(E), 4 Mar 2024}}.

## What else do the rules say?

- Wiring in a hazardous area must be sealed at joints, mechanically protected and of the listed cable types {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 107}}.
- Each electric pump circuit has its own fuse or circuit breaker and an individual isolating switch {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 107}}.
- Apparatus is kept as approved, and a flameproof unit is not opened until its supply is cut off {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rules 112 and 113}}.
- A competent person who is an electrical engineer certifies the installation first and after each repair or alteration; the occupier keeps the certificate {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 114}}, {{G.S.R. 154(E), 4 Mar 2024}}.
- The certification label must stay readable {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 115}}.

For earthing see [earthing and bonding](@safety-earthing); for the oil industry's own area standard and others see [OISD standards](@safety-oisd-standards). OISD lists OISD-STD-113 on area classification, as a general standard {{OISD standards list}}.
