This is what an attendant does and which rules apply. All as on 17 November 2026. The tasks differ from pump to pump, and the rules below come from the licence conditions and the public sector oil companies' guideline.

## Rules that bind the attendant

| Rule | Source |
|---|---|
| Engine off: no petrol into a vehicle while the engine is running | {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 15}} |
| No smoking by a person in or connected with the vehicle being refuelled | {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 15}} |
| No smoking, fire or light that can ignite vapour in a service station, except in authorised places | {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 117}} |
| Fill only into containers clamped or fitted to a vehicle, bar narrow exceptions | {{Petroleum Rules 2002, Form XIV, condition 16}} |
| Meter back to zero after each delivery, and the zero shown to the customer before the next | {{MDG 2024, para 1.4(f)}} |
| Uniform and badge for staff at the driveway (public sector) | {{MDG 2024, para 5.1.17}} |

## What a shift looks like

In practice, an attendant's day runs like this. None of it is a rule unless tagged above.

1. Before sales start, check that each nozzle's product name and the zero sticker are in place and the meter reads zero.
2. Ask the driver to switch off the engine, and ask passengers to stay clear.
3. Show zero, fill the amount or value asked, close the nozzle and hang it back.
4. Say the amount, take payment, and give a bill if the customer asks.
5. Report leaks, a nozzle that does not stop, or a meter that does not reset to the dealer or manager at once.

The dealer is asked to check every nozzle for correct delivery before sales each day; a unit that delivers short is reported to Weights and Measures and not run until re-stamped {{MDG 2024, paras 1.4(d) and 5.1.2}}. Staff also need training: oil companies are asked to train dealers' staff in safe handling and courteous behaviour {{MDG 2024, para 3.3}}. For how a customer can complain, see [customers](@customers).
