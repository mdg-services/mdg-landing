There is one clear sentence about uniform in the public sector oil companies' guideline (MDG 2024), and not much more. This page says what it states, what it leaves open, and what to ask your oil company. A private company's pump should ask for its own guideline. As on 21 November 2026.

## What does the guideline say about uniform?

The guideline's list of irregularities includes "Driveway Salesmen at the ROs not in uniform/wearing badges" {{MDG 2024, 5.1.17}}. "RO" is the retail outlet. So at a public sector pump the dealer is expected to have driveway staff in uniform and wearing badges, and it can be recorded as an irregularity when they are not.

## What is the penalty?

The guideline classes it as a minor irregularity {{MDG 2024, 8.4(e)}}. The action is:

| Instance | Action |
|---|---|
| First | Warning-cum-guidance letter |
| Second | Rs 10,000 per irregularity |
| Third onwards | Rs 25,000 per irregularity |

These are minimum actions; the company can take higher action {{MDG 2024, 8.5.1}}. The count of instances runs over five years {{MDG 2024, 8.5.5}}.

## What the papers do not say

- No colour, design, logo or fabric is given in the guideline we read.
- No list of staff designations is given. The guideline names only "Driveway Salesmen". Manager, supervisor and attendant are titles used in practice.
- The Petroleum Rules 2002 have no clause on staff uniform that we could find. Rule 118 asks that operations run under an experienced supervisor who knows the licence terms and has safety training {{Petroleum Rules 2002, rule 118}}.
- HPCL's retail outlets brochure we read does not mention uniform.

Uniform details, if any, usually come from your oil company's circulars or dealer agreement. See [attendant duties](@running-attendant-duties) and [manager duties](@running-manager-duties).
