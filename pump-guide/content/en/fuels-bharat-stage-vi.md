BS-VI is the auto fuel standard implemented in India from 1 April 2020 {{PIB 31 Dec 2020}}. This page explains what changed, as on 26 November 2026.

## What BS-VI is, and when it came

BS stands for Bharat Stage, India's series of emission and fuel standards. Under the earlier policy, BS-II, BS-III and BS-IV quality fuels were introduced in the country by 1 April 2017 {{PIB 25 Jul 2022}}. A committee had proposed BS-V by 2020 and BS-VI from 2024, but the government chose to leapfrog directly from BS-IV to BS-VI from 1 April 2020 {{PIB 25 Jul 2022}}. The road transport ministry announced that decision on 6 January 2016 and said BS-V would be skipped altogether {{PIB 6 Jan 2016}}.

Delhi came first. The petroleum minister launched BS-VI fuel there on 2 April 2018, instead of waiting for April 2020 {{PIB 2 Apr 2018}}. The ministry's year-end review for 2020 records that BS-VI auto fuel was implemented in the country from 1 April 2020 as per schedule {{PIB 31 Dec 2020}}.

## What changed in the fuel

| Point | BS-IV | BS-VI |
|---|---|---|
| Permitted sulphur | 50 ppm | Maximum 10 ppm {{PIB 25 Jul 2022}} |

The minister called this a fivefold, 80 percent cut in sulphur {{PIB 2 Apr 2018}}. We are not quoting other fuel properties because we could not open the BIS fuel standards for this page; the standard to check is on the BIS site.

## Why it matters for vehicles

The lower sulphur made advanced emission control possible: diesel particulate filters to cut particulate matter and SCR systems to cut nitrogen oxides in BS-VI vehicles {{PIB 25 Jul 2022}}. That is why many BS-VI diesels use [AdBlue](@fuels-adblue). The minister also said BS-VI fuel improves emissions from older vehicles, but full benefit needs BS-VI vehicles {{PIB 2 Apr 2018}}.

For the grades your pump sells today, see [premium petrol](@fuels-premium-petrol) and [diesel grades](@fuels-diesel-grades).
