Many pump owners ask whether CCTV is compulsory. This page says what we could and could not find in official texts on 19 November 2026. A point with no source is left out, and nothing here is legal advice.

## What did we check?

We searched the full text of four official documents for the words CCTV, camera and surveillance:

- The public sector marketing discipline guideline for dealers {{MDG 2024}}.
- BPCL's dealer selection guidelines {{BPCL DSG 2023}}.
- The Petroleum Rules 2002 on the PESO site {{Petroleum Rules 2002}}.
- HPCL's customer brochure on retail outlets {{HPCL retail outlets brochure}}.

None of them contains those words. The brochure has a list of mandatory facilities that every outlet must provide: free air, display of working hours, display of the name and telephone number of oil company personnel, a first aid box, a toilet, and safety equipment as per statutory requirements, such as fire extinguishers and sand buckets {{HPCL retail outlets brochure}}. Cameras are not on it.

## What about OISD standards?

OISD, the Oil Industry Safety Directorate, writes safety standards. OISD lists six of its standards as included in the Petroleum Rules 2002: numbers 105, 116, 117, 118, 141 and 156 {{OISD statutory list}}. None is titled for a petrol pump. We did not read the standards themselves, so we cannot say what they say about cameras.

## What should you still check?

A state or city order, or a term in your own dealer agreement, may ask for cameras. We did not search every state and city order, so this page cannot say that none exists. Ask your oil company's sales officer for the terms of your agreement, and your local police or district office whether an order applies. See [automation at the pump](@equipment-automation) and [the manager's day](@running-manager-duties) for the checks that are rules.
