## What each one is

This page is for a business that gives goods on credit to regular business customers.

| Option | What it is | In your favour | The catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security deposit | Money the customer hands over before credit starts, kept as cover | The cover is already in your hands | It ties up the customer's cash, and you must return it as agreed |
| Post-dated cheque (PDC) | A cheque dated for a later day, usually the day a bill falls due | The customer commits to a date | The account must hold the money that day. A bounce starts short deadlines |
| Security cheque | A cheque kept only as cover for dues that may arise later | Simple to ask for | It may not be treated like a cheque for a debt that is due {{NI Act 1881, s.138}} |

If a cheque bounces, see [cheque bounce: steps and time limits](@paid-cheque-bounce). Whether the cheque bounce law covers a security cheque is for an advocate to advise.

## What to write down

**A deposit:** a receipt both sign, with the customer's name, amount, date, how it was paid, what it covers, when it will be returned and whether you may set it against an unpaid bill.

**A cheque:** the number, bank, date on the cheque, amount, the drawer's name, the day you received it and the bill it relates to. Say why you hold it: to pay a bill, or only as cover. That choice may change how the law treats it, so ask an advocate first. Photograph both sides.

## Cheques have a clock

A cheque must be presented within its validity: 3 months in practice {{NI Act 1881, s.138}}. Present a PDC soon after its date. A cheque kept in a drawer for a year may be too old to present.

## Keep it apart from the khata

A deposit is not a payment against a bill. Keep its receipt apart from the customer's account. If you later set it against an unpaid bill, record that then as a payment received, with the date.

The limit is still yours to set. See [the credit policy](@credit-policy) and [the udhar agreement format](@credit-agreement). Khata Kavach never lends and never holds money, so a deposit or cheque stays with you. It keeps the account both sides can see, and checks the limit at the counter before goods go out.

General information, not legal advice. For your own case, speak to an advocate or a chartered accountant.
