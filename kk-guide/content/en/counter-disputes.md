This page is for a business with regular credit customers. A quarrel over one sale costs time and trust, so settle it from the record, not from memory.

## Settle it in five steps

1. **Stay calm and pull the record.** Date, time, vehicle or pickup code, item, quantity, rupees, who served, who collected, the request it was made against, and the photo or slip number.
2. **Read it out together.** Go line by line with the customer. Ask what differs: the quantity, the rupees, the vehicle or the day.
3. **Show the customer the same line.** In Khata Kavach the customer sees it on their own phone as soon as the phone is online, with the staff member's name and the photo if the counter added one.
4. **If the record is wrong, correct it openly.** Write a new line that cancels the old one, keep the old one visible, and write the reason. Never overwrite.
5. **If the record is right, explain it and keep the line.** Note what was agreed so it is not raised again.

A made-up example: the line says 200 L of diesel at Rs 90 a litre, Rs 18,000. The customer says the truck took 180 L. The photo of the slip reads 180 L, so staff typed the requested figure by mistake. The correction cancels Rs 18,000 and adds 180 L, Rs 16,200. Owed falls by Rs 1,800.

## Stop the next one

- **Request first.** The customer sends a request, with a quantity or a rupee amount, before the goods go out. For a pickup code, the customer passes it only to whoever collects.
- **A photo with the sale.** It is optional, one per sale, and helps a quick answer.
- **A slip number.** Optional too, for those who still keep slips.
- **The customer sees each line.** Once the phone is online, they can raise a doubt that day, not at month end.

## In Khata Kavach

A wrong sale shows up on both phones, with who served and the photo if one was added. After a correction, the old line stays marked struck out and the new line sits below it. The customer sees both. Where goods go by pickup code, the line can say who collected, such as \"Taken by Raju\", and the bill shows it.
