## The steps and their clocks

This page is for a business whose regular business customer gave a cheque against credit, and it came back.

| Step | Time limit | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Present the cheque to the bank | Within its validity: 3 months in practice | {{NI Act 1881, s.138}} |
| Bank returns it unpaid (not enough money, or more than the arranged amount) | You get a return memo | {{NI Act 1881, s.138}} |
| Send a written demand to the customer | Within 30 days of the bank's return memo | {{NI Act 1881, s.138}} |
| The customer's time to pay | 15 days from receiving your demand | {{NI Act 1881, s.138}} |
| File the complaint | Within 1 month after those 15 days end | {{NI Act 1881, s.142}} |

File only after the 15 days have ended.

## What may fall outside

The offence needs a cheque given for a legally enforceable debt. A security cheque, or a disputed or time-barred debt, may fall outside. The result depends on the facts of your case.

## After the cheque bounces

Record it as not paid: cheque number, date, bank and the reason on the memo. Stop new credit until the account is clear: see [when to stop credit](@paid-stop-credit). The wider route is on [the legal steps page](@paid-legal-steps).

In Khata Kavach, an owner or manager can reverse a payment entered by cheque, with a reason. The old line stays, and the bill it was recorded against shows as due again. See [how a wrong entry is corrected](@feature-corrections).

General information, not legal advice. For your own case, speak to an advocate or a chartered accountant.
