## Two ways to keep udhar

A ledger app copies the old bahi khata onto a phone. The seller writes each credit given and each payment received, and the app keeps every customer's balance. [OkCredit](https://okcredit.in/) is one example: its homepage says the customer does not need the app and gets the balance by SMS.

A shared credit book has two apps. The business uses one, the customer the other, and every sale is one line that both see.

## Side by side

| | Ledger app (OkCredit as the example) | Shared credit book (Khata Kavach) |
|---|---|---|
| Who types the entry | The seller; its homepage says the customer needs no app | A business app and a customer app |
| How the customer sees a sale | By SMS; its Google Play listing says balances are shared after every transaction | One line in the customer's own app, with who served and the photo if one was added |
| Who starts a credit sale | The seller makes the entry; we did not find a customer request on its website or Play listing | By default, the customer's request, per vehicle or with a pickup code; no request, no credit |
| Limit at the counter | We did not find a limit setting on its website, pricing page or Play listing | Green, amber or red; by default a manager's PIN to go past it |
| Price as published | A free Basic plan with ads; paid plans Rs 30, 75 and 99 for 30 days | Call us; the price depends on your business |
| Languages, platforms | 11 languages; Android, iOS, web | Hindi first, English one tap away; phone browser today |
| Works with no signal | Yes, per its listing | At the counter, once opened with internet |
| Business loans | Offers loans through partners, per its listing | No. It never lends and never holds money |

## Which suits whom

- **A shop with walk-in families:** the owner sees every sale, and a ledger app may be enough. OkCredit, for one, lists a free Basic plan, 11 languages and an iPhone app.
- **A business with regular credit customers:** staff serve while the owner is away, so the owner wants the customer to ask first and both to see the line. A shared book is built for that.
