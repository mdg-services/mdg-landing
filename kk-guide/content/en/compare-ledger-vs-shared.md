## Two ways to keep udhar

A ledger app copies the old bahi khata onto a phone. The seller writes each credit given and each payment received, and the app keeps every customer's balance. [OkCredit](https://okcredit.in/) is one example: its homepage says the customer does not need the app and gets the balance by SMS.

A shared credit book has two apps. The pump uses one, the transporter the other, and every fill is one line that both see.

## Side by side

| | Ledger app (OkCredit as the example) | Shared credit book (Khata Kavach) |
|---|---|---|
| Who types the entry | The seller; its homepage says the customer needs no app | A Pump app and a Transporter app |
| How the customer sees an entry | By SMS; its Google Play listing says balances are shared after every transaction | One line in the transporter's own app, with who filled and the photo if one was added |

## Which suits whom

- **A pump with a few credit customers:** the owner sees every fill, and a ledger app may be enough. OkCredit, for one, lists a free Basic plan, 11 languages and an iPhone app.
- **A pump with regular transporters on credit:** nozzle men fill while the owner is away, so the owner wants the transporter to ask first and both to see the line. A shared book is built for that.

The line between the two is not fixed: myBillBook's site says it shares live ledgers. See how [Khatabook](@compare-khatabook) and [OkCredit](@compare-okcredit) compare in full, and how [transporter requests](@feature-requests) work.
