## Should you give udhar at all?

This page is for a petrol pump that gives diesel and petrol on credit to its regular customers: a transporter whose trucks fill every day, a fleet owner, a bus or tractor owner, a contractor whose machines fill at your pump. It is not about lending money to a friend.

At a pump, credit is common. Regular customers send their own truck with their own driver, and pay later. A pump that says no to all credit may find that some of them fill at the next pump.

Credit has a cost. Your money stays with the customer until he pays, and some customers pay late. So the useful question is not "credit or no credit". It is "credit on what rules".

## The five decisions

Every pump that gives credit makes the same five decisions, written down or not. Make each one once, on purpose, and each fill stops being a fresh worry.

| Decision | What to decide | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Who gets credit | Which transporters, and what you check before saying yes | [Who to give credit to](@credit-who) |
| How much | One limit in rupees for each transporter | [How to set a credit limit](@credit-limit) |
| For how long | The credit period (7, 15 or 30 days) and when new credit stops if it runs late | [When to stop credit](@paid-stop-credit) |
| Who may say yes | Who at the nozzle can allow a fill past the limit, and who cannot | [Over the limit](@counter-over-limit) |
| How it is recorded | The same things written for every fill: truck number, litres, rupees, who filled, time | [Credit at the nozzle](@counter) |

Put the five answers on one page, in plain words, so the whole team applies them the same way. The [credit policy template](@credit-policy) shows how. For plain meanings of the words, see the [credit and khata glossary](@glossary).

## Why the stress comes from not knowing

Most of the worry in udhar is not the credit itself. It is not knowing: what a transporter owes today, whether the nozzle man gave more than was asked, who allowed the last fill over the limit. [Seven habits that take the stress off](@credit-without-stress) are built from the five decisions above.

## Where a shared credit book helps

Khata Kavach is a shared credit book between a petrol pump and its regular transporters. By default, the transporter asks from his own phone before each truck fills. The nozzle man's phone shows a green, amber or red light against the limit you set. Both of you see every fill, payment and bill as soon as the phone is online.

You set every limit. Khata Kavach never lends and never holds money: the transporter pays you directly, and you record it.

It is for petrol pumps, of any oil company, that give credit to regular customers who fill their own trucks and vehicles. See [diesel on credit: the pump's side](@trade-petrol-pump).
