This page is for a petrol pump whose regular customers send drivers to fill their trucks on credit.

## Three people, one fill

The transporter owes the money. The driver stands at your nozzle. Your nozzle man fills one truck after another and cannot phone the transporter for each.

| Who | Where it slips |
|---|---|
| Transporter | May find out what each truck took only when your bill comes |
| Driver | Asks for a full tank when the transporter meant Rs 5,000 |
| Your nozzle man | Cannot tell what the transporter meant for this truck today |

## What each side needs

You need four things:

1. The transporter's say-so for **that truck, today**.
2. A cap: litres, a rupee amount, or a full tank.
3. A record the transporter also sees: truck number, litres, rupees, who filled, time, and a photo if you add one.
4. A rule for a truck that is not on the transporter's list.

The transporter needs one thing: to see what each fill took, on his own phone, as soon as it is online.

## How a request does this

The transporter sends it from his own phone, one per truck: litres, rupees, or a full tank. At the nozzle, the nozzle man types the last 4 digits of the truck number. He then sees a light: green "You can fill" when the truck has a request; red "No credit" when there is no request ("You can fill for cash") or the truck is not on the list. The fill shows on the transporter's phone with the nozzle man's name. If he types more than was requested, the line carries a mark, and the transporter sees it.

For example (made-up): Sharma Roadlines sends a request for truck UP00 SH 1005, 200 litres. The driver pulls in, the nozzle man types 1005 and sees green. He fills 200 litres at Rs 90 a litre, Rs 18,000, and types both figures off the dispenser. Both phones show the line.
