## The steps, in order

This page is for a petrol pump that gave diesel on credit to regular customers such as transporters, not for money lent to a friend.

| Step | When | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Check your own record | Before anything else | Bills, signed slips, payment entries and the balance. See [what counts as evidence](@paid-proof) |
| 2. Written demand (legal notice) | Reminders have failed | The amount, the bills, a date to pay, and a way to show it was sent. Usual practice; a cheque-bounce complaint needs one {{NI Act 1881, s.138}} |
| 3. Mediation | A commercial dispute of Rs 3 lakh or more, before filing | Mediation must be tried first for a commercial dispute of Rs 3 lakh or more {{Commercial Courts Act 2015, s.12A}}. Ask an advocate whether your dispute is covered |
| 4. Summary suit | A written contract or bill supports a fixed sum | A faster route {{CPC, Order XXXVII}} |
| 5. Ordinary suit | A summary suit does not fit your case | An advocate drafts it; mediation may still come first (step 3) |

Whether your dispute is a "commercial dispute" is a question for an advocate.

## Interest

Interest on late payment is safer when agreed in writing before the first credit fill, for example in bill terms the transporter accepted. Where nothing was agreed, a court decides {{Interest Act 1978, s.3}}. Put it in your [credit terms](@credit-policy) from the start.

## What we do not cover

Court fees, how long a case takes and small-causes court limits vary, so we give no figures. Whether a khata alone is a written contract is not settled. No step guarantees payment, and each has time limits of its own: see [the time limit to recover dues](@paid-time-limit). A registered micro or small supplier may also have a payment-time rule on its side, though whether a pump qualifies is doubtful: see [the MSME 45-day rule](@paid-msme). Plain meanings of notice, mediation and summary suit are in the [glossary](@glossary).

Calm reminders come before all of this: [udhar wapas kaise le](@paid-udhar-wapas). Public shaming or threats can backfire. If in doubt, ask an advocate. Keep every message polite.

Khata Kavach does not send notices or collect dues. It keeps the account both sides can see: every fill, numbered bills, and each payment you record.

General information, not legal advice. For your own case, speak to an advocate or a chartered accountant.
