## What the sheet has

| Tab | What you type | What it works out |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Name, phone, credit limit, credit days (for your reference), balance from the old book | Total udhar given, total received, balance now, credit left |
| Khata | Date, customer (picked from the list), vehicle (or who collected), item, quantity and unit, udhar given or money received, how paid, UTR or cheque number, slip or bill number, who served, note | The customer's balance after every line |
| How to use | Nothing | Short steps and what the colours mean |

Yellow cells are for typing; grey cells work out by themselves. On the Customers tab a row turns red when the balance is over the limit, and orange when less than a fifth of the limit is left. On the Khata tab the balance turns red on a line that took the customer over. The colours follow the limit only, not late payments.

A box at the top of the Customers tab counts Khata lines whose customer is not on the list, so a misspelt name is caught instead of quietly dropping out of a balance. The sheet holds 200 customers and 3,000 lines. The rows in italics are a made-up example: delete them, or type over every cell including the Note.

## How to use it

1. Add each customer on the Customers tab, with their limit and the balance from your old book. To pick a limit, use the [credit limit calculator](@tool-credit-limit).
2. Write every credit sale and every payment on the Khata tab the day it happens. Pick the customer from the list.
3. Never type over an old line. Cancel it with the same amount as a minus figure in the same column and the reason in Note, then write the right one.
4. Each morning, look at the Customers tab: red and orange rows first, and the name-check box.
5. Each month, filter the Khata tab by a customer's name and send them the lines and the balance.

What each line should show, and why: [how to keep an udhar khata](@credit-khata).

## The printable page

One page per customer, A4 across: date, vehicle, item, quantity and unit, udhar given, money received, how paid with the UTR or cheque number, slip or bill number, who served, balance and the customer's sign. Start from the balance brought forward, write each line when the goods go out, and carry the balance down. Strike a wrong line through and leave it out of the balance.

## What a sheet cannot do

A sheet or a register is your own record. The customer sees it only when you send it, and nothing stops a sale over the limit unless someone checks. Khata Kavach keeps the same lines in a shared book: by default the customer sends a request before goods go out, the counter sees a light for the limit, and both sides see each line as soon as the phone is online. See [a paper khata or an app](@compare-paper).
