## The rule in short

This page is for a business that sells on credit to regular business customers, not for money lent to a friend.

You can claim interest with most confidence when the customer agreed to it in writing before the sale. Where nothing was agreed, a court decides whether to give interest and at what rate {{Interest Act 1978, s.3}}.

## What to write before the sale

- **Rate:** per month or per year.
- **Start day:** for example, the day after the due date.
- **What it applies to:** the unpaid amount of the overdue bill.
- **How it is counted:** simple interest, for the days late.
- **How the customer agreed:** a signed credit form, or terms accepted before the first sale. Keep the signed copy or a written "Agreed".

Show the terms before the first credit sale, not after a bill is late. A form to start from: [the udhar agreement format](@credit-agreement). The wider terms are in [the credit policy](@credit-policy).

## A worked example

Made-up figures. A bill of Rs 50,000 falls due on 30 June and is paid on 30 July, 30 days late. The agreed rate is 12% a year, simple. The rate is only an illustration, not a suggestion.

Interest = Rs 50,000 x 12% x 30 / 365 = about Rs 493. The [late-payment interest calculator](@tool-late-interest) does this sum for any rate you enter.

## Registered micro and small suppliers

A registered micro or small supplier has a separate right to interest on late payment from its buyer {{MSMED Act 2006, s.16}}. Whether a trader such as a fuel station or a dealer qualifies is doubtful. Quote it in your reminders only if you hold Udyam registration and have checked. See [the MSME 45-day rule](@paid-msme).

## Before you reach for interest

Interest does not bring the bill back; it adds a second amount to chase. A credit period and a limit decide how many bills turn late, so set those first: [how to set a limit](@credit-limit) and [when to stop credit](@paid-stop-credit). For a late customer, start with [a calm reminder](@paid-reminder-messages), then the [legal steps](@paid-legal-steps) if needed.

Khata Kavach keeps the account both sides can see: every sale, numbered bills and each payment you record. It adds no interest and collects no dues.
