## The rule in one table

| Point | What it says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Who it protects | A micro or small supplier with a valid Udyam registration | {{MSME Samadhaan portal}} |
| Payment time | By the agreed date, at most 45 days from acceptance of the goods or services; 15 days if nothing was agreed in writing | {{MSMED Act 2006, s.15}} |
| Late interest | Compound interest, with monthly rests, at three times the RBI bank rate | {{MSMED Act 2006, s.16}} |
| Where to claim | The government's [MSME ODR portal](https://odr.msme.gov.in/) | {{MSME Samadhaan portal}} |
| The buyer's tax | If the buyer pays after the limit, its tax deduction for money owed to a micro or small supplier is allowed only once it is actually paid | {{Income-tax Act, s.43B(h)}} |

The Income-tax Act has been rewritten, so the section number may have changed. Ask a chartered accountant.

## Who may not be covered

- **Traders.** In 2021 retail and wholesale trade was allowed on Udyam "for the limited purpose of Priority Sector Lending" {{RBI circular, 7 Jul 2021}}. Some writers read this to mean traders are outside the payment rule. We found no court ruling or official statement either way. So a fuel station or a dealer should not assume it is protected as a seller. Ask a lawyer.
- **Registration date.** Courts have differed on whether Udyam registration must come before the contract.
- **The 2026 amendment.** A 2026 amendment may change how claims are heard. Check the portal for the current steps before you file.

## Who may gain most

A registered micro or small transporter or contractor who supplies services to a larger firm may gain most, if they qualify: see [transporters](@trade-transporter) and [contractors](@trade-contractor). If you are the buyer, this is the rule your own registered micro and small suppliers may rely on.

A Khata Kavach [bill](@feature-bills) can carry a due day, and the Bills screen shows what is past due. That makes your own dates easy to check. Khata Kavach does not collect dues.

General information, not legal advice. For your own case, speak to an advocate or a chartered accountant.
