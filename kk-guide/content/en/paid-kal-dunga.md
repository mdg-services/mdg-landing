This page is for a business that sells goods on credit to regular business customers, not for money lent to a friend.

## What "kal dunga" usually means

- The money is not there yet, and the customer is embarrassed to say so.
- They are waiting for their own customer to pay them.
- A line in the account looks wrong to them, and they have not said so.

Before anything else, ask: "Is any line in the account wrong?" Reminders cannot fix a dispute you do not know about.

## What to do each time

| The promise | What to do |
|---|---|
| Not missed yet | Ask for a date and an amount, not "kal": "Which date, and how much?" Send it back in a message the same day. Take any part payment now. |
| Missed once | Call the same day and ask what stopped it. Agree a new date and a part payment. Send the statement. |
| Missed twice | Stop new credit until the dues are cleared. Cash sales stay open. Say it kindly, and put the part-payment plan in writing. |
| Missed three times | Send the firm letter from [the letter formats](@paid-outstanding-letter), changing its last lines to say credit is already stopped, then the final one if needed. After that see [the legal steps](@paid-legal-steps). |

Decide the rule before the next sale: see [when to stop credit](@paid-stop-credit).

## Words to say

- "Kal ka matlab kaunsi tareekh? Aur kitna aayega?"
- "Aaj Rs 10,000 de dijiye, baaki ki tareekh likh lete hain."
- "Aapke hisaab mein koi line galat lag rahi hai kya?"
- "Naya udhar pichla clear hone par. Cash par maal aaj bhi milega."

## A made-up example

Made-up figures. Gupta ji owes Rs 48,500 (Bill 14, due 25 October) and says "kal". You ask for a date and an amount: Rs 20,000 on 28 October. He misses it. You call, and he agrees Rs 20,000 on 2 November. He misses that too: the second miss, so new credit stops and you send the statement.

## Write it down

Send the date and amount back in one message: "Gupta ji, as we spoke: Rs 20,000 on 2 November. Thank you." Keep the chat. Ready words: [the reminder maker](@tool-reminder-maker) and [seven reminder messages](@paid-reminder-messages).

Do not shame the customer in public or send people to his shop. Public shaming or threats can backfire.

A shared credit book such as Khata Kavach shows the customer each sale and the balance, with its date, as soon as their phone is online, so both sides see the same lines. It keeps the account and does not collect dues.
