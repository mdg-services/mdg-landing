## Who can correct what

Mistakes happen at any counter. Khata Kavach does not hide one. It keeps the wrong line, marks it, and adds the right one next to it.

| What went wrong | Who fixes it | How |
|---|---|---|
| Staff typed a wrong sale, less than 2 minutes ago (the default window) | The staff member who made it | "Wrong, fix it", then "Save the fix", or "This sale never happened" |
| A wrong sale, any time after that | Manager or owner | "Correct this sale", with a reason |
| A payment recorded wrongly | Manager or owner | The payment is cancelled; a bill it had paid opens again |
| The sale is already on a bill | Owner | Cancel the bill first, then correct the sale |
| The balance itself is wrong | Owner | "Correct the balance", with a reason |
| A request sent by mistake | The customer | Cancel it and send a new one |

The reason box asks "Why are you correcting it?". The manager or owner then picks "Cancel it and enter the right sale" or "Only cancel it". A sale on a bill shows "This sale is on bill X. Cancel that bill first, then correct it."

## What both sides see

The old sale stays on the account, tagged "Struck out". A new line, "A wrong line was struck out", records the correction, and the right sale follows. The business and the customer see the same lines. A sale that has not yet left the counter phone is fixed or dropped on the phone, before the customer has seen it.

For example (made-up figures): a customer owes Rs 3,00,000. The business types a payment of Rs 50,000 instead of Rs 5,000, so the balance shows Rs 2,50,000. A manager cancels it and the balance goes back to Rs 3,00,000. The manager then records Rs 5,000 and the balance is Rs 2,95,000. All three lines stay on both phones.

## Why this takes the stress off

Once a sale has reached the customer's phone, a correction is a visible new line with a reason. The customer does not have to wonder whether last week's figure was altered. The owner does not have to argue from memory. See [how a disputed sale is settled](@counter-disputes).
