## Six checks before you say yes

Giving credit is your decision, and the time to be careful is before the first sale. This is a checklist for a business that sells to regular business customers.

| Check | What to ask or note |
|---|---|
| 1. How long you have known them | Have they bought from you before? Did they pay in cash and on the spot? Weeks of cash-and-carry first is a fair start. |
| 2. Who may collect, and who owns the vehicles | The names of the people allowed to collect on credit and, if they come by vehicle, the owner's name against each vehicle number. |
| 3. One responsible person | A named person who answers for the account, with a number you have actually called. |
| 4. GSTIN or PAN, where they have one | Note it down. It can go in the customer's billing details and print on the bill (a bill, not a GST tax invoice). |
| 5. A small start | A low first limit, raised after on-time payment. See [how to set a credit limit](@credit-limit). |
| 6. A trade reference | Ask for a reference the customer names, with their OK, and record only what they agreed to share. Never pass names around. |

## What to write down when you agree

Write it before the first credit sale, and give the customer a copy:

- the customer's name and the responsible person's phone number
- who may collect the goods: vehicle numbers or names
- the limit in rupees and the credit period in days
- who at your counter may allow a sale past the limit

The [credit policy template](@credit-policy) turns this into one page. The [seven habits](@credit-without-stress) show how to keep the account afterwards.

## What Khata Kavach adds

You add the customer in the business app: name, mobile number, limit, when you collect, and, if you work by vehicle number, the vehicle numbers. One tap opens your own WhatsApp with their link ready to send. The customer reads the terms and taps "I agree" before they can send a request. Until then the customer cannot send requests, so by default the counter light stays red.

By vehicle number, a vehicle number can be active with one customer at a time. By pickup code, each request gets a 4-digit code that the customer passes to whoever collects. Choose "Trust him" for a customer and staff can give goods on his name with no request, up to his limit. You decide who gets credit; Khata Kavach never lends and never holds money.
