## Six checks before you say yes

Giving credit is your decision, and the time to be careful is before the first fill. This is a checklist for a petrol pump that gives diesel to regular business customers.

| Check | What to ask or note |
|---|---|
| 1. How long you have known them | Have they filled with you before? Did they pay in cash and on the spot? Weeks of cash fills first is a fair start. |
| 2. Which trucks, and who owns them | The truck numbers allowed to fill on credit and, against each number, the owner's name. |
| 3. One responsible person | A named person who answers for the account, with a number you have actually called. |
| 4. GSTIN or PAN, where they have one | Note it down. It can go in the customer's billing details and print on the bill (a bill, not a GST tax invoice). |
| 5. A small start | A low first limit, raised after on-time payment. See [how to set a credit limit](@credit-limit). |
| 6. A trade reference | Ask for a reference the customer names, with his OK, and record only what he agreed to share. Never pass names around. |

## What to write down when you agree

Write it before the first credit fill, and give the transporter a copy:

- the transporter's name and the responsible person's phone number
- which truck numbers may fill on credit
- the limit in rupees and the credit period in days
- who at your pump may allow a fill past the limit

The [credit policy template](@credit-policy) turns this into one page. The [seven habits](@credit-without-stress) show how to keep the account afterwards.

## What Khata Kavach adds

You add the transporter in the Pump app: name, mobile number, limit and his truck numbers. One tap opens your own WhatsApp with his link ready to send. The transporter reads the terms and agrees on his phone before he can send a request. Until then he cannot send requests, so by default the light stays red.

A truck number can be active with one transporter at a time. You decide who gets credit; Khata Kavach never lends and never holds money.
