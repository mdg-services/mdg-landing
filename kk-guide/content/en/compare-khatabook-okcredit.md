## Khatabook and OkCredit at a glance

Khatabook is made by ADJ Utility Apps Pvt Ltd. OkCredit is made by OkCredit Psi Phi Global Solutions Pvt Ltd. Both keep a business's udhar khata on a phone.

## Side by side

| | Khatabook | OkCredit |
|---|---|---|
| Price as published | Its site calls the apps free; ads on Play; its terms reserve the right to charge a convenience fee | Basic plan with ads; paid plans Rs 30, Rs 75 and Rs 99, each for 30 days |
| Customer sees entries | By SMS if the shop switches it on; we did not find a customer app on the pages we could read | By SMS; its home page says the customer needs no app |
| Staff access | Staff access mentioned; we did not find roles on the pages we could read | Several phones on one login on a paid plan; we did not find roles on its website, pricing page or Play listing |

## Where each is stronger

- **Khatabook:** more downloads and reviews on Google Play, and a desktop app on its own (OkCredit lists desktop on a paid plan).
- **OkCredit:** plan prices on its pricing page, offline use stated on its Play listing, a web app.
- **Both:** iPhone apps, GST and non-GST bills, and loans through partners.

## Where Khata Kavach fits

If your customers collect goods by vehicle and you want them to ask first and see each sale, that is the job Khata Kavach does. The customer has their own app and, by default, sends a request for that vehicle before credit is given. Counter staff see a green, amber or red light before goods go out. A correction is a new line that both sides see. It keeps the account: it never lends and never holds money. Price: call us.
