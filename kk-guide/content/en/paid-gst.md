## Two rules, one table

| Who | The rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| You, the registered seller | GST falls due on the supply, not on the payment | {{CGST Act 2017, s.12}} |
| Your buyer | If the buyer does not pay you within 180 days of the invoice, the buyer must reverse the input tax credit claimed, and can claim it again once paid | {{CGST Act 2017, s.16(2)}} |

So you may pay GST on money you have not yet received. Plan your cash for it.

## A made-up example

Made-up figures, and 18% is a made-up rate. A registered seller invoices goods on 10 July 2026.

| Line | Rs |
|---|---|
| Value of goods | 1,00,000 |
| GST at 18% | 18,000 |
| Invoice total | 1,18,000 |

The seller pays the Rs 18,000 for July's period, paid or not. If the buyer has not paid by 6 January 2027 (180 days after the invoice), the buyer must reverse the Rs 18,000 of credit, and can claim it again once paid.

## What this means for you

- **A polite nudge.** If your buyer is GST-registered, you can mention the 180 days as a fact. See [reminder messages](@paid-reminder-messages).
- **Fuel.** Petrol and diesel carry state VAT and central excise, not GST.
- **Your bill.** Khata Kavach's bill is a numbered bill, not a GST tax invoice. See [kachcha bill, pakka bill and tax invoice](@credit-bills), and [billing apps and a credit book](@compare-billing-apps).

## What this page does not cover

Registration limits change: check the current limit. For your own books, ask a chartered accountant, with your invoices in hand.

Every Khata Kavach bill carries its date, so you can see how long each one has been open. It does not file or calculate tax.

General information, not legal advice. For your own case, speak to an advocate or a chartered accountant.
