## Fuel first

This page is for a petrol pump owner whose transporters take diesel on credit. Petrol and diesel are outside GST {{Finance Ministry reply, Rajya Sabha, 21 Jul 2026}}. They carry state VAT and central excise. So for the fuel your pump sells on credit, the two GST rules below do not apply, and this page does not cover the VAT rules. They apply only to what GST does cover.

## Two rules, one table

| Who | The rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A registered seller | GST falls due on the supply, not on the payment | {{CGST Act 2017, s.12}} |
| The buyer | If the buyer does not pay the seller within 180 days of the invoice, the buyer must reverse the input tax credit claimed, and can claim it again once paid | {{CGST Act 2017, s.16(2)}} |

So a registered seller may pay GST on money not yet received.

## A made-up example

Made-up figures, and 18% is a made-up rate. A registered seller invoices a taxable supply on 10 July 2026.

| Line | Rs |
|---|---|
| Value of supply | 1,00,000 |
| GST at 18% | 18,000 |
| Invoice total | 1,18,000 |

The seller pays the Rs 18,000 for July's period, paid or not. If the buyer has not paid by 6 January 2027 (180 days after the invoice), the buyer must reverse the Rs 18,000 of credit, and can claim it again once paid.

## What this means for a pump

- **Diesel on credit.** No GST on the fuel, so no 180-day clock. A transporter's unpaid diesel is handled by your own steps: see [getting paid](@getting-paid).
- **A polite nudge.** For anything GST does cover, if your buyer is GST-registered, you can mention the 180 days as a fact. See [reminder messages](@paid-reminder-messages).
- **Your bill.** Khata Kavach's bill is a numbered bill, not a GST tax invoice. See [kachcha bill, pakka bill and tax invoice](@credit-bills), and [billing apps and a credit book](@compare-billing-apps).

## What this page does not cover

Registration limits change: check the current limit. For your own books, ask a chartered accountant, with your invoices in hand.

Every Khata Kavach bill carries its date, so you can see how long each one has been open. It does not file or calculate tax.

General information, not legal advice. For your own case, speak to an advocate or a chartered accountant.
